Think of an agent‑driven AI like a well‑trained robot chef that can order ingredients, decide what to cook, and finish the dish all on its own.  
Its “stack” has seven layers, each doing a specific job:

1. **Perception** – the sensors (cameras, microphones) that let it see and hear the kitchen.  
2. **Representation** – the recipe book: it stores facts and past experiences in an organized way.  
3. **Planning** – the menu planner: it figures out which steps to take to reach a goal (e.g., “make lasagna”).  
4. **Decision‑making** – the chef’s judgment: it chooses the best step among alternatives.  
5. **Control** – the arms and tools that actually stir, slice, and bake.  
6. **Learning** – the kitchen diary: it updates its recipe book after each cook, improving over time.  
7. **Meta‑control** – the kitchen manager: it monitors overall performance, balances resources, and decides when to ask for help.

Benchmarks test how well each layer works—speed of perception, accuracy of plans, quality of finished dishes—and combine them into a single score that tells you whether your robot chef is truly “agentic” (acting independently toward goals).
