Imagine a chef who can learn recipes in two ways.

**1️⃣ “Tasting the dish first” (Supervised learning)**  
The chef watches a master cook prepare many meals while the master says, *“This is a pizza.”* Each time the chef tastes a finished dish and hears its label, they remember the ingredients and steps that produced it. Later, when the chef sees a new set of ingredients, they can guess which recipe will make a tasty pizza.

**2️⃣ “Exploring flavors on their own” (Unsupervised learning)**  
Here no labels are given. The chef simply samples many dishes, noting patterns—spices that always appear together or textures that feel similar—and groups them into clusters like “savory,” “sweet,” or “tangy.” The chef discovers hidden structures without being told what they’re called.

In both cases the chef learns from data: with labels (supervised) or by finding hidden groupings (unsupervised). This is how machine‑learning models learn to predict or discover patterns.
