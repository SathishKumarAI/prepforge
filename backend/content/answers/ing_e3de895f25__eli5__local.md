Imagine a school hallway where every student has a routine: they walk straight, take the same lockers, and leave at the usual time. **Anomaly detection** is like having a friendly guard who watches for anyone who takes a different path—maybe someone turns left, skips a locker, or stays late. That guard’s job is to spot these odd behaviors because they might signal something unusual: a lost student, a prank, or even a problem in the school.

In machine learning, we train computer “guards” on what normal looks like (the usual walking patterns). The algorithm learns to flag data points that deviate from this pattern—those are **anomalies**. We call them *outliers* because they’re rare and differ from the rest. This technique is used in fraud detection (credit card purchases that look odd), health monitoring (a sudden spike in heart rate), or network security (unusual traffic). The key idea: learn the normal, then flag the unexpected.
