**Case Study 04 – Scaling Semantic Search**

Semantic search replaces keyword matching with *vector similarity*: every document and query is mapped to a high‑dimensional embedding \(v\in\mathbb{R}^d\). Retrieval boils down to finding the nearest neighbours of a query vector, i.e. solving  
\[
\min_{i}\;\|q-v_i\|_2 \quad\text{or}\quad
\max_{i}\; q^\top v_i .
\]

Why this formulation? 1) **Information geometry** – embeddings encode latent semantics as distances in a Euclidean manifold, turning textual relevance into a continuous optimisation problem.  
2) **Probabilistic modelling** – under the assumption that words are generated by a distribution conditioned on a topic vector, the dot product \(q^\top v_i\) approximates log‑likelihood ratios of relevance.  
3) **Computational tractability** – nearest‑neighbour search admits sub‑linear algorithms (LSH, HNSW), essential for millions of vectors.

Scaling hinges on two intertwined optimisations:

* **Compression** – Product Quantisation or IVF‑PQ reduces memory from \(O(nd)\) to \(O(n\log d)\) while preserving cosine similarity within a bounded error.  
* **Parallelism** – Sharding the index across GPUs and using SIMD‑accelerated dot products turns an \(O(n)\) scan into \(O(\frac{n}{G})\), where \(G\) is the number of devices.

A non‑obvious insight: *the trade‑off between quantisation error and search recall is not linear*. Small increases in codebook size drastically reduce collision probability, yielding super‑linear gains in recall for a fixed memory budget. This “knee” point often lies well below the theoretical optimum, guiding practical system design.
