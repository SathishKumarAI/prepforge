Imagine you’re at a busy pizza joint that can only serve customers through a single counter. A **load balancer** is like a smart waiter who decides which customer goes to which cashier so everyone gets served quickly.

*Hardware‑based load balancers* are the dedicated, heavy‑duty waiters built into the restaurant’s kitchen—specialized machines whose sole job is juggling orders. They’re fast and reliable but expensive, and if you need more capacity you must buy a whole new waiter unit.

*Software‑based load balancers* are like a group of staff members who use a tablet app to share work. They run on the restaurant’s existing computers, so adding more “waiters” is just installing more software copies—cheaper and easier to scale, but they may be slightly slower because they’re sharing the kitchen’s general resources.

In both cases, the goal is the same: keep customers moving smoothly without waiting in line.
