Imagine you’re at a big party where everyone talks to each other through a single hallway.  
A **proxy server** is like a friendly doorman who lets people (your computer) into the hall, takes your request for something (like a website), and then brings back the answer. You never see the inside of the building; the doorman keeps your address private and can block bad visitors.

A **reverse proxy** flips that role: it sits in front of the party itself. Visitors walk up to the hallway, but instead of going straight into the house, the reverse proxy decides which part of the building (which server) should answer. It hides the real layout from guests, balances traffic so no one room gets overwhelmed, and can add extra safety checks.

Both keep things organized—proxy protects you, reverse proxy protects the host.
