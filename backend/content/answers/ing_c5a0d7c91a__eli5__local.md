Imagine you have a huge library where every book is written in a secret language that only computers can read. **Embedding** is like turning each book into a short, colored postcard that captures its main ideas—so the computer can quickly see how similar or different any two books are.

Cohere’s models take text (a sentence, paragraph, or whole document) and transform it into these postcards called *embeddings*. Each embedding is a list of numbers that encode meaning: words about dogs sit close together, while those about cars stay far apart. The model learns this mapping by reading millions of texts, learning patterns like “‘happy’ feels near ‘joyful’” or “‘economy’ is far from ‘art’.”

Once you have embeddings, you can do many things: find the most relevant documents for a question, cluster similar articles, or feed them into another machine‑learning model. In short, embedding turns raw words into a compact, comparable format that lets computers understand and work with language efficiently.
