Imagine an agent as a traveler who must navigate a city. **Context engineering** is like giving that traveler a detailed map and a clear list of the streets that matter at each moment—so they don’t waste time looking in the wrong direction or getting lost.

In machine‑learning agents, the “city” is the world of data and tasks. The agent must decide what information to pay attention to (the useful streets) and how to interpret it (read the signs). Context engineering designs that map: it selects relevant facts, filters out noise, and structures them so the learning algorithm can quickly understand *where* in its environment it is and *what* it should do next. Without this map, the agent spends energy on irrelevant details and may make wrong choices. With good context engineering, it learns faster, reacts better, and stays focused—just like a traveler who knows exactly which streets lead to their destination.
