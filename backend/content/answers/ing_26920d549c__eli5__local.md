**AI‑native vs. “just an app on top of existing IT”**

Think of a company’s tech stack like a kitchen.

* **Infrastructure** is the pantry, stove, fridge, and plumbing—everything that lets you cook at all. In business terms, it’s servers, networking, storage, and the basic operating system. It works for any program but doesn’t know what you’re cooking.

* **AI‑native** means the kitchen itself was built with a recipe in mind: the stove has smart temperature sensors that adjust automatically, the fridge can reorder ingredients when they’re low, and there’s a voice assistant that tells you how long to bake. The tools were designed *for* AI from the start—data pipelines flow directly into models, training happens on‑the‑fly, and inference (making predictions) is baked into the hardware.

During an interview, ask about:
- How the company’s infrastructure supports continuous model training and deployment.
- Whether they use purpose‑built AI services or rely on generic cloud compute.
- If their architecture lets them iterate quickly without rebuilding pipelines every time a new model comes out.

In short, *infrastructure* is the “what” that runs everything; *AI‑native* is the “how” that makes AI the default way of doing things.
