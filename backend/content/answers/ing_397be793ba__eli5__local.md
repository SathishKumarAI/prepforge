Think of a time‑series like a diary that records your daily mood, but instead of feelings it logs numbers—temperature, stock price, heart rate, or traffic volume. In machine learning, the diary’s pages are fed into an algorithm so it can spot patterns: does your temperature spike every Tuesday? Does the stock rise after you hear a particular news headline? The algorithm learns these “habits” and then predicts future entries.

**Time‑series data** = measurements taken at successive, evenly spaced times.  
**Analytics** = using math to find trends, seasonality (repeating cycles), or sudden changes in that diary.  

Just as a careful reader can anticipate your next mood from past patterns, an ML model uses time‑series analytics to forecast tomorrow’s temperature, next week’s sales, or the next click on a website.
