Imagine a smart robot vacuum that keeps your house clean. It is more than just a machine; it *acts* based on what it sees, decides where to go next, and learns from the rooms it has already cleaned. In AI, an **agent** is anything that can do this: perceive its surroundings through sensors, make decisions with some rules or learning, and perform actions via effectors. Think of a chef who watches a pot, chooses the right spice, stirs, and tastes the result to adjust. That chef’s ability to observe, decide, act, and learn makes them an agent. In AI terms, the “environment” is the world they interact with, the “policy” is their decision‑making rule set, and the “percept” is what they sense at each step. An agent exists whenever these four parts—sensing, deciding, acting, learning—are present, even if only in a computer program.
