Imagine you’re cooking a big family meal with a recipe book that’s too long to read all at once. The **Model Context Protocol (MCP)** is like the kitchen rules that let you keep only the most relevant parts of the recipe in your mind while you work.  

- **Agents** are the cooks who decide what needs to be done next—whether to chop, stir, or season—based on what’s happening right now.  
- **Tools** are the utensils and appliances (spatula, stove, blender) that help them act.  

MCP tells each agent: “Look at the current state of the dish, pick out the few most important instructions from the recipe, and then choose a tool to execute the next step.” This keeps the kitchen efficient and prevents cooks from getting lost in an endless scroll of notes. It’s the same idea for AI systems: they keep only the needed context, let agents decide actions, and use tools (APIs, databases) to finish tasks.
