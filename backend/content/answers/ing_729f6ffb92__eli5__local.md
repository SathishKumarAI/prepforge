Imagine you’re a chef trying to create a new recipe for a crowd that loves pizza.  
Your **data model** is the “recipe book” that tells the kitchen what ingredients (features) to use and how much of each (weights).  

Just as a good recipe needs a clear list of toppings, dough type, and baking time, a data model lists all the pieces of information it will look at—age, income, past purchases—and assigns importance to each.  
When you bake many pizzas, you tweak the amounts until the taste is just right; similarly, a machine‑learning algorithm adjusts those importance values (called parameters) so its predictions match real outcomes as closely as possible.  

So, think of a data model as the structured set of instructions that turns raw ingredients—your data—into a finished dish: an accurate prediction or decision.
