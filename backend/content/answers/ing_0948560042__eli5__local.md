Imagine you’re chatting with a friend over text while each of you is typing at the same time. **Turn‑taking** is the rule that says “wait until your friend stops typing before you start.” In conversational AI it means the system decides when to pause its own reply and let the user speak again. **Endpointing** is how the computer tells when the user has finished speaking – like a tiny bell that rings once the voice stops.

**Latency** is the delay between the user’s last word and the AI’s next response. If turn‑taking or endpointing are slow, you feel as if the AI is “thinking” too long; if they’re fast but inaccurate, the AI might cut off your sentence or start talking while you’re still speaking.

Why it matters: Good turn‑taking keeps the flow natural and prevents interruptions, while accurate endpointing keeps responses timely. Together they make conversations feel like real human dialogue instead of a robot that waits too long or talks over you.
