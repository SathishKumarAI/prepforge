Imagine a detective who has to solve many cases at once.  
**Context** is the fresh clues that come into the room right now – a witness’s testimony, a new photo, or a sudden phone call. The detective reads these and instantly decides what matters for the current case.  

**Memory engineering** is like keeping a well‑organized notebook. After each case, the detective writes down only the facts that will help in future investigations—names, patterns, tricks that worked before—and leaves out everything else.

In an agentic AI system, “selective inclusion” means choosing what new information (context) to feed into the model for a specific task and which past data (memory) to keep for later use. The system filters incoming inputs so only relevant clues enter the short‑term reasoning, while it stores useful patterns in long‑term memory for future decisions. This keeps the AI focused, efficient, and ready to learn from its own experience.
