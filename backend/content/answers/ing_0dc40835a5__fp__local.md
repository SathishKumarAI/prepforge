### What Candidates Actually Experience

In AI‑engineering interviews the **process is a carefully engineered feedback loop** that mirrors how production systems evolve: *observe → hypothesize → test → iterate*.  

1. **Observation phase** – candidates first face a “warm‑up” or coding problem, designed to surface their baseline skill set and problem‑solving style.  
2. **Hypothesis phase** – interviewers ask clarifying questions that force the candidate to articulate assumptions (e.g., about data distribution, model capacity). This mirrors how an engineer must justify design choices before committing resources.  
3. **Testing phase** – a live coding or whiteboard exercise evaluates algorithmic reasoning and code quality. The key is that tests are **parameterized**, not fixed; they probe the candidate’s ability to generalize beyond memorised patterns, akin to stress‑testing a model on unseen data.  
4. **Iteration phase** – feedback is given in real time, and the candidate refines their solution. This reflects continuous integration pipelines where models are retrained and validated iteratively.

#### Non‑obvious insight
Interviewers rarely ask *what* you would code; they probe *how* you think about trade‑offs (latency vs accuracy) because the true bottleneck in AI products is not the algorithm but its deployment context. Understanding this hierarchy turns a good engineer into a great one.
