Imagine you’re at a birthday party where some guests are wearing hats and some aren’t.  
You want to know the chance that a person is wearing a hat **if** you already see they’re holding a balloon. That’s *conditional probability*—the likelihood of one event happening given that another has happened.

Think of it like a vending machine: if you press button A, the machine tells you the probability of getting a chocolate bar instead of a candy bar. The “given” part (pressing A) changes the odds compared to just picking randomly from all snacks.

In machine learning, we use this idea to update beliefs about data after seeing evidence—like revising the chance that an email is spam once you spot certain words inside it. It’s simply “probability of X when Y is known.”
