Imagine you’re trying to teach a child to throw a ball into a basket.  
*Bias* is like the child’s **pre‑conception** about how far the ball should travel. If they always aim too hard, every throw misses—too much bias.  
*Variance* is the child’s **inconsistent aim**: sometimes they swing wide, other times just right. High variance means the throws keep changing with each attempt.  

In machine learning we build a “throwing model.” A very simple model (like a straight line) has high bias but low variance—its predictions are steady but often wrong. A very complex model (a wild curve that follows every training point) has low bias but high variance—it fits the training data perfectly but flails wildly on new data.  

The sweet spot is like a child who has learned the right distance and swings consistently: small enough bias to be accurate, but not so much variance that each throw differs too much. That balance is the bias‑variance trade‑off.
