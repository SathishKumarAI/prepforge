Imagine your computer’s memory as a library that can be either a dusty old bookcase or a high‑speed digital shelf.  
A **solid‑state drive (SSD)** is the digital shelf: it stores data on tiny flash chips instead of spinning disks, so reading and writing are almost instantaneous.

SSDs come in a few “flavors” based on how they fit into your computer:

| Type | Where it lives | What you’ll feel |
|------|-----------------|------------------|
| **SATA SSD** | Uses the same 3.5 in or 2.5 in slots as old hard drives, but with a SATA cable. | Faster than an HDD, but still limited by the older interface. |
| **NVMe SSD** | Connects directly to the motherboard’s PCI‑e lanes via a small M.2 slot. | Lightning‑fast; great for gaming or heavy multitasking. |
| **External SSD** | Wrapped in a USB‑C or Thunderbolt case, like a portable flash drive. | Portable power and speed—good for backups or moving large files. |

So an SSD is simply flash memory that gives your computer the speed of a digital shelf, and its type tells you how fast it can talk to the rest of the machine.
