## Why a “recruiter call” is the logical first step

At its core, hiring is an **information‑collection problem**: the firm must discover whether a candidate’s latent skill vector aligns with the job’s requirement vector. A phone conversation is the cheapest, most flexible *measurement* that can reduce uncertainty about this alignment.

1. **Signal extraction** – The recruiter probes background (education, projects) and motivation (why you’re interested). Each answer is an observable sample of your hidden competence distribution.  
2. **Noise filtering** – By asking follow‑up questions the interviewer gauges depth versus surface knowledge, effectively performing a Bayesian update on the posterior probability that you’ll succeed in the role.  
3. **Resource allocation** – The call is a low‑cost filter: only candidates who survive this test are given time for technical interviews or case studies, which are expensive in terms of interviewers’ bandwidth.

### A non‑obvious insight
Most people think recruiters merely “screen” resumes, but the call is actually an *optimization* step. It maximizes expected hiring value by balancing two costs:  
- **False positives** (investing in a bad hire)  
- **False negatives** (missing a great fit)

The recruiter’s line of questioning is therefore designed to tilt that balance toward higher‑quality candidates, not just anyone who checks boxes.

In short, the call isn’t an arbitrary courtesy—it’s a principled, low‑cost experiment that sharpens the hiring pipeline by reducing uncertainty about a candidate’s true fit.
