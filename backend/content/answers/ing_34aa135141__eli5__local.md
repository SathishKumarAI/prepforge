Think of machine learning like teaching a child how to recognize different kinds of fruit.  
You show the child many apples and bananas, telling them “this is an apple” or “this is a banana.” The child watches the shapes, colors, and textures (the data) and starts noticing patterns—apples are round and red, bananas are long and yellow. After seeing enough examples, the child can guess whether a new piece of fruit is an apple or a banana without being told again.

In the computer world, **data** are those pictures or numbers you feed in. A **model** is the child’s memory of patterns; it learns by adjusting tiny internal knobs called **weights** so that its guesses (predictions) get better over time. When you give it new data, it uses what it learned to make a decision—just like the fruit‑recognizing child.

That’s the core idea: show examples → learn patterns → predict new things.
