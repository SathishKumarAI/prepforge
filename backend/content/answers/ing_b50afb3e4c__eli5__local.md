Imagine a recipe book that only has one dish written in it. A smart cook (the LLM) can read that single recipe, taste the dish, and then try to make a new version of it—maybe swapping an ingredient or changing the cooking time. That’s what “learning from a single example” means for large language models: they see one sentence or paragraph, pick up patterns like word order, style, or meaning, and use those clues to guess how other sentences should look.

In practice, LLMs are trained on millions of examples at once, so the one‑example trick is more about fine‑tuning. You give the model a tiny new dataset (even just a single text), and it adjusts its internal weights slightly to better match that style or topic. It’s like adding a pinch of spice after tasting a dish—quick, but not enough to replace the whole recipe.
