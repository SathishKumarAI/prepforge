Imagine you’re cooking a big family dinner in a farmhouse. **Data locality** is like keeping all the ingredients for each dish right where you’ll cook them—so the pot of soup stays near the stove, the salad greens stay in the fridge next to the cutting board, and the dough sits on the counter beside the mixer. In computing, data (the “ingredients”) are stored in different places (servers or storage disks). When a program (the “cook”) needs that data, it’s best if the data is already where the program runs—so the program doesn’t have to travel far over the network. That reduces waiting time and speeds up processing, just as having ingredients nearby lets you cook faster and smoother.
