Imagine you’re hosting a movie night and only invite people who already love cinema. The guests you get will tell you that movies are great, but you’ll never learn about the opinions of people who don’t watch films. That’s just one example of *selection bias* – when the group you study isn’t representative of everyone.

Other common kinds:

1. **Sampling bias** – picking a sample in a way that favors certain people (e.g., surveying only on a college campus).  
2. **Non‑response bias** – those who refuse to answer give you a skewed view (like only hearing the loudest voices at a party).  
3. **Survivorship bias** – focusing on successes while ignoring failures (celebrating only the top‑selling books, not the ones that flopped).  
4. **Attrition bias** – losing participants over time and those who drop out differ from those who stay (a marathon where the early finishers are all strong runners).  

Each type leaves your data leaning toward a particular story, so researchers must use careful methods to keep the picture balanced.
