Think of an AI system as a kitchen where different chefs (agents) can cook meals.  
**Level 1 – Reactive Chef:** The chef only follows the recipe step by step, reacting to the stove’s heat or the timer—no planning.  
**Level 2 – Deliberative Chef:** Before cooking, the chef looks at the pantry, decides which dish fits the guests’ taste, and then follows the steps—this is goal‑driven behavior.  
**Level 3 – Meta‑Chef (Higher Agency):** The chef not only plans meals but also reviews past dishes, learns new techniques, and can change recipes to improve future outcomes.

In AI terms:  
- **Reactive agents** act on current inputs.  
- **Deliberative agents** choose actions based on a plan toward a goal.  
- **Higher‑level agents** learn from experience and adapt their own strategies.  

So agency levels are simply how much an agent can think ahead, plan, and modify its own behavior.
