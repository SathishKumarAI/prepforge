**Context in machine learning** is like the “room” where a conversation happens.  
Imagine you’re teaching a robot how to answer questions about cooking. If you only give it recipes, it learns that *“flour”* means a powder and *“bake”* means heat, but it has no idea whether the kitchen is a bakery or a lab. **Context engineering** is adding those extra clues—what type of kitchen, what ingredients are available, who the audience is—so the robot can tailor its answers appropriately.

In practice, you supply data that tells the model *“This text is from a medical journal”* or *“These numbers come from a weather station.”* By giving it that background, the model interprets words and numbers correctly, just as a person uses room cues to understand meaning. This extra layer of “room details” turns a generic answer into one that fits the specific situation.
