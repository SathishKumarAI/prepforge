Think of machine learning as teaching a child to recognize apples in a basket.  
You show the child many pictures: some with red, round fruit labeled “apple,” others with green or misshapen fruit labeled “not apple.” Each picture is a **data point**—the child’s eye sees color, shape, and size (the *features*). The labels are the correct answers (the *targets*).  

After many examples, the child learns a rule: “If it’s round and red, call it an apple.” That rule is what we call a **model**. When you later show a new picture, the model predicts whether it’s an apple or not without needing explicit instructions—just like the child uses its learned pattern.  

So, machine learning = giving a computer lots of examples so it can learn a useful rule and then apply that rule to fresh data.
