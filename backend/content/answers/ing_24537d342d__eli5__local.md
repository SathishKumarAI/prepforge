Think of your computer network as a big gated town where people (data packets) come and go through a single security checkpoint— the firewall. An IP address is like each person’s unique ID card that says where they’re coming from or going to. “IP address filtering” is the guard who checks those IDs against a list, letting only approved folks in.

Top 6 ways this guard is used:

1. **Block known troublemakers** – deny traffic from bad IPs (like banning a vandal).  
2. **Allow trusted friends** – permit only certain IP ranges (VIP guests).  
3. **Hide the town’s layout** – hide internal addresses so outsiders can’t see them (privacy).  
4. **Stop smuggled goods** – block ports that are often used by malware or hackers.  
5. **Separate traffic streams** – route specific IPs to different servers for load balancing.  
6. **Prevent denial‑of‑service attacks** – limit how many requests one IP can send, so no single user floods the town.

Just as a guard keeps the town safe and organized, IP address filtering keeps your network secure and efficient.
