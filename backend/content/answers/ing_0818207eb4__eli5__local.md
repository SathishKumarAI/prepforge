Imagine you’re teaching a child to recognize apples and oranges. You show many pictures, say “apple” or “orange,” and the child learns patterns: color, shape, size. After enough examples, the child can guess correctly on new pictures.

In machine learning, that child is an **algorithm**—a set of rules written in code. The **data** are all the pictures you feed it. The algorithm’s job is to find hidden relationships (like “round and red → apple”) without being told explicitly. When you give it a fresh picture, it uses those learned patterns to make a prediction.

So, writing an expression like `model.predict(new_image)` is just asking that trained child: “What does this look like?” The magic happens inside the algorithm, which has already memorized how apples and oranges differ from many examples.
