Think of a neural network like a giant “recipe book” that learns to cook by tasting many dishes. Each page in the book (a **neuron**) holds a tiny rule: if the ingredient amounts you give it are right, it will shout out a flavor note; otherwise it stays quiet. When you want a new dish (an answer), you feed the recipe book all the ingredients at once—your data—and let every page talk to its neighbors in layers. The first pages decide on simple flavors (edges, colors); later pages combine those into richer ideas (objects, emotions). After trying many dishes, you tweak the rules a little so the book tastes better each time—that’s **training**. In short, a neural network is a layered system of tiny decision‑makers that learns patterns by adjusting its own “rules” from examples.
