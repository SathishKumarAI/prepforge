Think of an agent‑AI as a chef in a bustling kitchen.

**Context engineering** is like giving the chef a clear recipe book before each dish. The recipe tells the chef exactly what ingredients to use, how much, and in what order—so every time they cook, they follow the same steps and taste consistent results. It’s about setting up the “rules” that shape the AI’s short‑term behavior.

**Memory engineering**, on the other hand, is like a pantry that remembers what was bought last week. The chef can pull from this pantry whenever a dish needs something that isn’t in the recipe book—perhaps a special spice or a leftover sauce. This pantry stores past experiences and lets the AI refer back to them across conversations or tasks.

So, context engineering provides the immediate instructions for each task; memory engineering supplies the long‑term knowledge the agent draws on when new problems arise. Both are essential: without recipes the chef can’t cook at all, but without a pantry they can’t improvise or learn from past meals.
