Imagine a giant library where every book is an image and the librarian’s job is to find hidden patterns—like spotting faces or cars—without being told exactly what to look for.  
Deep learning is that librarian, but instead of reading text, it reads pixels. It uses many “layers” like stacked shelves: the first shelf looks for tiny edges, the next one stitches those edges into shapes, and so on until the final shelf recognizes a whole object. Each layer learns by tweaking its own little rules (weights) after seeing thousands of labeled examples—like practice rounds that sharpen its guessing skill.
