Imagine a busy kitchen where a master chef (the *teacher* model) learns to cook every dish perfectly by watching countless professional cooks in real life. Now, the chef wants to train a new sous‑chef who will work only in one specific restaurant—so that she can adapt instantly to its unique menu and customer tastes. The chef records her own cooking while serving those customers (that’s *on‑policy* data). She then teaches the sous‑chef by showing her the exact flavors, timing, and seasoning choices she made for each dish.  

In machine‑learning terms, “distillation” is this teacher‑student hand‑off: a large, well‑trained model compresses its knowledge into a smaller one. *On‑policy distillation* means the student learns from data generated by the current policy—here, the chef’s own real‑world service—so it becomes finely tuned to that specific environment (personalization). The result is a lightweight model that behaves just like the big teacher but only for the restaurant it was trained in.
