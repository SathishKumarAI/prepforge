Imagine you’re learning to recognize apples by looking at pictures. You see only a handful of bright‑red apples on white plates and you make a rule: *“Anything that looks like this picture is an apple.”* When you later look at a green apple or one on a wooden table, your rule fails because it was too tightly tuned to the few examples you saw. That’s overfitting – the model learns the quirks of its training data instead of the true pattern that defines “apple.”

In machine learning, overfitting happens when an algorithm memorizes noise and specific details in the training set, so it performs great on those data but poorly on new, unseen data. The cure is to give it more varied examples, simplify the model, or use techniques that make it generalize better—just like practicing with apples of all colors, shapes, and backgrounds.
