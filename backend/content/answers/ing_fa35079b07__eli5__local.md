**Cardinality in a database is like the variety of ingredients in a recipe book.**  
Imagine you have a huge cookbook that lists every dish (a *row*) and each ingredient it uses (a *column*). Cardinality tells you how many **different values** appear in a particular column—how many unique ingredients are used across all recipes.

For example, if the “Spice” column contains “salt,” “pepper,” “cumin,” “turmeric,” and “paprika,” its cardinality is 5. A high‑cardinality column (many distinct values) is like a spice rack full of unique spices; a low‑cardinality one (few distinct values) is like a table that only ever lists “yes” or “no.”  

Understanding cardinality helps database designers decide how to index data and how efficiently the system can look up information. It’s simply counting unique items in a column, no equations needed—just a quick tally of variety.
