Imagine you’re sorting a huge pile of mixed fruit into baskets and then making sure each basket looks similar before sending it to the market.

**Classification (the sorting step)**  
You look at each fruit’s color, size, shape, and taste—features that tell you whether it’s an apple, banana, or orange. In AI, classification is a machine learning model that takes data (like images or text), examines its features, and assigns it to a category (“spam” vs “not spam,” “dog” vs “cat”). It learns the patterns that distinguish each class.

**Normalization (the prep step)**  
Before you can trust your sorting, you want every fruit to be measured on the same scale: all apples are cut into 1‑inch cubes, bananas into 2‑inch slices. Normalization rescales or standardizes data so that no single feature dominates because of its units or size. It makes the model’s job easier and fairer.

So, classification is the “who is this?” question; normalization is the “let’s put everything on a level playing field” preparation that lets the classifier work best.
