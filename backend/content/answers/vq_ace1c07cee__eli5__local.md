Think of a chef who already knows how to bake bread.  
**Transfer learning** is like giving that chef a new recipe book: she keeps her basic baking skills (the kitchen layout, oven temperature) but learns just the special ingredients and steps for a different pastry. The core knowledge stays intact; only the new details change.

**Fine‑tuning** is more like the same chef taking the entire kitchen and re‑arranging it to make a completely new type of cuisine—maybe sushi instead of bread. She starts with her general cooking sense, but then adjusts every knob: she learns how to hold knives, what temperatures to use for rice, etc., tweaking all her old habits to fit the new food.

So, transfer learning keeps most of the original “knowledge” and only adds a few new pieces, while fine‑tuning revises much of the original knowledge to specialize in something different.
