Think of a library that offers two ways to read books.

**API (cloud‑hosted)**  
It’s like calling a librarian over the phone: you ask, “Show me this story,” and the librarian fetches it from the shelves, brings it to you, and even updates the book for you if new editions come out. You don’t own the books; you just pay for the service each time you read. It’s fast, always up‑to‑date, but you can’t keep a copy or change how the librarian presents the story.

**Self‑Hosted LLM**  
Here you buy the entire library and set it up in your own room. You get full control: you can add or remove books, tweak the shelving layout, and even write your own cataloging rules. The downside is that you must maintain the shelves (hardware, software updates, backups). If something breaks, you’re on your own.

In short, an API gives convenience with external maintenance; self‑hosting gives ownership and flexibility at the cost of extra responsibility.
