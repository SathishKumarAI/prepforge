**Why “missing” matters for sorting**

When a query asks to sort by field *f*, the engine must order every document, even those that lack *f*.  
If it simply skips missing docs, the result set shrinks; if it pushes them arbitrarily to the top or bottom, the ranking becomes unintuitive.  
Elasticsearch solves this with a **sentinel value**: a pseudo‑value that represents “no data” and can be positioned relative to real values.

---

### The fundamental problem

Sorting is an *order‑preserving* operation on a totally ordered set.  
Documents without field *f* do not belong in the order defined by actual numeric or lexical values; they are outside the domain.  
To integrate them, we must embed them into the same ordering structure without breaking monotonicity.

---

### The solution: a virtual bound

Elasticsearch introduces two options:

| option | effect |
|--------|--------|
| `missing: "_first"` | treat missing as less than any real value (prepend) |
| `missing: "_last"`  | treat missing as greater than any real value (append) |

Internally it maps a missing document to an *infinite* or *negative‑infinite* key that the B‑tree index can compare.  
This preserves sorting semantics and keeps the index structure intact.

---

### Deeper insight

The trick is **extending the domain** rather than altering the data.  
By adding a single sentinel, we convert a partial order (only documents with *f*) into a total order.  
This mirrors how “null” values are handled in relational databases: they’re not compared to real numbers but placed at one end of the ordering.

---

### Non‑obvious takeaway

Most people think missing‑value sorting is purely cosmetic.  
In reality, it’s an *index‑level* decision that affects query performance: inserting a sentinel keeps the B‑tree balanced and avoids costly post‑processing of nulls during search.
