Think of machine‑learning as cooking a gourmet meal.  
A **data architecture** is the kitchen layout—cabinets, appliances, and plumbing that decide where ingredients go, how they’re moved, and what tools are available. If the pantry is cluttered or the fridge is far away, you’ll waste time looking for salt; if the stove is unreliable, your sauce will burn. In ML, a well‑designed data architecture keeps raw facts (the “ingredients”) in clean tables, streams them to models on demand, and lets analysts trace every step back to its source. It saves money, speeds experiments, and ensures that when you tweak a recipe (a model) you can see exactly which ingredient changed. Without this kitchen plan, your ML “dish” will taste off or never reach the table at all.
