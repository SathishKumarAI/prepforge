Imagine you’re a chef preparing a huge buffet of dishes (the data). Each dish has a chance of being the perfect flavor for any guest (a prediction). The “probability concepts” are like the kitchen tools that help you decide how often to add salt, spice, or sauce so every plate tastes just right.  

1. **Probability** – the chance something happens, like flipping a coin.  
2. **Random variable** – an outcome of a random event (e.g., the weight of a cookie).  
3. **Distribution** – a recipe book telling how often each outcome appears.  
4. **Mean (average)** – the central flavor point around which all dishes vary.  
5. **Variance** – how much the flavors spread out from that average.  
6. **Standard deviation** – the typical “distance” from the mean, measured in the same units as the data.  
7. **Covariance** – how two ingredients’ amounts move together (e.g., salt and pepper).  
8. **Correlation** – a normalized version of covariance, telling whether they rise or fall together.  
9. **Bayes’ theorem** – updating your flavor guess after tasting a bite.  
10. **Expectation** – the long‑run average taste if you served many plates.

Just as a chef tweaks ingredients based on these tools to delight diners, machine learning uses probability ideas to adjust models so they predict accurately for new data.
