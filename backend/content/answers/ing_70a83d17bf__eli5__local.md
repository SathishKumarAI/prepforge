Imagine you’re cooking a big, fancy meal but you have two helpers: one is a *Chef* who follows your exact recipe (the “Agentic” part), and the other is a *Sous‑chef* who suggests tweaks based on taste tests and available ingredients (the “Engineering” part). A workflow framework in machine learning is that kitchen setup. It lets the Chef (your main algorithm) do what you told it to—train on data, make predictions—while the Sous‑chef (automation tools like Airflow or Prefect) keeps everything running smoothly: scheduling jobs, handling failures, and moving data between steps.

Agentic Engineering, as Neo Kim and Paul Hoekstra call it, is simply a fancy name for building these two sides together. The “agent” is the ML model that acts on your inputs; the “engineering” is the infrastructure that watches over it, making sure it gets fed fresh data, retrains when needed, and stops if something goes wrong.
