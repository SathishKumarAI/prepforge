Imagine you’re teaching a child to recognize cats.  
**Under‑fitting** is like giving the child only a few blurry pictures of cats—so simple that they can’t tell a cat from a dog or a rabbit. The model learns too little and performs poorly on new images.  

**Over‑fitting** is like showing the child thousands of exact, identical photos of one particular cat. They memorize every whisker pattern, but when a slightly different cat walks in, they’re confused because the details don’t match their memory. The model knows its training data perfectly but fails elsewhere.

So, under‑fitting = “not enough learning”; over‑fitting = “too much memorization.” A good model balances both—learns general patterns while ignoring irrelevant quirks.
