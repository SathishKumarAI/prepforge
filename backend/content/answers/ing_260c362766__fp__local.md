**Why a “Wellness Stipend” is an optimal HR tool**

At its core a stipend is a *budget‑allocation problem*: the firm wants to maximize employee productivity \(P\) while keeping total cost \(C\) within a fixed budget \(B\). Productivity is a function of health, stress, and engagement:  
\(P = f(\text{health}, \text{stress}, \text{engagement})\).

Traditional models treat *wellness* as an externality—something employees manage on their own. A stipend turns it into a *controlled variable*: by giving a fixed sum \(S\) to spend on exercise, meditation, or healthy food, the firm nudges employees toward actions that statistically reduce stress and improve cognitive function.

Mathematically this is akin to a constrained optimization with Lagrange multipliers:  
maximize \(P\) s.t. \(C = w \cdot \text{salary} + S \le B\).  
The multiplier associated with the stipend constraint reveals its *shadow price*: how much extra productivity one would gain per dollar invested in wellness.

**Non‑obvious insight:** The stipend’s value lies not just in health benefits but in *reducing variance* of productivity. By smoothing out individual differences (some people naturally spend more on wellness), the company lowers the risk of extreme dips in performance, which is often overlooked when only mean effects are considered. Thus a small, uniform stipend can yield disproportionate gains by stabilizing the entire workforce’s output distribution.
