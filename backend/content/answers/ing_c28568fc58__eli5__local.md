Think of machine‑learning services like different **kitchen appliances** that help you cook a dish (your model).  
*One appliance is the “all‑in‑one” oven: it preheats, bakes, and even cleans itself—just drop your ingredients in and let it do everything. That’s a fully managed ML service (e.g., AWS SageMaker or Azure ML).  

Another appliance is a **cooktop** that gives you burners but you must add pots, pans, and the recipe yourself. That’s an “infrastructure‑as‑a‑service” platform (like Google Cloud AI Platform Pipelines) where you hand‑craft the workflow but get powerful hardware.  

A third is a **microwave**: fast and simple, but only for reheating or quick tasks—representing lightweight APIs that run pre‑built models.  

When comparing services, look at how much “cooking” they handle for you versus how much control you keep over the recipe. The more hands‑off the appliance, the easier it is to use; the more manual control, the more flexibility (and complexity).
