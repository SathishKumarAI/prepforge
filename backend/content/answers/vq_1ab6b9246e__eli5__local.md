Think of a city’s power grid.  
An **edge node** is like a small solar panel on your roof. It sits close to the houses it serves, collects sunlight (data) right where it’s produced, and does quick work—filtering out bad signals or summarizing what people need before sending anything farther.  

A **data node** is like the big power plant in the city center. It receives electricity from many panels, stores it, runs heavy calculations, and then distributes the final product to everyone.  

So, edge nodes are first‑stop processors that act locally on raw data, while data nodes are central hubs that store, analyze, and share processed information across the whole system.
