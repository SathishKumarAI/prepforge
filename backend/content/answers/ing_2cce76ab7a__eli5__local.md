Imagine you’re a friendly librarian who knows every book in a huge library and wants to suggest the next great read for each visitor.  
A **recommendation engine** is that librarian, but it works with data instead of shelves. It looks at what books (or movies, products, songs) people have already liked, how they rated them, and what other visitors who shared those tastes also enjoyed. Then it “recommends” items the visitor hasn’t seen yet but are likely to be a hit.

In an interview, you’ll be asked to explain how such a system learns from past choices (collaborative filtering), handles new items or users (cold‑start problem), and balances showing familiar favorites with surprising discoveries. Think of it as teaching your librarian to predict the next perfect book for every reader based on patterns in everyone’s reading history.
