Imagine a library that keeps every book it receives and wants to decide how many shelves (or servers) it needs and how much money to spend on hiring librarians (or training data).  
**Cost analysis** is the process of looking at all the expenses—buying books, storing them, cataloguing, and paying staff—and comparing them with the benefits, like faster searches or better recommendations. In AI knowledge‑management, the “books” are pieces of information the system learns from, the “shelves” are the computing resources that hold that data, and the “librarians” are algorithms that retrieve and organize it.

We first list every cost (hardware, software, human time) and estimate how much each adds to the budget. Then we ask: *Will this investment let us answer questions faster or make better decisions?* If the benefit outweighs the cost, we keep it; if not, we look for cheaper ways—perhaps re‑using old data or simplifying the model.

So, cost analysis in AI knowledge management is like budgeting a library: you weigh every expense against the value of having information ready when needed.
