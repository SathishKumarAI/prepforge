Think of a programming language as a kitchen where you can cook any dish. In a **dynamically typed** kitchen, every ingredient (variable) carries its own label only when you first put it in the pot—like a “tomato” or “flour.” You’re free to change that label later: the same bowl might hold ketchup one moment and then flour the next, and the stove will happily adapt. The recipe doesn’t need a strict ingredient list up front; instead, each step checks what’s actually inside when it runs.

In contrast, a **statically typed** kitchen would require you to write a detailed shopping list before cooking—each bowl must be declared as “tomato” or “flour,” and the chef can’t swap them without re‑checking everything. Dynamic typing lets programmers experiment quickly, but they must watch for mistakes that only show up when the dish is actually prepared.
