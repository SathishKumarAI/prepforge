Imagine a chef learning to cook from three different kitchens.

**1. Supervised kitchen (Supervised Learning)** – The chef is given recipes that list every ingredient and step. Each dish comes with a label: “sweet,” “savory,” or “spicy.” The chef practices, tasting each finished dish and comparing it to the label, learning how to match ingredients to taste.

**2. Unsupervised kitchen (Unsupervised Learning)** – Here no recipes are supplied. The chef simply sees many dishes and must group them by similarity—perhaps by color, texture, or flavor profile—discovering hidden patterns like “all citrus‑based desserts” or “hearty soups.”

**3. Reinforcement kitchen (Reinforcement Learning)** – The chef cooks a dish and receives feedback: applause, a critique, or a score. Based on that reward or penalty, the chef adjusts future choices, gradually learning which actions lead to the best reception.

These three kitchens—supervised, unsupervised, and reinforcement—represent the main types of machine‑learning data models, each guiding how a system learns from information.
