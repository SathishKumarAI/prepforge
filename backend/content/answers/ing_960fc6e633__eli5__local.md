A **Content Delivery Network (CDN)** is like a global library that keeps copies of your website’s books—images, videos, and code—in many cities around the world.  
When someone opens your site, the CDN hands them the copy nearest to their location instead of sending it all the way from your main server. This reduces travel time for data, so pages load faster and are less likely to crash when lots of people visit at once.  

Think of it as a chain of local bookstores: each one stocks the same titles, so readers can buy books quickly without waiting for them to ship from a distant headquarters. In web terms, the CDN’s “bookstores” are servers (called edge nodes) that sit close to users and serve content with minimal delay.
