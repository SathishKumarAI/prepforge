### The Most Important Quality in a Good Friend

From first principles, friendship is an **information‑sharing system** that optimizes mutual well‑being. A friend must reliably transmit signals (emotions, advice, support) and receive them without distortion or loss. The key property enabling this is **reciprocal *trust*—the expectation that each party will act in the other’s best interest when information is exchanged.**

Why trust?  
1. **Cognitive efficiency** – Without it we must constantly evaluate every message for deception, wasting resources.  
2. **Signal fidelity** – Trust acts as a Bayesian prior that weights incoming data more heavily, improving decision quality.  
3. **Risk sharing** – It permits vulnerability (sharing secrets), which is the only way to create deep emotional bonds.

A non‑obvious insight: *trust is not static; it is a dynamic, resource‑bounded function of past interactions.* A friend who occasionally breaks trust (e.g., by revealing a secret) may still be valuable if the overall trust trajectory remains positive and recoverable. Thus, the most important quality is **dynamic reciprocal trust**—the ability to maintain high‑fidelity communication while allowing room for growth and occasional missteps.
