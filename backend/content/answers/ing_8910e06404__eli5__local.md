Imagine you’re at a busy coffee shop that serves drinks through a single counter.  
**Bandwidth** is the width of that counter – how many cups can be placed side‑by‑side at once. A wider counter lets more customers queue in, so the shop can handle more orders per minute if everyone moves fast.  

**Throughput** is what actually happens: the number of finished drinks you deliver to customers each minute. Even with a wide counter, if baristas are slow or the espresso machine jams, throughput drops.  

So bandwidth is like the physical capacity (the counter’s width), while throughput is the real output rate after all bottlenecks—mixing time, equipment speed, and human workers—are considered. Both matter: you need enough space *and* fast service to keep customers happy.
