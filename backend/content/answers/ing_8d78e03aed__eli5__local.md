Imagine an AI is a weather forecaster who predicts whether it will rain tomorrow. The **confidence score** is like the forecaster’s certainty about that prediction—how sure they are that the forecast is right. In a product, this score tells users how much trust to place in the answer: a 90 % confidence means “I’m almost certain,” while a 30 % score says “I’m pretty unsure.” 

The AI calculates it by looking at how many internal “experts” (different models or data points) agree and how strongly they agree. The product then displays this number, letting people decide if the answer is reliable enough for their needs. Without that confidence bar, users would have no way to gauge whether to act on the AI’s suggestion.
