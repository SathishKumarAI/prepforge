Imagine you’re building a tiny robot that can sort coins into pennies, nickels, dimes and quarters. The **80‑20 Rule – Frugal Architecture** says: “Use only the parts that actually help the robot do its job.”  
In machine learning, a model is like that robot. It has many layers (the robot’s gears), but most of them might not change the final decision much. The rule tells us to keep the **80 % of the parameters that really matter** and drop the rest—just as you’d leave out unnecessary screws or wires from your coin sorter.

So, a *frugal architecture* is a lean model: it uses fewer calculations (fewer gears), runs faster, saves memory, yet still gets most of the accuracy. Think of it like trimming a garden: keep the healthy vines that give fruit, prune away the overgrown ones that waste space and effort. This keeps the system efficient without sacrificing performance.
