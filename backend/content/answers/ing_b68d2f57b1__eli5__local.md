**Cost regression from over‑routing to a teacher**

Imagine you’re driving to school and the GPS keeps sending you on winding detours that are longer than necessary—this is *over‑routing*. The extra distance means more gas, more time, and higher wear on your car. In AI, when a model learns a task by “copying” from many examples (like listening to a teacher who gives lots of similar stories), it can end up over‑routing: its internal map becomes cluttered with unnecessary twists.

*Cost regression* is the process that trims those detours. It measures how much extra “cost” (time, memory, error) each twist adds and then removes the least useful ones, just as a driver would take the straightest route after learning which turns were superfluous. The result: a leaner, faster model that still remembers the essential lessons from the teacher but without the needless baggage.
