Imagine you’re sending a secret recipe to a friend who lives far away.  
**Compression on Arrival (CoA)** is like packing the recipe into a tiny, compressed envelope that fits in your mailbox. When your friend receives it, they unzip it—getting exactly the same instructions but with no extra fluff.  

In an agentic AI system, **Context Engineering** is the “mailbox” itself: you decide what parts of the world (context) to send so the AI can act wisely.  
**Memory Engineering** is like a cookbook your friend keeps on their kitchen shelf—information the AI can look back at later without having to re‑receive it each time.

CoA lets the AI get all the context in one efficient burst, while memory engineering gives it a long‑term storage that remembers past interactions. Both are needed: CoA for quick, accurate decisions; memory for learning and continuity over many tasks.
