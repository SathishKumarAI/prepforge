**Machine Learning (ML) – a principled view**

At its core, ML is *probabilistic inference under model uncertainty*.  
Suppose we observe data \(x_{1},\dots ,x_{n}\) generated by an unknown stochastic process that depends on latent parameters \(\theta\). The fundamental problem is to infer a function
\(f_\theta(x)=\mathbb{E}[y|x,\theta]\)
that predicts future outputs \(y\) from inputs \(x\).

1. **Optimization** – We pose learning as minimizing an expected loss  
   \[
   L(\theta)=\mathbb{E}_{(x,y)\sim P}\!\big[\ell(f_\theta(x),y)\big].
   \]
   Because the true distribution \(P\) is unknown, we approximate it with empirical risk
   \[
   \hat L(\theta)=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\ell(f_\theta(x_i),y_i).
   \]
   Gradient‑based methods find \(\theta^\ast=\arg\min_{\theta}\hat L(\theta)\).

2. **Information theory** – The empirical risk minimizer can be seen as maximizing the *log‑likelihood* of a probabilistic model, i.e., minimizing Kullback–Leibler divergence between \(P\) and the model’s distribution.

3. **Geometry** – Parameter space \(\Theta\) is often high‑dimensional; optimization traces paths on this manifold guided by gradients (tangent vectors). Regularization terms add curvature, preventing overfitting.

4. **Probability & Bayes** – Bayesian ML replaces a single point estimate with a posterior \(p(\theta|D)\), yielding predictions
   \[
   p(y|x,D)=\int p(y|x,\theta)p(\theta|D)d\theta,
   \]
   naturally encoding uncertainty.

### Non‑obvious insight

Most practitioners treat the *loss surface* as a static object, but its geometry *changes during training*: early iterations explore wide valleys (high curvature), while later steps shrink step sizes to refine narrow basins. Recognizing this dynamic allows adaptive optimizers (Adam, RMSProp) to tune learning rates per parameter, effectively performing an online second‑order method without explicit Hessians.

In short, ML is the disciplined practice of turning data‑driven uncertainty into predictive function by solving a principled optimization problem that blends probability, information, and geometry.
