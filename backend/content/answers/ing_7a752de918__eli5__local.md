Think of a digital wallet as a modern purse that holds money you can tap to buy things online.  
**Banks** run these purses like a big, well‑known store: they keep your coins in a secure vault (their servers), let you add or take out cash through ATMs, and make sure only you can use it by checking your ID each time. The store’s staff know exactly how many coins are in every purse because the bank keeps a master ledger that updates instantly.

**Blockchain** is like a public marketplace where everyone writes down every coin transfer on a shared notebook. Each page (block) is sealed and cannot be altered once written, so anyone can see the exact history of your purse. The market doesn’t have a single owner; instead, many participants verify each transaction together (the “network”), making fraud very hard.

So, banks keep control with centralized security, while blockchain spreads trust across many verifiers, offering transparency but less instant convenience for everyday use.
