Think of a bakery that sells cupcakes. **Vertical scaling** is like buying a bigger oven or adding more ovens to the same shop floor—everything stays in one place, but you’re limited by how big a single kitchen can get. **Horizontal scaling** is opening new bakery shops next door and letting each shop bake its own batch of cupcakes.

The perks of this “next‑door” approach are:

1. **No single point of failure:** If one shop breaks down, the others keep serving customers.
2. **Easy to grow:** Add another shop whenever demand rises; you don’t need a huge, expensive upgrade in one spot.
3. **Better use of resources:** Each shop can specialize (e.g., one focuses on vanilla, another on chocolate) and share the workload.

So, horizontal scaling spreads load across many smaller units, making systems more resilient and flexible than cramming everything into one giant machine.
