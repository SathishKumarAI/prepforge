Think of a SQL string as a long sentence written on a piece of paper.  
Just like you can cut, glue, or rewrite parts of that sentence, SQL gives you “string functions” – little tools that let you pick out, change, or combine pieces of text without touching the whole document.

* **SUBSTRING** (or *SUBSTR*) is like cutting a specific word from the middle of the sentence.  
* **CONCAT** stitches two separate sentences together, just as glue joins paper strips.  
* **LEN** tells you how many letters the sentence has—like counting words in a line.  
* **UPPER / LOWER** flip every letter to all caps or all lowercase, like turning a typed page into bold or italic.  
* **REPLACE** swaps one word for another across the whole sentence, similar to editing a typo everywhere it appears.  
* **TRIM** removes leading and trailing spaces—like cutting off the blank margins of a printed page.
