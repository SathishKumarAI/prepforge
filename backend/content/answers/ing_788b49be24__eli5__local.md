- **Model** – the chef’s brain; a set of mathematical rules that learns from data and makes predictions.  
- **Training Data** – the pantry full of ingredients (examples) the model tastes to learn patterns.  
- **Inference** – the cooking step where the model uses what it learned to produce an answer or action.  
- **Architecture** – the layout of appliances (layers, neurons, etc.) that decide how information flows.  
- **Loss Function** – the taste test; a score that tells the chef how close the dish is to perfect and guides improvement.  
- **Optimizer** – the kitchen manager who tweaks knobs to reduce errors, moving the model toward better performance.  
- **Deployment** – serving the final dish to users in real time, often on servers or edge devices.  
- **Latency** – the wait time between ordering a meal and getting it; in AI it’s how fast inference happens.  
- **Throughput** – how many meals can be cooked per minute; for AI, how many predictions can be made simultaneously.  
- **Explainability** – a menu that shows why each ingredient was chosen, helping users trust the chef’s decisions.
