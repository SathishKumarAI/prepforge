Think of a peer‑to‑peer (P2P) network like a neighborhood potluck where every house brings its own dish and also shares what it has with the next table.  
**Feature:** Every computer is both a client and a server, so data can hop directly from one node to another without a central kitchen.  
**Pros:**  
- *Scalability:* Adding new neighbors automatically expands capacity.  
- *Redundancy:* If one house drops out, others still serve the dish.  
- *Cost‑efficiency:* No expensive central server is needed.  
**Cons:**  
- *Security risk:* Anyone can snoop or tamper with the shared plates.  
- *Consistency issues:* Different houses might use slightly different recipes, leading to confusion.  
- *Management complexity:* Coordinating who shares what and when becomes harder as the potluck grows.

In short, P2P gives you a flexible, low‑cost way to share data directly, but it also demands careful handling of privacy, consistency, and coordination.
