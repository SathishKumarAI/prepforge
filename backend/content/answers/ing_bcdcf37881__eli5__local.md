Think of a library that holds thousands of books, but instead of looking at titles it looks at the “feel” of each book—its mood, style, and themes.  
**Exact search** is like asking the librarian to find every single book whose feel matches your query perfectly. It’s precise but slow; the librarian has to compare every book word‑by‑word.  

**Approximate search** lets the librarian use a “feel map” (a vector) for each book and then grab the nearest neighbors on that map. It may miss a few books that are almost perfect, but it finds the best matches in a fraction of the time—just like quickly pulling out the most similar stories from a huge collection.  

*Vector*: a list of numbers representing an item’s features; *database*: a storage system for many such vectors.
