**Flow engineering** is the art and science of making data move smoothly through an artificial‑intelligence system—like designing a highway that keeps cars (data) flowing without traffic jams.

Imagine building a city’s road network. Each street must be wide enough, turn lanes placed at the right spots, and traffic lights timed so cars never pile up. In AI, “roads” are the data pipelines: raw information from sensors or users goes through stages—cleaning, labeling, training models, and finally delivering predictions. Flow engineers decide how much bandwidth each stage needs (compute power), where to store data temporarily, and when to pause or speed up processing so that the system stays fast and reliable.

Key terms:
- **Pipeline** – a sequence of steps that data passes through.
- **Throughput** – how many data items are processed per second.
- **Latency** – time it takes for one item to travel from start to finish.

By treating data movement like traffic, flow engineering keeps AI systems running smoothly and efficiently.
