Imagine you’re a bookshop that sells the same bestseller to readers all over the world. If every customer had to wait for the book to travel from your single warehouse in New York, some would have to wait hours or even days—especially those on the other side of the planet. A Content Delivery Network (CDN) is like opening small, cozy branches of that shop in major cities around the globe: Paris, Tokyo, São Paulo, etc. Each branch keeps a copy of the book so readers can pick it up almost instantly.

In tech terms, a CDN is a distributed network of servers positioned close to users. When someone requests content (a webpage, image, or video), the CDN routes the request to the nearest server, which delivers the data quickly and reduces strain on the original source. This makes websites feel snappy, lowers bandwidth costs, and keeps traffic smooth even during sudden surges—just like your global bookshop keeps every reader happy without overloading a single warehouse.
