Imagine you’re visiting a cozy café that keeps track of your orders.  

**Cookies** are like sticky notes you leave on the counter. The café (your browser) writes a tiny note with your name and favorite coffee, stamps it with an expiration date, and hands it back to you. Every time you return, you hand the note back, and the café reads it to remember you. Those notes live in your backpack (the browser’s storage) until they expire or you throw them away.

**Sessions** are like a temporary table assigned to you while you’re inside the café. The server creates a unique ticket for that table, stores all the details about what you’re doing on its side, and gives you a ticket number. As long as you stay at that table (keep the browser open), the server can look up your session using that number. Once you leave or close the window, the table is cleared.

So: cookies are small data snippets stored on your device for future visits; sessions are server‑side records tied to a temporary identifier, alive only while you’re actively interacting.
