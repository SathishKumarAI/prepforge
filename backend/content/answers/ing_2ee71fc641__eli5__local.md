Imagine you’re training a dog to fetch a ball. Every time the dog brings the ball back, it gets a treat; if it doesn’t, it gets nothing. The dog learns over many trials which actions (running, leaping, dropping the ball) lead to treats. That’s **reinforcement learning** – a type of **machine learning** where an “agent” (the dog) tries different actions in an environment and receives feedback called a *reward* or *penalty*. The agent’s goal is to learn a strategy that maximizes total rewards, just like the dog learns to fetch the ball efficiently.

In machine learning broadly, we give computers data and let them find patterns or make decisions. Reinforcement learning adds the element of trial‑and‑error with delayed feedback, mimicking how living beings learn from experience. The agent improves its policy—its rulebook for choosing actions—until it performs well in the task.
