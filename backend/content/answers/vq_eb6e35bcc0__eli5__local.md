Imagine you’re teaching a child to sort pictures of animals into “pets” and “wild.” If you only show the child pictures of cats, dogs, and horses (all pets) and never show any wild animals, the child will quickly learn that every animal is a pet. When later asked about a tiger or an eagle, the child will mistakenly label them as pets because its training data was biased toward one group.

In machine learning, *bias* happens when the data used to train a model doesn’t represent all possibilities fairly—just like the child’s limited picture set. The algorithm then makes decisions that favor the over‑represented examples and may misclassify or ignore under‑represented ones. This is why diverse, balanced training data are crucial for fair AI systems.
