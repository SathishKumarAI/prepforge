Imagine you’re writing a family history book that not only lists who is related to whom, but also when those relationships started and ended—like “Anna married Ben in 2010” or “Carlos worked at Acme Corp from 2015–2020.”  
A **temporal knowledge graph** is exactly that: a map of facts (nodes) connected by edges that have timestamps.  

A **framework for building one** acts like a smart editing tool. First, it gathers raw data—text, spreadsheets, or sensors—and pulls out the people, places, and events (the *entities*). Next, it automatically links them with verbs (“married,” “employed”) and attaches dates to each link, turning a static list into a living timeline. Finally, it lets you query the graph: “Who was at Acme in 2018?” or “When did Anna leave her job?”  

So think of the framework as an automated family‑history editor that turns scattered records into a clear, time‑aware map of everything that happened.
