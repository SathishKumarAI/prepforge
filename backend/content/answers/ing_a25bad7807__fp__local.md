**AI Labs vs. AI Companies – What the Interview Really Tests**

At its core, an *AI lab* is a research sandbox: it pursues open‑ended scientific questions to push the frontier of knowledge (e.g., discovering new architectures). A *company* translates those discoveries into marketable products, balancing speed, reliability, and regulatory constraints. This distinction shapes interview focus.

| Lab | Company |
|-----|---------|
| **Goal** – maximize *expected information gain* from experiments. | **Goal** – minimize *cost‑to‑value* for customers. |
| **Questions** – “How would you design a new loss function to reduce bias?” or “Which data augmentation maximizes mutual information with the target?” | **Questions** – “How do you ensure model robustness under adversarial attack?” or “What trade‑off between latency and accuracy will meet user SLA?” |

Why this matters: Labs operate under *exploratory* optimization (high variance, low constraints), whereas companies optimize a constrained objective—profit, compliance, user experience. Interviewers probe whether you can shift from high‑variance research to low‑variance production thinking.

**Non‑obvious insight:** In both settings the same mathematical tool appears—*regularization*. Labs view it as *prior over model space* (encouraging exploration), while companies treat it as a *budget constraint* on compute and data. Recognizing this dual role lets candidates frame answers that satisfy both interviewers’ expectations.
