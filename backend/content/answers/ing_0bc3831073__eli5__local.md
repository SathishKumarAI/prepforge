Think of learning a new language like gardening a rose bush.  
Every time you visit the garden, you pull out a few roses (the facts) and look at them again after a short pause—maybe a few minutes. Then, if they still feel fresh, you let the interval grow: next time you’ll see those same roses after an hour, then after a day, then after a week, and so on.  
In machine‑learning terms, each “rose” is an item in your memory set, and the *spaced repetition algorithm* schedules when to review it again. It keeps track of how well you remembered each item (a simple score), then calculates the next review time so that items you know well are shown less often, while those you struggle with appear more frequently.  
The result is a steady “garden” where every rose stays healthy without over‑watering or neglecting—just enough practice to keep knowledge blooming long‑term.
