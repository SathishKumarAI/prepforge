**Whiteboard exercises are like planning a road trip before you hit the highway.**  
Imagine you want to drive from City A to City B. Before you leave, you pull out a map (the whiteboard) and sketch where the roads go, where traffic jams might appear, and how long each leg will take. In AI system design, the “roads” are the data flow and decision points; the “traffic jams” are bottlenecks or security risks; the “leg times” are performance expectations.  

On the whiteboard you draw boxes for *data ingestion*, *pre‑processing*, *model inference*, and *output delivery*. Arrows show how information moves, and you label each step with its purpose—like a GPS showing why you take a detour. This visual plan lets designers spot missing steps, test different routes (algorithms), and agree on the best path before coding starts.  

**Key terms:**  
- **Data ingestion**: collecting raw input.  
- **Inference**: the model making predictions.  
- **Pipeline**: the sequence of processing stages.  

By sketching first, you avoid getting stuck in traffic later—just as a well‑planned trip saves time and frustration.
