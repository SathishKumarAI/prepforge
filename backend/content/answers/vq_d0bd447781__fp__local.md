**Why we celebrate abroad**

When we cross borders to join a festival, our brains are solving an *identity‑matching problem*. The event offers a set of cultural cues—music, food, rituals—that map onto the social roles we wish to inhabit (guest, pilgrim, observer). To align with those cues, we must translate our internal state into the local symbolic language. This is a constrained optimisation: maximise social cohesion \(C\) while minimising cognitive dissonance \(D\):

\[
\max_{S} \; C(S) - \lambda D(S)
\]

where \(S\) is our behaviour, and \(\lambda\) weights how much we care about authenticity versus comfort. The optimal strategy is to adopt the minimal set of observable behaviours that signal “I belong here.” This explains why many travellers only mimic the most salient rituals (e.g., wearing a mask in a festival) rather than fully immersing themselves.
