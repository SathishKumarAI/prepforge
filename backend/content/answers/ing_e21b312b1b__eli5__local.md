Think of creating an AI system like building a smart home that you want everyone to trust.  
**Model/System cards** are the instruction manuals: they list what data was used, how the model behaves in different situations, and any known quirks—just as a homeowner’s guide tells neighbors about the security system and energy use.  

The **EU AI Act** is the city ordinance that says which homes need safety inspections, labeling, or special permits based on risk level; it forces developers to document and test their “smart appliances.”  

The **NIST AI RMF** (Risk Management Framework) is a checklist for homeowners: identify threats, assess how serious they are, choose controls, and monitor them—so engineers can prove the AI behaves responsibly.  

Finally, **audit logging** keeps a daily logbook of every action the smart system takes; if something goes wrong, the log shows who did what and when, just like security cameras that record activity for later review.
