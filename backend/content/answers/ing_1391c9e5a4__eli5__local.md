**Pitfall 20: Security as an Afterthought**

Imagine building a fancy new playground for kids. You spend weeks designing colorful slides, swings, and climbing walls—everything looks great. But you forget to install sturdy fencing or safety mats until the last minute. When the playground opens, children can wander off or get hurt because safety wasn’t built into the plan from the start.

In AI, developers often focus on making a model smart first: training it on data, tweaking its accuracy, and adding cool features. Security—protecting the data, guarding against tampering, ensuring privacy—is added only after the model is ready, like putting a fence on a finished playground. That delay can let hackers exploit weak spots or leak sensitive information before you even realize the risk.

**Key terms**

- **Data poisoning:** Trickery that injects bad data into training to corrupt the model.  
- **Model inversion:** Recovering private inputs by probing the AI’s outputs.

Just as a playground needs fencing, an AI system needs security woven in from day one—otherwise it becomes vulnerable and unsafe for users.
