Think of a large language model (LLM) like a gigantic library that has read almost every book, article, recipe, and chat you can imagine. Instead of just storing the texts, it learns how sentences are usually put together—what word tends to follow another, what phrases sound natural, and how ideas connect. When you ask it a question, it doesn’t search for an answer; it predicts the most likely next words based on all that practice, weaving them into a fresh reply.

**Key terms:**  
- **Large** – “huge” because it contains billions of tiny pieces of text (called *parameters*).  
- **Language model** – a computer program trained to guess the next word in a sentence.  

So an LLM is like a super‑savvy writer who has absorbed countless examples and can generate new, coherent language on demand.
