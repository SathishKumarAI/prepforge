Think of Elastic Cloud like a coffee shop that offers two ways to get your espresso: **serverless** (you just order a cup and the barista takes care of everything) and **hosted** (you rent a whole counter, set up your own machines, and run it yourself).

*Serverless* is “pay‑as‑you‑drink.” You only pay for the exact amount of data you process each month—no need to think about servers, maintenance, or scaling. It’s great when your usage jumps suddenly; the shop handles the extra grind.

*Hosted* is “rent a counter.” You pay a fixed monthly fee that covers a dedicated server (or cluster) and all its upkeep. You decide how much coffee you’ll brew, and you have full control over settings, but you also bear the cost even when your machine sits idle.

Elastic’s official pricing shows these two models side‑by‑side: serverless starts low but grows with traffic; hosted has a predictable baseline that can be cheaper if you’re consistently busy. Pick the one that matches how often and how much data you’ll analyze.
