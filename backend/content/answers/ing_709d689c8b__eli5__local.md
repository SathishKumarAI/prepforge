Imagine you’re a chef learning to bake the perfect chocolate cake. You practice by following one recipe so many times that you memorize every tiny detail—how long to stir, when to turn the oven on, even how the light in your kitchen looks on a rainy day. When you finally serve the cake at a party, it tastes amazing because it matches exactly what you practiced. But if someone asks you to bake a cake with a slightly different oven or a new brand of chocolate, you’ll flounder; you’ve learned the recipe *too well* for that single set of conditions.

In machine learning, **overfitting** is the same idea. A model “learns” the training data so precisely—including its random quirks and noise—that it performs brilliantly on those examples but fails to generalize to new, unseen data. Think of a student who memorizes exam answers instead of understanding concepts; they’ll ace that test but struggle with different questions. Overfitting happens when a model’s complexity (too many parameters or too deep a network) lets it chase every tiny fluctuation in the training set, losing its ability to predict beyond it.
