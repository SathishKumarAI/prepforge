Imagine you’re learning a new dance routine at a club that’s always a little behind the latest music. The dancers (AI models) have seen all the steps before, but they can’t see the newest beat because the instructor only gives them the choreography one day late. That one‑day lag is the **temporal gap**: the time between when new information appears and when it reaches the AI.

The **knowledge distribution** is like the spread of that dance routine among all the dancers. Some know it perfectly, some have forgotten a step, and others never learned it at all. When you share the routine (train or update the model), it’s not just one person who gets it; the knowledge spreads through a network—like gossiping in the club—until most dancers can perform it.

So, the temporal gap is the delay before fresh data arrives, while knowledge distribution describes how that data travels and settles across all parts of the AI system.
