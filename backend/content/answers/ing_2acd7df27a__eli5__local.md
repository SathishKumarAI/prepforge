Think of live streaming as sending a movie from your kitchen to a friend’s living room in real time, without any buffering delay. The “standard protocols” are the recipe cards that tell the video how to travel safely and quickly over the internet.

* **RTMP (Real‑Time Messaging Protocol)** – Like a sturdy, dedicated TV cable that keeps the signal steady while you’re still cooking. It’s great for pushing video from your camera straight into a streaming service (YouTube, Twitch, etc.).  
* **HLS (HTTP Live Streaming)** – Imagine slicing the movie into tiny bite‑sized clips and sending them over normal web traffic. The friend’s device downloads each clip as it arrives, reassembling them on the fly; this works even if their connection dips a bit.  
* **DASH (Dynamic Adaptive Streaming over HTTP)** – Similar to HLS but smarter: it automatically picks the best‑quality slice based on how fast your friend can receive data, so the picture stays smooth.

These protocols are the “delivery instructions” that let video travel from your camera to viewers’ screens with minimal lag and maximum quality.
