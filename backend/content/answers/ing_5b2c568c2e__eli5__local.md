Imagine a group of people in a room, each holding a short note about a story they’re reading. In a classic neural network, only one person (the “hidden layer”) would read all the notes and then write a new note that everyone else uses later—slow and limited.

A Transformer is like a fast‑moving roundtable where every person can instantly see and comment on **every** other person’s note at once. Each person first looks at their own note, then “attends” to all others, weighing which parts of each note are most relevant (this is *self‑attention*). After that, they rewrite their note based on the combined insights. Repeating this process in several rounds lets the whole group build a deep, shared understanding of the story without any single person getting overloaded.

In short: Transformers let every piece of text talk to all other pieces simultaneously, learning context quickly and accurately, which is why they excel at language tasks.
