Imagine you’re teaching a child to recognize different kinds of fruit. At first, you show them apples, oranges, bananas, pointing out the color, shape, and taste each time. The child learns patterns—red and round often means an apple, yellow and curved usually a banana—and starts guessing on their own. That learning process is **machine learning**: a computer looks at many examples (data) and discovers hidden rules to make predictions or decisions.

Now picture giving that same child a tiny robot friend who can not only guess the fruit but also decide what to do with it—like deciding whether to eat it, put it in a salad, or make juice—based on what you’ve told it. That broader ability to understand context, reason, and act is **artificial intelligence** (AI). So, machine learning is the skill of spotting patterns from examples; AI is the larger toolbox that lets a system use those patterns to think and act like a human would in new situations.
