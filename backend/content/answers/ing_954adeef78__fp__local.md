**Pitfall 8 – Not Understanding Token Economics**

At its core a token is a *unit of exchange* that must satisfy three fundamental conditions for any ecosystem to survive:  
1. **Scarcity** – the supply curve must be tight enough that holders feel their stake has value; otherwise they will sell immediately, collapsing price and liquidity.  
2. **Utility** – every transaction or governance action must consume tokens in a way that aligns participants’ interests with the network’s health (e.g., staking rewards vs. voting power).  
3. **Incentive alignment** – token holders should be rewarded for behaviors that increase network security, data quality, and user growth, not for short‑term speculation.

If these conditions are ignored, the system behaves like a *self‑reinforcing bubble*: early adopters hoard tokens until the price skyrockets, then dump them once they can cash out. The network collapses because new users cannot afford to participate, and core services become underfunded.

**Non‑obvious insight:**  
Token economics is not just about supply–demand curves; it’s a *feedback loop* between **information asymmetry** (who knows the true value of data) and **market depth**. A well‑designed token that rewards accurate data sharing reduces information asymmetry, which in turn deepens liquidity—creating a virtuous cycle. Ignoring this link turns tokens into mere speculation rather than instruments of knowledge creation.
