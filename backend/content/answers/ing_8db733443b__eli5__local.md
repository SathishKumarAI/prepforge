**Static (Plan‑and‑Solve)**  
Think of a chef who wants to bake a cake before the guests arrive. The chef first *plans* every step: gather flour, eggs, sugar; mix batter; preheat oven; set timers; and decide how long each part will take. Once the plan is written down, the chef follows it exactly—no improvisation, no re‑thinking during baking.  
In AI, a “static” or *plan‑and‑solve* system does the same: it builds a full sequence of actions (the plan) before it starts acting in the world. The plan is *decomposed* into smaller tasks (mixing, baking, cooling), each with clear conditions and expected outcomes. The robot or program then executes these steps one after another without changing course unless something goes wrong.  
**Key terms**  
- **Plan**: a complete list of actions that will achieve the goal.  
- **Decomposition**: breaking a big task into smaller, manageable subtasks.  
This approach is fast and reliable when the environment doesn’t change unexpectedly.
