**Machine Learning in plain language**

Think of machine learning like teaching a child to recognize animals by showing many pictures.  
*The “child” is the computer program.*  
*Each picture is data – a set of numbers that describe color, shape, size, etc.*  
When you show the child thousands of labeled photos (e.g., cats vs. dogs), it starts noticing patterns: fur texture, ear shape, tail length. After enough practice, the child can look at a new photo and guess whether it’s a cat or a dog with high confidence.

**Key terms**

- **Data** – The raw information you give to the computer (e.g., images, numbers).  
- **Model** – The “child” that learns patterns.  
- **Training** – The process of showing many examples so the model can adjust its internal rules.  
- **Prediction** – Using the trained model to guess the category or value for new data.

In essence, machine learning is a hands‑on way of letting computers learn from experience rather than being told every step explicitly.
