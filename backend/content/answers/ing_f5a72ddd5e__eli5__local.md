Think of a context graph like the mind‑map you draw when studying a new book.  
Each **node** is a fact or idea—“Paris,” “Eiffel Tower,” “France.”  
A **link** shows how two nodes relate, such as *is located in* or *was built by*.  
The whole map lets you see not just isolated facts but the web of connections that give them meaning.  

In machine learning, a context graph stores these facts and links so an AI can ask, “What’s near Paris?” or “Which landmarks are in France?” The AI walks along the links, gathering clues from related nodes to answer questions accurately.  
So, a context graph is simply a network of labeled items that captures how everything in its domain relates—just like your study map turns isolated notes into a coherent picture.
