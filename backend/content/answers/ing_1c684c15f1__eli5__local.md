Imagine you’re baking a cake and you only have one oven. If that oven breaks, your whole batch is ruined. A smart baker keeps a second oven ready—if the first one stops working, the second takes over instantly, so the cake still comes out fine. In engineering, that “second oven” is called **redundancy**.

Redundancy means building extra parts or paths that can do the same job as the main ones. If one component fails (like a server crashing or a sensor glitching), another identical component steps in automatically. This keeps the system running without interruption and prevents single points of failure. It’s why critical systems—airplane controls, data centers, medical devices—often duplicate key parts: they’re designed to stay reliable even when something goes wrong.
