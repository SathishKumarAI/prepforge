Think of a library that knows not just what books you want, but why you’re looking for them—your mood, the conversation you’ve had with your friend, or the project you’re working on. When you ask it for “a recipe,” it doesn’t just pull up any recipe; it brings back the one that matches the exact context of your question: maybe a quick week‑night dinner, vegan ingredients, or a dish you tried last summer.

In AI, **contextual retrieval** works the same way. The system first remembers every detail you’ve mentioned—your earlier questions, the tone of the chat, even the time of day—and then uses that memory to fetch the most relevant information. It’s like having a librarian who reads your notes and can instantly hand you the perfect page, instead of scrolling through an entire catalog.

The **Anthropic Pattern** is simply a design rule: “Treat every piece of context as if it could be a clue.” That means the AI always keeps track of all past messages (the *anthropic* idea that everything observed matters) and uses them to guide its answers, so you get responses that feel tailored, not generic.
