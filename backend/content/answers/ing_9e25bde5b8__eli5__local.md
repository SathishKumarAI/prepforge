Think of Docker as a **portable kitchen** that lets you cook the same dish anywhere without worrying about missing ingredients or messy tools.  
In this kitchen, each “recipe” (a Docker image) contains everything needed to run an app: the code, its libraries, and even the operating‑system bits it depends on. When you want to serve a meal, you pull that recipe into a fresh, isolated cooking space called a **container**—just like putting ingredients into a clean pot. The container runs independently of other pots (other containers) and never interferes with your home kitchen’s state.  

So Docker is the system that builds these self‑contained recipes (images) and launches them as lightweight, reproducible units (containers). It guarantees that “it works on my machine” becomes “it works everywhere.”
