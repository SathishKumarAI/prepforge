Imagine you’re learning to paint with a huge library of photographs—every brushstroke, color, and angle is already laid out for you. That’s the “Data Wall”: a massive collection of real images that AI uses as its training set.

Now picture that after you’ve built your entire studio on those photos, someone says, “No more real pictures; you must create your own.” The synthetic shift is like moving from copying a master’s work to painting entirely from imagination. Your AI can still learn style and composition (it knows how paint looks), but it has to generate its own scenes instead of just re‑using existing ones.
