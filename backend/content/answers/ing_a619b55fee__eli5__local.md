Imagine you’re a chef who wants to understand how people taste a new soup. Instead of tasting every single spoonful, you take a handful and note three things: the average saltiness (mean), how much each bite differs from that average (variance), and whether there are any unusually salty or bland spots (outliers). In AI engineering, “summary statistics” work the same way for data sets. They give quick, clear numbers—like mean, median, standard deviation—that describe the whole collection without looking at every single entry. This lets engineers spot patterns, detect anomalies, and decide if the data is ready to train a model. Think of it as the recipe’s flavor profile: you know enough from a few samples to adjust seasoning before cooking the full batch.
