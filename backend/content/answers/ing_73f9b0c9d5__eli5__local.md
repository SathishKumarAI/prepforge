**Spaced repetition** is a study method that shows up in many learning apps. Think of it like watering a plant: you give the seed a small drink now, then again after a few days, and later after weeks. Each “watering” keeps the roots alive without drowning them.  

*Evidence*: Experiments with thousands of students on flash‑card sites (e.g., Anki) found that people remember facts 2–3 × better than if they study everything at once. The brain’s memory system, called *long‑term potentiation*, strengthens the connection each time the material is revisited after a gap.

*Criticism*: Some researchers say the gains are modest and depend on how well you pick the gaps; too long or too short can hurt. Others argue that the method mainly helps with rote facts, not deeper understanding, and that it may create “learning fatigue” if users keep receiving many reminders.

In short, spaced repetition is a proven trick for memorizing, but its limits and user experience need careful tuning.
