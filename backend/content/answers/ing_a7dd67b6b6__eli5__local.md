**Trees & Graphs in Machine Learning**

Think of a *tree* like a family tree that branches out from one ancestor to many descendants, but never loops back. In ML it’s used for decision trees: each node is a question (e.g., “Is the price > $500?”), and every branch leads to another question until you reach a leaf—your final prediction.

A *graph* is more like a city map where streets (edges) connect places (nodes). Unlike trees, graphs can have loops. In ML we use graphs for things like social‑network analysis or recommendation systems: each node might be a user or item, and edges represent interactions (friendships, purchases). By walking along the edges, algorithms discover patterns that help predict new connections or recommend content.

So, trees give you tidy, non‑looping decisions; graphs let you explore complex, interconnected relationships.
