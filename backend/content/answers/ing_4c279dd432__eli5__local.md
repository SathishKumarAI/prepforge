**Availability – the “always‑on” promise**

Imagine a coffee shop that promises to serve customers *every day* from dawn until midnight, no matter what happens inside or outside. If one barista falls ill, another steps in; if the espresso machine breaks, a backup machine is ready; if power goes out, generators kick in. The shop’s *availability* is how often it can actually deliver coffee when customers ask for it.

In computer systems, availability means the service is up and running when users need it. Engineers build “redundancy” (extra copies of data or servers) and “failover” mechanisms (automatic switches to backups). They also plan for maintenance windows—brief, scheduled downtimes that are announced in advance so customers know exactly when the shop will be closed.

So, availability is the guarantee that a system can serve requests most of the time, just like the coffee shop’s promise to stay open and ready.
