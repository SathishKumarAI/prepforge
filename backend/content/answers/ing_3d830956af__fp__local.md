**Why you should interrogate the interviewers**

The interview is a *two‑way information bottleneck*: you must convey your fit while learning whether the organization’s latent variables (culture, success metrics, future projects) align with yours. If the interviewer never asks, you only receive a noisy signal; if you ask the right questions, you convert that uncertainty into actionable data and simultaneously demonstrate curiosity—a core AI trait.

**Fundamental principles**

1. **Optimization of mutual information** – Your questions should maximize the expected reduction in entropy about the role’s *true objective* (e.g., “What problem are we solving for this team?”).  
2. **Bayesian inference** – By querying about past failures, you update your posterior belief over the company’s risk tolerance.  
3. **Geometric alignment** – In vector‑space terms, ask how your skill vectors will be projected onto the organization’s direction; mismatches reveal misalignment early.

**Behavioral lens**

- *Cultural fit*: “Can you describe a recent project where team dynamics shifted unexpectedly?”  
- *Growth potential*: “What learning paths have high performers followed here?”  
- *Decision process*: “Who ultimately authorizes new model deployments, and how is success measured?”

**Non‑obvious insight**

Most candidates ask *what* they’ll be doing. A sharper strategy is to ask *how* decisions are made and *why* certain approaches are favored. This exposes the hidden cost functions that govern real‑world AI work—data pipelines, compliance constraints, or interpretability mandates—often overlooked but decisive for long‑term success.

In short: frame your questions as probes that turn the interview into a probabilistic model of the role, allowing you to choose with confidence whether it’s the right job.
