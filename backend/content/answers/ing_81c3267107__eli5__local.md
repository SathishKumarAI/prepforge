Imagine a busy highway that carries cars (data) from one city to another.

**Latency** is the time it takes for a single car to travel from its start to its destination. It’s like how long you wait before your own delivery arrives—shorter latency means faster response.

**Throughput** is how many cars can pass a point on the highway each hour. It tells you how much total traffic the road handles over time, not just one vehicle. High throughput means many deliveries are completed quickly in aggregate.

**Bandwidth** is the width of the highway lanes—how wide each lane is and how many lanes there are. It limits how many cars can drive side‑by‑side at once. More bandwidth lets more cars travel together, boosting potential throughput but not reducing the time a single car needs to cross (latency).

So: latency = individual trip time; throughput = total trips per unit time; bandwidth = road capacity that enables those trips.
