## Why the numbers matter

At its core, **AI is an optimization engine**: it takes data \(D\), a cost function \(C\), and a search space \(\Theta\) (model parameters) and produces an output that maximizes utility \(U(D,\theta)\). In enterprise terms, \(U\) translates to *time saved* or *revenue gained*. The ROI equation

\[
\text{ROI} = \frac{\sum_{t=1}^{T}\bigl(U_t - C_t\bigr)}{\sum_{t=1}^{T}C_t}
\]

captures this trade‑off. Because AI’s marginal cost diminishes as models are reused, the denominator plateaus while \(U_t\) rises sharply when a model is first applied—hence the dramatic lift in productivity reported for 2026.

### Non‑obvious insight

Most firms treat AI as a *feature* rather than a *systemic shift*. The hidden lever is **data lineage**—tracking provenance and drift. A model that learns from stale data actually reduces \(U_t\) over time, turning an early ROI spike into a long‑term loss. Embedding continuous monitoring of data quality turns the optimization loop into a self‑correcting engine, sustaining high productivity gains beyond the initial rollout.

In short, AI’s ROI is not just about flashy metrics; it’s about embedding the *optimization loop*—data → model → outcome—into every business process and continuously recalibrating it.
