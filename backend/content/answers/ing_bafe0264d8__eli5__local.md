Think of an AI job interview like a cooking show where you’re asked to make a dish (the technical part) and then to talk about how you cook (the behavioral part).  

**The interview process** usually has three rounds:  
1. **Phone/Zoom screening** – the host asks quick questions just to see if your recipe fits the menu.  
2. **Technical interview** – you’re given a “cook‑off” problem, like building a small neural network or debugging code. The judge watches how you think and write.  
3. **On‑site (or video) panel** – a group of chefs (engineers, managers, data scientists) asks deeper questions about teamwork, learning from mistakes, and handling pressure.

**Behavioral questions** are the “why” part: they want to know how you work with others, how you handle setbacks, and whether your cooking style (work ethic) matches their kitchen culture.  

Typical behavioral prompts:  
- *Tell me about a time you fixed a bug that nobody else could solve.*  
- *Describe a project where deadlines clashed and how you negotiated.*  
- *How do you stay curious in fast‑moving tech?*  

The goal is to see if your personality, habits, and problem‑solving approach blend smoothly with the team’s flavor. Remember: be honest, use concrete stories, and show that you learn from every “taste test.”
