Imagine your computer’s storage as a huge library that keeps all the books (data) you need for learning new things. In machine‑learning, this “library” holds the raw data and the models (the trained knowledge). There are two main types of shelves: **primary storage** (RAM) and **secondary storage** (hard drives or SSDs).  

Primary storage is like a desk where you keep the books you’re reading right now—fast to grab but limited in size. Secondary storage is the library’s big shelves; it can hold millions of pages, but retrieving them takes a bit longer.  

When a machine‑learning algorithm trains, it pulls data from secondary storage into primary memory, processes it, and writes the resulting model back to secondary storage for future use. This dance between “quick‑access desk” and “big shelf” lets computers learn efficiently without losing any information.
