Think of **machine learning** as teaching a child how to talk by showing them many stories. In the 1950s computers were tiny and slow, but researchers began giving machines simple “stories” (data) and letting them find patterns—like a child noticing that “dog” always follows “bark.”

Later, **neural networks** appeared—tiny mathematical “brain cells” that could learn from examples without explicit rules, similar to letting the child guess what comes next in a sentence by trying many times.

Fast forward: the **transformer** architecture (used in GPT) is like giving the child a huge library of books and a super‑fast scanner.
