Imagine you’re giving your robot a recipe book, but the book is full of typos and weird measurements. If the robot tries to cook straight from it, it will either burn the dish or make something inedible.  
An **input‑validation layer** is like a careful editor that first checks every line of the recipe: does it use proper units? Are all ingredients spelled correctly? Is the order of steps logical? The editor fixes mistakes and rewrites unclear parts so the robot can follow them safely.

When building *tool agents*—software helpers that perform tasks such as booking flights or ordering groceries—you need this editing step. It guarantees that the agent receives clean, well‑structured instructions (the “recipe”) before it tries to act.  
So, just as a chef needs a reliable cookbook, an AI agent needs a validated input layer to avoid missteps and produce accurate results.
