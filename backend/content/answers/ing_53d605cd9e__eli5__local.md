Imagine a robot that writes stories by saying each sentence aloud while it thinks about the next one. If we could listen in on those whispered thoughts, we’d know whether the story is heading toward something dangerous or helpful. “Chain‑of‑Thought Monitorability” is exactly that idea for AI: the system’s internal reasoning steps (“chain of thought”) are made visible so humans can see how it arrives at an answer.

**Key terms (simple):**  
- **AI** – a computer program that learns patterns and makes decisions.  
- **Chain of Thought** – the sequence of intermediate ideas or calculations the AI uses before giving a final result.  
- **Monitorability** – the ability to observe those internal steps safely.

When an AI’s chain of thought is open, we can spot mistakes early, correct bias, or shut it down if it starts planning something harmful. But this openness is fragile: small changes in how the model is trained or prompted can hide its reasoning, making monitoring hard again. Thus, while a promising safety tool, it requires careful design and constant vigilance to keep the “whispered thoughts” accessible and trustworthy.
