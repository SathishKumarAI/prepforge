**Step 1: Clarify the Problem – What’s the real question you’re asked to solve?**

Imagine a chef who receives an order: “Make a dessert for ten people, but it must be gluten‑free and ready in twenty minutes.” The chef can’t just start baking; first they ask clarifying questions: “Do we have any allergies beyond gluten?” “What kitchen tools do I have?” “Is there a time limit on each person’s plate?” This is exactly what you do in the interview.

In machine‑learning terms, “clarify the problem” means:

* **Define the goal** – Is the system supposed to predict future sales, recommend movies, or classify images?
* **Identify constraints** – How fast must predictions be? What data volume will it handle? Are there privacy limits?
* **Ask about success metrics** – Accuracy, precision, recall, latency, cost?

By turning vague “build a model” into specific questions—like the chef’s clarifying list—you set the stage for a focused, realistic design. This step prevents you from building something that works in theory but never fits the real world.
