**Why these three concepts matter for an AI repo**

1. **Copyright**  
   *Problem*: Software is a creative expression; we want to prevent others from copying it verbatim.  
   *Principle*: Copyright protects the *specific arrangement* of code, not ideas or algorithms. It arises automatically upon creation and gives the author exclusive rights to reproduce, distribute, and create derivative works. In an AI repo, this means you can’t simply copy a model‑training script and claim it as your own.

2. **Patent**  
   *Problem*: The *idea* behind an algorithm (e.g., a novel loss function or inference optimization) may be commercially valuable.  
   *Principle*: Patents grant exclusive rights to the *invention*, not the expression, for a limited term. They require novelty and non‑obviousness, ensuring that only truly innovative methods are protected. For AI, patenting can secure a competitive edge on a new architecture or training trick.

3. **License**  
   *Problem*: We want others to use our code while preserving our rights.  
   *Principle*: A license is a legal instrument that specifies which permissions (copy, modify, redistribute) are granted and under what conditions (e.g., attribution, copyleft). It turns the abstract rights of copyright into concrete rules that developers can comply with.

**Non‑obvious insight**  
Most people think licenses only control *how* code is shared. In reality, they also shape *innovation*: permissive licenses encourage derivative research; restrictive ones can protect commercial viability but may stifle community contributions. Balancing these three layers—expressional protection (copyright), idea protection (patent), and usage terms (license)—is the core of responsible AI software stewardship.
