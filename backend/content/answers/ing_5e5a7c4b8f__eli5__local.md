Imagine a huge kitchen where every chef has a tiny recipe book. A **neural network** is like that kitchen: it’s made of many layers (stages) of chefs who each follow simple instructions—“look at the ingredients, add a little spice, pass the dish to the next chef.” The first layer looks at raw data (images, words). Each chef (a neuron) takes what it receives, does a tiny calculation, and hands its result forward. After many layers, the final chef decides if the dish is a cat or a dog.  

**Neural network** – a computer system that learns by adjusting these small instructions through practice, just as chefs improve their recipes over time. It’s the core engine behind deep learning: layers of “neurons” that transform input into useful output.
