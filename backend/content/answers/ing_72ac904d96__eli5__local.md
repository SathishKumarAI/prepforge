Imagine a librarian who wants every book in the library to stay on top of her mind forever. She doesn’t reread each title all at once; instead she schedules quick “check‑ins” that get longer each time a book is remembered correctly. That’s the core idea of **spaced repetition** – learning that repeats information at increasing intervals, just enough to keep it fresh but not waste time.

The technique dates back to ancient Greek and Roman teachers who used “repetition with delay.” In the 1960s, psychologist Hermann Ebbinghaus formalised the forgetting curve, showing how memory decays over time. Later, in the 1990s, software like *SuperMemo* turned this into a digital flash‑card system.
