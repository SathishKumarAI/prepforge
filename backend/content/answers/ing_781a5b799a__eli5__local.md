Imagine a detective looking for clues at a crime scene—every question is a clue that helps them piece together what happened. In machine‑learning interviews, the “detective” (the interviewer) asks **diagnostic questions** to see how you think through problems, not just whether you know the answers.

A diagnostic question might be: *“If your model’s accuracy drops after adding more data, what could be going wrong?”*  
It forces you to consider several possible clues: overfitting, data leakage, class imbalance, or feature quality. You explain each clue and how you would test it—just like a detective checks fingerprints, footprints, and footprints’ patterns.

Key terms:
- **Overfitting** – the model memorizes training data instead of learning general rules.
- **Data leakage** – information from the test set accidentally influences training.
- **Class imbalance** – one category appears far more often than others, biasing predictions.

By walking through these possibilities, you show that you can diagnose problems and propose fixes—exactly what interviewers want to see.
