**Decoding & Sampling in Large‑Language Models**

At its core, a language model (LM) is an *information source* that assigns a probability \(P(x_1,\dots ,x_T)\) to every possible token sequence.  Inference is the problem of extracting a single “best” or “diverse” continuation from this distribution.  
The most principled way to do this is **sampling**: draw tokens sequentially according to their conditional probabilities \(P(x_t|x_{<t})\).  This preserves the model’s uncertainty, allowing exploration of multiple plausible futures—essential for creative tasks.  

However, raw sampling can produce incoherent or repetitive text.  **Decoding algorithms** are *post‑processing* strategies that bias this stochastic process toward high‑quality outputs while respecting the underlying distribution.  
- **Greedy decoding** chooses the argmax at each step; it is fast but often myopic, ignoring long‑term coherence.  
- **Beam search** keeps a fixed number of partial hypotheses, trading off breadth for depth and approximating a dynamic programming solution to maximize joint probability.  
- **Top‑k / nucleus (top‑p) filtering** truncates the tail of the distribution before sampling, ensuring that only tokens with significant mass are considered—this is an *entropy regularization* that combats “hallucinations.”  

A non‑obvious insight: **decoding choices act as a form of implicit curriculum learning**.  By adjusting temperature or beam width during generation, we shape the model’s exploration–exploitation trade‑off on the fly, effectively fine‑tuning its latent policy without retraining. This bridges decoding with reinforcement‑style optimization, opening pathways to adaptive, task‑specific generation strategies.
