**What is Data Engineering?**  
Think of a city’s water system. The *data engineer* builds and maintains the pipes, pumps, and treatment plants that move raw water (information) from the source to every house (users, apps, analysts). They decide where to put valves (databases), how fast to flow (batch vs real‑time), and how to keep it clean (quality checks).

**Will everything be created in all places?**  
No. Just as a city doesn’t install a water main under every building, data isn’t duplicated everywhere. Engineers choose the right “pipeline” for each need: a high‑speed line for live dashboards, a slower batch line for deep analytics, and maybe a separate storage vault for archival records. They create the *infrastructure* that lets all parts of the city access the water when they need it, but they don’t lay pipes everywhere—only where useful.

So, data engineering builds the network; it doesn’t flood every corner with copies, only the ones that matter.
