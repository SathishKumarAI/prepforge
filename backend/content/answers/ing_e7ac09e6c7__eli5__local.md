Think of your computer’s storage like a library.  
A hard‑disk drive (HDD) is a huge stack of books on shelves that you have to reach with a robotic arm; each time you look up a page it must spin and the arm move, which takes time.  

An SSD is like a digital library where every book sits in its own shelf slot and can be grabbed instantly by a computer’s “hand.” Because there are no moving parts, SSDs read and write data much faster—often ten times quicker than an HDD. For machine learning, this means training datasets load quickly, models save promptly, and experiments run with less waiting, just like having all your reference material at arm’s reach instead of digging through a crowded aisle.
