Imagine you’re baking a cake that everyone will love.  
**Data synthesis** is like mixing the flour, sugar, eggs, and vanilla in just the right amounts so the batter has all the flavors you need. If you add too much salt or skip the eggs, the cake falls flat—similarly, if your AI data is missing key examples or contains noise, the model will perform poorly.

**Curation** is the careful tasting of that batter before baking. You sift out lumps, adjust sweetness, and make sure each ingredient is fresh. For AI, curators review data, remove duplicates, balance classes, and label things correctly so the model learns from clean, representative examples.

Finally, **evaluations (evals)** are like sending slices of your cake to friends for feedback. You ask them whether it’s sweet enough, moist, or too dense. Those ratings guide you to tweak the recipe. In AI, evals test the model on new, real‑world inputs and measure accuracy, fairness, and safety—so you know when the product truly works before launching it.

Just as a good baker refines ingredients, tests batter, and listens to taste testers, an AI team must synthesize clean data, curate it carefully, and run rigorous evaluations to build a reliable product.
