Imagine your machine‑learning model is a kitchen chef that can make any dish you ask for, but you never see how it cooks inside the oven. **Observability** is like installing smart sensors and cameras on every part of the kitchen so you can watch the recipe unfold: the temperature of the oven, the speed of the mixer, the time each ingredient spends in the pot. In ML terms, observability means collecting real‑time data—metrics (how fast predictions come out), logs (what steps were taken), and traces (the path a single request followed). By looking at these “kitchen cameras,” you can spot problems early: slow inference, wrong inputs, or hidden bugs. Just as a chef uses the kitchen’s feedback to improve taste, developers use ML observability to keep models reliable, safe, and transparent.
