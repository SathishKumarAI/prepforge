Imagine you’re learning to play a new musical instrument. Before you can perform a piece, you first practice scales and simple songs so your fingers know how to move and your ear learns the notes. That early practice is like **pre‑training**: the model (your “musician”) reads lots of text from the internet, learning patterns, grammar, and facts without any specific task in mind.

Once that foundation is set, you ask it to play a particular tune—answer a question, write a story, or translate a sentence. This second step is **inference**: using what it learned before to solve a concrete problem. The “Inference‑Optimal Paradigm” says we should design the pre‑training so that when inference comes, the model can use its knowledge as efficiently as possible—just as a well‑practiced musician can improvise quickly on any new piece. In short: train broadly first, then let the model apply that broad learning to specific tasks with maximum effectiveness.
