Imagine you’re cooking a stew and your recipe book keeps getting updated with new ingredients and steps. **Context engineering** is like deciding which parts of that evolving cookbook you bring into the kitchen at any moment so the dish turns out just right.

In machine learning, especially large language models, *context* means all the text (or data) fed to the model before it answers a question. Because the model can only “see” a limited amount—like the pages that fit in your hands—it’s crucial to pick and arrange those pages carefully. You might keep the most relevant instructions, remove outdated ones, or reorder them so the model understands what you really need.

So context engineering is simply choosing, trimming, and ordering the information given to a model, just as a chef selects the right ingredients and steps from a constantly changing cookbook to make the perfect stew.
