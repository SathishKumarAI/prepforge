Imagine an orchestra where each musician is a specialized robot (the agents) and the conductor is the “Supervisor.” The Supervisor’s job is to tell everyone when to play, what notes to hit, and how loud to be. In practice, three common problems pop up:

1. **Mis‑communication** – if the Supervisor’s instructions are vague or garbled, some musicians may miss cues or play wrong parts, just like a robot that misinterprets its goal.

2. **Over‑control** – when the Supervisor tries to micromanage every note, it slows the whole group; agents become stifled and can’t react quickly to new music (or changing tasks).

3. **Blind spots** – if the Supervisor can’t see what’s happening on stage—maybe its sensors are blocked—it will keep giving outdated or wrong orders, leading the entire orchestra astray.

So a good Supervisor balances clear guidance, enough freedom for agents, and reliable perception of the world.
