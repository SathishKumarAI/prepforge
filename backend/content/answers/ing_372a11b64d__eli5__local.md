Imagine a master chef (the teacher) who can cook any dish perfectly but takes hours to train apprentices. Instead of sending the apprentice through endless practice, the chef invites a quick‑learning sous‑chef (the student) into the kitchen and demonstrates each step while explaining why it matters. The sous‑chef watches closely, noting flavors, timing, and techniques, then tries the recipe on their own, tweaking as they go. After a few attempts, the sous‑chef can produce a dish almost indistinguishable from the master’s version, even though they never saw every possible variation.

In AI, a large, powerful model is the master chef. It “teaches” a smaller, faster model by sharing its predictions—its “taste” of the data. The student learns to mimic those outputs, gaining most of the teacher’s knowledge without needing the same size or training time. This process is called **knowledge distillation**.
