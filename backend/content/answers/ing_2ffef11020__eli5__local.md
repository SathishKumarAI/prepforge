**CPU cycle (in machine learning)**  
Think of a CPU like a chef in a busy kitchen. The “cycle” is the chef’s turn at the stove: he reads a recipe card (fetches an instruction), decides what to do with it (decodes), and then puts the pot on the burner, stirs or flips the food (executes). In machine learning, each cycle lets the processor look up a tiny piece of data—like a pixel value or a weight in a neural network—do a math operation on it, and write the result back. The chef can only do one small step per cycle, but by repeating millions of cycles fast enough, he turns raw ingredients into a finished dish (a trained model). So a CPU cycle is simply one tick of the processor’s “heartbeat,” moving data through its instruction pipeline.
