Think of a long‑running agent like a **gardener who tends a perennial garden**.  
The gardener (the agent) watches the plants every day, waters them, prunes when weeds appear, and notes which flowers bloom best in different seasons. Over time the gardener learns from these observations—if a certain shade of mulch keeps soil moist, or if early spring rain makes some buds wilt—and adjusts the routine accordingly.  

In machine learning, the agent receives **feedback** (rewards) each time it acts. It uses this feedback to update its internal model—a set of rules that predict what will happen next—so that future actions become smarter. Just as the gardener’s experience lets her make better choices year after year, a long‑running agent refines its strategy continuously, learning from every interaction without resetting to scratch each time. This ongoing improvement is the heart of building agents that keep working and getting better over months or years.
