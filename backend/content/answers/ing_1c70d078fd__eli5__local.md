**Encapsulation** is like a sealed lunchbox for your code.  
Inside the box (a *class*) you keep all the ingredients—variables that hold data and methods that act on that data. The outside world can’t reach into the box directly; it must use the lunchbox’s *public* doors (methods) to add or check items.  

This keeps the inside safe from accidental changes, just as a sealed lunchbox protects your sandwich. It also lets you change the recipe inside without breaking other parts of your program—because they only see the doorways.  

So, encapsulation is one of the three pillars of object‑oriented programming (the others being inheritance and polymorphism). It’s simply the practice of bundling data and its functions together while hiding the details from outside use.
