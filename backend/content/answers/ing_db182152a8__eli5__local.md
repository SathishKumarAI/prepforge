Think of a **peer‑to‑peer (P2P) network** like a neighborhood potluck: every house brings its own food and shares it directly with neighbors, no central kitchen or server controls the menu.  
**Features:** Each computer (a *peer*) acts both as a client and a server, storing data locally and serving it to others. Connections are made on demand; there is no single point that everyone must go through.  
**Pros:**  
- **Scalability:** Adding more houses automatically adds more food—more storage and bandwidth.  
- **Resilience:** If one house leaves, the rest keep running; nothing collapses because there was no single “boss” server.  
- **Cost‑effective:** No need for a pricey central data center.  
**Cons:**  
- **Security risk:** Anyone can sneak in bad food (malware) since there is no central gatekeeper to check each contribution.  
- **Data consistency:** Different houses might have slightly different recipes; keeping everyone’s version of the same dish identical is hard.  
- **Management overhead:** Coordinating who shares what and when becomes chaotic without a clear organizer.  

In short, P2P spreads power and load across all participants, but it also requires extra care to keep everything safe, consistent, and well‑organized.
