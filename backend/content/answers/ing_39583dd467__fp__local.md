*Fundamental problem*: an interview is a high‑stakes Bayesian inference task. The interviewer has hidden knowledge (company culture, role specifics) and the candidate must infer it from limited signals (answers, body language). Practice with seasoned interviewers turns this one‑shot estimation into a low‑variance estimator.

**Why it works**

1. **Calibration of prior** – Repeated exposure to real interview dynamics sharpens the candidate’s internal model of what questions are likely and how they’re weighted.  
2. **Signal amplification** – Experienced interviewers provide nuanced feedback (e.g., “that answer was too vague” or “you missed a key point”), turning otherwise noisy signals into precise gradients for learning.  
3. **Noise reduction** – Mock interviews with real people expose the candidate to random variations (tone, timing) that are absent in scripted practice, preventing overfitting to artificial patterns.

- *Optimization*: Each mock session is a step on a gradient‑descent path toward an optimal policy of answering.  
- *Information theory*: The interviewer’s feedback maximizes the mutual information between the candidate’s latent skill vector and observable interview performance.

**Non‑obvious insight**

Mastering these meta‑features yields disproportionate gains because they shift the interviewer’s inference across multiple axes simultaneously.
