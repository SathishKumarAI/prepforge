**Whiteboard exercises are like “designing a map before you travel.”**  
When a team builds an AI model, they first sketch the whole journey on a whiteboard: what data comes in (the road), how it moves through layers (the turns), and where predictions land (the destination). The most common patterns—*feed‑forward*, *convolutional*, *recurrent*, and *transformer*—are just different map layouts that solve particular navigation problems.  

1. **Feed‑forward**: a straight highway; data flows one way, layer by layer, no loops.  
2. **Convolutional**: a city grid that scans neighborhoods (images) for local patterns.  
3. **Recurrent**: a circular road where the current position depends on past turns—ideal for text or speech.  
4. **Transformer**: a high‑speed rail network that lets every station instantly talk to every other, great for long‑range dependencies.

By drawing these maps early, teams spot bottlenecks, swap in better roads, and ensure the AI reaches its goal efficiently.
