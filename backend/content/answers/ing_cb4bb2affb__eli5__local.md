A **Content Delivery Network (CDN)** is like a network of friendly post offices that sit close to people who want your website or app content—images, videos, files, and code.  
Imagine you run a bakery in one city but customers are all over the country. If every order had to travel from your single kitchen to each customer’s door, it would take a long time and many orders might get delayed or lost. Instead, you set up small pastry‑making stations (the CDN servers) in several cities. When someone places an order, they’re automatically sent to the nearest station, which quickly delivers the pastries right to them.

**Benefits of this setup:**

1. **Speed:** Content reaches users faster because it travels a shorter distance.
2. **Reliability:** If one station fails, another nearby can still serve the content.
3. **Scalability:** The network can handle many more visitors without overloading a single server.
4. **Reduced bandwidth costs:** Traffic is spread across many servers, lowering the load on your main origin site.

So, a CDN is simply a distributed system of edge servers that deliver web content quickly and reliably to users wherever they are.
