Imagine an AI as a chef who can only use words like ingredients.  
A small “vocabulary” is like a pantry with just a few staples—tomato, salt, pepper. The chef (the model) can cook quickly and keeps the recipe simple, but it can’t make many different dishes; it will keep repeating the same flavors.  

A huge vocabulary is like a supermarket stocked with thousands of spices and exotic foods. The chef now has endless options to create nuanced recipes, but it takes much more time and memory to remember where each spice comes from and how it should be used. The kitchen gets crowded, the cooking process slows, and you need a bigger fridge (more computing power) to keep everything organized.

So the tradeoff is: **speed & simplicity vs. richness & complexity**—you choose based on how many “dishes” (meaningful outputs) you want the AI to produce and what resources you can afford.
