### Why “runway” matters for an AI‑startup

**Fundamental problem:**  
A startup must keep operating long enough to reach a *product‑market fit* (PMF). In the tech world, PMF is often measured by user engagement, revenue, or data volume. The question **“How many months of runway do you have?”** forces founders to confront two intertwined realities:

1. **Capital constraints** – capital is finite; spending on talent, cloud compute, and data acquisition quickly depletes it.
2. **Learning curves** – AI systems improve only with more data and iterations; each iteration costs time and money.

The *runway* is the ratio of cash on hand to monthly burn rate. It tells investors whether the startup can afford the *learning phase* before hitting PMF. If runway < 6 months, a founder risks running out of funds before the model converges or before enough users validate the product.

**Deeper principle:**  
This is an application of **resource‑constrained optimization**: we must maximize expected return \(E[R]\) subject to a budget constraint \(C \leq B\). Runway turns the abstract budget into a tangible horizon, converting *probabilistic* model performance into a *deterministic* survival metric.

**Non‑obvious insight:**  
Most founders treat runway as “how many months before we need more money.” The trick is to **align runway with data acquisition milestones**, not just burn rate. For example, if an AI model requires 10 M labeled examples and each label costs $0.05, the *data budget* may be a larger portion of the runway than compute or personnel. A disciplined founder will allocate runway by *information cost*—the amount of new data needed to close the performance gap—rather than by generic operating expenses.

In interviews, probing how founders partition runway into *learning*, *validation*, and *scaling* phases reveals whether they truly grasp the economics of AI development.
