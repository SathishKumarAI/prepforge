Imagine a smart home where every device—lights, thermostat, fridge—can “talk” whenever something important happens. That’s an **event‑driven architecture** (EDA). In EDA, *events* are like little notes that get written when something noteworthy occurs: the door opens, the temperature rises, or a new order is placed. A **notification system** listens for these notes and instantly reacts—sending a text, turning on lights, or updating a dashboard.

Think of it as a newsroom: reporters (devices) send breaking news (events) to a central editor (the notification engine). The editor doesn’t wait for a daily report; they act right away, forwarding the story to readers via email, push alerts, or social posts. This way, every part of the system stays in sync without constantly checking each other—just like a newsroom that never misses a headline because it’s built around instant notifications.
