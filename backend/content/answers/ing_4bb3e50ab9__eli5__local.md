Imagine you’re trying to teach a child to draw a cat. If you let the child copy every tiny detail of a photo—every whisker, shadow, and stray hair—they’ll end up with a picture that looks exactly like that one photo but won’t work well on other cats. Regularization in machine learning is like telling the child, “Focus on the big shapes: ears, tail, body.” It gently nudges the model to ignore tiny, noisy quirks that only fit the training data and may not generalize.

**Regularization** adds a small penalty for making the model’s parameters (the numbers it uses) too large or too complex. Think of it as a gentle “no‑over‑fitting” coach: the bigger the penalty, the simpler the model stays. This keeps predictions accurate on new, unseen data while still learning useful patterns from the training set.
