Imagine you’re at a busy coffee shop that has one front counter where customers place orders and pay. A **proxy** is like a friendly friend who sits in line for you: you tell them what drink you want, they go to the counter, get it, and bring it back. The friend keeps your identity hidden from the barista.

A **reverse proxy** flips that idea. Picture a bustling airport terminal with many gates (the real servers) but only one large reception desk (the reverse proxy). Passengers (users) arrive at the single desk, which decides which gate to send each person to based on their destination. The passengers never see the individual gates; they just interact with the reception. The reverse proxy hides the internal layout of the airport and can balance traffic, cache popular pages, or add security before handing off requests to the right server.
