**Context Engineering in machine learning**

Imagine you’re teaching a child to recognize animals by showing them pictures. If you only show cats and dogs, the child learns those two but can’t tell a rabbit from a cat when both appear together. *Context engineering* is like giving that child extra clues—“the picture also shows a ball,” “there’s a fence in the background”—so it understands how objects relate to one another.

In ML, we feed a model not just raw data (the animal pictures) but also surrounding information (“weather,” “time of day,” “location”) that helps it interpret each piece correctly. By carefully choosing and arranging this extra context—just as you’d pick the right background clues—you help the model build richer, more accurate predictions.

So, *context engineering* is the art of selecting, formatting, and feeding relevant surrounding details to a machine‑learning system so it can make sense of its inputs just like a child learns from useful hints.
