Think of machine learning (ML) like training a puppy to fetch. **Improvements** are the better tricks the puppy learns over time—jumping higher, fetching farther, and obeying more commands. In ML, these “tricks” are higher accuracy, faster predictions, or smarter decisions.

The way we make those improvements is two‑step:

1. **More practice data** – giving the puppy a variety of toys, sounds, and owners helps it generalize. For an algorithm, this means feeding it more examples (images, text, sensor readings) so it can recognize patterns in new situations.
2. **Better training methods** – teaching the puppy with positive reinforcement instead of punishment. In ML we tweak the learning rule (called a *loss function*) and use smarter “optimizers” that adjust the model’s internal weights more efficiently. Regularly checking progress (validation) prevents over‑training, just as rewarding correct fetches keeps the dog focused.

Together, richer data and sharper training turn an ordinary puppy into a top‑class fetcher—just as they turn a basic algorithm into a powerful ML system.
