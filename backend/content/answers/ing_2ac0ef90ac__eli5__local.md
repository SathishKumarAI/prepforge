Imagine your computer as a kitchen and machine‑learning models as recipes. A **cache** is like a small pantry that keeps frequently used ingredients (data or model pieces) right at hand so you don’t have to fetch them from the fridge every time. It speeds up cooking, but it also brings some hidden flavors.

First, **stale ingredients**: if the pantry holds an old version of an ingredient while the fridge has a fresh one, your dish will taste off. In ML this means a cached model or data might be out‑of‑date, giving wrong predictions.

Second, **limited space**: the pantry can only hold so many items. When it’s full, new ingredients must push old ones out—sometimes you lose something important.

Third, **security crumbs**: if anyone can reach the pantry, they might steal or tamper with your precious data, just as a thief could take a secret spice blend.

Finally, **sync headaches**: keeping the pantry and fridge in sync (caching policy) takes effort; if you forget to refresh, everyone gets stale food.

So when you use caching for machine‑learning, remember to keep it fresh, not too crowded, secure, and well‑synchronized.
