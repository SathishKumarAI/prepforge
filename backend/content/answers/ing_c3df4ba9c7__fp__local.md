**From the problem to a principled solution**

Recruiters face a *resource‑allocation* dilemma: they must attract highly specialized AI talent while keeping compensation budgets below those of tech giants. The fundamental cost driver is **scarcity of expertise**—a few individuals command top pay because their skill set unlocks high‑value projects. Thus, the recruiter’s objective function becomes

\[
\max_{\text{offer}}\; \underbrace{\text{Talent Value}}_{V} - \underbrace{\text{Total Cost}}_{C},
\]

where \(V\) is the expected incremental revenue from a hire and \(C\) includes salary, benefits, and opportunity cost.

**Why “frontier pay” must be avoided**

If recruiters match frontier salaries, they shift the *budget constraint* to the right, forcing them to cut elsewhere (e.g., hiring fewer people or reducing investment in training). Moreover, paying a premium erodes *signal‑to‑noise*: many capable candidates will accept higher offers, diluting the recruiter’s ability to identify those who truly add strategic value.

**A principled alternative: skill‑bundling and upside sharing**

Instead of equalizing pay, recruiters should:

1. **Bundle complementary skills** (e.g., NLP + cloud ops) so that a single hire delivers more \(V\) per dollar.
2. **Introduce performance‑linked equity or profit‑sharing** that scales with the AI product’s success. This turns fixed cost into *variable risk*, aligning incentives without inflating base salary.

**Non‑obvious insight**

The real leverage lies in **“upside capture” via product ownership**, not merely cash. By giving early‑stage talent a stake in the outcomes they help create, recruiters convert scarce expertise into a scalable reward mechanism that outperforms static frontier wages while preserving budgetary discipline.
