Imagine you’re cooking a big meal for guests.  
A **tool** is like a kitchen gadget—say, a blender or a whisk. You use it when the recipe calls for it; it does one job well and then you set it aside.  
A **subagent**, on the other hand, is a helper who stays in the kitchen all day: they can chop vegetables, stir sauces, check the oven, and even suggest new recipes if needed. They’re autonomous enough to act on their own but still follow your overall plan.

In AI, tools are simple modules that perform specific tasks (image recognition, language translation) when you ask them. Subagents are mini‑AI “people” inside a larger agent; they can decide what to do next, cooperate with other subagents, and adapt without you telling every step. Using tools keeps the system lean; adding subagents lets it grow smarter but risks over‑engineering if not managed carefully.
