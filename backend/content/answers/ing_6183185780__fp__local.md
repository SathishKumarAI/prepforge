**Why a leaderboard can be the *health bar* of a ML service**

The core problem is monitoring a model’s **utility drift**—the gap between its expected performance (at deployment) and what it actually delivers in production. Utility is a function of data distribution, latency, resource usage, and user‑feedback signals. To keep the system healthy we must quantify these dimensions *in real time* and rank them against acceptable thresholds.

A leaderboard naturally turns each metric into an ordinal score:

1. **Feature‑wise drift** → compute per‑feature KL divergence from training distribution; lower is better.  
2. **Latency & throughput** → map to percentiles (p95, p99); higher latency penalizes the rank.  
3. **Error rates** → weighted loss on live predictions; larger error pushes the service down.

By normalising each axis into a common scale and aggregating with a convex combination, we obtain a single *health score*. The leaderboard then ranks all deployed models or micro‑services, exposing which ones are slipping.

*Non‑obvious insight:* A leaderboard is not just a vanity metric—it enforces **feedback‑loop closure**. Because the ranking directly influences which model gets promoted to traffic, teams optimise for stability and robustness rather than raw accuracy alone. This aligns incentives with the deeper principle of *information bottleneck*: we trade off information (accuracy) against entropy (resource cost), and the leaderboard makes that trade explicit.

Thus, a well‑designed leaderboard turns disparate health signals into an actionable, interpretable order—exactly what operators need to keep ML services alive.
