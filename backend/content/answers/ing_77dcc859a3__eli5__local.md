**Context engineering** is like giving a recipe the right kitchen tools before you start cooking.  
Imagine you’re making soup: you need a pot, a ladle, and spices. In machine learning, the “spices” are pieces of information that tell the model what’s important—like the season or region of a dish. Context engineering is the process of picking, preparing, and arranging those spices so the model can taste them correctly.

*Unavoidable terms:*  
- **Model** – a computer program that learns patterns from data.  
- **Data** – raw facts (images, text, numbers) fed into the model.  

By carefully selecting which data to feed and how to label it (the “spices”), you help the model understand its task—just as a chef uses the right ingredients to make a dish taste great.
