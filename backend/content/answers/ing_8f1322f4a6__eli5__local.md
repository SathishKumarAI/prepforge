Imagine you’re teaching a child to recognize apples and oranges. You show many pictures, telling the child each time whether it’s an apple or an orange. The child starts noticing patterns: “Apples are usually red, round, and have a small stem; oranges are often orange, slightly dimpled.” After enough examples, the child can guess the fruit in new pictures without being told.

In machine learning this child is a *model*. The pictures are the *data*, and each apple‑or‑orange label is the *target* we want the model to learn. The model’s job is to find the hidden patterns (the “rules”) that map any new picture to its correct label, just like the child does.

A key term: **training** – the process where the model looks at many labeled examples and adjusts itself until it can guess correctly most of the time. Once trained, the model can *predict* or *classify* unseen data, just as the child recognizes a new fruit picture instantly.
