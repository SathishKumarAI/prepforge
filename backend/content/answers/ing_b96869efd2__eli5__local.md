Imagine your brain as a huge library that learns how to read new books just by looking at many examples.  
**Machine Learning (ML)** is like giving the library thousands of picture‑books and asking it, “What’s inside this page?” The library builds rules from those examples—if you see a cat with whiskers, it remembers *cat*. It doesn’t know the word *cat* beforehand; it learns the pattern.  

**Deep Learning** takes that idea further: instead of one set of rules, the library stacks many tiny “mini‑libraries” on top of each other, each one learning simpler shapes (edges, colors) and passing what it found to the next layer. The final layer stitches all those simple cues into a full picture—like recognizing a face or translating speech.  

So ML is the broad skill of learning from data; deep learning is a powerful, layered way of doing that, mimicking how our own neurons build up complex understanding step by step.
