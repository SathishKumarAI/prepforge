Think of a neural network as a **kitchen brigade** that learns how to cook a dish from many trials.  
The *neurons* are the chefs, each one tasting (receiving) an ingredient—an input value—and deciding how strongly to pass it on.  
Each chef has a little “spice dial” called a **weight**; turning the dial up makes the ingredient taste stronger in the next dish, turning it down weakens it.  
The *layers* are the stations: raw ingredients go through the prep station (first layer), then cooking station (second layer), and finally plating (output layer).  
When the brigade gets a wrong dish, they adjust their spice dials so that next time the taste matches the desired flavor.  
In this way, a neural network learns to transform inputs into correct outputs by iteratively tweaking its weights—just like chefs mastering a recipe through practice.
