Imagine you’re at a birthday party where everyone wears different colored shirts—red for the cake, blue for balloons, green for the confetti machine. The host wants to know exactly which parts of the room belong to each color so she can clean up or decorate accordingly. In computer vision, that “host” is an algorithm and the “room” is a picture. **Image segmentation** splits the photo into tiny regions (pixels) and labels each one with a category—car, tree, sky, person—just like tagging every spot in the room with its shirt color.

*Semantic segmentation* goes further: it doesn’t just separate objects; it gives every pixel a meaningful label (“road,” “building”). Think of painting a map where every square meter is colored to show what it represents. This lets self‑driving cars see drivable lanes, robots pick up objects, and medical scans highlight tumors.
