Think of Elastic Cloud like a restaurant that serves data‑search “food.”  
*Hosted (VM) offering*: You rent a whole kitchen (a virtual machine). The kitchen is always open, and you decide how many chefs (CPU), how much oven space (memory), and how long to keep it running. You pay for the entire kitchen even if no one orders.  
*Serverless offering*: You only pay for the exact portion of the kitchen you use—just the minutes your “chef” cooks a dish. If nothing is being searched, the kitchen stays idle and costs zero.  

Both give you the same menu (Elastic Search, Kibana, etc.), but with serverless you get automatic scaling: when many people order at once, more chefs appear; when orders drop, they vanish, saving money. Hosted gives predictable performance but higher fixed cost, while serverless offers pay‑as‑you‑go flexibility for variable workloads.
