Imagine you’re building a team of tiny robots (the *sub‑agents*) that each has one job—one robot paints the walls, another installs the lights, and another plants flowers. If each robot only knows its own task and nothing else, they’ll finish their parts but the house may look mismatched: the paint might clash with the décor, or the lights could be too bright for the new plants.

*Context engineering* is like giving every robot a shared “house‑design plan.” It tells each sub‑agent what the overall style should be, what colors work together, and how its task fits into the big picture. In machine learning, we do the same: before a model (or sub‑agent) learns or acts, we supply it with relevant background information—data patterns, constraints, or goals—so that all parts of the system cooperate smoothly and produce consistent, useful results.
