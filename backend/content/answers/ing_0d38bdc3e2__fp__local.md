### Why the “FAANG” interview pipeline is so structured  

At its core, a hiring interview must **verify that a candidate can solve novel, high‑impact problems**—exactly what ML teams face daily. To do this at scale, top tech firms decompose the problem into three orthogonal dimensions:

1. **Conceptual fluency** – does the applicant understand *why* an algorithm works?  
2. **Algorithmic skill** – can they derive or implement a solution efficiently?  
3. **Systems intuition** – can they reason about data pipelines, latency, and scaling?

Each interview stage is designed to isolate one dimension, allowing multiple interviewers (often 5–7) to evaluate the same candidate from different angles while keeping bias low.

### The typical pipeline

| Stage | Goal | Typical format |
|-------|------|----------------|
| **Phone / video** | Conceptual fluency + quick coding | 1‑2 algorithm questions, 30‑min code review |
| **On‑site first round** | Algorithmic skill | 3–4 medium‑difficulty problems, timed (45 min each) |
| **On‑site second round** | Systems intuition & deep dive | Project discussion, ML architecture design, “whiteboard” scaling questions |
| **Final interview** | Cultural fit + edge cases | Behavioral questions + “what would you do if…” scenarios |

The *time budget* (≈ 3 hrs total) reflects the trade‑off between depth and breadth: deeper dives would leave little time for others, while shallow checks risk over‑rejection.

### Non‑obvious insight

Many candidates assume the **“coding test”** is merely a gate. In reality it’s a *probabilistic estimator* of future performance. A well‑designed problem forces the candidate to exhibit the same mental model used in production: think in terms of time/space complexity, edge cases, and maintainability. Thus the interview itself is an engineered **simulation** of real work, calibrated so that a correct answer correlates strongly with successful ML deployment.

In short, FAANG’s multilayered interview is not arbitrary fluff; it is a carefully derived, multi‑criteria assessment pipeline tuned to predict high‑impact machine‑learning engineers.
