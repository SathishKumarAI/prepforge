Imagine you’re baking a cake for a big event. **Reliability** is the guarantee that every slice will taste good and arrive on time—no one gets an empty plate or a burnt bite. In software terms, it means your system keeps working correctly over long periods, even when things go wrong.

Related ideas:

* **Availability** is like having a backup oven ready. It measures how often the cake can be served (uptime) versus being down for repairs.
* **Fault tolerance** is the recipe that lets you keep baking even if one ingredient fails—like using a substitute flour without ruining the taste.
* **Redundancy** is keeping extra ovens and ingredients on standby, so a single failure doesn’t stop production.

All these concepts aim to keep your “cake” (system) consistently good, but reliability focuses on correctness over time, while availability cares about being ready when needed.
