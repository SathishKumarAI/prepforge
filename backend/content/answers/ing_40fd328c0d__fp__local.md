**Why the “big‑firm bias” matters for every organization**

The core problem AI solves is *information extraction at scale*. A model can sift millions of data points, detect patterns invisible to humans, and generate actionable insights in seconds. The cost of building such a system—data collection, labeling, compute, talent—is a function of **scale**: the more data you have, the better the model; the more people you can afford, the faster you iterate.

Large firms already possess both high‑volume datasets (customer interactions, sensor streams) and deep pockets for hiring top ML talent. Their marginal benefit from AI is higher because the *learning curve*—the time to converge on a useful model—is steepest when you start with a rich foundation. Small or medium enterprises, lacking data depth or specialist staff, experience diminishing returns: each new feature adds complexity but little incremental insight.

**Non‑obvious insight:** *Data is the currency of AI; it can be monetized like capital.* Even a modest investment in structured data (e.g., cleaning legacy logs) can unlock disproportionate value. Small firms should therefore focus on creating high‑quality, domain‑specific datasets first—this lowers the barrier to entry and levels the playing field.
