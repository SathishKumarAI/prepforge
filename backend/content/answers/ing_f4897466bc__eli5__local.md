In machine‑learning terms, an **offline client** is a device or program that can understand and predict things on its own, without needing to talk to the cloud every time it learns or makes a decision.

Think of it like a chef who has all his recipes written down in a notebook. The chef (the offline client) doesn’t need to call the restaurant’s central kitchen (the online server) for each dish; he can look up the recipe, mix the ingredients, and serve the meal right away. When new recipes arrive—maybe from a cookbook exchange—the chef adds them to his notebook. Once the notebook is full, he can cook any dish on demand, even if the internet goes down.

In ML, the “recipes” are the trained model parameters. The offline client stores these locally, so it can classify images, predict weather, or translate text instantly, without sending data back and forth to a remote server.
