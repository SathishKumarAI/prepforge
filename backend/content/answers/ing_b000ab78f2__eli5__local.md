Think of an AI system like a smart robot helper you’re hiring to work in your kitchen.  
Before you let it start cooking, the chef (the interviewer) looks for two warning signs: **Evaluation** and **Observability**.

*Evaluation* is how well the robot can prove its recipe skills. If the chef only asks it to “make a cake” without giving clear ingredients or taste tests, the robot’s performance stays vague. A red flag is when the robot shows results that look great on paper but never get tested against real customers—like claiming a soup tastes good without letting anyone actually eat it.

*Observability* is how easy it is to see what the robot is doing inside its “brain.” If the chef can’t track why the robot chose a particular ingredient, or if the logs vanish when something goes wrong, that’s a red flag. It means you’ll have trouble debugging or improving the robot later.

So, interviewers warn: make sure the AI’s results are verifiable (evaluation) and its inner workings are transparent (observability). If either is missing, trust the robot less.
