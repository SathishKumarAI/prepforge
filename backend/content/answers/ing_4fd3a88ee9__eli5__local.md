**Step 7 – “Define an Agent”**

Think of an AI agent like a smart robot chef in a kitchen.  
The *agent* is the whole system that decides what to cook, how to cook it, and when to serve it. It has three parts:

1. **Sensors (taste buds)** – read data from the world (camera, microphone, sensor).  
2. **Actuators (hands)** – send commands to appliances or software (move a robot arm, click a button).  
3. **Brain (recipe book)** – runs the logic that maps sensory input to actions.

When you ask the chef “Make me a salad,” the sensors check what ingredients are available, the brain decides which steps to follow, and the actuators stir, slice, and plate everything.  

In AI, an agent is any program that perceives its environment through inputs, processes those inputs with rules or learning models, and produces outputs (actions) that influence the world. It’s a closed loop: perceive → decide → act → perceive again. That’s how AI agents work—just like a chef following a recipe but guided by data instead of taste alone.
