Imagine you’re making a sandwich that tastes like your favorite dish, but you can’t taste it until all the layers are stacked together.  
A **Multilayer Perceptron (MLP)** is a computer “sandwich” made of three parts:

1. **Input layer** – the first slice that takes in raw data (e.g., pixels or numbers).  
2. **Hidden layers** – each slice inside where we add flavor by multiplying the data with weights and adding a bias, then passing it through an activation function (a simple rule like “if‑else” that decides how much of this piece to keep).  
3. **Output layer** – the final slice that gives the answer (e.g., class labels or predicted values).

Just as you adjust each ingredient until the sandwich tastes right, the MLP learns by tweaking its weights so that, when all layers are combined, the output matches the desired result. The whole process is called *training* and uses a technique called back‑propagation to fine‑tune every layer.
