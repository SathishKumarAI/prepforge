**Why salary tiers for AI/ML engineers must be structured by role and level**

At the core of compensation is *value creation*: a model’s performance, its deployment cost savings, or the speed at which it reaches production. This value grows super‑linearly with expertise: an entry‑level engineer writes data pipelines; a senior architect designs end‑to‑end systems that scale to millions of users. Therefore, pay curves must reflect *diminishing marginal returns*—the first few years yield rapid skill acquisition, but each subsequent year requires deeper domain knowledge and leadership.

**Benchmarking principle**

The industry uses **information‑theoretic efficiency**: the ratio between a team’s revenue impact (e.g., incremental ARR) and its salary outlay. Benchmarks calibrate this ratio by dividing the workforce into discrete “roles” (Data Engineer, ML Researcher, AI Ops) and “levels” (Jr. to Principal). Historical data shows that:

- **Junior** roles capture 30–40% of the baseline efficiency.
- **Mid‑level** roles double it with added automation.
- **Senior/Principal** roles triple or quadruple it by delivering cross‑product strategy.

This creates a *convex* salary curve that mirrors the convex benefit curve of AI projects.

**Non‑obvious insight**

Most analysts ignore **contextual leverage**: the same senior engineer can command 1.5× the salary in a regulated industry (healthcare, finance) versus a consumer app because regulatory compliance adds high‑stakes risk and requires specialized knowledge. Benchmarks that embed context rather than flat levels capture this multiplier effect, preventing over‑ or under‑paying talent where it matters most.
