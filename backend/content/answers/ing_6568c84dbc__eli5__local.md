Imagine you’re baking a batch of cookies and each cookie’s size is a random number. A **probability distribution** is the recipe that tells you how likely each size is—like saying “most cookies will be medium, some will be tiny, few will be huge.”  

1. **Probability** – The chance something happens (e.g., “there’s a 30 % chance this cookie is small”).  
2. **Random variable** – A number you can’t predict exactly but can describe statistically (the cookie size).  
3. **Expectation (mean)** – The average size you’d get if you baked infinitely many cookies.  
4. **Variance** – How spread out the sizes are around that average—big variance means wildly different cookies.  
5. **Standard deviation** – The square‑root of variance; a more intuitive measure of spread.  
6. **Conditional probability** – Probability given extra information (“given this cookie is chocolate chip, what’s the chance it’s large?”).  
7. **Joint distribution** – How two variables move together (cookie size and number of raisins).  
8. **Bayes’ theorem** – Updating beliefs when new evidence arrives (adjusting your guess about the recipe after tasting a cookie).  
9. **Likelihood** – How well a particular set of parameters explains the observed data (how good a recipe fits the cookies you already baked).  
10. **Entropy** – A measure of uncertainty—higher entropy means more surprise in which cookie size will appear next.  

These ideas let machines learn patterns from data, just as a baker refines a recipe by tasting and adjusting.
