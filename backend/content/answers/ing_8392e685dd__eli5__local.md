Imagine a library where every book is carefully organized on shelves. **Anomaly detection** is like having a librarian who watches the books’ positions: if a book suddenly appears on the wrong shelf or in an odd spot, the librarian flags it as “anomalous.”  

In machine learning, we give a computer this same job. First, it learns what “normal” looks like by studying lots of data—just as the librarian learns the usual layout. Then, when new data arrives (a new book), the algorithm checks whether it fits that learned pattern. If something doesn’t match—a sudden spike in sales, a strange network packet, or an unusual medical test result—it raises a warning.

*Key terms:*  
- **Algorithm**: A step‑by‑step recipe for solving a problem.  
- **Normal data**: Information that follows the usual pattern.  
- **Anomaly**: An observation that deviates from normal.

Applications are everywhere: fraud detection in credit cards, spotting faulty sensors in factories, or catching early signs of disease from medical images. The librarian’s eye becomes an automated, fast, and often invisible guard against problems before they become serious.
