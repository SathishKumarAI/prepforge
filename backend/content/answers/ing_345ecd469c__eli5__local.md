Imagine a smart robot librarian that can answer questions, recommend books, and even write short stories. **Agent reliability** is like the librarian’s track record: how often it gives correct answers and follows your instructions safely. Researchers watch this “track record” closely, just as a teacher checks homework grades, because any slip could lead to misinformation or harm.

But every robot has **failure modes**—ways it can go wrong. One mode is *misinterpretation*: the librarian might read “red book” as the color rather than the title. Another is *hallucination*: it makes up facts that sound plausible but are false, like a student inventing a story to finish an essay. A third is *over‑confidence*: the robot insists on an answer even when unsure, much like someone giving an opinion without evidence.

The **research radar** is a dashboard that keeps all these possibilities in view. It shows which failure modes have happened, how often, and under what conditions—so developers can tweak the librarian’s training or safety rules before it serves anyone else. This way, we build AI agents that are trustworthy, transparent, and ready to help without surprising us.
