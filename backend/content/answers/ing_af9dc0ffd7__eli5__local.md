**Deep learning is like training a child to recognize faces by showing many pictures.**  
You give the system thousands of photos of people and tell it who each one is. The “child” (the computer) looks at each picture, learns which parts—eyes, nose, mouth—are important, and then starts guessing new faces on its own.  

*Layers* are the child’s different stages of thinking: first they notice simple shapes, then patterns, finally whole faces. Each layer adjusts tiny “weights” (the importance it gives to each feature) based on how wrong its guesses were. Over time those weights settle so the system can quickly identify new faces with high accuracy.

So deep learning is simply a very layered, data‑driven way of teaching computers to see and understand complex patterns—just like a child learns from many examples.
