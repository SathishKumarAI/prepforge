Imagine a huge classroom where every student wants to know the latest rumor—say, which snack will be served tomorrow. Instead of asking the teacher directly (which would overwhelm her), each student whispers the rumor to just a few classmates. Those classmates then whisper it to their own friends, and so on. Because everyone participates, the rumor spreads fast, even if the class has thousands of students, and no single person gets stuck with too many questions.

That’s the essence of a gossip protocol in computing: **“gossip”** means passing information from node to node; **nodes** are computers or servers. Each node randomly selects a handful of neighbors to share its data, and those neighbors repeat the process. The result is high scalability—information reaches everyone quickly without any single point of congestion—just like a rumor that spreads efficiently through a bustling classroom.
