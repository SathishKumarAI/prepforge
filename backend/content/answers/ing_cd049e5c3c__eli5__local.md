Imagine you’re trying to teach a child how to throw a ball into a bucket.  
**Bias** is like giving the child a rigid rule—“always aim straight ahead.” If that rule is wrong (the bucket isn’t directly ahead), every throw will miss, no matter how many times it’s practiced. That’s high bias: the model makes a strong assumption that can’t fit the real pattern.

**Variance** is what happens when you let the child practice in a chaotic playground—every time they swing differently because of wind or uneven ground. One throw might hit, another miss, even though the rule was right. High variance means the model changes a lot with new data.

The trade‑off is finding the sweet spot: a rule that’s flexible enough to adapt (low bias) but stable enough not to overreact to every small change (low variance). That balance lets the child—your machine learning algorithm—learn the true shape of the task.
