Think of a kitchen where you’re preparing meals for a big party.  
**Latency** is the time it takes from when you put an order on the counter until that dish leaves the kitchen – the *wait time* for one customer.  
**Throughput** is how many dishes the kitchen can finish in a given hour – the *overall output rate*.  
**Bandwidth** is like the width of the kitchen’s conveyor belt: how much food it can carry at once, which limits both latency (a narrow belt slows each dish) and throughput (more belt space lets more dishes move together).

So, low latency means quick service for a single order; high throughput means many orders finished per hour; ample bandwidth allows those orders to flow smoothly. All three work together: fast cooking, enough space, and minimal waiting give the best dining experience.
