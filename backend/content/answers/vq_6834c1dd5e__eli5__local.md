Imagine you’re teaching a child to recognize animals by showing pictures. At first the child just copies what they see, but after many examples they start noticing patterns—four legs, fur, whiskers—and can guess correctly even with new images. That’s essentially what machine learning does for computers: it learns from lots of data instead of being told every rule.

The purpose is to let machines find hidden regularities and make predictions or decisions on their own.
