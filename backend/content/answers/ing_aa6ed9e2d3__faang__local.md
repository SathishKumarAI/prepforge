**Clarify**  
You’re asking about *on‑policy distillation*, a technique that blends reinforcement learning (RL) and knowledge distillation. The goal: keep an agent’s policy up‑to‑date with the environment while leveraging a teacher network for faster convergence and regularization.

Assumptions to confirm:  
- We have a trained or online‑learning “teacher” policy πₜ.  
- A student policy π_s is being updated concurrently.  
- The environment provides on‑policy samples (i.e., trajectories generated by the current student).  

**Approach**  
1. Sample trajectory *τ* = {(sᵢ, aᵢ)} using the student’s current policy.  
2. Compute teacher logits or action probabilities for each state in τ.  
3. Form a loss that blends: (a) standard RL objective (policy gradient / actor‑critic) and (b) KL divergence between teacher and student actions.  
4. Update the student with this combined loss, ensuring gradients flow only through on‑policy data.

**Depth**  
- **Loss**: L = –E[∑ log π_s(aᵢ|sᵢ)·Rᵢ] + λ·KL(πₜ(·|sᵢ)‖π_s(·|sᵢ)).  
  *λ* balances exploration vs. imitation.  
- Complexity: O(|τ|·C), where C is cost of forward pass through teacher (usually negligible if teacher is a lightweight network).  
- Guarantees: Since samples come from π_s, convergence properties of on‑policy RL remain intact; the KL term acts as a regularizer, mitigating catastrophic forgetting and smoothing policy updates.

**Edge Cases**  
- If λ is too high, the student may overfit to stale teacher actions, hurting adaptability.  
- Teacher drift: if πₜ changes rapidly, stale logits can mislead the student—use a moving‑average teacher or update schedule.  
- High variance in returns Rᵢ can destabilize training; baseline subtraction helps.

**Optimize & Communicate**  
To improve stability, clip the KL divergence (like PPO’s surrogate) and anneal λ over time. In interviews, explain that on‑policy distillation retains theoretical guarantees of vanilla policy gradient while borrowing the sample efficiency of imitation learning—hence “best of both worlds.” This trade‑off is especially valuable in safety‑critical or data‑scarce RL settings.
