Think of a restaurant menu (the **REST API**) that lists fixed dishes: you order “pizza” or “salad,” and the kitchen always gives you exactly what’s on the plate.  
A **GraphQL** server is like a kitchen where you can ask for *any* ingredients you need—“give me the tomato, the basil, and the cheese from the pizza but leave out the pepperoni.” You describe the shape of the data you want, and the server fetches just that, no more, no less.

Machine learning (ML) isn’t a replacement for this ordering system. ML is more like the chef’s recipe book: it learns patterns to predict what dish you’ll enjoy next or how to cook faster. The API—whether REST or GraphQL—is still the front‑door interface letting clients request data; ML sits behind, making smarter decisions about that data. So, GraphQL gives flexible requests, while ML adds predictive intelligence on top of those requests.
