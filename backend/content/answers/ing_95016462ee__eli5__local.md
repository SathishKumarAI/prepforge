Imagine learning to play a piano piece by practicing the same passage every day. If you practice it once a week, your fingers forget it quickly; if you practice it twice a day, your memory gets overloaded and you waste time on things you already know. **Spaced repetition** is like a smart coach that schedules each review just before you’re about to forget it—longer gaps after you’ve mastered a part, shorter ones right after you first learn it.

In research, scientists study how the brain’s *memory decay* curve works and design algorithms that pick the optimal next practice time. In applications, apps (like flashcard programs) use this idea: they show you words or facts at increasing intervals so you remember them with minimal effort. The goal is to keep knowledge fresh while saving your time—exactly what a good teacher would do.
