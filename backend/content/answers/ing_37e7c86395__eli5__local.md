Imagine you’re looking for a recipe in a huge cookbook.  

**Contextual retrieval** is like reading the table of contents first, then flipping straight to the “Chocolate Cake” page because you already know what you want. Your brain grabs just that section and uses all the surrounding pages (ingredients, notes, variations) to finish the task.

**Late chunking** is a different strategy: you skim the whole cookbook to see where the “Cake” sections are, but you don’t read them immediately. You first collect a few page numbers (chunks), then later dive into each one only when you need it—like pulling out a stack of recipe cards and reading them one by one.

In AI terms, contextual retrieval pulls in relevant information on demand, while late chunking waits to gather larger pieces before processing. Both help the system stay focused without being overwhelmed.
