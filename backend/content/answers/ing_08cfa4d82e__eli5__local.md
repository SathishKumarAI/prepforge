Think of an AI system as a **robot chef**.  
*Agents* are the individual kitchen stations—one station chops vegetables, another grills meat, and yet another handles sauces. Each agent has its own recipe (algorithm) and works autonomously, but they all aim for the same dish.  

*Tool use* is like giving each station a special gadget: a mandoline slicer, an immersion blender, or a sous‑vide cooker. The agents learn how to operate these tools from instructions (tool‑specific code) so they can finish their part faster and more accurately than if they tried to do everything with bare hands.

*MCP* stands for **Multi‑Component Planning**. It’s the head chef’s master schedule that decides when each station starts, what order the tasks go in, and how resources are shared. MCP ensures the stations don’t step on each other’s toes—e.g., it won’t tell the grill to start before the veggies are sliced—and keeps the whole kitchen running smoothly.

Together, agents, tool use, and MCP let an AI “chef” cook a complex meal reliably, just like a well‑coordinated human kitchen.
