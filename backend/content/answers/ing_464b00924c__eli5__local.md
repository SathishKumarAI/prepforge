**Step 2: Plan Before You Code – the “Recipe” of an AI Project**

Think of building a neural network like cooking a gourmet meal.  
First you decide *what* dish to make (the problem), then you gather ingredients and tools (data, libraries, hardware). Next comes the recipe sheet: a clear list of steps—how many layers, what activation functions, how to split data, which loss function, how long to train, and how to evaluate results.  

In AI terms, *planning* means writing that recipe before you write code. You outline the model architecture, choose the algorithm, set hyper‑parameters (the seasoning), decide on training/validation splits (portion sizes), and plan evaluation metrics (taste tests). This pre‑coding map saves time, prevents wasted experiments, and ensures everyone on the team knows exactly what to cook.

**Unavoidable terms**

- **Neural network**: a series of layers that transform input data into predictions.  
- **Hyper‑parameters**: adjustable settings like learning rate or number of hidden units.  
- **Loss function**: a formula that tells the model how wrong its guesses are, guiding training.  

By drafting this recipe first, you turn chaotic coding into a smooth, repeatable cooking session.
