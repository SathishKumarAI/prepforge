Imagine you’re baking a cake that needs three ingredients—flour, sugar, and eggs—to decide how sweet it will taste. In machine learning, the “cake” is your prediction (how sweet), and the ingredients are *input variables* like age, income, and education level.  

Regression with multiple input variables works just like mixing those ingredients: you give the model a recipe that tells it how much of each ingredient to use, then it mixes them together to produce a single number—the predicted outcome. The model learns from past cakes (data) which proportions make the cake taste right, and then applies that learned recipe to new cakes.  

So, think of regression as a smart kitchen assistant: it watches many recipes, figures out how each ingredient influences sweetness, and then helps you predict the sweetness of any new recipe using all the ingredients at once.
