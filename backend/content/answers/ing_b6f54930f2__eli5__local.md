**What’s a “sample” in machine learning?**

Think of training a dog to fetch a ball. You give the dog a few balls, let it learn how they look and feel, then test it with new ones. In ML, each ball is a *sample*: one piece of data (an image, a sentence, a sensor reading) that carries two parts – the **input** (the ball’s shape, color, texture) and the **label** (what we want the model to predict, like “dog” or “cat”). Just as the dog learns from many balls before it can fetch any ball correctly, a machine‑learning model learns patterns from thousands of samples so it can make accurate predictions on unseen data. A sample is simply one example that teaches the algorithm how input relates to output.
