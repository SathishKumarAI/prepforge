**Think of a robot chef who learns to cook from tasting and tweaking recipes.**  
At first it follows a recipe exactly—no surprises. As it tastes each dish, it notes what works (flavor, texture) and what doesn’t (too salty, undercooked). It then *updates* its cooking rules: “If the sauce is too thick, add more water.” This continual tasting–adjusting loop is like **machine learning**.

- **Data** are the ingredients and taste tests.  
- A **model** is the chef’s set of rules (the recipe).  
- The **algorithm** decides how to tweak those rules after each taste.  
- **Training** is the period when the chef practices many times, learning from mistakes.  
- **Prediction** happens once the model has learned enough; it can now cook a new dish without tasting first.

Just as a chef improves by learning from feedback, a machine learns patterns in data and gets better at tasks—classifying emails, recognizing faces, or recommending music—without being told every step explicitly.
