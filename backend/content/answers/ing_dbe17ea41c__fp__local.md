**Re‑applying after an interview: a principled view**

At its core, hiring is a *resource allocation* problem: the company has a fixed budget of positions and must choose candidates who will maximize future performance. When you’re not selected, the firm has already made a decision based on the information it had at that moment—your résumé, interview scores, fit with culture, and the pool’s relative strength.

Because hiring decisions are *non‑reversible* (you can’t “undo” a rejection), firms typically treat each application as a fresh, independent sample. Re‑applying is therefore permissible: it gives you another chance to present new evidence that changes the expected value of your candidacy. The deeper principle here is **information asymmetry**—the more information you can convey (new projects, updated metrics, skill gains), the better the decision maker’s estimate.

**Non‑obvious insight:** Even if a candidate is rejected once, the *probability* that they will be hired on a subsequent attempt does not simply reset to zero. Each re‑application updates the posterior belief about their suitability; the employer’s prior (e.g., “I didn’t hire you before”) becomes part of the likelihood function, but it can be outweighed by strong new evidence. Thus, strategically improving your profile and framing your application as a distinct opportunity can tip the balance in your favor.
