Imagine a busy restaurant kitchen (OLTP) and a quiet planning office (OLAP).  

In the kitchen, chefs must handle each order as it comes: take‑out tickets are written down, ingredients are taken from the pantry, and dishes are cooked quickly. That’s **OLTP** – “Online Transaction Processing.” It focuses on many small, fast actions—adding a sale, updating inventory, or booking a table—so customers get instant results.

Now picture the planning office where managers review last month’s sales reports, forecast next quarter’s menu, and decide which ingredients to stock. That’s **OLAP** – “Online Analytical Processing.” It deals with large amounts of data, runs slow, complex queries, and aggregates information to find trends or patterns.

So: OLTP is the kitchen that handles day‑to‑day orders; OLAP is the office that analyzes all those orders to improve future service.
