Think of an SSD as a filing cabinet that keeps your data in drawers you can’t open with a key—only with the right software. Inside those drawers are different kinds of “memory cells,” each chosen for how many times they can be written before they wear out, just like paper that can be rewritten more or less often.

| Memory type | How it works | When you’d use it |
|-------------|--------------|-------------------|
| **SLC (Single‑Level Cell)** | Stores 1 bit per cell. | For high‑end servers needing fast, reliable writes. |
| **MLC (Multi‑Level Cell)** | Stores 2 bits per cell. | Mid‑range SSDs balancing speed and price. |
| **TLC (Triple‑Level Cell)** | Stores 3 bits per cell. | Mainstream consumer drives where capacity matters more than endurance. |
| **QLC (Quad‑Level Cell)** | Stores 4 bits per cell. | Budget or archival storage, but writes are slower and wear out quicker. |

All of them are *non‑volatile*—they keep data even when power is off—just like a filing cabinet that stays closed in the dark. The choice depends on how often you’ll rewrite the drive and how much space you need.
