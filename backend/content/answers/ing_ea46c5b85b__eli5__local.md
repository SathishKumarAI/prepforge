Imagine a quiet library where every book is expected to sit on its shelf in the same spot. **Anomaly detection** is like having a friendly librarian who watches for books that are out of place—maybe one has slipped off its rack or its spine is bent. The librarian doesn’t need to know what each book contains; she only cares that something looks different from the usual pattern.

In machine learning, we give the computer a picture of “normal” data (the properly shelved books). It learns this pattern and then flags anything that deviates—anomaly detection algorithms spot unusual transactions in credit cards, unexpected network traffic, or early signs of equipment failure. The key idea: detect what doesn’t fit, not to explain every detail of the anomaly itself.
