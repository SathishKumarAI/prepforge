Imagine building a sandwich that can learn how to taste itself.  
The **architecture** is the recipe and structure of that sandwich – where each ingredient sits and how they’re connected.

1. **Layers** are like bread slices. The first slice (input layer) receives raw data—images, words, numbers. Each subsequent slice mixes the previous slice’s flavors through a set of “nutrients” called *neurons*.  
2. **Neurons** act as tiny taste‑buds that decide whether a particular pattern should pass on to the next slice. They do this by multiplying the incoming flavor by a weight (a number telling how important that flavor is) and adding a bias (a little extra seasoning).  
3. The result goes through an *activation function*, which decides if the flavor is strong enough to move forward—much like a chef deciding whether to keep or discard a bite.

When many slices stack, the sandwich learns complex patterns: it can recognize faces, translate languages, or predict stock prices. That layered, connected structure is what we call the model’s **architecture**.
