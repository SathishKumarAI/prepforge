Imagine you’re building a huge, friendly library that talks to people—like a giant chatbot. The **non‑functional requirements** are the rules that tell the library how well it must run, not what stories it should read.

Think of them as the *household chores* for the library:  
- **Reliability** – the lights (servers) stay on 24/7 so visitors never get stuck in darkness.  
- **Scalability** – adding more shelves (compute nodes) when crowds grow, without slowing down checkout.  
- **Latency** – a librarian should hand back a book in seconds; long wait times break the conversation flow.  
- **Security** – locks and guards keep visitors’ secrets safe.  
- **Maintainability** – cleaning schedules let you fix parts quickly without closing the whole library.

Each requirement is like a chore that keeps the chatbot healthy, fast, and trustworthy, letting users focus on asking questions instead of dealing with glitches.
