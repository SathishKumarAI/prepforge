Imagine your brain as a giant library that keeps two kinds of books.  
**Tier 1 (Short‑Term)** is like the desk where you hold the book you’re reading right now—just enough to keep it handy for a few minutes.  

**Tier 2, Episodic Memory (L2),** is the dusty back shelf where you store whole stories about your life: “the day I visited Grandma’s house” or “my first bike ride.”  
When you look on that shelf, the book opens to a specific page, and you can remember what happened, when it happened, and how you felt.  

In AI, an episodic‑memory architecture works the same way: it records complete events (time, context, outcome) rather than isolated facts. Later, when the system needs to act or learn, it pulls out a relevant “story” from this shelf, giving it rich background instead of just raw data. This lets the AI reason like we do—by recalling past experiences in full detail.
