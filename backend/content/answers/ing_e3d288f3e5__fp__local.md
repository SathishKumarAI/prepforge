## Writing an Effective Machine‑Learning Engineer Resume Summary  
*(Austen McDonald & Neo Kim)*

### 1. Problem Statement
Hiring managers skim the first 30–60 seconds of a résumé. Their goal: decide whether the candidate *can* solve **real‑world ML problems** and fit into the team’s workflow. The résumé summary must therefore act as a *probability density function* over three latent variables:

| Variable | What it represents |
|----------|--------------------|
| **Domain Expertise** | Ability to turn data into models (feature engineering, algorithm choice). |
| **Systems Engineering** | Deploying, scaling, monitoring ML pipelines. |
| **Team Collaboration** | Communicating insights and integrating with product/engineering cycles. |

### 2. Why the Three‑Component Structure Works  
- **Information Theory:** Each component reduces uncertainty about the candidate’s fit. The first term tells *what* they know; the second shows *how* they apply it; the third proves *social alignment*.  
- **Optimization Principle:** Recruiters aim to maximize *value per hour*. By presenting evidence for all three axes, you reduce the expected cost of hiring a misfit.  
- **Geometry Analogy:** Think of your résumé as a vector in a 3‑dimensional space; the summary is its projection onto the “Hiring Success” axis. A balanced projection yields the highest dot product with the recruiter’s objective vector.

### 3. One Non‑Obvious Insight  
Most summaries overemphasize *technical buzzwords* (e.g., “deep learning,” “reinforcement learning”). The missing dimension is **“Impact‑Metrics.”** Quantify how your models changed a KPI—accuracy ↑ 5%, latency ↓ 30 ms, revenue +$1M. This anchors the abstract components in tangible business outcomes, satisfying both data‑driven and outcome‑driven stakeholders.

> **Data‑Science Engineer** with 4 yrs of experience building end‑to‑end ML pipelines that increased product engagement by 12 %. *End with a sentence tying your technical chops to the company’s mission.*
