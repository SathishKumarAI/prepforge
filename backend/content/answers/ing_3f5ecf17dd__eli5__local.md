Imagine a line of people waiting for a coffee machine that has only one cup holder.  
**Circular Queue** is like a revolving door where the last person can hand off their empty cup back to the first spot—no empty gaps, and the “queue” keeps moving around a circle until it’s full again.

The four most common queue types are:

1. **Simple (Linear) Queue** – people line up one after another; the first in is the first served (FIFO).  
2. **Circular Queue** – the line wraps around so that once the last spot is reached, new arrivals can use the free spot at the front again.  
3. **Priority Queue** – a “VIP” system where some customers jump ahead based on importance, not just order.  
4. **Deque (Double‑Ended)** – like a two‑way street: people can join or leave from either end.

Each type tweaks how we add and remove items, but the core idea is still “first in, first out,” just with different rules for who gets to move where.
