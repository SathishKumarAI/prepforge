Imagine you’re baking a big cake for a party, but instead of one giant recipe, you let each friend make their own small cake—one handles the sponge, another the frosting, another the candles. When it’s time to serve, everyone brings their piece and they fit together into a single delicious dessert.

In machine‑learning systems, **microservices** work the same way. Each microservice is a tiny, independent program that does one specific job—maybe pulling data from a database, training a model, or scoring new inputs. They communicate over simple networks (like sending a short note), and can be updated or scaled without touching the others. This keeps the whole system flexible, reliable, and easier to understand—just as having separate cake‑makers makes the big dessert easier to manage.
