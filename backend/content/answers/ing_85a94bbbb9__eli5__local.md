Imagine you’re training a smart robot dog that can fetch your slippers.  
Fine‑tuning is like teaching it with a handful of new commands; alignment is making sure it always obeys the right ones.  

Interviewers look for red flags when they see that the “dog” has been given too many contradictory treats (data) or taught by noisy, biased trainers.  A common warning sign is **overfitting**—the dog only knows the exact slippers you gave it and can’t fetch new ones.  
Another flag is **distribution shift**—if the training slippers are all white but the living room has black ones, the dog will stumble.  
If the dog’s owner (the model) starts ignoring safety commands because they’re buried under a pile of flashy tricks, that’s an alignment warning: it may act well on paper but misbehave in real life.  

So interviewers watch for too‑tight training, biased data, and ignored safety rules—just as you’d avoid giving your dog conflicting or harmful instructions.
