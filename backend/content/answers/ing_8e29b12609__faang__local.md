**Clarify**  
Catastrophic forgetting occurs when a model that has been pre‑trained on a broad corpus loses performance on its original tasks after fine‑tuning for a new domain or objective. I’d confirm the setting: is it single‑task fine‑tuning, multi‑label transfer, or continual learning across many domains? Also ask whether we have constraints on storage, compute, and acceptable accuracy drop.

**Approach**  
1. Identify critical parameters (e.g., heads vs. shared layers).  
2. Choose a mitigation strategy: *regularization*, *parameter isolation*, or *rehearsal*.  
3. Implement, evaluate on validation splits of both old and new tasks.  
4. Iterate hyper‑parameters and monitor memory/latency trade‑offs.

**Depth**  
- **Elastic Weight Consolidation (EWC)** adds a quadratic penalty proportional to the Fisher information matrix:  
  \[
  L_{\text{total}} = L_{\text{new}} + \lambda \sum_i F_i(\theta_i-\theta^*_i)^2
  \]
  where \(F_i\) captures importance of weight \(i\). It keeps important weights near their pre‑trained values.  
- **Parameter Isolation** (e.g., LoRA, adapters): freeze the backbone and train a small rank‑decomposition matrix; old knowledge remains untouched.  
- **Rehearsal / Replay**: interleave mini‑batches from a held‑out buffer of original data or synthetic pseudo‑samples generated by the model itself.

Complexity: EWC requires \(O(n)\) extra storage for Fisher values and \(O(n^2)\) if computed exactly; approximations (diagonal) keep it linear. LoRA adds negligible overhead (<5 % parameters). Replay doubles GPU memory but often yields best accuracy.

**Edge Cases**  
- When the new task is highly dissimilar, even EWC may over‑penalize useful shifts → tune λ.  
- If the replay buffer is too small or biased, it can introduce sampling error.  
- Parameter isolation fails if the downstream task needs fine‑grained control of deep layers.
