Imagine writing a note at the top of a recipe card so anyone can read it later and instantly know what the dish is about.  
In Python that note is called a **docstring** – a short paragraph written in plain text that explains what a function, class, or module does. It sits right after the `def` line (or at the start of a file) and is surrounded by triple quotes (`"""…"""`).  

When you type `help(my_function)` or view a library’s documentation, Python pulls this docstring to show users.  
Think of it as the “title card” for your code: it tells readers *what* will happen without having to read every line inside. No formulas are needed—just clear description and, if useful, example usage.
