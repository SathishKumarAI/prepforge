Think of a random variable like a **weather‑forecasting app** that tells you the chance of rain on a given day. The app doesn’t give a fixed answer; instead it says “There’s a 30 % chance of rain.”  
In machine learning we use similar “chance” numbers to describe how likely different outcomes are when we don’t know what will actually happen.

**Unavoidable terms**

- **Random variable** – a quantity that can take on many possible values, each with its own likelihood.  
- **Probability distribution** – the complete list of all those values and their chances (like the app’s full set of weather possibilities).  
- **Expectation (mean)** – the average value you’d get if you repeated the random experiment many times (the long‑term “average rain”).  
- **Variance** – how spread out the possible outcomes are around that mean (how wildly the forecast could swing).  

Just as the app helps you decide whether to carry an umbrella, a machine learning model uses these probability concepts to predict and learn from data that isn’t perfectly predictable.
