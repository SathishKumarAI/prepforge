Imagine you’re renting a kitchen to bake cakes. **Hosted** is like signing a long‑term lease: you pay a fixed monthly fee for a specific oven, counter space, and storage. You know exactly how much you’ll spend each month, but if the kitchen gets crowded or your cake batch grows, you may need to upgrade, which costs extra.

**Serverless** is more like using a food truck that only charges when you actually bake. You pay per hour of oven use, per kilogram of flour, or per cake you produce. If you’re quiet one week, the bill stays low; if you run a big batch, you’ll see a higher cost—but no hidden rent.

Elastic Cloud’s pricing follows this same pattern:  
- **Hosted (self‑managed)**: a predictable monthly rate for fixed resources.  
- **Serverless**: pay only for the compute and storage you actually use, which can be cheaper for sporadic workloads but may spike during busy periods.

Choose hosted when you want steady costs and full control; choose serverless when you value flexibility and want to avoid over‑provisioning.
