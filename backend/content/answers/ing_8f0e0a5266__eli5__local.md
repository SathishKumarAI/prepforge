Think of a large‑language model (LLM) as a chef who can cook many dishes. Evaluating the chef’s work isn’t just about taste; you also look at how fresh the ingredients are, how quickly the dish is served, and whether it looks good on the plate. In AI terms, “quality” has several dimensions that must all be checked:

* **Accuracy** – does the answer match what’s true? (like checking a recipe’s instructions).  
* **Relevance** – does it address the exact question asked? (the dish fits the menu item requested).  
* **Coherence** – is the response logically organized and easy to follow? (the chef presents the course in order).  
* **Hallucination rate** – how often does it invent facts? (like a chef adding a fake ingredient).  
* **Speed / Latency** – how fast can it produce the answer? (time from order to table).  
* **Robustness** – does it stay correct when the prompt is tweaked? (the dish still tastes good if you change a seasoning).  

Just as a restaurant reviews every plate on these fronts, AI researchers score LLMs across all these axes to get a full picture of performance.
