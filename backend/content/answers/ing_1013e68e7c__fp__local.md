### Why a Machine‑Learning résumé must be *objective* yet *strategic*

**The fundamental problem:** recruiters scan thousands of CVs in seconds, looking for the smallest set of signals that predict future performance on their specific tasks.  
A good résumé therefore has to **maximise information gain about your fit while minimising noise**.

1. **Signal → Technical Breadth & Depth** – List only those languages, libraries and algorithms you can *apply*, not just “Python” or “TensorFlow”.  
   *Why?* Every keyword is a hypothesis; the recruiter tests it by reading the context.  
2. **Noise → Irrelevant Personal Details** – Skip hobbies unless they demonstrate transferable skills (e.g., “volunteer data‑cleaning for a charity”).  
   *Underlying principle:* Bayes’ rule—unnecessary data dilutes posterior probability of hiring.
3. **Evidence → Quantified Impact** – Replace vague “improved model accuracy” with concrete metrics: *+12 % F1 score on a 500k‑sample dataset, reducing inference latency by 30 ms*.  
   *Why?* This turns subjective claims into measurable features the recruiter can compare.
4. **Context → Problem Orientation** – Frame projects as problems solved (e.g., “Reduced churn predictions’ false positives from 18 % to 7 % for a telecom client”).  
   *Deep insight:* Recruiters are not looking for algorithms; they want problem‑solving stories.

#### One non‑obvious trick
Include **one line about your learning pipeline** (e.g., “Automated feature selection with Bayesian optimisation, cutting training time by 40 %”).  
This signals that you understand *how* to engineer end‑to‑end systems, a skill recruiters often overlook when they only list tools.

> **Bottom line:** Your résumé is an experiment. Keep the hypothesis (your fit) sharp, evidence strong, and extraneous variables out of the equation.
