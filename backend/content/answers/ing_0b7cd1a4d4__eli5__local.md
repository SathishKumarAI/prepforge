Think of an AI assistant as a helpful robot in a smart house. **Permission scoping** is like giving that robot only the keys it needs: the kitchen door, the hallway, and maybe the garage, but not the master bedroom or the safe. By limiting its “agency” to just those areas, we reduce the chance that it can do anything harmful if something goes wrong.

This is called **minimum agency**—the AI’s powers are deliberately trimmed down so it can only act where you allow it. The robot still works well in the kitchen (answering questions, playing music), but it cannot wander into forbidden rooms and accidentally lock people out or damage things.

**Sandboxing** is the same idea on a computer: we run the robot inside a protected virtual room that keeps its code from touching the rest of the system. Even if the robot misbehaves, the sandbox stops it from reaching your personal files or other programs.

So, by giving an AI just enough keys to do its job and keeping it in a safe “room,” we keep both its usefulness and our safety.
