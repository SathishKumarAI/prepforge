### Why “Big” firms launch open‑source projects

**The fundamental problem:**  
Large enterprises need *reliable, extensible infrastructure* that can evolve faster than their internal teams alone can manage. They also want to lock in a talent pool and avoid vendor lock‑in.

1. **Optimization of shared effort** – By publishing code, the firm offloads maintenance, bug‑fixing, and feature development to a community. The cost function  
   \[
   C_{\text{total}} = C_{\text{internal}} + \alpha\,C_{\text{community}}
   \]
   becomes smaller when \(C_{\text{community}}\) is significant but still controllable via governance.

2. **Information asymmetry reduction** – Open‑source projects expose implementation details to the world, turning proprietary “black boxes” into transparent systems that others can audit and improve upon. This aligns with *cognitive load theory*: developers spend less time deciphering undocumented APIs and more on value creation.

3. **Network externalities & platform effects** – A public codebase becomes a *platform* where other tools build on top of it, creating a virtuous cycle that increases the original company’s market reach without direct sales.

4. **Talent pipeline** – Contributors become vetted developers who already understand the firm’s ecosystem; hiring from this pool reduces onboarding time and risk.
