Imagine building a big Lego city.  
The **Zachman Framework** is like the master blueprint that tells you how to keep all the Lego pieces organized so everyone can see the whole picture and add new parts without breaking anything.

It has **six columns**—each one is a different *view* of the city:
1. **What** (data) – the bricks themselves.  
2. **How** (functions) – the instructions for putting them together.  
3. **Where** (network) – the roads and streets that connect everything.  
4. **Who** (people) – the builders who decide what to do.  
5. **When** (time) – the schedule for construction phases.  
6. **Why** (motivation) – the purpose of the city, like a park or a school.

Each column is split into **six rows**—the people who look at it: from the CEO who sees the big picture to the IT worker who focuses on details.  

So, the framework gives you a clear, layered map that keeps every stakeholder’s view consistent, making it easier to design, build, and evolve an enterprise system without losing track of any piece.
