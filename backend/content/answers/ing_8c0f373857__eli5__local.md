Think of a chef who has to cook a new dish.

**Zero‑shot** is like the chef being handed a recipe card that says “make something tasty from scratch” and then immediately starting to cook without any example dishes or taste tests. The chef must rely only on their general culinary knowledge—what flavors usually pair, how heat affects ingredients—to guess what the dish should taste like.

**Few‑shot** is when the chef gets a few sample plates first: maybe one spicy stir‑fry and one sweet soup. From those examples, they learn the restaurant’s style, preferred textures, and flavor balance, then use that short “training” to create the new dish.

Use zero‑shot when you have no time or data to provide examples—just a clear instruction. Use few‑shot when you can give a handful of samples to guide the model toward the specific tone, format, or style you want. Both let the AI improvise, but few‑shot gives it a quick lesson on what counts as “good” in your context.
