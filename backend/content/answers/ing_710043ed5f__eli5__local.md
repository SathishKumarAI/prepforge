Think of a **reverse proxy** like a receptionist in a busy office building.  
When someone calls the building, the receptionist answers and forwards the call to the right desk, hides the actual phone numbers of each employee, and can even answer simple questions for you.  

- **Proxy (forward proxy)** is the opposite: it’s like a friend who goes out on your behalf to shop or make reservations, masking your identity from the store.

**Key benefits of a reverse proxy**

1. **Security** – It keeps the real servers hidden, so outsiders can’t target them directly.  
2. **Load balancing** – If many visitors arrive at once, the receptionist routes each one to an available desk, preventing any single desk from getting overloaded.  
3. **Caching** – The receptionist can remember frequently asked questions and answer them instantly instead of always calling the original employee.  

So a reverse proxy protects, distributes, and speeds up traffic that is headed toward your servers, just as a receptionist manages calls for an entire office.
