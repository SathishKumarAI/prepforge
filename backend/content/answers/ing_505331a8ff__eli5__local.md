Imagine you’re a chef tasting soup from a huge pot that keeps mixing itself.  
**Sampling** is like picking a spoonful of the soup—just a tiny part of the whole.  
The **Law of Large Numbers** says: if you keep taking more spoonfuls, your average taste will settle down to match the flavor of the entire pot. The pot’s total taste (the “true mean”) becomes clear as the number of spoonfuls grows.

Now picture each spoonful having a random splash that slightly changes its flavor.  
The **Central Limit Theorem** tells us: when you add up many such noisy spoonfuls, the combined flavor distribution looks like a smooth bell curve—regardless of how wild each individual splash was. In short, more samples make averages stable (law), and sums of random parts form a predictable normal shape (CLT).
