Imagine you’re at a huge library where every book is a point in space.  
If the library has only two shelves (2‑D), it’s easy to see how far one book is from another: just look down the aisle and measure the straight line between them.  

Now double the number of shelves—four, six, ten, maybe a hundred! The books are spread out in a gigantic, multi‑dimensional room. Even if two books sit right next to each other on the same shelf, they can still be extremely far apart when you consider all the other shelves at once.  
Because distances become almost equal, it’s hard for a computer to find “nearby” books or patterns; everything looks noisy and random. That’s the **curse of dimensionality**—more dimensions mean data becomes sparse, harder to cluster, model, or interpret, just like trying to locate a book in an infinite‑shelf library.
