Imagine a kitchen where a chef learns to make soup from scratch.  
**Machine Learning (ML)** is like that chef’s training: instead of memorizing recipes, the chef watches many pots of soup being cooked, noting how ingredients, heat, and timing affect taste. After seeing enough examples, the chef can predict what will happen when she adds a new ingredient or changes the heat—she doesn’t need to read a cookbook each time.

In ML, the “chef” is a **computer program** (the model).  
The “examples” are data points: pictures, numbers, words.  
The program looks for patterns in these examples and builds a rule set that lets it guess or decide on new, unseen inputs—just as the chef can now make a tasty soup without watching a video.

So, ML is training a computer to learn from experience, not from hard‑coded instructions.
