Imagine your data is a huge library of books.  
**HDFS (Hadoop Distributed File System)** is like a private warehouse: you own the building and pay for each shelf you use. The price is fixed per storage unit, but you must maintain the whole place—no one else can drop in or pick up books without your permission.  

**Amazon S3** is a public book‑exchange marketplace. You pay only for the space you actually put books into, and anyone with the right key can add or retrieve them instantly from anywhere. The trade‑off: every time someone reads a book (a “request”), you get charged a tiny fee, so heavy traffic can raise costs.

So, HDFS is a stable, self‑managed price but less flexible; S3 is pay‑as‑you‑go and highly accessible, but frequent access adds extra cost.
