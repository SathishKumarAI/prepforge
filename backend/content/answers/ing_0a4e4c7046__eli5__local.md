Imagine you’re a chef teaching a cooking class.  
**Supervised learning** is like giving the students a recipe (the *label*) while they practice making the dish from ingredients (the *input data*). The teacher checks each plate and says whether it’s correct or not, so the students learn to adjust their cooking.

- **Regression**: The label is a continuous number—think of predicting how many grams of sugar to add for a desired sweetness level. The student learns a smooth rule that maps ingredient amounts to a numeric score (e.g., 0–10 sweetness).  
- **Classification**: Here the label is a category—deciding whether a fruit is an apple or an orange. The student learns a decision boundary that separates two groups based on their features.

In both cases, the model (the student) “learns” from labeled examples to make predictions on new, unseen data.
