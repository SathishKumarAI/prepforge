**Software bugs and reliability in system design**

Think of a computer program as a **well‑planned city**.  
The *design* is the blueprint: streets (functions), bridges (interfaces), parks (data structures).  
A *bug* is like an unexpected pothole or broken traffic light that trips drivers (users) and can cause accidents (crashes, data loss).

Reliability is the city’s ability to keep traffic flowing safely over time. Engineers build reliability by:

1. **Planning** – designing clear routes so bugs are hard to hide.  
2. **Testing** – driving test cars through every street before opening the city.  
3. **Monitoring** – installing cameras (logs) to spot potholes as they appear.  
4. **Fixing quickly** – patching a broken light before more cars hit it.

Just as a city that fixes roads promptly stays safe, software that detects and repairs bugs early remains reliable for its users.
