**Reliability in AI is like a trustworthy friend’s promise to show up on time every day.**

Imagine you hire a delivery driver (the AI system). You want to be sure the driver will arrive with your package whenever you need it. Reliability measures how often that happens—how many times the driver actually shows up on schedule versus how many times they’re late or miss the drop‑off altogether.

- **Reliability**: The proportion of successful, error‑free deliveries (or predictions) out of all attempts.
- **Accuracy**: How close each delivery is to exactly what you asked for (e.g., correct address).
- **Precision/Recall**: Not part of reliability; they focus on the quality of specific kinds of results.

So, if an AI model has high reliability, it means that when you ask it to classify emails or recommend music, it consistently produces a result within acceptable bounds—just like a driver who almost never misses a delivery.
