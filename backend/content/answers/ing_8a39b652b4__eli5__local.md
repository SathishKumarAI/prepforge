Think of Elastic Cloud like a bakery that sells fresh bread.  
*Hosted* is a **brick‑and‑mortar shop**: you pay for the whole storefront (hardware, maintenance, security) and you get a fixed number of ovens that run all day. You know exactly how many loaves you can bake, but if more customers come, you must add another oven or pay extra for capacity.

*Serverless* is a **drop‑in kitchen**: you only pay for the exact minutes your dough spends baking in an oven. If no one orders, you pay nothing; if a rush happens, Elastic automatically brings in extra ovens without you managing them.  

Elastic’s official pricing reflects this: hosted plans charge a flat monthly fee based on node size and storage, while serverless bills per hour of compute and data processed. The choice depends on predictability (hosted) versus flexibility and cost‑sensitivity (serverless).
