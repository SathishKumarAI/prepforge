Think of a smart robot chef in a kitchen that can cook many dishes, but you want it to master just a few favorite recipes—say pancakes, lasagna, and chocolate cake.  
**Scope the problem** means telling the robot: “Only learn how to make these three dishes.” This keeps training fast, reduces mistakes, and guarantees good results for what matters most.  

In machine learning, scoping is like giving the algorithm a clear list of tasks (use cases). Instead of trying to predict every possible outcome—like weather, stock prices, or handwritten digits—you focus on one or two concrete goals, such as classifying emails as spam or not spam and predicting house prices in a specific city.  
This limits data needs, simplifies feature selection, speeds up training, and makes evaluation straightforward.  

So, scoping is simply narrowing the problem’s scope to a few well‑defined tasks so the model can learn them deeply and reliably.
