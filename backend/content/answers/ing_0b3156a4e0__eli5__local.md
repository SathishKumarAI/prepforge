Imagine a library that serves many readers at once, but instead of each reader having to walk all the way to the main shelf every time they want a book, copies of popular books are kept in small, nearby mini‑libraries around town. When someone asks for “The Great Gatsby,” they can grab it from the nearest copy instead of traveling far. That’s distributed caching.

**Distributed cache**: a network of fast, temporary storage spots (like those mini‑libraries) spread across multiple computers. They hold copies of data that many users or applications need quickly. When one spot runs out of space or becomes slow, another can step in—so no single point fails and the whole system stays speedy. This keeps large systems responsive, just like readers get books faster when copies are close by.
