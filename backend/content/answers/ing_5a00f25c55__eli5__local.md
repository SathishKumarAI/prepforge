A **neural network** is like a tiny city of decision‑makers, each called a *neuron*.  
Imagine the city receives a street sign (the input data). The first group of neurons reads that sign and passes their “yes” or “no” to the next group. Each neuron looks at all the messages it gets, gives them a weight (how important each message is), adds a bias (a little extra push), then uses a simple rule (an activation function) to decide whether to shout “yes!” or stay quiet. Those shouts travel to more neurons until the final district—called the output layer—gives the city’s answer, such as “dog” or “cat.”  

The network learns by adjusting the weights and biases so that its answers match the correct ones, just like a city refines traffic lights to keep cars moving smoothly. In short, a neural network is a layered system of weighted decision‑makers that transforms raw data into useful predictions.
