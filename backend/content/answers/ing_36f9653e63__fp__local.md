### Why Behavioral Interviews Exist  
A behavioral interview asks you *how* you acted in past situations, not what you know theoretically. The underlying principle is **predictive validity**: a person’s past behavior in comparable contexts statistically forecasts future performance. Employers therefore probe specific episodes to infer traits (e.g., resilience, collaboration) that align with job‑critical competencies.

### Proven Strategy 1 – The STAR Framework  
- **S**ituation: set the context with concrete details.  
- **T**ask: describe your responsibility or goal.  
- **A**ction: enumerate *exact* steps you took (avoid “I did it”).  
- **R**esult: quantify outcomes (percent improvement, cost saved).  

Why STAR works: it structures a narrative that satisfies the predictive‑validity test—clear evidence of the skill in action. It also keeps answers concise, reducing filler noise.

### Proven Strategy 2 – Match Competencies to Company Needs  
Research the firm’s core values and the job description’s key behaviors (e.g., “agile problem solving”). Map each competency you want to showcase onto a STAR story that demonstrates it. This alignment signals *fit*—a higher probability of hiring.

### Non‑obvious Insight  
**Use the “reflection gap” technique:** after describing an action, briefly reflect on *what you learned* and how you would apply that lesson in this role. Most candidates stop at results; reflection reveals metacognition, a predictor of long‑term adaptability that interviewers value but rarely ask directly.

By grounding your answers in the predictive‑validity principle, structuring them with STAR, aligning to company needs, and adding reflective insight, you convert anecdote into evidence—exactly what behavioral interviews demand.
