Imagine each microservice as a tiny kitchen in a giant restaurant chain. When a customer places an order (a request), the order travels from one kitchen to another—mixing sauces, baking bread, plating dishes—before it reaches the table. Distributed tracing is like giving every kitchen a unique, colored barcode that sticks to the dish while it moves. As the food passes through each station, the barcode records who handled it and how long they spent.

In modern microservices, this “barcode” lets developers see exactly where delays happen, which service is bottlenecking, or where a bug sneaks in. It turns invisible traffic into a clear trail, making debugging faster and helping teams keep the whole system running smoothly.
