Think of a library that grows every day. **Data architecture** is the blueprint that tells you where each book (data) should live, how to find it quickly, and who can read or edit it.

* **Data source** – The author’s manuscript, the raw information coming in.
* **Ingest pipeline** – The librarian who receives, checks, and files the new books into shelves.
* **Storage layer** – The actual shelves: fast‑access “card catalog” (databases) for daily use, slower but huge “archives” (data lakes) for everything else.
* **Metadata** – The index cards that describe each book’s title, author, and keywords so you can search it later.
* **Data model** – The library’s catalog system: how books are grouped (genres), what fields they have, and how those fields relate.
* **Governance** – Rules about who may borrow or edit a book, ensuring accuracy and privacy.

So, data architecture is the organized plan that turns raw information into a usable, searchable collection—just like a well‑run library transforms scattered manuscripts into an easily navigable catalog.
