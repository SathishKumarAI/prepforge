Imagine you’re learning to bake a new cake recipe by watching just a handful of videos and then trying it yourself, without looking at the cookbook again. **Few‑shot learning** is like that: the model receives only a few examples (the “shots”) of what the task looks like—say, a sentence translated from French to English—and then it must perform the same translation on new sentences.

Now picture you’re in a kitchen where every time someone starts baking, they whisper the recipe into your ear while you observe their hands. **In‑context learning (ICL)** is this: the model reads the examples right inside its input prompt—just like hearing the whispered steps—and uses that context to solve the next part of the task, all without any extra training.

So, few‑shot gives the model a small taste before it works; ICL lets it learn on the fly from the conversation itself.
