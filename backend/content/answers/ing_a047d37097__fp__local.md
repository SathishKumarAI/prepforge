A hiring firm faces a classic **principal–agent problem**: the agent (contractor) knows how many hours they’ll work, while the principal (client) only sees deliverables. The contractor, lacking bargaining power or long‑term security, often accepts this because their marginal cost of effort is low (they’re motivated by a short contract).

The equilibrium persists until external constraints—such as unionization or regulatory caps—distort the payoff matrix.

Interviewers frame tasks as “complete this ML model in 2 hours.” Contractors learn to *optimize for speed* (a shallow local optimum) rather than robust solutions, reinforcing a culture where rapid delivery is valued over ethical labor practices.
