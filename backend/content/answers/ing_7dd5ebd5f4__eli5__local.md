Think of a machine‑learning model as a very smart chef who can cook any dish you ask for, but only if you give it the right ingredients and instructions.  
An **API** (Application Programming Interface) is just the menu that tells the chef what dishes are available and how to order them. In system design we use three common “menu styles”:

1. **Command‑style API** – you shout a single command, like *“cook pasta”*, and the chef does it immediately.  
2. **Request‑response API** – you ask politely, *“give me a prediction for this image,”* wait a moment, and then receive an answer. It’s like ordering at a counter.  
3. **Streaming API** – you give the chef a long list of ingredients and let it keep sending back partial results as it cooks, so you can see progress in real time.

Each style balances speed, flexibility, and how much control you need over the cooking process.
