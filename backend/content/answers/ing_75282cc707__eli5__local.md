Think of an AI model as a recipe book.

**Proprietary (closed‑source) books** are written by a single chef who keeps the pages locked.  
*Pros*: The chef can polish every ingredient, add secret spices, and promise consistent flavor for everyone who pays. It’s easier to get customer support because one person owns the recipe.  
*Cons*: You can’t look inside or tweak it; if you want a different dish, you must ask the chef again, which costs time and money.

**Open‑source books** are published online for anyone to read, copy, and modify.  
*Pros*: Anyone can experiment—add new ingredients, fix mistakes, or translate into another language—often faster than waiting for the original author. Communities grow around improving the recipe.  
*Cons*: Because many cooks try it, quality can vary; there’s no single person guaranteeing how good the dish will be. Support is informal and may take time.

So, choose proprietary when you need guaranteed consistency and professional support, and open‑source when you value flexibility, community innovation, and cost savings.
