Imagine a city where every neighborhood owns its own park instead of one giant municipal garden. Each park (data domain) is tended by the local residents (domain teams), who plant and harvest their own flowers (data sets). The city’s map (metadata catalog) tells everyone which park has what plants, and a shared irrigation system (inter‑domain communication) lets people borrow water or share seeds when needed.

In this “data mesh,” the data architecture is like that city plan: it gives each domain control over its own data while still linking them together so the whole city can thrive. Key terms:

- **Domain** – a logical group of related data, owned by one team.  
- **Self‑serve platform** – tools everyone uses to create, publish, and consume data without waiting for a central IT team.  
- **Metadata catalog** – a searchable index that records what data exists, where it lives, and how to use it.

The result is a flexible, scalable system where data flows naturally across domains, just as water and information flow through the city’s parks.
