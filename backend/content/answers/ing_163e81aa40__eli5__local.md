Think of a library that has two kinds of helpers.  

**Search engines** are like a quick‑ref librarian who knows every book’s title, author, and a few keywords. You ask, “Show me books about *climate change*.” She scans the index, pulls out all titles containing that phrase, and hands you a list—maybe hundreds of hits in seconds. Her job is speed, not depth.

**Databases** are like a meticulous archivist who knows where each page lives inside every book. If you want “the paragraph on *carbon pricing* from chapter 3 of the 2018 report,” she digs into that exact file, reads it, and gives you the precise sentence. It takes longer, but you get the exact information you asked for.

So, search engines are fast, broad sweeps; databases are slow, precise pulls—each fits a different need.
