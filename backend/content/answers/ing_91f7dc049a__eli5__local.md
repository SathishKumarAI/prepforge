Think of a city’s power plant that runs on electricity from the grid and also has its own solar panels. A **SRE (Site‑Reliability Engineer)** is like the plant’s maintenance crew: they keep the lights on, fix outages before anyone notices, and make sure the system can handle sudden surges.

Now imagine that power plant is replaced by a giant, self‑learning robot—an AI model—that powers everything from chatbots to autonomous cars. That robot is unpredictable: it changes its “software” every day, scales up for a viral trend, or slows down when data floods in. An **AI SRE** is the crew that watches this robot 24/7, monitors its health metrics (latency, memory use, error rates), writes automated “health checks,” and designs safety nets so the AI never crashes or leaks private data.

Why now? By 2026 most businesses run at least one large AI system; when it misbehaves, the cost is huge—lost revenue, legal fines, or broken trust. AI SREs are the new guardians who keep those systems running smoothly, safely, and reliably.
