Imagine you’re at a coffee shop where baristas (the interviewers) are watching how you order your drink (your prompt). A “red flag” is like a messy or confusing request that makes the barista unsure what to brew.

**Prompt Engineering**  
Think of it as writing a clear menu item. If you say, *“Make me something strong with milk,”* the barista gets lost. But if you write, *“Espresso shot topped with steamed oat milk, no sugar,”* the order is precise and easy to fulfill. Interviewers look for this clarity: do you ask questions in a focused way? Do you give enough detail so the AI (or your teammate) can answer without guessing?

**Context**  
This is the background information that tells the barista why you need the drink—maybe it’s for a presentation or a quick pick‑up. If you skip context, the barista might make a latte when you actually wanted a cappuccino. Interviewers check whether you supply enough context so your request fits the task.

So, a red flag appears when your prompt is vague or missing context, leaving the AI (or the person answering) unsure of what to deliver. The better you engineer the prompt and provide context, the smoother everything runs.
