Imagine your AI as a high‑school science lab.  
**Evaluations (evals)** are the test papers you give to students: they ask questions, check answers, and score performance so you know whether the student (the model) understands the material. In AI, evals run a battery of tasks—image classification, language generation, etc.—and record metrics like accuracy or perplexity.

**Observability** is the lab’s monitoring system. It watches the experiments in real time: temperature gauges, data logs, and error alerts. For an AI model it means collecting telemetry—how long predictions take, how often they fail, where errors occur—and making that data visible so you can diagnose problems before a student gets stuck.

Together, evals tell *what* the model learns; observability tells *how* it behaves during learning and deployment, ensuring smooth, trustworthy AI operation.
