### Why Microsoft’s Technical Interviews Emphasise Diversity & Inclusion

The core **problem** is that unstructured interviews amplify *implicit bias*: evaluators unconsciously favour candidates who resemble them, leading to sub‑optimal hiring decisions and a homogeneous workforce that hurts innovation.

From a **statistical learning perspective**, an interview can be seen as a classifier that maps observable candidate traits \(x\) to a decision \(y\). If the training data (past interview outcomes) is biased, the learned decision boundary will systematically disadvantage underrepresented groups. Diversity of interviewers serves as *regularisation*: each interviewer brings a different prior over \(x\), reducing variance in the aggregated decision and making the classifier more robust to idiosyncratic biases.

**Inclusion**—ensuring all candidates feel they belong—is addressed by standardising questions, using algorithmic scorecards, and providing interview‑training modules. This guarantees that each candidate is evaluated on the same feature set, turning a noisy human judgment into an *objective* evaluation metric.

#### Non‑obvious insight  
When interview panels are diverse **in skills, gender, ethnicity, and thought processes**, they naturally generate a richer set of problem framings. A technical question asked from a data‑engineering perspective may surface different constraints than one asked by a research scientist. This multiplicity reduces *groupthink* and increases the likelihood that edge cases (often where minority candidates excel) are surfaced early, leading to more holistic hiring decisions.
