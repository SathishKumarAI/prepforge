Imagine you’re building a LEGO city. **Bottom‑up analysis** is like starting with the smallest bricks: you look at each piece, how it fits with neighbors, and gradually assemble neighborhoods before adding streets or skyscrapers. You focus on tiny details—color, shape, strength—and only later decide where everything goes.

**Top‑down analysis**, by contrast, begins with a blueprint of the whole city. You set big goals—how many parks, traffic flow, overall height limits—then choose bricks that satisfy those plans. Here you look at the “big picture” first and let that shape every small choice.

In AI products, bottom‑up means testing individual models or data snippets to see what works, while top‑down starts with user goals or business metrics and designs the system to meet them. Both are useful; combining them lets you build robust, fast‑improving AI tools.
