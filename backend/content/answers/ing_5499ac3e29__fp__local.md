**Product Sense + System Design: Why the Combo is a Must‑Know**

At its core, a tech company asks you to *build something that users will actually use and pay for*. Product sense is the art of turning an abstract problem into a concrete, measurable solution—identifying user pain points, defining success metrics, and prioritizing features under constraints. System design, meanwhile, guarantees that the chosen solution scales, remains reliable, and stays maintainable.

Why this pairing?  
1. **Optimization vs. Feasibility** – Product sense gives you the objective function (e.g., maximize daily active users while minimizing latency). System design supplies the feasible region: network topology, database sharding, caching layers. The intersection is where a viable product lives.  
2. **Information‑theoretic limits** – Every system consumes bandwidth and storage; by framing the problem as an *information bottleneck*, you can justify trade‑offs (e.g., compressing logs vs. real‑time analytics).  
3. **Geometry of User Experience** – Think of a feature space where each dimension is a user action. Product sense sketches the desired manifold, while system design ensures that the underlying architecture can faithfully map inputs to points on that manifold without distortion.

**Non‑obvious insight:** Interviewers often expect you to *anticipate friction* before it appears. A common blind spot is overlooking “edge‑case” data flows (e.g., a sudden spike in a niche demographic). Proactively modeling these edge scenarios shows mastery over both product impact and system robustness—exactly what top tech firms need.
