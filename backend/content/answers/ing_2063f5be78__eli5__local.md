Think of a **site cookie** like a tiny sticky note you leave on the kitchen counter when you visit a friend’s house. The note tells your friend “I’m here, and I like chocolate ice‑cream.” When you come back, they read it and remember what you liked.

In machine learning, we don’t use those sticky notes to teach a computer how to learn. A cookie is only a *very small piece of data* that the browser keeps on your own device so a website can remember things about you (like whether you’re logged in). It’s not a dataset, and it isn’t shared with other sites or with an ML algorithm.

So if you’re trying to build a model that learns from user behavior, you’ll need much larger, properly collected data sets—not the little “I’m here” notes that sit in your browser. Those cookies simply don’t provide the kind of information machine learning needs to learn patterns.
