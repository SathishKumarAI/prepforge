**Why “quality” must be measurable in the first place**

In a deployed model the *objective* is to preserve its utility over time while it sees data that may drift from the training distribution.  
Utility can be formalized as expected reward \(R = \mathbb{E}_{x\sim P_{\text{prod}}}[L(f(x),y)]\).  Any systematic shift in \(P_{\text{prod}}\) or a change in the mapping \(f\) that increases loss is *quality degradation*.  
Because we cannot observe future data, we must infer it from observable statistics: the model’s outputs and their confidence.

**The core principle – statistical hypothesis testing on output distributions**

Let \(\hat{p}_{t}\) be the empirical distribution of predictions at time \(t\).  Under stationarity, \(\hat{p}_{t}\) should remain close to a reference distribution \(\hat{p}^{*}\) (e.g., training or a recent baseline).  
We test \(H_{0}:\hat{p}_{t}=\hat{p}^{*}\) versus \(H_{1}:\hat{p}_{t}\neq\hat{p}^{*}\) using a multivariate distance metric such as the *Maximum Mean Discrepancy* (MMD).  
A statistically significant MMD indicates that the predictive distribution has shifted, implying potential degradation.

**Non‑obvious insight: monitor *confidence entropy*, not just accuracy**

Accuracy alone is insensitive to calibration drift.  By tracking the entropy of the softmax probabilities \(H_t = -\sum p_{t}\log p_{t}\), we detect when the model becomes overconfident or underconfident—precursors to performance loss.  
Combining MMD on outputs with a rising entropy curve gives a robust, early‑warning system that signals degradation before downstream metrics fail.

In practice:  

1. Maintain a rolling window of predictions.  
2. Compute MMD against the baseline; flag if \(p\)-value < 0.01.  
3. Simultaneously track mean confidence entropy; trigger alerts when it exceeds its moving‑average by > 2σ.  

This principled, statistically grounded approach ensures timely detection of quality decay in production AI systems.
