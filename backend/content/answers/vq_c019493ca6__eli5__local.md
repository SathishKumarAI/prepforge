Imagine you’re cooking a big pot of soup, but you only have space for ten ingredients in your pantry drawer. Feature selection is like deciding which ten spices and veggies will make the soup taste best without overcrowding it. In data engineering, each “ingredient” is a feature—an attribute or measurement that describes an observation (like age, income, click‑rate). The goal of feature selection is to pick only the most useful ones so the model learns faster, uses less memory, and doesn’t get confused by irrelevant or duplicate information. Think of it as trimming a cluttered garden: you remove weeds (unhelpful features) and keep the flowers that truly brighten the landscape. This keeps your data clean, efficient, and ready for analysis.
