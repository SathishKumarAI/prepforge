**Redundant architectures in machine learning are like having backup plans for your favorite recipe.**  
Imagine you’re baking a cake and you’ve got two ovens, each set to the same temperature. If one oven overheats or stops working, the other keeps going, so you still finish the cake on time. In system design, “redundancy” means duplicating critical parts—servers, data stores, or even whole ML models—so that if one fails, another takes over instantly. This boosts reliability and uptime, just as a spare oven guarantees your dessert is ready. The key terms:  
- **Redundancy** – duplicate components for safety.  
- **Reliability** – the system’s ability to keep working without interruption.  
By mirroring this cooking backup strategy in ML infrastructure, we avoid single points of failure and deliver smoother, more dependable services.
