**Clarify**  
The interviewee wants a concise explanation of how *Artificial Intelligence (AI)*, *Machine Learning (ML)*, and *Deep Learning (DL)* differ, assuming they’re familiar with basic AI concepts but not the technical hierarchy.

**Approach**  
1. Define each term at a high level.  
2. Show their relationship in a “nested” diagram: AI ⊃ ML ⊃ DL.  
3. Highlight key distinguishing features—goals, methods, and typical use‑cases.  

**Depth**  
- **AI** is the umbrella field aiming to build systems that *exhibit* intelligent behavior (planning, reasoning, perception).  
- **ML** is a subfield of AI where models learn patterns from data instead of being hand‑coded; it uses statistical inference and optimization (e.g., linear regression, decision trees).  
- **DL** is a further subset of ML that employs multi‑layer neural networks to automatically extract hierarchical representations, enabling breakthroughs in vision, speech, and NLP.  

**Edge Cases**  
- Rule‑based expert systems are AI but not ML/DL.  
- Classical ML algorithms (e.g., SVM) can solve problems DL tackles but may need feature engineering.  
- DL requires large labeled datasets; with scarce data, traditional ML often performs better.

**Optimize & Communicate**  
Conclude by stressing that the hierarchy is *conceptual*—practitioners often mix techniques across layers. Emphasize that understanding this spectrum helps choose the right tool for a given problem, a skill interviewers value in AI‑focused roles.
