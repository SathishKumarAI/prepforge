Think of a customer‑support agent as a detective who just got a case file.  
The **clarifying questions** are the notes the detective writes: “Did you try turning it off and on again?” or “What exactly happened when you clicked that button?” They’re simple, direct asks that help the detective (the agent) see what’s actually going on, not guess.

The **assumptions** are the detective’s first‑thought hypotheses—“Maybe the problem is a software glitch,” or “Perhaps the user just didn’t know how to reset.” These are educated guesses based on past cases. They guide the investigation but must be checked against the notes (clarifying questions) before deciding on a solution.

So, in support, ask clear follow‑up questions first, then test your assumptions with evidence you gather from those answers. This keeps solutions accurate and saves time for both agent and customer.
