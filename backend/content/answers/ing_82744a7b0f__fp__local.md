**Before the Interview – Common Pitfalls in AI Job Hunting**

The core problem is *alignment*: you must convince a hiring committee that your technical acumen, domain knowledge, and cultural fit match their needs. The pitfalls arise when this alignment fails on three fronts—knowledge, presentation, and psychology.

1. **Shallow “buzzword” coverage**  
   A resume brimming with terms like *“deep learning”* or *“reinforcement learning”* is a high‑variance signal. Recruiters model your expertise as a distribution over topics; if you only sample the surface, their posterior for depth collapses to zero. Instead, anchor each claim in a concrete project, quantify results (e.g., “improved F1 by 12% on X dataset”), and explain the *why* behind algorithm choices.

2. **Misreading the problem space**  
   Interviewers often frame problems as engineering puzzles; if you treat them purely mathematically, you’ll miss the constraints that make a solution viable (compute budget, latency, regulatory limits). Treat each scenario as an optimization over multiple objectives—accuracy, cost, interpretability—and articulate trade‑offs.

3. **Psychological mismatch**  
   The interview is a two‑way Bayesian inference: they estimate your fit, and you gauge theirs. Over‑confidence or under‑confidence skews both posteriors. Practice *calibrated humility*: admit gaps, but immediately propose how you’d acquire the missing knowledge (online courses, internal mentorship).

**Non‑obvious insight:**  
Most candidates neglect *meta‑learning*—the ability to learn new models quickly from limited data. Highlight a past instance where you improvised a solution on a novel dataset in hours; this demonstrates both technical skill and adaptability, which recruiters value more than any single algorithmic achievement.
