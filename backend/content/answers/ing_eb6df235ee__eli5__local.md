Think of the internet as a giant library and your computer is a curious visitor (the **client**).  
When you want a book, you send a polite request—“Could I see *Page 42*?”—to the librarian’s desk (the **server**).  
The server reads its catalog, finds the right page, and hands it back to you. The whole conversation happens over a quiet hallway called the **network**, so your request travels from client to server and the answer returns in a predictable rhythm.

In this model:

* **Client** – Your browser or app that asks for information.
* **Server** – A powerful computer that stores web pages, images, and data, ready to hand them out on demand.
* **Request/Response** – The back‑and‑forth messages that carry your question and the server’s answer.

Just like a library, the client never touches the books directly; it relies on the server to fetch and deliver exactly what was asked for. This simple, reliable pattern lets millions of users share web content at once without chaos.
