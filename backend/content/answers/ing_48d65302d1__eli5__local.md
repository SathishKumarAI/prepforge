Think of the Internet like a giant city where every building (computer) needs a reliable address system, roads, traffic lights, and a mail service so people can send messages safely and quickly. The **TCP/IP stack** is that city’s infrastructure, built on eight key “standards” that keep everything running smoothly.

1. **IP (Internet Protocol)** – the street‑name system that gives each device a unique address (like a house number).  
2. **IPv4/IPv6** – two versions of that address book; IPv6 is the newer, larger one that can fit more houses.  
3. **ICMP (Internet Control Message Protocol)** – the city’s emergency call center that reports broken roads or delivery failures.  
4. **TCP (Transmission Control Protocol)** – a dependable courier service that guarantees every parcel arrives in order and intact; it opens a “conversation” between two devices.  
5. **UDP (User Datagram Protocol)** – a fast, lightweight messenger that sends parcels without waiting for confirmation—great for live video or gaming where speed matters more than perfection.  
6. **ARP (Address Resolution Protocol)** – the phone book that translates an IP address into the device’s local hardware “phone number” (MAC).  
7. **DHCP (Dynamic Host Configuration Protocol)** – a friendly receptionist who hands out temporary addresses and network settings to new visitors, so they can join the city without manual setup.  
8. **DNS (Domain Name System)** – the city’s map that turns easy‑to‑remember names (“mail.example.com”) into IP addresses.

Together these eight standards are like the roads, traffic lights, postal service, and maps of a bustling metropolis—every developer should know how they work so their apps can communicate reliably across any network.
