Imagine you’re in a city and every landmark (a coffee shop, a museum, a subway station) has a tiny invisible radio beacon that says, “I’m here.” A **location‑based service** is like a friendly guide who listens to your phone’s GPS signal (your *current location*) and then tells you the nearest places that match what you’re looking for.  

A **proximity service** is just a special kind of guide that focuses on distance: it watches how close you are to each beacon and, when you get within a set radius—say 100 meters—it triggers an action, like sending you a “You’re near the museum! Check out our exhibit.” The key terms are *GPS* (the satellite system that tells your phone where you are) and *beacon* (a small device that broadcasts its presence). Together they let apps give you timely, place‑specific help without asking for your exact address.
