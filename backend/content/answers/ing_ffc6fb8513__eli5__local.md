Think of a machine learning model like a **chef who learns to cook from tasting dishes**.  
At first the chef has no idea how to make a perfect soup, so they try a recipe (a set of numbers called *parameters*) and taste the result. If it’s too salty or bland, the chef adjusts the spices—this is the *learning* step where the model tweaks its parameters to better match what we want.  

The dishes the chef tries are the **training data**: each dish has ingredients (input features) and a final taste (label). The chef keeps tasting many dishes, learning which ingredient combinations produce the best flavor. Once trained, the chef can cook new soups from scratch—this is the model making predictions on unseen data.  

In short, machine learning is a system that *learns from examples* by adjusting itself until it can make accurate predictions, just like a chef improves through practice.
