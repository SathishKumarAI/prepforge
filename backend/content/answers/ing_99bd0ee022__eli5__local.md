Imagine your favorite library has a secret index that turns every book into a tiny colored marble. The color tells you the main idea (like “history” or “science”), and the size shows how important that theme is in the book. An **embedding model** does exactly this for words, sentences, or whole documents: it converts them into a short list of numbers that act like the marble’s color and size. These lists are called **representations**.

In **semantic search**, when you type a question, the system turns your query into its own marble and then looks for marbles in the library that sit close together—meaning they share similar colors and sizes. It finds documents that talk about the same ideas even if they don’t use the exact words you typed, just like finding books on “World War II” even if you wrote “global conflict of the 1940s.” This way, the search feels natural and understands meaning rather than matching letters alone.
