**Fault‑tolerant AI** is like a smart kitchen that keeps cooking even if one stove burner goes out.  
Imagine you’re making soup on four burners. If one burner stops working (a “fault”), the others can keep heating, and the pot still boils. The kitchen has a backup plan: it automatically switches the recipe’s heat to the remaining burners and adjusts the timing so the soup finishes right on schedule.  

In AI systems, a fault might be a corrupted data packet, a misbehaving sensor, or a software crash. Fault‑tolerance techniques detect that something went wrong, isolate the problem, and reroute the computation—often by running duplicate calculations on spare hardware or using error‑correcting codes—to keep the model’s predictions accurate and the system online.  

So, just as a kitchen never stops cooking when one burner fails, a fault‑tolerant AI keeps learning and answering even when parts of it stumble.
