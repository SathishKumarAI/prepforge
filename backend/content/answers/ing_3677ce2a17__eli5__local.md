Think of an AI model as a student who has to remember and use information while doing schoolwork. A **memory benchmark** is like a standardized test that checks how well the student can store, retrieve, and use facts over different time spans.

* **LoCoMo (Local‑to‑Global Memory Test)** asks the AI to keep track of short‑term details (like a paragraph) and then connect them to bigger ideas later in the conversation—much like remembering a friend’s name while also recalling their birthday month.  
* **LongMemEval** is the “long‑essay” version: it gives the model a story that unfolds over many turns and tests whether it can still use early clues after dozens of steps, similar to writing an essay where you must remember your thesis from the beginning.  
* **BEAM (Benchmark for Efficient Attention Models)** measures how quickly and accurately the AI can pull up relevant memories when needed—like a student who instantly finds the right page in a textbook without flipping through every chapter.

Together these tests give researchers a clear, comparable picture of an AI’s memory strengths and weaknesses, just as school exams let us compare students.
