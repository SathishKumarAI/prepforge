Imagine your computer as a hungry student that can learn to recognize patterns, like spotting cats in photos or predicting tomorrow’s weather. **Machine Learning (ML)** is the teacher who shows this student how to study from examples instead of being told every rule.

In ML, we give the computer many labeled pictures—“this is a cat,” “this is not a cat.” The computer looks for common features (fur color, whisker shape) and builds an internal recipe. When it sees a new picture, it applies that recipe to guess whether a cat is present. It’s like learning to identify cats by noticing the same clues you’d use—no need to memorize every cat breed.

Key terms:  
- **Dataset**: the collection of examples we hand to the student.  
- **Model**: the computer’s internal recipe built from the data.  
- **Training**: the process of letting the model learn from the dataset.
