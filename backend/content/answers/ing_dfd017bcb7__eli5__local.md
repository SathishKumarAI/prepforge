Imagine you’re baking cookies.  
*Self‑hosting* is like buying all the ingredients, owning your oven, and making the dough yourself. You control temperature, timing, and can tweak the recipe any way you want. The trade‑offs are: you must keep the oven (hardware) running, update the cookie mix (model weights), and deal with power outages or a broken mixer.

*Calling an API* is like ordering from a bakery that already has ovens and skilled bakers. You send them a list of ingredients (your data), they bake the cookies on their side, and you receive finished dough in a box. You never worry about ovens or maintenance, but you pay for each order, can’t change the recipe, and must trust the bakery’s timing.

Choose self‑hosting if you need full control, low latency, or want to keep data local. Choose an API if you value speed of deployment, lower upfront cost, and don’t mind paying per use.
