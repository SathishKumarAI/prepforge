**From the fundamental problem to the job title**

Both roles answer the same core question: *how do we turn data into reliable, deployable intelligence?*  
An **ML Engineer** focuses on the *algorithmic pipeline*: data ingestion → feature engineering → model training → validation → deployment. Their optimization lens is *performance metrics* (accuracy, AUC, latency). They treat the model as a black box that must be calibrated and scaled; they are experts in hyper‑parameter search, ensemble design, and model versioning.

An **AI Engineer** treats the same pipeline as part of a larger *cognitive system*. Their optimization lens is *system coherence*: how do perception, reasoning, planning, and control modules interact under uncertainty? They architect pipelines that fuse heterogeneous modalities (vision, language, sensor streams), implement knowledge graphs or symbolic planners, and ensure real‑time inference on edge devices. Their job is to make disparate models cooperate like a brain’s cortical columns.

**Non‑obvious insight:**  
A true AI engineer must be fluent in *probabilistic graphical models*—not just neural nets—because the ability to encode prior knowledge and reason under uncertainty is what lets hybrid systems generalize beyond data. ML engineers often treat priors as static hyper‑parameters; AI engineers weave them into the model’s structure, enabling continual learning without catastrophic forgetting.
