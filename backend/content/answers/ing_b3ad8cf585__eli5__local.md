Think of training an AI like teaching a student to identify apples in a picture book. If the teacher shows only red, round, shiny apples and never any other fruit or even slightly bruised apples, the student will get very good at spotting those exact apples but will fail when asked to find an apple that looks different—maybe green or half‑peeled. That’s overfitting: the model learns the tiny details of the training data (the book) instead of the general idea of “apple.” It memorizes noise, so it performs poorly on new pictures. Just as a student needs varied examples to truly learn what an apple is, an AI needs diverse data to avoid overfitting and become useful in real life.
