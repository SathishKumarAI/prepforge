**Why behavioral interviews exist**

In any job the *observable* part of a candidate’s performance is what matters: past actions are statistically correlated with future behavior. A hiring manager can’t directly see how you’ll act in a new context, but she can examine patterns from real situations you’ve already navigated. This reduces uncertainty and turns hiring into an evidence‑based decision.

**The structure**

1. **Context → Action → Result (CAR)** – The interviewer asks for a concrete scenario.  
2. **Probing depth** – Follow‑ups drill into motivation, obstacles, alternatives, and lessons learned.  
3. **Relevance filter** – Each example must map to the role’s key competencies (e.g., teamwork, problem‑solving).

**Why this works**

- *Cognitive bias mitigation*: People remember anecdotes, but CAR forces you to provide data points that can be cross‑checked.  
- *Probabilistic inference*: If a candidate repeatedly demonstrates effective conflict resolution, the probability that they’ll handle future conflicts well rises sharply.  
- *Signal extraction*: The interviewer discerns not just what you did, but how you *thought*—a proxy for cultural fit and learning potential.

**Non‑obvious insight**

Most candidates focus on **outcomes**; yet the interview’s real value lies in the *process*. A candidate who explains why they chose a particular approach, how they weighed trade‑offs, and what they would do differently reveals adaptability and self‑reflection—traits that predict long‑term growth far better than any single success story.
