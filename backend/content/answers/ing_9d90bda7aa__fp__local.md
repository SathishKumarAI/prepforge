**Life, AD&D, and LTD – Join Us**

At its core, this trio of coverages is a *risk‑sharing contract* that turns uncertainty about future income into predictable cash flows.  
- **Life** pays a lump sum if the insured dies; it converts the *probability* of death into an *expected value* that protects dependents’ consumption.  
- **Accidental Death & Dismemberment (AD&D)** is a narrower, higher‑rate policy that covers only catastrophic events. By separating accidental risk from natural mortality, insurers can price each component optimally: accidental loss has lower variance but higher severity, so the premium reflects its *expected cost* plus a loading for administrative complexity.  
- **Long‑Term Disability (LTD)** replaces a portion of earned income when an illness or injury prevents work. The policy’s benefit schedule is designed to satisfy the *smoothing principle*: it keeps consumption close to pre‑disability levels, thereby reducing welfare loss in the face of stochastic income shocks.

These products are linked because they all address *income volatility* from different sources. Bundling them reduces transaction costs and leverages cross‑product underwriting data: a healthy life profile lowers both AD&D and LTD risk, allowing insurers to offer attractive premiums.

The real innovation lies in the *interaction term* between AD&D and LTD. When an accidental injury leads to permanent disability, the policy can trigger both benefits—AD&D for dismemberment and LTD for loss of earning capacity. This dual trigger captures a joint tail event that pure life insurance cannot, making the bundle mathematically more efficient than selling each product separately.
