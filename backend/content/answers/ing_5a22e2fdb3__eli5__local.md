Imagine you’re trying to read a long novel in a single sitting. **Chunking** is like deciding whether to split the story into chapters (big chunks) or into short paragraphs (tiny chunks).  

*Big‑chunk strategy:* Treat a whole page as one unit. Use it when each page contains a clear, self‑contained idea—like reading a news article where every paragraph is related. It saves time but can overwhelm if the page is dense.

*Tiny‑chunk strategy:* Break text into single sentences or even phrases. Pick this when the material is complex or highly detailed—think of studying a scientific paper; each sentence carries a specific fact, so focusing on one at a time prevents confusion.

In practice:  
- Use **large chunks** for straightforward, linear information (lists, recipes).  
- Use **small chunks** for dense, technical, or unfamiliar content (legal documents, coding tutorials).  

By matching chunk size to how much can comfortably fit in your short‑term memory, you keep the learning process smooth and efficient.
