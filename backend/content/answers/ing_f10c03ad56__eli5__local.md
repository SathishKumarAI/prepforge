Think of your brain as a smartphone that stores photos (episodic memories).  
At first, every picture helps you remember how to tie shoes or where you left your keys—an asset.  
But if the phone keeps thousands of blurry, outdated snaps, scrolling becomes slow and you keep re‑seeing old heartbreaks or embarrassing moments. That’s a liability: the memory overload blocks new learning, saps energy, and can trigger negative emotions.  

So episodic memory turns from an asset to a liability when it piles up unfiltered details that no longer serve your goals—like clutter on a hard drive—making it harder to focus, decide, or feel well‑being. Regular “cleaning” (letting go of irrelevant memories) keeps the phone fast and the mind healthy.
