**Image Augmentation – a quick cheat‑sheet**

Imagine you’re training a chess coach who has only one board set to practice on. Every time a student asks for help, the coach would have to copy that exact board—no variety, no surprises. In real life, people see many different board positions, so the coach learns to spot patterns rather than memorizing a single layout.

Image augmentation does the same thing for computer‑vision models: it takes one picture and automatically creates dozens of new “looks” by flipping, rotating, zooming, changing colors, or adding noise. Each new version is still recognizably the same object (a cat, a stop sign), but its appearance varies just enough that the model learns to recognize the object no matter how it’s presented.

So, think of augmentation as a creative teacher who keeps giving fresh copies of the same lesson, ensuring students learn the underlying idea instead of the exact wording.
