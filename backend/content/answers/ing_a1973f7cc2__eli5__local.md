Imagine you’re learning to play a long song on the piano. Every few measures you press a “record” button so that if you forget a phrase or need to pause, you can stop and start again from that exact spot instead of beginning the whole piece.  

In machine learning, **handwritten checkpointing code** does the same thing for training models: it’s a small set of instructions written by a programmer that tells the computer to save the current state of its calculations (weights, biases, optimizer settings) at chosen moments. If something goes wrong—like a power outage or an error—you can reload from that saved “record” and resume learning without starting over.  

Key terms:  
- **Checkpoint** – a snapshot of the model’s internal data stored on disk.  
- **Handwritten code** – the explicit instructions written by the developer, rather than automatically generated by a library.

So, checkpointing is like taking safety notes during practice; it protects your progress and lets you pick up where you left off whenever needed.
