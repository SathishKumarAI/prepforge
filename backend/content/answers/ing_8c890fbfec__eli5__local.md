Think of a class of students who are all trying to learn how to sort their homework papers by size so they can hand them out in order.  
Each paper is like an **item** you want to arrange, and the “size” is the value we’re sorting on.  
A **data structure** (like an array, linked list, or tree) is simply a way of holding those papers in the classroom—some are kept in a single line, others in piles that can grow or shrink.  
A **sorting algorithm** is the teacher’s rulebook: “First line up the biggest paper, then the next biggest, and so on.”  
Examples: *Bubble sort* (students bump their papers to the front one by one), *Merge sort* (divide the pile into two groups, sort each group, then merge back together).  
Just as a teacher chooses a rule that fits the class size and paper weight, in computer science we pick a sorting method that best matches our data structure and speed needs.
