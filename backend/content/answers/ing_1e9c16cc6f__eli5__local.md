Think of an AI “agent” like a robot that can act in the world.  
Its **tools** are the *limbs* it uses to get things done—hands, wheels, cameras, or even software libraries. Each tool is just a function that turns an input into an output (e.g., a camera takes light and produces an image). The agent’s job is to pick the right limb for each task: if it needs to move, it activates its wheels; if it needs to understand speech, it calls on a speech‑to‑text tool.  

**Agent fundamentals** are the simple rules that let the robot decide which limb to use and when:  
1. **Perceive** – gather data from sensors or APIs.  
2. **Decide** – choose a goal or action based on its plan.  
3. **Act** – activate the chosen tool to change the environment or produce information.  

Just like a person uses different body parts for different jobs, an AI agent uses its tools—its limbs—to interact with the world efficiently and flexibly.
