**Knowledge Gap Detection in Knowledge Management**

Imagine a library that wants to keep its shelves filled with the books people actually want to read. Every day, patrons browse and pick titles; the library watches which shelves are empty or rarely visited. When a gap appears—say no one has checked out any book on “climate‑change policy” for months—the librarians know they need to add new material or update what’s there.

In knowledge management this is the same idea: an organization tracks who asks what questions and which answers are used. If many people keep asking a question that isn’t answered, the system flags a *knowledge gap*. The team then creates or updates content to fill it, so future workers find the information they need right away. This keeps knowledge current and useful—just like a library that always knows which books to restock.
