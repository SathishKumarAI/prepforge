Imagine a chef who learns to cook by tasting many dishes and remembering what makes each one good or bad.  
In **machine learning** the computer is that chef: it sees a lot of data (the “tasting”), tries to guess an outcome (like predicting tomorrow’s weather), and then checks if it was right. Each time it gets feedback, it tweaks its recipe—this tweaking is called **training**.  

The data you give are the ingredients; the hidden rules the computer learns are the cooking techniques. Once trained, the model can quickly predict new dishes without having to taste them again.  
So, machine learning is just a smart, iterative tasting process that turns raw information into reliable predictions.
