### Live‑Streaming From First Principles

At its core, a live stream is the *real‑time mapping* of an incoming signal (camera, microphone, screen) to a distributed audience over the Internet. The problem can be phrased as **“how do we preserve temporal coherence while minimizing latency and bandwidth?”**  
1. **Capture** – Sensors produce raw samples at their native frame/bit rates.  
2. **Encoding** – An encoder compresses these samples into a bitstream (H.264/H.265, VP9) using motion‑compensated prediction to reduce redundancy.  
3. **Packetization & Segmentation** – The compressed stream is split into small RTP packets; each carries sequence numbers so the receiver can detect loss.  
4. **Transport** – UDP (or QUIC) delivers packets with minimal handshake overhead, accepting occasional dropouts that can be hidden by forward error correction or retransmission at higher layers.  
5. **Adaptive Bitrate (ABR)** – The server hosts multiple quality variants; a client’s bandwidth estimator triggers switches to maintain smooth playback, an application of control‑theoretic optimization.  
6. **Distribution** – A CDN forwards packets from the origin to edge nodes closest to viewers, reducing path length and queuing delay.  
7. **Playback** – The receiver decodes, reorders, and renders frames, buffering just enough to absorb jitter while keeping latency low.

#### Non‑obvious insight
The *most critical* resource is not bandwidth but **entropy**: each encoder must decide which macroblocks are worth transmitting at high fidelity versus those that can be reconstructed from previous frames. By treating entropy as a constrained optimization variable, modern encoders achieve near‑lossless quality with a fraction of the raw bitrate—an insight that underpins every real‑time streaming service today.
