Imagine a farmer who must harvest tomatoes and bring them to market. **Batch processing** is like the farmer waiting until the entire field is ripe, then hauling all the tomatoes in one big truck at once. The truck stops only after it’s full; the farmers can plan exactly how many tomatoes will arrive, but they have to wait for the whole crop.

**Stream processing** is like the farmer walking through the field and dropping each tomato into a small basket that rolls straight to the market as soon as it’s picked. Every single tomato gets delivered immediately, even though the baskets are tiny; the farmer never has to wait for a full truck.

In machine learning, batch processing gathers many data points before computing results—fast when you can afford the wait. Stream processing handles each data point instantly—essential when real‑time decisions matter.
