Think of **data architecture** as the blueprint for a city that stores and moves information instead of cars.  
Just like a city’s streets, parks, and power lines must be laid out so people can get from one place to another safely and quickly, data architecture plans where raw facts (the “raw traffic”) live, how they’re stored, how they’re linked together, and how software programs can fetch or update them without getting lost.

Key parts:  
* **Data sources** – the factories that produce information.  
* **Storage layers** – warehouses (databases) that keep data safe and organized.  
* **Integration routes** – roads (ETL pipelines) that transport data from one warehouse to another.  
* **Access layer** – city gates (APIs, dashboards) where users pull the data they need.

Just as a well‑designed city lets residents reach their destinations efficiently, a good data architecture lets analysts and machines find, share, and learn from information quickly and reliably.
