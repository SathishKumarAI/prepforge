Imagine you’re at a huge library where every book is tagged with tiny labels—author, genre, and so on. A **functional** system is like a super‑smart librarian who not only reads those labels but also understands the *purpose* of your request. If you ask for “books that help me plan a wedding,” she skips the label “cookbook” even if it has “recipe” in its tags, because she knows the function you need.

**Semantic search** is the librarian’s ability to grasp meaning. Instead of matching exact words (“wedding”), she looks at concepts: planning, events, celebrations. She can answer “I want a guide on wedding budget” or “tips for venue selection,” even if those exact phrases never appeared in the books.

So functional semantic search = a system that uses meaning (semantics) to find items that *serve your goal* (function), not just items that share keywords. It’s like having a librarian who reads between the lines and knows exactly what you’re trying to do.
