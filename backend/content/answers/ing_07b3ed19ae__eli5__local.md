Imagine a neighborhood where every house has its own garden shed that can store tools and share them with anyone who needs them—no central store is required.  
**Peer‑to‑peer (P2P) network** works the same way: each computer (a *peer*) both offers resources (storage, bandwidth, computing power) and uses others’ resources.  

*Features*:  
- **Decentralized** – no single server controls everything.  
- **Direct connections** – peers talk straight to one another.  
- **Scalable** – more peers add more capacity.

*Pros*:  
- **Resilience** – if one house falls, the rest keep going.  
- **Cost‑effective** – no need for expensive central servers.  
- **Speed** – nearby peers can share data quickly.

*Cons*:  
- **Security risks** – bad actors can hide inside a “house.”  
- **Unpredictable performance** – some houses may be slow or offline.  
- **Management difficulty** – coordinating many independent devices is hard.

In short, a P2P network is like a cooperative community garden: everyone contributes and benefits, but you must trust each neighbor and handle the chaos of many self‑managed plots.
