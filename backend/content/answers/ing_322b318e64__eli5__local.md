Think of machine learning like building a house that can learn to recognize faces, translate languages, or drive a car. **Architecture** is the blueprint that tells you how all the rooms (components) fit together and what each room does.

In this house:
- The *foundation* is the data you feed in.
- The *rooms* are layers of neurons or decision trees, each with a specific job—some look for edges in an image, others combine those edges into shapes.
- The *doors and hallways* are the connections that let information flow from one room to another.
- The *roof* is the learning rule (e.g., back‑propagation) that adjusts everything so the house gets better over time.

Just as a building’s design determines its strength, size, and function, an ML architecture decides how fast it learns, how much memory it uses, and what tasks it can perform. It’s not just one thing; it’s the whole arrangement of parts working together to solve a problem.
