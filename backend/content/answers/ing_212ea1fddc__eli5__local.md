Imagine you’re asked to build a new kitchen for a busy family. The interviewer isn’t just checking if you can cook; they want to see how you plan the layout, choose appliances that work together, and keep the space safe and efficient.

In a machine‑learning system‑design interview you’ll be expected to:

1. **Understand the problem** – ask clarifying questions about data volume, latency, accuracy, and user privacy.
2. **Sketch the big picture** – diagram how raw data moves into preprocessing, model training, inference, monitoring, and feedback loops.
3. **Choose components wisely** – pick storage (SQL vs. NoSQL), compute (GPU clusters or serverless), and serving frameworks that fit the scale.
4. **Plan for reliability** – think about fault tolerance, scaling, versioning models, and data drift detection.
5. **Explain trade‑offs** – show why you favor one algorithm over another or why a batch pipeline is better than real‑time for this case.
