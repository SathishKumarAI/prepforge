**Step 1: Outline use cases, constraints, and assumptions**

Think of building a new recipe book for your kitchen.  
First, you decide what dishes (use cases) the book will cover—maybe quick week‑night dinners, gourmet desserts, or healthy salads. Next, you list the ingredients you have on hand (constraints)—spices in limited supply, dietary restrictions, or time limits. Finally, you write down the rules of your kitchen (assumptions)—you’ll always have fresh produce, you can bake at 350°F, and you trust that the oven works.

In machine learning, this is the same:  
- **Use cases** are the tasks the model will perform—classifying emails, predicting sales, translating text.  
- **Constraints** are real‑world limits like data size, privacy laws, or hardware speed.  
- **Assumptions** are conditions you believe hold true—labels are correct, training data represents future inputs, and the algorithm can learn from patterns.

By clarifying these three parts up front, you set a clear roadmap for the rest of the project.
