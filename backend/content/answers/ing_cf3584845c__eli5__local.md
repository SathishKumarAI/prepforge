Imagine you’re learning to juggle and you keep adding more balls. At first the extra ball makes juggling harder, but after a point, adding another ball doesn’t change how you juggle; it’s already at its limit. In AI evaluation, “theoretical saturation” is that same point: the moment a model has learned everything it can from the data set, so any further training or more examples won’t improve its performance.

Think of the model as a student who practices on a fixed test book. Once the student memorizes every question and answer, additional practice on the same book gives no new benefit. That’s saturation—no matter how many more passes you run, accuracy stays flat. It tells researchers when a model is fully trained for that task and helps decide whether to move on to better data or different models.
