Think of a recipe book in your kitchen.  
A **stored procedure** is like one of those recipes that you can keep on the fridge forever, ready to use whenever you want.  

When you open the book (your database), you don’t have to write out every step again; you just say, “Give me the chicken stir‑fry.” The kitchen (the database engine) follows the recipe exactly: it knows which ingredients to grab (tables), how to mix them (SELECT, INSERT, UPDATE), and what final dish to serve (a result set or a change in data).  

Just as a recipe can be reused many times by different cooks, a stored procedure lives inside the database and any application can call it with new inputs. It keeps your “cookbook” tidy, protects you from making mistakes, and often runs faster because the kitchen already knows the best way to prepare that dish.
