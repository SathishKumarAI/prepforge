**Compensation Signals in a Software‑Engineer Interview**

At its core, the *compensation signal* is an information‑theoretic shortcut: the interview panel infers your market value from observable cues—salary history, equity offers, negotiation style—without explicit disclosure. The panel’s objective is to **optimize resource allocation** (pay vs performance) under uncertainty about your true ability. Thus they design a *signal extraction* process that transforms noisy observations into an estimate of your intrinsic worth.

1. **Process Overview**  
   - **Pre‑screen:** HR requests prior compensation; this establishes a baseline *prior*.  
   - **Technical round:** Performance updates the *posterior*—a higher score shifts the belief upward, but if it’s marginal, the prior dominates.  
   - **Negotiation phase:** Your counter‑offer is treated as a *strategic signal*: aggressive asks imply high confidence (or low tolerance for risk), while modest requests suggest risk aversion.

2. **Typical Questions**  
   - “What was your last salary?”  
   - “How did you decide on your target compensation?”  
   - “If we offered X, would that be acceptable?”

3. **Prep Insight**  
   Most candidates treat the negotiation as a *zero‑sum game*. The hidden principle is **Bayesian updating**: by revealing only what’s necessary (e.g., a range rather than an exact figure), you keep your posterior flexible for future offers. A non‑obvious tactic is to anchor with *industry data* first, then let the company’s counter shift the signal in your favor.

4. **Practical Tip**  
   Prepare a concise “compensation story” that links past roles, growth metrics, and market benchmarks. This narrative becomes the controlled variable the panel uses to calibrate their estimate, ensuring you’re neither undervalued nor over‑priced.
