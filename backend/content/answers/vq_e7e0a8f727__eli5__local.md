**DDL (Data Definition Language) statements are like the blueprint tools you use to build a house.**

Think of your database as a building and its tables, columns, indexes, and relationships as rooms, walls, doors, and floors.  
* **CREATE** is the “draw the floor plan” command – it builds a new table (room).  
* **ALTER** is like adding or removing a wall or door – you change the structure of an existing table.  
* **DROP** is tearing down a room entirely.

Just as a blueprint decides what rooms exist and how they’re connected, DDL defines *what* data structures will hold your information. Once you run a DDL statement, the changes are permanent (unless you undo them), just as a built wall stays until it’s demolished. This is why DDL is called “definition” language – it tells the database **how** to organize and store data.
