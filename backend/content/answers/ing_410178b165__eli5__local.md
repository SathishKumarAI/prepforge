Imagine a big kitchen where many chefs (neurons) work together to cook a dish (the output).  
Each chef has a list of ingredients (inputs) and a simple rule: “Add X grams of salt, stir Y times.” The result they hand off becomes the next ingredient for other chefs.  

A **neural network** is this chain of kitchens: a set of layers where each layer’s chefs take what the previous layer cooked, apply their own rules (weights), and pass on the new mixture.  
The “rules” are tiny numbers that get tweaked during learning, so after many meals the kitchen learns to make exactly the dish you want—say, recognizing a cat in a photo.  

So, a neural network is nothing more than a series of simple processors (neurons) connected in layers, each transforming data step by step until the final answer emerges.
