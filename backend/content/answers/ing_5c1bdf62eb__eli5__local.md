**Selected Prototypes – Think of a “mini‑museum” of data**

Imagine you’re building a museum that showcases only the most interesting paintings from a huge gallery. Instead of hanging every single piece, you handpick a few that best represent each style: one bold abstract, one serene landscape, one vibrant portrait. In machine learning, *selected prototypes* are those handful of data points chosen to stand in for whole groups.  

A prototype is simply a typical example—an actual data point that captures the essence of its cluster. By training on these few “museum pieces” instead of every original painting, a model learns the main patterns quickly and stays lightweight. It’s like giving your algorithm a concise guidebook: enough to recognize new artworks without remembering every single brushstroke. This keeps learning fast, reduces storage, and often improves generalization when new data arrives.
