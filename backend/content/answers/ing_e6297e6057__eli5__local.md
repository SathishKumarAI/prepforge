Imagine you’re chatting with a friend over the phone while standing in different cities. The time it takes for your words to reach them and their reply to come back is the **network latency**—the delay caused by the distance and traffic on the lines that carry the data.

In conversational AI, “latency” means how long it takes from when you type or speak a question until the bot’s answer appears. If the latency is high, the conversation feels slow or stilted, like waiting for your friend to answer after every message. Low latency keeps the chat natural and responsive, just as instant phone calls feel conversational.

Why does it matter? Users quickly notice delays; a laggy AI can frustrate them, reduce trust, and make tasks harder—especially in time‑sensitive scenarios such as customer support or medical triage. Engineers therefore optimize network paths, use faster servers, and sometimes pre‑load answers to keep latency minimal, ensuring the AI feels like a real human partner.
