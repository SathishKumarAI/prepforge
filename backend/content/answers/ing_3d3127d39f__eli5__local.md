**Printing in a Python notebook is like sending a postcard to the screen.**

When you write `print("Hello, world!")`, you’re telling Python: “Take this text and hand it straight to the display.”  
Think of the **`print()` function** as a messenger that grabs whatever you give it—strings, numbers, or even complex objects—and writes them out in plain sight.  

Just as a postcard shows exactly what you wrote on its front, `print()` outputs your data exactly as it is, without changing it.  
If you want to send more than one item, separate them with commas: `print("Score:", 42)` will show `Score: 42`.  
The message ends at the end of the line; if you need a new line, add `\n` or just call `print()` again.

So, printing is simply Python’s way of making your thoughts visible on the screen.
