### Why “Runs” Matter for Evaluating LLM Workflows

At its core, an LLM workflow is a **probabilistic mapping**  
\(x \mapsto y\) where the model’s output distribution depends on both the input prompt and any auxiliary context (retrieval, memory, etc.).  
To *measure* performance we must compare many sampled outputs to ground‑truth or reference signals.  

A **Run** is simply a single instantiation of that mapping:  
- the chosen prompt, chain configuration, and seed;  
- the exact token sequence generated by the model;  
- any intermediate artifacts (retrieved passages, memory states).  

Why this granularity?  
1. **Variance capture** – LLMs are stochastic; a single sample can be misleading.  
2. **Traceability** – each run records its context so we can attribute success or failure to a specific prompt version or retrieval set.  
3. **Statistical rigor** – aggregating runs lets us compute unbiased estimators of metrics (BLEU, ROUGE, F1) and their confidence intervals.

Evaluation in LangChain therefore proceeds as:
1. **Generate Runs**: execute the chain multiple times with controlled randomness.  
2. **Collect Outputs**: store each run’s tokens and metadata.  
3. **Score & Aggregate**: compare against references to produce per‑run scores, then average across runs for a robust estimate.

#### Non‑obvious insight
Many practitioners treat “evaluation” as a one‑off comparison. In fact, the *distribution* of run outputs is itself a valuable signal: a narrow spread indicates deterministic behaviour (often undesirable), while a wide spread may reveal hidden biases or over‑confidence. By analysing run variance we can diagnose when a model’s uncertainty aligns with its true error likelihood—bridging evaluation with calibration and downstream risk assessment.
