**Closing the Loop in a Cursor Software‑Engineer Interview**

*Why it matters.*  
The “closing” phase is where an interviewee’s *cumulative value* is distilled into a single decision: does the candidate possess the *right mix of problem‑solving, communication, and cultural fit*? It is a Bayesian update on prior evidence (coding tests, portfolio, references). The interviewer must compress noisy observations into a probability that the candidate will deliver high‑impact code in production.

### Process

1. **Re‑affirm Objectives** – Restate the role’s core responsibilities and success metrics.  
2. **Ask “What If” Scenarios** – Pose a realistic edge case (e.g., scaling to 10 M users). The answer reveals architectural intuition and risk assessment.  
3. **Clarify Next Steps** – Outline remaining interviews, timelines, and any additional technical artifacts required.

### Typical Questions

| Category | Example | What the interviewer gauges |
|----------|---------|-----------------------------|
| Technical depth | “How would you refactor this legacy module to reduce coupling?” | Design thinking & code quality judgment |
| Behavioral fit | “Describe a time you disagreed with a teammate’s design.” | Conflict resolution & collaboration |
| Growth mindset | “What new technology are you learning now, and why?” | Curiosity & adaptability |

### Prep Guide

1. **Map the Job Description to Core Competencies** – Create a 2‑column sheet: *Requirement → Interview Question*.  
2. **Simulate Edge Cases** – Practice with mock scenarios (e.g., latency spikes) and explain trade‑offs aloud.  
3. **Reflect on Past Projects** – Prepare concise stories that link impact, decisions, and learning outcomes.  

*Non‑obvious insight:* The closing interview is less about *showing off* than about *demonstrating humility*. Candidates who openly admit knowledge gaps, but frame them as learning opportunities, score higher because the interviewer infers a growth trajectory—a critical asset for rapidly evolving tech stacks.
