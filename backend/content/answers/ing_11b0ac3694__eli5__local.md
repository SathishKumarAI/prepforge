Think of a language model as a gigantic library that can answer questions, write stories, or chat with you.  
The “big” part means it has **millions to billions of tiny books**—each book is called a *parameter*. A parameter is just a number that the model uses to decide how words fit together, like a knob on a radio that tunes into the right station.  

When we train the model, we flip through every page in the library many times, adjusting each knob so the next word it predicts feels natural. The more knobs (parameters) there are, the finer the tuning and the richer the understanding of language patterns. That’s why bigger models can produce more accurate, creative, or context‑aware responses—they simply have a larger, more detailed map of how words connect.
