Think of an AI project like a tiny city you’re building with Lego blocks. The interviewer wants to know that the city will stay stable, grow, and keep its people happy. Red‑flag signals are the warning signs that something might fall apart.

* **No data hygiene** – If your “Lego bricks” (data) are mixed up or broken, the whole city can collapse. Interviewers look for evidence you clean, label, and validate data before feeding it to a model.
* **Over‑fitting to past traffic** – A city that only works on yesterday’s map but fails on tomorrow’s is useless. They’ll ask if your model has been tested on fresh, unseen data and how you avoid memorizing the training set.
* **Unclear impact** – If you can’t explain what problem the AI solves or who benefits, it’s like building a bridge with no traffic to cross. Expect questions about business value and user outcomes.
* **Lack of reproducibility** – A city that disappears when someone else rebuilds it signals sloppy code. Interviewers check if your experiments are version‑controlled, documented, and can be rerun.

If you spot any of these red flags in your work, the interviewer will likely probe deeper to see whether you’ve addressed them.
