Imagine you’re teaching a puppy to fetch a ball. At first, the puppy just follows your hand—no real understanding. In machine learning, the “puppy” is an algorithm and the “ball” is the answer we want (like predicting tomorrow’s weather). We give the puppy lots of past games (data) and show it where the ball ended up each time. The puppy learns a pattern: if the ball was thrown near the tree, it usually ends up there. After many trials, the puppy can guess where the ball will land on its own.

Key terms:  
- **Algorithm**: A set of instructions that learn from data.  
- **Data**: Past examples used for training.  
- **Model**: The learned pattern that makes predictions.  

Just as the puppy improves with practice, a machine learning model gets better the more it sees examples.
