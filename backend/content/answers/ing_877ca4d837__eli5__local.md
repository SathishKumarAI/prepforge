Think of deep learning as learning a new language by starting from scratch—first you learn letters, then grammar, then how people actually speak.  
Transfer learning is like studying a second language when you already know one: you keep the vocabulary and sentence patterns you learned before and just tweak them to fit the new tongue.

In practice, a big “teacher” network has already seen millions of images and built a library of useful visual tricks (edges, shapes, textures). Instead of building that library again from zero for your own small dataset, you take the teacher’s layers as a starting point. You then let only the last few “conversation” layers adapt to your specific task—say recognizing cats vs. dogs.

So transfer learning saves time and data by re‑using knowledge already learned in a related domain, just like a bilingual person can learn another language faster because they’re already fluent in one.
