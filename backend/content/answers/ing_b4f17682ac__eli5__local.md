Think of an AI model as a well‑trained chef who has memorized countless recipes (the training phase). **Inference** is the moment you give that chef a new dish request—say, “make me a vegan pizza.” The chef looks at the ingredients you’ve provided (input data), pulls out all the relevant recipe steps from memory, and produces the final pizza (output) without learning anything new.  

In machine‑learning terms, inference is when a pre‑trained model takes fresh data, runs it through its learned weights, and spits out predictions or decisions. It’s like flipping a switch: no extra training, just applying what’s already known to solve a specific problem.
