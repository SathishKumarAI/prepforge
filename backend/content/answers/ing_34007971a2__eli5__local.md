Imagine you’re baking cookies and want to know how good your recipe is.  

**Expectation (average)** – Think of it as the “typical” cookie size you’ll get if you bake a lot. It’s the long‑run average after many batches.  
**Variance** – This tells you how spread out those cookie sizes are around that typical size. If every cookie comes out almost the same, variance is tiny; if some are huge and others tiny, variance grows.  
**Standard deviation** – Simply the square root of variance, giving a spread measure in the same units as the cookies (millimeters). It’s easier to interpret: “Most cookies fall within one standard deviation of the average size.”  

So expectation is the central trend, variance measures how much that trend wiggles, and standard deviation translates that wiggle back into familiar cookie‑size terms.
