Imagine you have two piles of LEGO bricks that represent different ideas—say “cat” and “pet.” Each pile is built from the same set of brick types (colors, shapes), but in different proportions. The **embedding** is like a recipe that tells you how many of each brick type goes into each pile. A **vector space** is simply the room where all these recipes live; every idea becomes a point defined by its bricks.

Now, to see how similar the two ideas are, you compare their recipes side‑by‑side. **Cosine similarity** measures the angle between the two recipe vectors: if the angle is tiny (they point almost in the same direction), the ideas are very similar; if the angle is wide, they’re quite different. It ignores how big each pile is and focuses only on the pattern of brick types—exactly what we want when matching words or images in AI.
