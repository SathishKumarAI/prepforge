**When are behavioral skills evaluated?**  
During a *structured* interview, after the candidate has answered the technical “what do you know?” questions and before any final verdict is reached. Recruiters first probe the *process*—how you approached problems, how you handled conflict, how you learned from failure—because those actions are stable predictors of future performance in the same role.

**Why must it work this way?**  
Behavioral questions rest on *situational judgment theory*: past action is the best proxy for future action. By asking candidates to narrate concrete episodes (e.g., “Tell me about a time you resolved a disagreement”), interviewers map observable evidence onto latent traits—resilience, collaboration, initiative—that cannot be captured by a résumé alone.

**Deeper principle:**  
This mirrors *Bayesian inference* in decision‑making. The interviewer’s prior belief (“this role requires teamwork”) is updated with the candidate’s evidence (the story). The posterior probability of hiring success is higher when the narrative aligns with the job’s behavioral requirements.

**Non‑obvious insight:**  
Candidates often tailor their stories to *what they think* the interviewers want. Yet the most predictive responses are those that reveal *adaptive flexibility*: how a person pivots strategies mid‑project or learns from a failed experiment. Highlighting such moments demonstrates not just competence but the capacity to evolve—exactly what dynamic roles demand.
