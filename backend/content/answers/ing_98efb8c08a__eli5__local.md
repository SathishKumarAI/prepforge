Think of a photo as a painting you want to practice drawing from. To get better, you don’t just copy the same picture over and over—you change it in small ways: flip it left‑to‑right, tilt it a bit, or cover part of it with a patch. Those little tweaks are “image augmentations.”  

Common tricks include:

* **Flip** – mirror the image horizontally (like looking at it in a window).  
* **Rotate** – turn the picture a few degrees clockwise or counterclockwise (just like turning a card).  
* **Crop/Scale** – zoom in or cut out a section, then resize back to the original size.  
* **Color jitter** – lightly alter brightness, contrast, or saturation (like adjusting the light in a room).  
* **Noise addition** – sprinkle random grain to simulate camera shake.  

Each method keeps the core content but changes its appearance slightly, teaching AI to recognize objects no matter how they’re flipped, rotated, or lit.
