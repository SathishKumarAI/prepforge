Think of machine learning like teaching a child to recognize apples and oranges. You show the child many pictures, saying “apple” or “orange.” Each picture has features—color, shape, texture—that the child notices and starts grouping them. After enough examples, the child can guess the fruit in a new picture without being told. In the same way, a computer runs a program that looks at data (the pictures) and learns patterns by adjusting tiny knobs called weights. When you give it fresh data, those learned patterns let it predict or classify automatically. The “learning” happens inside the algorithm; the child’s brain is like the model, the training examples are the data, and the guesses are predictions. That’s machine learning in a nutshell—training a system to spot patterns so it can make smart decisions on its own.
