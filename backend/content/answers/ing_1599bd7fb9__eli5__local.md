Imagine your video is a giant, colorful storybook that people want to read on many different devices—phones, tablets, computers, or smart TVs. **Encoding** is like translating that book into a universal “language” so every reader can understand it without losing the pictures or sound.

First, you pick a **codec** (the translator). Think of it as a skilled interpreter who knows how to compress the words (video frames) and keep the meaning clear. Then you decide on a **resolution** (how big each page is), a **bitrate** (how much detail you want to keep), and an **audio format** (the voice’s tone). The encoder stitches these choices together, turning raw footage into a tidy file that loads fast and looks sharp no matter the screen.

In short: encoding turns messy, huge video data into a smooth, portable version that every device can play without hiccups.
