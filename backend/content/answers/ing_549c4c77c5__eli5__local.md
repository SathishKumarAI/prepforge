Imagine a chef (the machine learning model) tasting soup and guessing which spice is missing. The “probability concepts” are the chef’s tools for making that guess.

1. **Probability** – the chance something happens, like 70 % that the soup needs salt.  
2. **Random variable** – a measurable outcome, such as the amount of sugar added.  
3. **Distribution** – the recipe book showing how often each spice appears in soups.  
4. **Expectation (mean)** – the average taste score across many bowls.  
5. **Variance** – how wildly those scores spread out.  
6. **Bayes’ rule** – updating the guess after tasting a new spoonful.  
7. **Conditional probability** – the chance of needing pepper given the soup is already sweet.  
8. **Joint probability** – both salt and pepper being needed at once.  
9. **Independence** – spices that don’t affect each other’s presence.  
10. **Entropy** – how uncertain the chef feels before tasting.

Just as the chef refines his guess with every spoonful, a model learns from data, using these concepts to predict accurately.
