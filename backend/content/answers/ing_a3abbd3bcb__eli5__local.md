Imagine you’re baking a cake that must taste just right for your friends.  
**Bias** is like always using the same recipe and over‑cooking the batter—every cake turns out too dry, no matter how many times you bake it. That’s a systematic error: the model (the recipe) is wrong about what “good” looks like.  

**Variance** is like letting each friend choose their own oven temperature and baking time; one might burn the top while another leaves it under‑baked. The results differ wildly because small changes in the data (friends’ choices) produce big swings in the outcome.

The **bias–variance trade‑off** says: if you keep your recipe simple (high bias, low variance), every cake will be predictable but maybe not tasty. If you make it very flexible (low bias, high variance), some cakes will be perfect while others flop. Good machine learning finds a middle ground—enough flexibility to learn the pattern but enough consistency to avoid over‑reacting to noise.
