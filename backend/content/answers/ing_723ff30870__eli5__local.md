**Declarative programming** is like giving a recipe instead of step‑by‑step cooking instructions.  
You say, “Make a cake with flour, eggs, sugar, and bake at 350 °F for 30 minutes,” not “mix the flour, add eggs, stir, pour into pan, turn on oven.” The computer figures out *how* to do each low‑level operation while you focus only on what the final result should be.

In programming terms:  
- **Declarative** = state *what* you want.  
- **Imperative** (the opposite) = give explicit commands for *how* to get there.

Think of a spreadsheet: you set formulas (“=A1+B1”) and let Excel compute the numbers; you don’t write loops to add each cell manually. That’s declarative style—clear, concise, and often easier to read and maintain.
