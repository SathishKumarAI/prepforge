Imagine you’re hosting a party and want everyone, even those far away, to feel like they’re right there with you. Live streaming is that party on the internet, and it happens in a few clear stages:

1. **Capture** – You “take a picture” of what’s happening: video from your camera, sound from a mic, maybe screen‑share or graphics.  
2. **Encode** – That raw footage is compressed into a small file that can travel fast over the internet, like packing groceries into a sturdy box.  
3. **Send (Upload)** – The encoded stream travels through your network to a streaming server (think of it as a post office).  
4. **Distribute** – The server rewrites the stream into tiny packets and pushes them out to every viewer’s device—like a delivery truck dropping packages at many houses.  
5. **Play** – Each viewer’s app receives the packets, reassembles them in order, decodes the video, and shows it on their screen in real time.

That’s live streaming: capture → compress → send → distribute → play, all happening so fast that viewers see your party as it unfolds.
