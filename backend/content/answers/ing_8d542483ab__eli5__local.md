**The Problem – “Document Intelligence”**

Imagine a huge library where every book is written in a different language, on paper that’s crumpled or faded, and the covers are all mixed up. If you need to find a single fact—say, the date of a company’s founding—you’d have to flip through pages, decode handwriting, and translate notes into English just to read it.

In the digital world, “documents” (PDFs, emails, contracts) behave like that messy library: they come in countless formats, with varied fonts, tables, images, and sometimes handwritten stamps. A computer can’t automatically read them all because each one looks different and contains hidden clues that only a human would notice.

**Document intelligence** is the AI’s attempt to become that helpful librarian. It must first *recognize* what’s in the page (text, pictures, tables), *understand* the meaning of words and their relationships, and then *answer* questions or extract specific data—like pulling out a phone number or verifying a signature—without human help.

The challenge is that current AI systems still struggle with noisy, unstructured documents. They can misread handwriting, miss hidden notes, or ignore context, just like a student who’s great at reading but forgets the overall story of a book. Building robust document intelligence means teaching machines to see, interpret, and reason about these messy pages as reliably as an experienced librarian.
