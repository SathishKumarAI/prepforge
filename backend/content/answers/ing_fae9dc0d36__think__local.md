**Metacognitive Blueprint for Crafting an Elevator‑Pitch‑style Response to “Tell Me About a Time You Demonstrated Leadership” (or similar behavioral interview questions)**  

1. **Clarify the Question & Identify the Core Competency**  
   - *What is being asked?* A concrete instance that showcases leadership (or another trait).  
   - *Assumptions:* The interviewer wants evidence, not a generic definition; they expect a brief yet complete story.

2. **Select a Structured Mental Model**  
   - Use the **STAR** framework: Situation, Task, Action, Result.  
   - Treat each component as a “stop” in an elevator ride—quick stops that build the whole journey.

3. **Step‑by‑Step Reasoning**  
   - **Situation:** Pick a scenario with clear stakes (e.g., project deadline, team conflict).  
   - **Task:** Define your specific responsibility or goal within that context.  
   - **Action:** Detail 2–3 concrete actions you took, emphasizing decision points and leadership behaviors.  
   - **Result:** Quantify impact (time saved, revenue gained, morale improved) and tie back to the original objective.

4. **Avoid Common Pitfalls**  
   - Don’t let the story drift into irrelevant details—keep each “stop” concise.  
   - Avoid passive voice; use active verbs (“I spearheaded…”) to reinforce ownership.  
   - Resist the urge to brag; focus on impact, not ego.

5. **Sanity‑Check & Communicate**  
   - Run a quick mental test: “If I had 30 seconds, could I convey this story?”  
   - Practice aloud, ensuring natural flow and that each element links logically.  
   - End with a brief reflection on what you learned or how it shaped your leadership style.
