Think of an ordinary computer program like a well‑trained chef who follows a recipe: you hand it the ingredients (data), and it produces a dish (output). To evaluate this chef, you taste the final meal—if it’s good, the recipe works.

A reasoning model is more like a detective solving a mystery. The “ingredients” are clues, but the detective must decide which clue to investigate next, how they connect, and what conclusions follow. Evaluating such a model means watching its investigative steps: does it ask the right questions? Does it link clues correctly? You’re not just tasting the final verdict; you’re following the reasoning trail, checking that each inference is sound.

So, while a standard model’s quality is judged by its end result, a reasoning model must be scrutinized at every logical step to ensure its thinking process is trustworthy.
