Think of a new AI model like a student taking a standardized test that everyone else has taken before.  
The **public benchmark** is the exam paper— it gives the same questions (tasks) to every model so we can compare scores.  
But just because a student gets a high score on that one test doesn’t mean they’ll ace all real‑world exams. The benchmark only shows how well the AI does on those specific, pre‑written problems; it can miss quirks like bias, safety, or how the model behaves when it encounters something totally new.

**Evaluation** is the grade we read from the paper— a single number that says “good” or “bad.”  
**Observability** is watching the student in class: seeing how they solve each problem, whether they use tricks, and if they get stuck on unexpected questions.  

So, benchmarks give us an easy comparison, but without deeper observation we might miss hidden strengths or weaknesses of the AI.
