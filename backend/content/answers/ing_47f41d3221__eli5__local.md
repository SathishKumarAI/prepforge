Imagine a gigantic digital pantry that stores every kind of food you might need—no matter how big or oddly shaped it is. That pantry is like an **object store**: it keeps data as “objects” (files) and labels each with a unique address so anyone can grab it from anywhere, anytime.

Here are the six most common reasons people use these pantries in cloud‑native apps:

1. **Backing up and archiving** – just drop your old photos or logs into the pantry; they’re safely kept for years.
2. **Serving static web content** – host images, videos, or HTML files that everyone can fetch instantly.
3. **Data lake storage** – collect raw data from sensors, apps, or logs so analytics tools can later sift through it.
4. **Disaster recovery** – keep a copy of critical databases in the pantry; if your main server crashes, you pull the backup fast.
5. **Content distribution** – feed popular media to CDNs that cache it close to users for quick delivery.
6. **Application container images** – store Docker or OCI images so new servers can download them on demand.

Just like a pantry lets you find any food item by its label, an object store gives developers a reliable, scalable place to stash and retrieve data across the cloud.
