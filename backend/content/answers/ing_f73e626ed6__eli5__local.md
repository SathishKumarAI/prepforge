Think of writing a recipe book. **Prompt‑engineering** is like hand‑picking one fancy dish from the cookbook, tweaking its name and ingredients until a kitchen robot can cook it exactly as you want. It’s very precise but brittle—if any word changes, the robot might misinterpret the dish.

**DSPy (Data‑Structured Prompting)** flips that idea. Imagine instead of forcing the robot to follow one fixed recipe, you give it a *set of simple instructions* and let it assemble the meal from its own pantry. You tell the robot: “use these ingredients, follow this cooking style, and finish with a garnish.” The robot then decides how best to combine them. It’s less about locking in every detail and more about guiding overall structure.

So DSPy is “anti‑prompt engineering” because it removes the need for micromanaging each word; it gives high‑level rules that let the AI flexibly craft the final output.
