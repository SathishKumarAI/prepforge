**Agent loops are the brain‑cycles that let an AI “think, act, learn.”**  
Imagine a smart home robot that keeps trying to keep your living room tidy. Each time it checks (observe), decides what to do next (plan), moves around (act), and then sees how well it did (learn). That’s one loop.  

Neo Kim and Logan Thorneloe break this idea into **seven loops**—tiny, specialized versions of that cycle that tackle different tasks: sensing the world, choosing actions, updating beliefs, sharing knowledge with other agents, reflecting on mistakes, planning long‑term goals, and finally executing a plan.  
Think of them as seven “mini‑brain sessions” inside one robot, each focusing on a specific part of getting from raw data to smart behavior. By running all seven together, the AI stays flexible, improves over time, and can collaborate with other AIs—just like a well‑coordinated team of helpful robots keeping your house spotless.
