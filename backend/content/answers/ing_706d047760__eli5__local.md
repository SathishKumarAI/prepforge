**What is a “synchronization service” in the context of cloud‑based docs?**  
Think of it as an invisible librarian who keeps every copy of a book (your document) the same no matter where you are reading or editing.

* When you open a file on your laptop, the librarian downloads the latest version from the library’s central shelf.  
* If you make changes while offline, the librarian notes those edits and saves them locally.  
* Once you’re back online, the librarian compares the local copy with the current shelf copy, merges any differences (like combining two people’s edits), and updates the shelf so everyone else sees your work.

Dropbox, Google Docs, and similar services run this librarian in the background, so collaborators never see conflicting copies—they always share a single, up‑to‑date document.
