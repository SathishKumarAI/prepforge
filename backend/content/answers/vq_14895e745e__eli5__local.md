Think of a **schema** as the recipe card for a cookie batch.  
The card lists every ingredient (data field), how much of each is needed, and where it goes in the dough (its position in the data structure). It tells you whether a cookie needs sugar, flour, or chocolate chips, and whether they’re optional or required.  

Just as a recipe keeps bakers consistent so everyone ends up with the same cookies, a schema keeps programmers and databases consistent: it defines what data can exist, its type (e.g., integer, string), and where it lives. If someone tries to add a “butter” field that isn’t on the card, the system will flag it.  

So, a schema is the blueprint or contract that says, “This is how our data should look.”
