**Portfolio moves – what they mean for an AI Product Engineer**

At its core, a *portfolio move* is a deliberate re‑allocation of time and effort across projects so that the sum of expected value exceeds the sum of individual costs. 1. **Problem framing**  
   Every engineering task can be seen as a Bayesian update: you start with a prior belief about what customers want, then you build a model (or product) that generates evidence to shift that belief. A portfolio move is choosing which evidence streams will most efficiently tighten your posterior over the true utility function.

2. **Why it must work this way**  
   - *Diminishing returns*: Early projects give high signal‑to‑noise; later ones add less marginal value.

   - *Skill synergies*: Learning a new deep‑learning framework on one project accelerates deployment speed on the next.

3. **Connection to deeper principles**  
   The strategy is mathematically analogous to *portfolio theory* in finance, where you maximize expected utility under uncertainty while controlling variance. Here the “assets” are code bases, datasets, and research papers; the “risk” is technical debt or misaligned product-market fit. Information‑theoretic bounds (e.g., mutual information between user behavior and model predictions) guide which projects reduce uncertainty most efficiently.
