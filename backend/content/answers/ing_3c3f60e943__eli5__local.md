Imagine your computer is a tiny town and each program that runs on it is like a shopkeeper who does a specific job—one sells coffee (a web browser), another stores groceries (a file manager). A **local agent** is a shopkeeper you invite to live in your town, not traveling elsewhere. It stays on your machine (“self‑hosted”), so only you can see and control it. Think of it as a personal assistant who keeps all its notes right next to you, never sending them out to the cloud.

Because the agent lives locally, you decide what data it reads, writes, or talks about. You can update its software yourself, shut it down when you’re done, and keep your secrets private. This is in contrast to a “cloud agent” that would live on someone else’s servers and might share information with others.

So, a local agent is just a program that stays on your computer, giving you full ownership of its actions and data.
