**Why take‑home assignments appear in AI interviews**

At the core, an interview must expose a candidate’s *latent* skill set—how they frame problems, trade off models, and communicate solutions—without the noise of a live demo. A take‑home task turns the interview into a mini‑research project: the candidate writes code, documents assumptions, and justifies choices under realistic constraints (time limits, incomplete data).  

**Key principles it tests**

| Principle | What it reveals |
|-----------|-----------------|
| **Problem‑solving architecture** | Does the candidate decompose a complex AI pipeline into modular stages? |
| **Optimization mindset** | Are they aware of bias–variance trade‑offs and regularisation? |
| **Probabilistic reasoning** | Do they justify model selection with likelihood or Bayesian arguments? |
| **Communication & reproducibility** | Is the notebook self‑contained, with clear visualisations and a logical narrative? |

The assignment also mirrors real work: you must balance speed and rigor, handle noisy data, and deliver a *product* rather than just an answer.

**Non‑obvious insight**

Many interviewers overlook the **feedback loop** embedded in take‑homes. A good candidate not only solves the problem but also *documents* limitations and proposes follow‑up experiments—exactly how you would iterate on a research paper. This meta‑skill, often hidden behind code quality, is what separates a competent engineer from an innovative AI scientist.
