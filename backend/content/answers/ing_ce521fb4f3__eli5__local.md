**Machine Learning in a Nutshell**

Think of a robot that learns to recognize cats by looking at pictures. At first it has no idea what a cat looks like, so it guesses randomly. Every time it sees a new photo, the robot checks: “Did I get this right?” If not, it adjusts its internal settings a little—just like you might change how you draw a shape when your teacher points out an error. Over many pictures, those tiny adjustments accumulate, and soon the robot can spot cats almost instantly.

In machine learning, that “internal setting” is called a *model*. The robot’s *data* are the pictures, and the *algorithm* is the rule that tells it how to tweak its model after each guess. The goal? A model that makes accurate predictions on new, unseen data—just like your cat‑detecting robot finally knows what a cat looks like without being told again.
