Think of the Amazon interview loop as a **round‑table cooking contest**.  
First, you’re invited to a kitchen (the “loop”) where several chefs—each an interviewer—will taste your dish in different ways.

1. **Preparation (your résumé)** – The hiring manager tells you what kind of recipe they need: data‑driven decisions, scalable models, or quick prototyping.  
2. **The cooking rounds** – You sit at a station and solve a problem on a whiteboard or code live. One chef asks about how you’d design an AI system; another focuses on your coding style; the next tests your ability to debug under pressure. Each round is short (≈15‑20 min) so everyone gets a slice of your skills.  
3. **The tasting panel** – After all chefs have sampled your work, they discuss among themselves. They compare notes: “Did you follow the recipe? Did you handle edge cases?” This gives them a balanced view.  
4. **Feedback & decision** – The panel meets again (often online) to decide whether you’re ready for the next round—maybe another loop or an offer.

In this way, Amazon ensures every candidate is evaluated from multiple angles—just like chefs judging a dish from taste, presentation, and technique—so the best fit emerges.
