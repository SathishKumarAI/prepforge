Think of a peer‑to‑peer (P2P) network like a neighborhood potluck where every house brings and takes food directly from each other—no central kitchen or menu.  
**Features:**  
* Every participant is both a *client* (requesting files) and a *server* (providing files).  
* Files are split into pieces, so many neighbors can send different parts at once.  
* The network discovers new hosts automatically, like people shouting “I have extra pizza!”  

**Pros:**  
* No single point of failure—if one house leaves, the potluck keeps going.  
* Fast downloads because multiple houses serve simultaneously.  
* Easy to start; no expensive infrastructure needed.  

**Cons:**  
* Security risks: bad neighbors can slip in harmful “food.”  
* Legal headaches if copyrighted items are shared.  
* Bandwidth hogging—your home’s internet can slow down when many people download at once.  

In short, P2P is a democratic, resilient way to share files, but it requires trust and careful oversight to stay safe and fair.
