Imagine you’re a librarian who can help people find books, photos, or videos that match what they’re looking for.  
**Cross‑modal retrieval** is like asking the librarian to pull up a photo when someone says “show me pictures of the Eiffel Tower,” even though the library catalog only lists books by title and author. The system learns how text and images are related so it can fetch the right image from a vast collection using just words, or vice versa.

**Multimodal Retrieval‑Augmented Generation (RAG)** takes this further: when you ask a question (“What does the Eiffel Tower look like at night?”), the librarian first searches the photo archive for relevant images, then stitches those pictures into a concise written answer. The AI combines text generation with retrieved visual evidence, giving richer, more trustworthy responses.

Both rely on learning a common language between modalities—text and image—so each can help explain the other.
