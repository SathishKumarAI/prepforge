Think of YouTube as a giant library that never runs out of books.  
When you upload a video, the system first makes a *master copy*—the full‑length film in one place. Then it creates many smaller “chapters” (called **segments**). Each segment is like a short chapter of the book; it’s stored on its own shelf so viewers can pick the part they want to watch right away, instead of waiting for the whole book to arrive.

The thumbnail—just a single picture that represents the video—is like the cover page. It lives in a fast‑access area so when someone scrolls through thousands of videos, the tiny image pops up instantly without having to fetch the entire film.

So: master copy = full file; segments = quick‑grab chapters on shelves; thumbnail = cover page that loads first. All are stored separately but linked together so you can stream a video smoothly and see its picture right away.
