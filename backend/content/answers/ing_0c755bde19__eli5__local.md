Think of AI regulation like two kinds of road signs on a busy highway.

**EU AI Act – “Speed limit” sign**  
It tells every driver (AI system) how fast it can go (how risky it is allowed to be). The EU writes a clear rulebook: if your AI is “high‑risk,” you must test it, document its logic, and get official approval before you hit the road. This matters when an organization sells or deploys AI in the European market; the law decides whether it can operate at all.

**NIST AI RMF – “Road‑maintenance checklist”**  
Instead of setting a speed limit, NIST gives a set of guidelines for checking how safe your vehicle is while you’re driving. It helps companies assess and manage risk, choose appropriate controls, and report incidents. It’s useful anywhere—inside a company or across borders—to keep AI running safely, but it isn’t a legal requirement in the EU.

So: EU AI Act sets mandatory limits; NIST RMF offers a practical toolkit for continuous safety management wherever you operate.
