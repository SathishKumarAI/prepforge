Think of a website as a busy restaurant.  
A **proxy** is like a waiter who sits in front of the kitchen, taking orders from diners and delivering their food back. The diner knows only the waiter; the kitchen stays hidden.

A **reverse proxy** flips that role. It’s the front‑door host who greets every guest, decides which kitchen station (server) should handle each order, and then hands the finished dish back to the guest. The diners never see the individual kitchens—they just talk to one friendly face.

So, a reverse proxy is a server that receives client requests, forwards them to one or more internal servers, and returns the responses—all while keeping the backend layout invisible. This keeps things organized, improves security, and can balance traffic like a smart host distributes orders efficiently.
