Think of an AI agent as a chef who can cook, remember recipes, and learn new tricks.

**Semantic memory** is the cookbook—facts about ingredients (e.g., “tomato is a fruit”) and how dishes fit together. It’s general knowledge that isn’t tied to any particular kitchen visit.

**Episodic memory** is the diary of each cooking session: “Last Tuesday I baked lasagna in my grandma’s oven, it was too salty.” It stores specific events with time and place, so the agent can recall what happened in a particular situation.

**Procedural memory** is the muscle‑memory for stirring, chopping, or flipping a pancake. Once learned, the agent performs these actions automatically without having to think about each step.

So, semantic = “what”; episodic = “when/where”; procedural = “how.” All three let an AI act intelligently in the world.
