**Concept Explanation Framework (ETA)** – *Explain‑Teach‑Ask*  

| Step | What it is | Why it matters |
|------|------------|----------------|
| **E – Explain** | Present the core idea in plain language, grounding it with familiar analogies or visuals. | Removes jargon and sets a shared mental model for the listener. |
| **T – Teach** | Dive into the mechanics: definitions, relationships, equations, or architecture. Use diagrams, code snippets, or step‑by‑step reasoning to show *how* the concept works. | Builds depth; demonstrates mastery and the ability to translate theory into practice. |
| **A – Ask** | Pose clarifying questions or thought experiments that probe the listener’s understanding (e.g., “What would happen if…?”). Offer a quick recap or summary for retention. | Encourages active learning, reveals misconceptions, and signals that you value feedback. |

### Why ETA works in interviews
- **Clarity & Structure** – Interviewers can follow your flow from intuition to detail to check comprehension.
- **Depth & Breadth** – You cover both high‑level intuition and low‑level implementation without getting lost.
- **Engagement** – Asking questions turns a monologue into a dialogue, showing communication skill.

### Quick example (GPT‑4)
1. **Explain:** “GPT‑4 is a transformer that predicts the next word by attending to all prior tokens.”
2. **Teach:** Show self‑attention equations, positional encoding, layer stack, and training objective (cross‑entropy on massive corpora).
3. **Ask:** “If we replace the softmax with a sparsemax, how would inference change?” – invites discussion of trade‑offs.

Use ETA to structure any technical explanation—whether you’re talking about neural nets, distributed systems, or algorithms—and you’ll hit the signal points FAANG interviewers love: clarity, depth, and communication.
