Imagine a classroom where every student follows the same routine: they sit at their desks, take notes, and raise their hands when asked. The teacher’s job is simple—spot any student who suddenly stands up, walks out, or starts speaking without permission. That unusual behavior is an **anomaly**.

In machine learning, anomaly detection works like that attentive teacher. We first define what “normal” looks like by training algorithms on a large set of regular data (the routine). The algorithm learns the typical patterns—like the usual shapes, colors, or numbers in our dataset. When it encounters something that doesn’t fit those learned patterns—an outlier—it flags it as an anomaly.

**Applications:**  
- **Fraud detection**: spotting unusual credit‑card transactions.  
- **Network security**: catching strange traffic spikes.  
- **Healthcare**: detecting abnormal heart rhythms.  

So, just as the teacher watches for a student who deviates from class norms, ML algorithms watch data for any deviations that might signal something important or dangerous.
