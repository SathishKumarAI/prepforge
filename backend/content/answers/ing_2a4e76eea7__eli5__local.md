Think of an embedding as a secret translator that turns every word, image or idea into a tiny “address” on a giant map.  
On this map—called a *vector space*—each point is a short list of numbers (the vector). Words that mean similar things get mapped to nearby points; words that are opposite end up far apart.  

Imagine you’re in a city where every street name is written on a postcard. If “cat” and “kitten” share the same neighborhood, a robot can guess that they belong together without ever seeing the actual animals. When the robot learns new things, it only needs to adjust its coordinates slightly—just like moving a pin on a map—so the relationships stay intact.

In practice, AI models use these coordinates to compare meaning, cluster related items, and even generate new content by navigating the space. The key idea: embeddings turn complex concepts into simple numeric locations that machines can walk around and understand.
