**Step 3: Splitting the Data – Think of a recipe book**

Imagine you’re learning to cook a new dish. First, you gather all the recipes (your whole dataset). Then you divide them into three piles:

1. **Training pile** – The recipes you’ll practice with repeatedly. You use these to learn how the ingredients combine and how long to cook.
2. **Development (dev) pile** – A handful of new recipes you try after training. They let you tweak your technique, see what’s working, and adjust seasoning before the final test.
3. **Test pile** – The last set of recipes that you only use once, after all adjustments are done. This shows how well you truly know the dish without any prior practice on those particular recipes.

In AI, “splitting” means exactly this: we give a model a big batch of data (the training set) to learn patterns, then a separate group (dev) to fine‑tune and avoid overfitting, and finally an untouched group (test) to judge real performance. This keeps the evaluation honest—just like tasting a dish you’ve never seen before.
