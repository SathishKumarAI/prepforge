Imagine you’re training a robot to learn how to play chess, but instead of just watching one game, you let it practice against many different opponents and in countless board setups. **Trajectory benchmarks** are like that: they give an AI system (the “agent”) a long series of moves—its *trajectory*—to evaluate how well it learns from each step.

Think of the agent as a student who keeps a notebook. Every time the student makes a decision, we write down what happened and check if the next entry improves or worsens the outcome. By looking at many notebooks (trajectories) across many tasks, we can see whether the student is truly learning to act independently—**agentic**—or just copying patterns.

*Unavoidable terms*  
- **Agentic system**: a program that makes its own choices and learns from experience.  
- **Benchmark**: a set of tests used to measure performance.  

So, trajectory benchmarks let us see if an AI is genuinely getting better at acting on its own across many situations, just like testing a student’s learning by reviewing many different lesson notebooks.
