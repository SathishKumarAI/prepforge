**From the ground up**

The *core problem* both titles solve is turning data into actionable intelligence that a system can use autonomously.  
An **ML Engineer** starts at the *model* level: they formalize the objective as an optimization problem (e.g., minimize expected loss), design the feature space, select appropriate inductive biases, and engineer pipelines that repeatedly train, validate, and deploy those models while ensuring statistical reliability and fairness. Their work is largely mathematical—gradient descent, regularization, hyper‑parameter search—and they are judged by predictive performance metrics.

An **AI Engineer** takes a higher‑level view: the same model is one component of an end‑to‑end intelligent system that interacts with users or other subsystems. They must translate the ML output into decisions, manage uncertainty propagation, handle real‑time constraints, and embed safety, interpretability, and human‑feedback loops. Their optimisation expands from a single loss function to *system‑wide objectives* (throughput × accuracy × robustness), often using control theory or multi‑objective planning.

**Non‑obvious insight:**  
Most people conflate “AI” with “ML.” In practice, AI engineering is less about building models and more about *architecting the environment* in which those models operate—ensuring that the statistical guarantees of ML translate into reliable, ethical behavior at scale. This systemic perspective is what truly differentiates an AI Engineer from an ML Engineer.
