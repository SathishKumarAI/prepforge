Think of a multimodal model as a curious kid who can read books, watch videos, and listen to music all at once.  
When you show the child a picture of a dog and ask “What’s it doing?” it also hears a bark in the background and remembers that dogs usually wag their tails. The answer it gives—“A happy dog wagging its tail”—uses visual clues (the image), audio cues (the bark), and past knowledge (dogs wag).  

In technical terms, each type of data—images, sound, text—is called a **modality**. The model learns to translate every modality into a shared language (a vector space) so they can talk to each other. By combining these “languages,” the model answers questions that need more than one sense, just like our kid who blends sight, hearing, and memory to understand the world.
