Think of **machine learning** like teaching a child to recognize fruit.  
You show the child many pictures of apples, bananas, and oranges, telling each time what it is. The child watches the colors, shapes, and sizes—these are the *features*. After enough examples, the child can look at a new picture and guess the fruit without you saying it.  

In computing, we give a computer thousands of labeled data points (the “examples”) and let it learn patterns in the numbers that describe them. The program builds an internal model—a set of rules or weights—so when it sees fresh data, it can predict the label (fruit type).  
That’s machine learning: training a system on past examples so it can generalize to new, unseen ones.
