**Imagine your website is a bustling train station.**  
When the station’s doors open for passengers, they need to see the platform and ticket machines before any trains arrive. *DOMContentLoaded* (DCL) is that “doors open” moment: it tells the browser, “the main HTML skeleton of the page is ready.”  

Think of DCL as the train schedule board lighting up. Until that happens, visitors can’t start moving through the station (scrolling, clicking). If the board lights late, everyone’s confused and delays pile up—just like a slow DCL makes users feel sluggish.

Other key metrics are the next stops on this journey:  
- **First Contentful Paint** – when the first train arrives.  
- **Largest Contentful Paint** – the biggest train in sight.  
- **Cumulative Layout Shift** – trains shifting unexpectedly.  
- **Time to Interactive** – all doors, platforms, and trains working together.  

If DCL is late, every subsequent stop suffers. That’s why it’s one of the nine performance checkpoints no site can afford to ignore.
