Imagine you’re a chef asked to create a brand‑new restaurant on the spot. You have to pick the menu, design the kitchen layout, decide how food travels from fridge to plate, and keep guests happy—all while explaining every choice in clear terms. A system‑design interview is exactly that: you’re building a big software “restaurant” (a scalable service, database, API, etc.) with no blueprint, just a brief description of what it must do.

Why it feels hard?  
1. **Open‑ended scope** – like the chef’s menu, there are many valid solutions, and the interviewers want to see how you narrow options.  
2. **Depth & breadth** – you need both high‑level architecture (the building layout) and low‑level details (how data flows between rooms).  
3. **Communication pressure** – you must talk through your thought process as if teaching a new cook, not just write code.

How to prepare:  
- **Practice “blueprints”**: sketch common systems (messaging queues, caching layers, sharding) before an interview so the design feels natural.  
- **Use the “restaurant” map**: start with user stories → outline components → decide on trade‑offs → talk about scaling and failure handling.  
- **Explain your choices**: say why you pick a CDN over a custom cache, or why you use a relational DB for transactions—just like a chef explains ingredient sourcing.  
- **Mock interviews**: rehearse with peers, record yourself, and critique clarity.

With these steps, the interview becomes another kitchen that you’ve practiced running, turning nervousness into confidence.
