Imagine you’re watching a live sports game on your phone. **Streaming** is like the video feed that keeps sending tiny packets of data (each packet is a frame) so you can keep watching without waiting for the whole game to finish. **Latency** is how long it takes from the moment a player hits the ball until the corresponding frame appears on your screen.

In machine‑learning services, especially those that give real‑time answers (like voice assistants or live translation), the system must process input and send back results as quickly as possible. Low latency means the user sees the response almost instantly; high latency feels like a laggy conversation. Think of it as how fast your phone can decode the incoming video packets—faster decoding = smoother viewing, just as faster processing = snappier AI responses.
