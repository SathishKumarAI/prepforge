Imagine a large library where every book is written by an enormous number of authors—this is your LLM. When you ask it a question, it flips through all the books and pulls out passages that seem relevant. But if the librarian (the model) has only ever read the same old, generic books, its answers can feel vague or off‑target.

Instruction tuning is like giving that librarian a specialized guidebook: a set of clear, step‑by‑step instructions on how to answer different kinds of questions—whether you want a concise summary, a friendly explanation, or a formal report. The librarian learns to follow those steps, so the passages it selects are not just relevant but also formatted and phrased exactly as requested.
