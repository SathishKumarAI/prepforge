Think of Kafka as a super‑fast, endless library that keeps shelves open all day so anyone can drop in a book (data) or take one out instantly. In machine learning, this “library” lets you do three key things:

1. **Real‑time data pipelines** – Like a conveyor belt that hands fresh sensor readings straight to your model as they arrive, so predictions stay current.
2. **Event‑driven training** – Every click or transaction is a story; Kafka stores them in order, letting you replay the entire history to retrain models without re‑collecting data.
3. **Scalable communication between services** – Imagine different departments (data ingestion, feature store, inference service) chatting through a shared mailbox that never gets full, keeping everything decoupled and fault‑tolerant.

So Kafka is the backbone that turns raw streams into clean, timely feeds for learning systems, all while staying simple to add or remove components.
