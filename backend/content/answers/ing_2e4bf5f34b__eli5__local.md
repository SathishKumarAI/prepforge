Imagine you’re driving a new electric car on a long road trip.  
**Guardrail metrics** are like the safety sensors that warn you if you’re drifting off the lane or about to hit an obstacle. They give quick, concrete signals—“speed too high,” “brakes not engaged”—so you can stop before something bad happens.  

**Quality metrics** are more like a dashboard showing how well your trip is going overall: average fuel efficiency, battery health, and smoothness of the ride. They don’t alert you to immediate danger but tell you whether the car’s performance is good or needs improvement over time.

In AI systems, guardrails monitor real‑time safety signals (e.g., bias spikes, privacy breaches) to catch problems instantly, while quality metrics evaluate long‑term reliability and usefulness (accuracy, fairness, robustness). Together they let developers observe what’s happening now and judge how well the model is truly working.
