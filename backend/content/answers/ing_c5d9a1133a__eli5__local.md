Imagine your brain as a kitchen where you learn to cook new recipes from watching others. **Machine learning** is like that kitchen, but the “chef” (a computer) learns by tasting many dishes first, noting what flavors work together, and then making its own dish without someone telling it step‑by‑step.

In this process:

* **Data** are the ingredients you hand to the chef—photos, numbers, words.
* **Training** is when the chef repeatedly tastes (runs) the recipes, adjusting seasoning (the computer’s internal settings) until the flavor matches what you liked.
* **Model** is the chef’s notebook of rules that tells it how to combine ingredients next time.
* **Prediction** is when the chef serves a new dish—something it never saw before but can make because it learned patterns.
