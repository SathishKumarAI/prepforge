Imagine you’re sending a postcard from New York to someone in Tokyo. If the post office in New York had to route it all the way around the world, the card would arrive slowly and could even get lost. A **Content Delivery Network (CDN)** is like having thousands of mini‑post offices spread across the globe. When you ask for a webpage or video, the CDN hands your request to the nearest mini‑office instead of the original server far away. That office instantly sends back the files—images, videos, code—so they arrive faster and with less chance of hiccups. The “mini‑offices” are actually servers that cache copies of popular content, so each user gets a quick, reliable copy from close to them. This is why using a CDN makes websites feel snappy everywhere.
