Imagine your favorite photo album that you keep for years—every picture is neatly labeled, stored in a safe drawer, and only opened when you want to look back. **Data archiving** works the same way for machine‑learning data.  
When a model learns from lots of images or texts, we first keep all those files in a “live” database where they’re easy to tweak and re‑train. Once training is finished, the most valuable part of that collection—raw inputs, labels, and sometimes intermediate results—is moved into an **archive**: a long‑term storage space that is inexpensive, durable, and protected from accidental deletion.  
The archive isn’t used for day‑to‑day experiments; it’s there so you can revisit the exact data later, verify past results, or re‑train with new algorithms. Think of it as the attic of your data: rarely opened but always available when needed.
