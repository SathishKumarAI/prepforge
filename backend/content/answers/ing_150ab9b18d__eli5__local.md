Imagine you’re a chef who just learned how to bake bread in a small town bakery. Suddenly you’re invited to cook at a fancy restaurant that serves many different breads and pastries. Instead of starting from scratch, you take the skills you already have—kneading dough, timing the oven—and apply them to new recipes. That’s transfer learning.

In deep learning, a model is like your chef: it learns patterns (e.g., recognizing edges or faces) on one large dataset. When you want to solve a different but related problem—say spotting tumors in medical scans—you don’t need to train a brand‑new network from zero. You “transfer” the earlier layers’ knowledge and fine‑tune only the top layers for your new task. The result is faster training, fewer data needed, and often better performance.
