**Machine learning is like training a pet dog to do tricks.**

*What it does*: You give the dog (the computer) lots of examples—like showing pictures of cats and dogs—and tell it which ones are cats and which are dogs. Over time, the dog learns patterns in those pictures that help it guess correctly on new ones.

*How it works*: The dog’s brain is a tiny network of “neurons” that fire when certain features appear (e.g., fur texture or ears shape). Each neuron starts with random strengths (weights). Every time you show an example, the dog checks its answer, sees if it was right or wrong, and then nudges the weights just enough to improve next time. This gradual adjustment is called *training*.

*What you get*: After many rounds, the dog can recognize cats and dogs it has never seen before—just like a machine‑learning model can predict unseen data.
