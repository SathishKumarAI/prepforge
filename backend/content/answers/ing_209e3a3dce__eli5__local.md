**Measuring Reliability – like a weather‑proof umbrella for your app**

Think of a reliable software system as an umbrella that keeps you dry no matter what the storm throws at it. **Reliability** is the chance that the umbrella will hold up when you need it—its “failure rate” tells how often it breaks or leaks.

In system design, we measure reliability by watching the umbrella over time: we count every break (a failure) and note how long it lasted before breaking (the *time‑to‑failure*). We then calculate a **Mean Time Between Failures (MTBF)**—how many hours on average the umbrella stays dry. A high MTBF means the system is trustworthy; a low one signals it needs better materials or design.

So, reliability in engineering is simply: “How often does this thing work when we expect it to?” and we quantify that with observed failures over time.
