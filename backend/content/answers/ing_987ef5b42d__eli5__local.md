Think of a movie as a giant, detailed painting that you want to show on a wall that only fits a tiny sketch. Video encoding is the artist’s careful sketching: it takes every brushstroke (pixel and color) and compresses it into a smaller drawing that still looks like the original when you step back. In machine learning, those compressed sketches are what we actually feed into models because they’re fast to download, quick to process, and still carry enough visual information for tasks like object recognition or style transfer. If you skip encoding, your “painting” becomes a huge file—slow to load, memory‑hungry, and hard for the model to handle in real time. Encoding keeps the picture sharp while making it practical for both humans and algorithms to use.
