**Semantic search** is like asking a friend to find the best recipe for “quick dinner” in a huge cookbook that mixes up all kinds of dishes.  
Instead of looking only for the exact words “quick” and “dinner,” your friend thinks about what you really want: something fast, tasty, and easy to make. They read the whole book, notice that “pasta alfredo” and “chicken stir‑fry” both fit those ideas, and suggest them even though those phrases don’t contain both words exactly.  

The system does a similar thing. It turns every word into a point in a big map of meanings (a *vector space*). When you type a query, it finds the points that are close to your request’s meaning, not just identical text. That way, you get relevant results even if they don’t use the exact words you typed.
