Imagine you’re running a big dinner party.  
The **orchestration framework** is like the maître‑d’ who sits at the head table, telling each waiter when to bring courses, making sure the timing is perfect and no dish goes cold. It coordinates many separate “cooks” (services) so they all play together smoothly.

An **agent framework**, on the other hand, gives each waiter a little robot assistant that can decide for itself when to fetch a plate or refill water—based on its own observations and goals. The robots act independently but still follow overall party rules.

Choosing between them is like deciding whether you want one strict host (orchestration) or many semi‑autonomous helpers (agents). Pick orchestration if you need tight, predictable coordination of many services; pick agents when components must adapt on their own to changing conditions. Use a mix if the party has both steady courses and spontaneous requests.
