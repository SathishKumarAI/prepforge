**Why data misuse matters**

At its core, data is a *signal* that encodes patterns about people and systems. When we treat it as a commodity rather than an information‑theoretic resource, the signal becomes distorted: privacy leaks, bias amplification, and opaque decision‑making emerge. These distortions violate two foundational principles:

1. **Causality & accountability** – If models learn from corrupted data, their predictions become unreliable, making it impossible to trace responsibility for adverse outcomes.
2. **Information conservation** – Misuse often discards or misrepresents the true variance in the signal, leading to over‑confident decisions that erode public trust.

Thus, protecting data integrity is not a moral nicety; it is an optimization problem where the objective is *maximizing the fidelity of inference while minimizing harm*.
