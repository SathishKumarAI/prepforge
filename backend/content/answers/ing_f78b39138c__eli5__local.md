Imagine you’re at a giant library that holds every book on the internet.  
**DNS (Domain Name System)** is like the librarian who knows where each book (website) sits. When you type “shopify.com” into your browser, DNS looks up that name and tells your computer the exact address—like a street number—so it can fetch the right page.

A **domain name** is just a human‑friendly label (“shopify.com”).  
An **IP address** is the machine’s numeric location (e.g., 23.45.67.89).  

In traffic management, DNS helps decide which “library shelf” to use by routing visitors through different servers or data centers, balancing load and keeping the site fast and reliable—just as a librarian might direct readers to the nearest copy of a popular book.
