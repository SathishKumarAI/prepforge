Think of AI as a toolbox for solving problems, and each tool is best chosen by the shape of the job.

**Rule‑based systems** are like a *cookbook*. You write clear, step‑by‑step recipes (if‑then rules). They work great when the problem has well‑defined rules—like sorting invoices or answering simple FAQ. If you change a rule, the whole recipe changes.

**Machine learning models** are more like a *photographer with a smart camera*. The camera doesn’t know the exact instructions; it learns from many photos how to recognize faces or predict weather. Use this when data is abundant and patterns are hidden—spam filtering, image recognition, language translation.

**Hybrid systems** combine both: the photographer’s camera (ML) follows a recipe of “take a photo only if the light is right” (rules). This is handy for complex tasks where you need human‑like flexibility but still want some safety checks.

So choose rule‑based when rules are clear, ML when patterns hide in data, and hybrids when you need both structure and learning.
