Imagine you’re in a big kitchen that can cook any dish you want, but the oven itself is just a simple pot that needs someone to stir it. In AI, *tool agents* are like helpers who know how to use that pot (the “building tool”) and help you make the food (your final program).  

There are two main kinds:

1. **Direct‑use agents** – they grab the pot, pour in the ingredients, and follow a recipe you give them. Use these when your goal is clear and doesn’t need extra thinking: “Bake a chocolate cake with 200 g flour.”  
2. **Planning agents** – they first decide *what* to bake, then ask the pot‑knowing helper for the steps. Use these when the task is complex or you’re not sure how to reach the goal: “Make a dessert that tastes like vanilla but looks like chocolate.”  

So pick a direct agent for simple, well‑defined jobs; choose a planning agent when you need strategy before execution.
