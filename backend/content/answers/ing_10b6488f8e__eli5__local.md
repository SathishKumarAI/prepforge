Think of machine learning as teaching a child to recognize animals in pictures, but instead of telling the child “that’s a dog because it has fur and four legs,” we let the child look at thousands of labeled photos and learn the pattern themselves.

1. **Collect data** – give the child many photos that are already tagged (“dog”, “cat”, etc.).  
2. **Train** – show each photo to a simple computer brain called a *model*. The model guesses an animal, compares its guess with the true label, and adjusts tiny internal knobs (weights) so it will guess better next time. This back‑and‑forth is like a child correcting their own mistakes.  
3. **Validate** – test the model on fresh photos it hasn’t seen to see how well it has learned.  
4. **Deploy** – once confident, use the trained model to label new pictures automatically.

So, machine learning is just an automated version of trial‑and‑error learning: feed examples, let the computer adjust itself, and then ask it to solve similar problems on its own.
