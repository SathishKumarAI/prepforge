Imagine you’re building a Lego castle. The biggest pieces are the walls—each wall is a *parent* block that can hold smaller blocks inside it. Those smaller blocks (windows, doors, towers) are *children*. In AI, hierarchical chunking works the same way: data is first split into big chunks (the parents), then each of those is broken down into sub‑chunks (the children).  

Think of a parent chunk as a chapter in a book; the child chunks are the paragraphs that make up that chapter. The system learns to read the whole chapter before zooming into individual sentences, so it remembers context while still understanding details. This layered approach lets models handle long text or complex tasks without losing track of the big picture—just like you can see your castle’s outline even when you’re busy adding tiny Lego details inside each wall.
