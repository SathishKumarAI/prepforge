**Request Count** is like the “foot‑traffic” of a physical store, but for your website. Every time someone clicks a link, types an address, or refreshes a page, their browser sends a **request** to your server. The Request Count tallies all those visits over a period (day, week, etc.).  

Think of the web server as a busy post office: each request is a letter that needs sorting and delivery. If the count spikes, it means more people are trying to get in—just as a surge of customers can overwhelm a clerk. High Request Count signals you might need extra “staff” (more servers or better caching) to keep things moving smoothly.

Key terms:
- **Request**: a browser’s ask for a web page or resource.
- **Server**: the computer that delivers the requested content.

Tracking this metric tells you how many “customers” your site serves, helping you scale resources and spot traffic bursts before they cause slowdowns.
