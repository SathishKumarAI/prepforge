Think of a student learning history.  
If the teacher only gives a textbook, the student can answer what’s on the pages but might miss recent events or different viewpoints.  
Now imagine the teacher has a giant library at hand and, when asked a question, pulls out the most relevant books, highlights key passages, and lets the student weave those facts into an answer.  

That’s Retrieval‑Augmented Generation (RAG). In machine learning, the model is like the student: it knows general patterns from training but can’t remember every fact forever. A RAG system first “retrieves” fresh documents or data snippets from a large database (the library), then feeds those snippets into a language generator that writes an answer incorporating both its own knowledge and the retrieved facts.  

So, RAG gives models up‑to‑date information, reduces hallucinations, and lets them answer specific questions more accurately—just as a student with instant access to the right books can give richer, more reliable answers.
