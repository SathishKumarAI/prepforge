Imagine you’re at a bustling farmers’ market.

**Push (like Facebook’s news‑feed)**  
The vendors stand on one side, shouting out their newest produce every hour: “Fresh strawberries! Fresh strawberries!” The shoppers hear the announcements as soon as they arrive, even if they didn’t ask for them. In push mode, the platform sends you updates automatically—posts, stories, ads—without you actively seeking them.

**Pull (like Instagram’s Explore page)**  
Here the vendors display their goods on a table and let people walk by and decide what to look at. You only see something when you point your eye toward it or click “see more.” The platform shows content because you requested it, either by scrolling or searching.

So, **push** = the network *delivers* information to you; **pull** = you *request* or discover information yourself. Both keep you engaged but work in opposite directions—one pushes out, the other pulls in.
