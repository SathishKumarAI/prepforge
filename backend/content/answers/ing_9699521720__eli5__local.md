Imagine a CDN as a giant library that copies books and places them on shelves all over the world so readers can get their copy fast.  
When something goes wrong—say a book is missing or the shelf lights flicker—you need to find which copy, which shelf, and which courier caused the problem.  

**Debugging complexity** in this setting means you have many moving parts:  
- **Edge servers** (the shelves) spread worldwide; each one may be behaving slightly differently.  
- **Cache rules** decide whether a book should stay on a shelf or be fetched again.  
- **Routing decisions** pick the nearest shelf for each reader.

If an error appears, you can’t just look at one shelf; you must trace the request through dozens of servers and networks, often in different time zones. That’s why debugging a CDN feels like solving a mystery with many suspects—each step adds another layer of uncertainty.
