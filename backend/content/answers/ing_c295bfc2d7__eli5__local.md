Think of machine learning like teaching a child to recognize animals in pictures.  
You show the child many photos of dogs and cats, telling each time “This is a dog” or “This is a cat.” The child watches patterns—dogs have floppy ears, cats have whiskers—and starts forming rules in their brain. After enough examples, they can guess the animal in a new picture without you saying anything.

In machine learning, the computer is that child. We feed it lots of labeled data (images with names) and let an algorithm learn the patterns. Once trained, the model can predict labels for unseen data—just as the child guesses the animal correctly.
