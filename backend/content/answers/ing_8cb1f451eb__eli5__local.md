Think of AI research like a marathon race in a city where many runners (researchers) try to finish the course as fast as possible.  
**Benchmarks** are the official timing mats placed at key points along the route—standard checkpoints that every runner must hit so we can compare their speeds. They’re fixed problems or datasets that everyone solves, giving us a clear yardstick.  

A **leaderboard** is the scoreboard at the finish line, listing each runner’s time and ranking them from fastest to slowest. It shows who’s leading the field at any moment.  

When the race gets *saturated*, it means almost every runner has already hit the top spot on that leaderboard. New participants can’t easily climb higher because the best times are so close together, and minor improvements no longer change the rankings dramatically. In AI, saturation happens when many models reach similar scores on a benchmark, making it hard to show progress without new tasks or more challenging tests.
