Imagine a robot trying to guess which door in a maze leads to the exit. Each door’s chance of opening is like a **probability**—a number between 0 (never) and 1 (always).  
- **Random variable**: the robot’s “door choice” that can change each try.  
- **Distribution**: the pattern of chances for all doors, e.g., a bell‑shaped curve if most doors are similar.  
- **Expectation (mean)**: the average door number the robot would pick over many trials.  
- **Variance**: how wildly the chosen door numbers spread around that average.  
- **Conditional probability**: the chance a specific door opens *after* seeing another door open.  
- **Bayes’ theorem**: updating our belief about a door’s safety when we learn new clues.  
- **Independence**: two doors opening without affecting each other.  
- **Joint distribution**: combined chances of picking two doors together.  
- **Marginal probability**: the chance of one door regardless of the other.  
- **Sampling**: testing a few doors to guess the whole maze’s layout.

These ideas let machines learn from data by treating every observation as a little “door clue” and refining its guesses step by step.
