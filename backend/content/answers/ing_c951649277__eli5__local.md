Imagine you have a huge library of picture‑sized books, each book is actually a tiny “vector” – a list of numbers that captures the book’s content. A **vector database** stores all those vectors so you can quickly ask for the ones most similar to what you’re looking for.

Now, suppose you only want books about *summer travel*. Those are the **metadata**—extra tags like “topic: summer”, “length: short”, or “author: Jane”. **Metadata filtering** is like putting a sorting hat over the library that first picks out just the books with the right tags before it even starts comparing the numbers. This saves time and keeps results relevant, just as a librarian would only show you travel guides when you ask for them.
