Imagine you’re a chef who has spent years mastering how to cook a perfect stew from scratch: you learn the right mix of spices, the exact simmer time, and how to balance flavors. Now suppose someone gives you a pre‑made stock that already contains most of those essential flavors. You only need to add your own ingredients—perhaps a splash of lemon or a pinch of herbs—to create a new dish quickly and with great taste.

In deep learning, training a neural network from the beginning is like making stew from scratch; it takes lots of data and time. **Transfer learning** is the pre‑made stock: you take a model that was already trained on a massive dataset (like ImageNet for images) and “fine‑tune” it with your own smaller set of examples. The model keeps its learned patterns—edges, shapes, colors—and just adjusts the final layers to recognize your specific classes.

**Key terms**  
- *Model*: the computer program that learns patterns from data.  
- *Fine‑tuning*: lightly adjusting a pre‑trained model with new data so it fits a particular task.  

By reusing this “stock,” you get better results faster, just as a seasoned chef can whip up a gourmet meal in minutes instead of hours.
