**Think of a program as a recipe book.**

A **statement** is like a single instruction in that recipe—“mix the flour and sugar.” It tells the computer *what* to do and usually ends with a semicolon (;) so the next line knows it’s finished.

An **expression** is the ingredient list for that instruction—“flour + sugar” or “2 × 3.” It produces a value but doesn’t itself perform an action unless it sits inside a statement.  

So, in our recipe:  
- *Expression* = the part that gives you a number or text (the ingredients).  
- *Statement* = the full instruction that uses that expression to change something or produce output.

Just like you need both an ingredient list and a cooking step, programming needs expressions inside statements to work.
