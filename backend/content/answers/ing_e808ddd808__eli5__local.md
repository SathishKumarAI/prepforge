Think of an AI model like a chef in a busy kitchen.  
A *prompt* is the recipe you give the chef, and *context* is all the ingredients already on the counter.  
If the same recipe shows up again, the chef can keep that dish “in memory” (cached) so he doesn’t have to re‑read or re‑measure the ingredients each time. That saves him time—just like caching a prompt lets the model skip redundant calculations and run faster.

*Context engineering* is how you arrange those ingredients on the counter: grouping similar items together, labeling them clearly, and keeping the most useful ones within easy reach. Good arrangement reduces the chef’s effort to find what he needs, just as smart context layout lowers the model’s workload and cost.  

So, caching keeps repeated recipes ready; engineering the context arranges the ingredients so every run is quick and inexpensive.
