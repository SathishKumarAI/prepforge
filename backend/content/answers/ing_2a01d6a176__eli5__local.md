**The Core Philosophy: Grounding vs. Training**

Think of a robot learning to bake cookies. **Training** is like giving the robot thousands of cookie‑recipes and letting it guess which ingredients make the best cookie. It learns patterns from those recipes, but it never knows *why* sugar sweetens or flour thickens—it just mimics what it saw.

**Grounding**, on the other hand, is teaching the robot to understand the kitchen itself: that sugar dissolves in heat, that dough rises when yeast ferments, and that an oven’s temperature can be measured. Now the robot knows *why* a certain step matters, so if it encounters a new recipe or a missing ingredient, it can still figure out what to do.

In AI terms, training is pattern‑matching from data; grounding connects those patterns to real‑world concepts, giving the system a reasoned understanding rather than just imitation.
