Imagine you’re at a school science fair where every student has built a robot that can pick up different objects—some grab pencils, others lift blocks, and a few even sort shapes. The “Benchmark Problem” is like the judge’s test: the teacher gives each robot the exact same set of items to handle and asks them to score how well they do. In AI, we create a *benchmark*—a carefully chosen collection of tasks (text questions, images, games) that all models must try. We then measure their *capability*, meaning how accurately or efficiently they perform those tasks. The benchmark lets us compare different AIs objectively, just as the science‑fair judge compares robots on identical challenges. This is essential because it turns vague claims (“this AI is smart”) into concrete numbers we can trust and improve upon.
