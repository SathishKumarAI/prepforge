Imagine training a smart robot that learns to sort fruit by looking at pictures. At first the robot sees a banana, a red apple and a green apple, but it has no idea what makes them different. You give it a label: “banana” for the yellow one, “apple” for the red and green ones. The robot stores this information in a tiny brain called a **model** – think of it as a recipe book that tells it how to recognize each fruit.

When you later show the robot an unseen picture, it uses its recipe book to decide whether it’s a banana or an apple. If it gets it wrong, you tell it again and tweak the recipe slightly. Over many pictures (data) and tweaks (learning), the robot becomes very good at sorting fruit without being told every single rule.

That simple “learn from examples” process is what **machine learning** does for all kinds of problems—speech recognition, medical diagnosis, recommending movies, and more.
