Imagine a chef who learns how to cook by watching many recipes (the data) and then tries the dishes herself (makes predictions). In **supervised learning**, that chef is guided by an instructor who tells her exactly what each dish should taste like—“sweet,” “sour,” or “savory.” The chef uses those labels to tweak her recipe until she can guess the correct flavor for new, unseen ingredients.

**Regression** is like teaching the chef to predict a continuous score: “How many teaspoons of sugar?” The answer can be any number.  
**Classification** is like teaching the chef to pick one category: “Is this dish dessert or main course?” It’s a discrete choice.

The instructor (the labeled data) keeps giving feedback, so over time the chef becomes better at predicting new dishes accurately—just as a supervised model learns from examples and then makes reliable predictions on fresh inputs.
