Imagine you’re at a kitchen where several chefs (the “tool agents”) are each good at making a single dish—one makes soup, another makes pasta, another chops vegetables. Instead of sending the raw ingredients to every chef and then gathering all their dishes back to your table, the kitchen’s head chef (the server) takes all the ingredients, calls the right chefs in turn, collects their finished parts, and assembles a complete meal on one plate for you.

In AI, **server‑side composition** works the same way. A central system (the server) receives a user request, decides which specialized “tool agents” it needs—like a language model to draft text, another to translate, another to fetch data—and then orchestrates them: it sends each agent its part of the job, waits for their replies, and stitches those replies together into one polished answer. The user never sees or interacts with the individual tools; they just get the finished result from the server’s assembly line.
