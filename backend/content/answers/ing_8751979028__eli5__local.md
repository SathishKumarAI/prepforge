**High‑level architecture of a machine‑learning system**

Think of it as a *smart chef* who learns to cook from watching many recipes.

| Layer | What it does | Analogy |
|-------|--------------|---------|
| **Data layer** | Gathers the ingredients (images, numbers, text). | The pantry that holds all raw foods. |
| **Feature extraction** | Turns each ingredient into useful bits (e.g., “has a round shape” or “contains salt”). | The chef’s knife and cutting board – turning whole items into pieces that matter. |
| **Model layer** | A set of rules (a neural network) that learns how to combine the pieces to make decisions. | The recipe book the chef consults, gradually improving with practice. |
| **Training loop** | Shows the model many examples, checks its mistakes, and tweaks the rules. | The chef tasting each dish, noting what’s wrong, and adjusting seasoning. |
| **Inference layer** | Uses the learned rules to predict on new data. | The finished meal served to guests – no more cooking from scratch. |

Unavoidable terms:  
*Model*: a mathematical representation that maps inputs to outputs.  
*Training*: the process of feeding data to the model and adjusting it so predictions improve.  

In this way, a machine‑learning system moves from raw data to polished predictions just as a chef transforms pantry goods into delicious food.
