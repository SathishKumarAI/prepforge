Think of a big kitchen where many chefs cook different dishes at the same time.  
In **microservices**, each chef (service) is tiny, does only one job—like chopping onions or grilling steak—and talks to the others through clear, simple recipes (APIs).  

Now imagine the kitchen can sometimes experience sudden power flickers or a fire alarm that turns off lights. That’s **chaos engineering**: Netflix purposely introduces these hiccups to see if the chefs keep cooking and customers still get their meals.  
If one chef stops, another can step in, or the recipe book tells them to skip a step and use what they have on hand.  

So, “earlier is fodder for a whole wide” means that past small experiments (the earlier hiccups) feed into a broader strategy—**Mastering Chaos**—which lets Netflix keep its streaming service smooth even when parts of the kitchen go haywire.
