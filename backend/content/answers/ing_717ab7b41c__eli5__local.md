Imagine you’re writing a story and your librarian is an assistant who can pull up any book you need.

**Linear RAG (the “direct‑lookup” librarian)**  
When you ask a question, the librarian pulls one or two relevant books from the shelf, gives you the passages, and you write the answer using only those excerpts. It’s fast and straightforward, but if the books don’t cover every nuance, your story might miss details.

**Agentic RAG (the “creative assistant”)**  
Here the librarian not only fetches books but also asks follow‑up questions, compares multiple sources, and even suggests new angles before you write. The assistant acts like a co‑author: it gathers information, reasons about it, and helps shape a richer, more accurate narrative.

*Key terms:*  
- **RAG (Retrieval‑Augmented Generation)** – a system that fetches external facts to help a language model answer questions.  
- **Linear** – simple one‑step retrieval before generation.  
- **Agentic** – interactive, multi‑step retrieval with reasoning and decision‑making.  

So, linear RAG is like pulling a single reference; agentic RAG is like having an inquisitive research partner that keeps digging until the answer feels complete.
