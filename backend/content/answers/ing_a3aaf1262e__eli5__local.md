Think of a classroom where most students follow the same routine—handing in homework on time, arriving at class, staying seated. One day, a student suddenly starts walking around the room during lessons, talking loudly, or turning pages upside‑down. That unusual behavior is an **anomaly**.

In machine learning, we give a computer many examples of “normal” data (like the routine students). The algorithm learns what typical patterns look like and then watches new data for anything that doesn’t fit—just as a teacher notices the off‑track student.  

Anomaly detection algorithms can be simple rules (“if temperature > 100°F, flag it”) or more complex models that learn subtle differences (like spotting a faint smudge on a clean sheet). Once an anomaly is flagged, we investigate: maybe there’s a sensor fault, a cyberattack, or a medical condition.
