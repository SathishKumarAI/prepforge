**Step 4: On‑Site – The Core of the Evaluation**

When a candidate reaches the on‑site stage, OpenAI has already verified that the résumé, coding test and phone screening satisfy *the problem‑solving skeleton* (algorithms, systems design, domain knowledge). What remains is to confirm *how the candidate will collaborate, innovate, and align with the organization’s mission*.  

The interview panel typically comprises a senior engineer, a product lead, and a peer from the same team. Each member probes **three intertwined axes**:

1. **Technical depth** – can the candidate solve an open‑ended problem that mirrors real research? The question often involves deriving a probabilistic model or proving a convergence property under non‑standard assumptions, forcing the interviewee to justify every assumption and trade‑off.

2. **Communication & ownership** – can they articulate intuition, defend choices, and iterate on feedback? A good answer shows not only the *final* solution but also the design space that was explored, revealing their ability to navigate ambiguity.

3. **Alignment with OpenAI’s values** – do they understand safety, interpretability, and societal impact? This is assessed through scenario questions (e.g., “How would you mitigate a hallucination in a policy‑guided agent?”) rather than rote knowledge.

A non‑obvious insight: the *order* of questions matters. Beginning with a hard technical problem sets the tone; the interviewee’s calmness under pressure predicts how they’ll handle high‑stakes research, where failure modes can have global consequences. The on‑site is thus less about “getting it right” and more about demonstrating a *robust, principled approach* to complex, uncertain problems—exactly what AI research demands.
