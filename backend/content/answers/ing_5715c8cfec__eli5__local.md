Imagine you’re in a huge library where every book is a piece of information. When you ask a question, the “context” is like telling the librarian exactly what you’ve already read or what topic you’re interested in—maybe you just finished a chapter on climate change and now want to know about renewable energy.

The librarian (the retrieval system) uses that context to sift through millions of books quickly. Instead of looking at every page, they jump straight to sections that mention both “renewable” and “climate.” They also consider the style of your question: if you asked in a casual tone, they’ll pull out friendly explanations; if it’s technical, they’ll fetch detailed data.

So contextual retrieval is just a smart librarian who remembers what you’ve already looked at, understands how you’re asking, and pulls up only the most relevant passages—fast, focused, and tailored to your current “reading mood.”
