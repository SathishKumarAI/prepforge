Think of a neural network as a tiny city of “neuron‑houses.”  
Each house receives messages (numbers) from its neighbors, decides whether to shout back based on a simple rule (a weighted sum followed by a squashing function), and passes that shout along. The whole city learns by adjusting the strengths of the connections between houses—like tuning how loudly each neighbor talks—so that when you give it a picture of a cat, the city’s final answer is “cat.”  
The learning happens in two stages: first, you show many labeled examples so the city slowly changes its connection strengths; second, you test new pictures and let the city refine itself further. In short, a neural network is an interconnected group of simple decision‑makers that, through repeated practice, can recognize patterns without being explicitly told how to do it.
