Imagine a recipe book that tells you how to make the best soup ever. **Data science** is like being the chef who gathers ingredients (data), decides which ones taste good together (analysis), and then follows the recipe to create something useful—like a menu recommendation or a health alert.

First, you collect raw ingredients: numbers from sensors, words from tweets, clicks on a website. These are your **data**. Next, you clean them—removing spoiled bits (missing values) and chopping them into uniform pieces (normalization). Then you mix them with tools that find patterns, like a blender that blends flavors to create a new taste (machine‑learning models). Finally, you serve the dish: a clear report or a decision‑making system that helps people choose the best pasta sauce or predict tomorrow’s weather.
