Imagine hiring a gardener for your backyard. A gardener who always follows the same watering routine—checking soil moisture first, then using the right amount of water—will keep plants healthy even if the weather changes. If you only look at the gardener’s resume (the “what” they did) and not how they actually behaved on the job (the “how”), you might hire someone who can read gardening books but never adapts to real‑world conditions.

In hiring, companies care about *behavior* because it reveals a candidate’s problem‑solving style, teamwork, and resilience—qualities that predict future performance. Common behavioral interview questions ask for specific past examples (e.g., “Tell me about a time you met a tight deadline”) so the interviewer can see how you acted, not just what you claimed to know.

**Unavoidable terms:**
- *Behavioral interview*: A set of questions designed to learn how you handled real situations in the past.
- *Soft skills*: Personal attributes like communication and teamwork that affect job performance.
