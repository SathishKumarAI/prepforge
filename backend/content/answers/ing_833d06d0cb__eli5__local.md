Imagine you’re running a busy pizza shop that only has one oven. When customers order many pizzas at once, the single oven can’t keep up—orders pile and people wait too long.  
**Horizontal scaling (scale‑out)** is like opening more ovens: each new oven works on its own batch of pizzas, so the total output rises in proportion to how many ovens you add. The shop’s overall capacity grows without upgrading a single oven to a bigger size.

*Scalability* means the system can keep performing well as demand increases.  
*Horizontal scaling* is a common way to achieve it: instead of making one machine more powerful (vertical scaling), you add more machines that share the load, just like adding ovens to serve more customers quickly.
