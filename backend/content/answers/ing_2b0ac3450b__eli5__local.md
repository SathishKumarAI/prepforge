Think of a multimodal AI as a chef who can cook with three ingredients at once—text, images, and sound—just like a fusion dish that blends flavors from different cuisines. Interviewers want to see the chef handle each ingredient smoothly; otherwise the dish falls apart.

**Red flags** are moments when the “chef” gets stuck or mismanages one of the streams:

- **Missing data in one stream**: If the model can read text but ignores the image, it’s like a recipe that never uses the spice.  
- **Unbalanced weighting**: Giving too much importance to the audio while ignoring the picture makes the dish taste off‑balance.  
- **No cross‑talk**: When the modalities don’t talk to each other—text says one thing, the image another—it's like serving a salad with a sauce that doesn’t match.  
- **Overfitting to a single mode**: The model performs great on text-only tests but crashes when an image is added, as if the chef only practiced plating and not cooking.

In short, interviewers watch for any sign that the AI treats its multiple “ingredients” in isolation or neglects their interaction—because a truly multimodal system must blend them seamlessly.
