Think of a multimodal model like a super‑savvy librarian who can read books, look at pictures, and listen to music all at once.  
When you ask it “Show me a picture of a cat that looks happy,” the librarian first pulls up every photo in the library (the image part). Then it checks the captions or tags next to those photos for words like *cat* and *happy* (the text part). Finally, it blends the visual clues with the word clues so it can pick the right picture.  

A “model” is just a set of computer rules that learn from many examples how to link these different kinds of data. By training on both images and words together, the model learns that a cat’s whiskers or a bright yellow background often mean “happy.” That way, when you give it new pictures or new sentences, it can match them across senses—just like our librarian who knows what a word means in a picture and vice versa.
