Imagine a chef who has never cooked Italian food before. The first time she tries making pasta, she follows a recipe step by step—boil water, add salt, stir the sauce. After each attempt she notices what worked and what didn’t: maybe the sauce was too thick or the noodles were under‑cooked. She tweaks her actions, remembers those adjustments, and next time prepares the dish faster and better without looking at the recipe again.

It “learns from experience” by repeatedly trying actions in a simulated kitchen (the environment), observing the results (rewards or penalties), and updating its *procedural memory*—a set of rules it can apply automatically.
