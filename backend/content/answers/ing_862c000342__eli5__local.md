Imagine a robot that follows a pre‑written recipe to bake cookies. The recipe is the *known path*: every step, ingredient amount, oven temperature—nothing surprises it. In AI terms, an agent with a known path has a clear map of the world and a set of rules that tell it exactly what action to take in each situation. It doesn’t need to learn or guess; it simply follows its plan until the goal is reached.

Sairam Sundaresan and Neo Kim describe this “known‑path” approach as one where the agent’s environment and optimal actions are fully specified ahead of time—much like a seasoned chef who never needs to improvise. The advantage? Predictable, fast decisions; the drawback? Inflexibility when something unexpected happens. Thus, known‑path agents are great for controlled settings but struggle in chaotic, real‑world situations where learning is required.
