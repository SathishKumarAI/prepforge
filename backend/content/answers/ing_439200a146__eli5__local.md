**Perplexity is like guessing a secret word from a dictionary.**  
Imagine you have a book that contains all the sentences your friend might say, but you don’t know which one she will actually choose next. If every word in the book were equally likely, you would be very “perplexed” because any guess could be wrong; you’d need many tries to get it right.  
Perplexity measures exactly that: how surprised a language model is by the words it sees. A low perplexity means the model has a good sense of which word comes next—like having a smaller, more relevant dictionary—so its guesses are accurate. A high perplexity signals confusion, as if the model had to flip through many possible words before landing on the correct one.  
In short: **perplexity = the average number of “choices” the model must consider for each word; lower is better because it shows less surprise and more precision.**
