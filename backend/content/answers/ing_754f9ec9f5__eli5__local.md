Think of logging into an online service like opening a safe. The “credentials” are the keys you need, and there are four common types of keys that keep your data secure.

1. **Password** – the classic key. You choose a secret word or phrase (the password) that only you know. It’s simple but can be guessed if it’s weak.

2. **Two‑Factor Authentication (2FA)** – a second, separate key. After you give the password, you also need something you have on hand—like a text code sent to your phone or an authenticator app. Even if someone steals the password, they still can’t open the safe without the second key.

3. **Biometrics** – the unique lock that only your body fits. Fingerprint scanners, facial recognition, or voice recognition let you prove who you are by measuring something naturally yours. It’s hard for others to duplicate.

4. **Hardware Token (or Smart Card)** – a physical key that plugs into a computer or is carried on a card. The device stores a secret code that the safe checks; it’s like having a master key that only works when physically present, adding extra protection against remote theft.

Just as a safe needs the right keys to stay secure, each authentication mechanism adds layers of confidence that you’re truly who you claim to be.
