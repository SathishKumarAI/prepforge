Think of data architecture as the blueprint for a city’s roads, bridges, and utilities—only it’s for information instead of cars.  
Imagine you’re building a smart city where every house (data source) sends traffic reports to a central control center (the analytics platform). The data architecture tells each house how to package its report (format, quality), which road (pipeline) to use to reach the center, and what guardrails (security rules) keep the flow safe. It also maps out where all the traffic is stored—temporary garages (databases) for quick access and permanent museums (data lakes) for long‑term archives.  
In IBM’s view, a data architecture is this whole system of “roads,” “storage,” and “rules” that lets dashboards and analytics tools read, combine, and display information reliably. It’s the invisible scaffolding that keeps your insights moving smoothly from raw numbers to clear visuals.
