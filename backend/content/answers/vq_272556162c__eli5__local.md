Think of an LLM like a very eager chef who can cook almost anything you ask.  
You give the chef a recipe (your first prompt), and it produces a dish.  
If the dish isn’t exactly what you wanted—maybe too salty or missing spice—you don’t throw it away; instead, you taste it and tell the chef, “Add more basil, cut back on salt.” That second instruction is an *iterative prompt*.  

Each new prompt refines the previous result: you point out what worked, what didn’t, and guide the model toward the flavor (or answer) you truly need. The process is like a conversation with a cooking partner who learns from your feedback; the more iterations, the closer the final output matches your vision.

*Iterative prompting*: repeatedly feeding a model updated instructions based on earlier outputs to improve accuracy or style. *LLM (Large Language Model)*: a computer program that generates text by predicting what comes next in a sentence.
