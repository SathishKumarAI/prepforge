Imagine you’re building a smart helper robot that can fetch items from a pantry and bring them to the kitchen. To know if it’s doing a good job, you’d look at two things: **accuracy** (did it grab the right item?) and **speed** (how fast did it get there?). In AI “tool agents,” the same idea applies.

*Accuracy* is called **task success rate** – the fraction of times the agent finishes the requested job correctly.  
*Speed* is measured by **time‑to‑completion** – how many steps or seconds it takes to finish a task.  

Think of a delivery drone: if it drops the package in the right spot (high accuracy) and lands quickly (low time), it’s a well‑built agent. These two metrics let developers compare different agents and improve them, just like tweaking a robot’s sensors and wheels makes your pantry helper faster and more reliable.
