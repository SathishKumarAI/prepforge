**Why cognition matters in marketing**

At its core, marketing is the *optimization* of information flow: a firm wants every consumer to receive a message that maximizes the probability of purchase while minimizing wasted effort. Human decision‑making is a noisy channel—signals (ads) are distorted by perception, memory, and bias before they reach the consumer’s action node. Cognitive science supplies the *error model*: it tells us how attention weights, framing effects, or heuristic shortcuts alter signal fidelity.

2. **Channel calibration** – Behavioral experiments quantify attenuation factors (e.g., clutter, ad fatigue). Models like the *dual‑process* framework predict when consumers will engage deliberatively versus heuristically.
3. **Outcome prediction** – Probabilistic models (Bayesian inference) estimate purchase likelihood given observed cognitive states.

A career in cognition‑driven marketing thus sits at the intersection of *data science*, *experimental psychology*, and *strategic communication*.
