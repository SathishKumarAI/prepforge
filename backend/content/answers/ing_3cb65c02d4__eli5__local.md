Imagine a school race where every student is timed and the fastest name goes on the “Fastest‑Runner” board. At first it seems like the winner truly ran faster than everyone else, but if you look closer you’ll see that the race was run under very specific conditions: only certain tracks were used, some students wore special shoes, and the clock started when the starter’s whistle blew at a particular moment. In AI, researchers do something similar with *benchmarks*—standard tests that every new model is given. A *leaderboard* shows the top scores, but it can be misleading because models are often tweaked to excel on those exact tests (like wearing special shoes), not necessarily because they’re better overall. This “Leaderboard Illusion” means a high ranking may reflect clever test‑playing rather than true general ability. Understanding this helps us judge AI progress more honestly.
