**High availability** means that a computer system stays up and keeps working even when something goes wrong—like a backup generator for your home electricity.

Imagine you’re baking a cake (the service) in a kitchen that’s always ready. If the stove suddenly stops, you immediately switch to a second stove that was already on standby. The batter never stops cooking; customers keep getting slices without noticing any hiccup. In IT, we set up “redundant” parts—extra servers, duplicate data copies, and automatic switches—so if one component fails, another takes over instantly.

**Unavoidable terms explained:**
- *Server* – a powerful computer that runs software for many users.
- *Redundant* – having an extra copy of something to use when the first one fails.
- *Failover* – the automatic switch from a failed part to its backup.
