**Hybrid Search: The “Best of Both Worlds” in a Head‑to‑Head Match**

Think of a library that has two ways to find books:  
1. A *catalog* (like Google’s keyword search) that lists every book by title, author, or any word you type.  
2. An *expert librarian* who reads the whole book and tells you if it really covers what you need.

Hybrid search lets a computer do both at once. First, it does the quick catalog scan to grab a handful of promising books. Then, just like the librarian, it opens each one and looks deeply for the exact facts or tone you asked for. In a head‑to‑head comparison, we run the same query on two systems—one pure keyword, one hybrid—and see which gives faster, more accurate answers. The hybrid system often wins because it narrows the field quickly but still checks details thoroughly.
