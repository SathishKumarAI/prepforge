Think of a machine‑learning model as a recipe book.  
When you train it, the “ingredients” (data) are mixed and the chef (algorithm) adjusts how much of each spice to use so that the dish tastes right for every test order. The final amounts of each spice—called *parameters*—are written down in the book.  

Those parameters are all the model keeps after training: a compact list of numbers that encode what it learned from the data. Later, when you give the model new ingredients (a new input), it follows the recipe by plugging those numbers into a simple formula to produce an answer.

So, a trained model “keeps” its knowledge in these parameter values—just as a chef keeps a recipe for future meals.
