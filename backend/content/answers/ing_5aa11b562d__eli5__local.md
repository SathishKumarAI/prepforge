Imagine you’re sending postcards to friends all over the world. If every postcard had to travel from your hometown library to each friend’s house, it would take a long time and the roads could get jammed. A Content Delivery Network (CDN) is like a chain of mini‑libraries placed in many cities. When someone requests a webpage, the CDN routes that request to the nearest mini‑library instead of the original one. This “closest‑server” routing cuts travel distance, reduces traffic jams, and lets people get the page faster. CDNs also cache popular pages so they’re ready to deliver on demand, much like having copies of your favorite postcards right next door. That speed, reliability, and reduced load on the original server make CDNs extremely popular for delivering web content worldwide.
