Think of a city’s public transport system. Each bus, train or tram is a **service** that offers a specific trip: “go from downtown to the airport” or “ride between two school zones.” The city planners don’t build all the roads themselves; they just make sure every vehicle can use the same streets and stop at common hubs. That way, if one route needs an extra seat or a new track, you can add it without redesigning the whole network.

In **Service‑Oriented Architecture (SOA)**, software is built in the same way. A *service* is a self‑contained program that performs one job—like calculating a user’s age from their birth date or charging a credit card. All services talk to each other through well‑defined interfaces, just as buses and trains share roads and stations. This lets developers mix, replace, or update individual services without touching the others, making large systems easier to grow and maintain.
