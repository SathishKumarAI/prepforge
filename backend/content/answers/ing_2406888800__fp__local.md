**Prompt engineering and context in AI interviews**

At its core, a language model is a probabilistic function \(P(x\mid C)\) that predicts the next token given a *context* \(C\). Interviewers ask about prompt engineering to probe whether you understand how to shape this probability distribution. The fundamental problem is *information bottleneck*: the prompt must encode enough constraints (semantic, syntactic, pragmatic) so that the model’s posterior concentrates on the desired answer while remaining computationally tractable.

From an optimization view, the prompt acts as a regularizer on \(C\). A well‑crafted prompt reduces variance in the output distribution and improves gradient flow during fine‑tuning. In information terms, it maximizes mutual information between the intended task and the model’s response: \(I(\text{task}; \hat{x}\mid C)\). Geometry enters via embedding space; prompts steer the hidden representation toward a manifold where the target class lies.

**Non‑obvious insight:**  
Most people treat context as “just the text before the question.” In reality, *the position of tokens* matters because transformer attention is relative. A prompt that repeats key constraints at the end of the sequence (where self‑attention has higher weight) often yields more faithful responses than one placed only at the beginning—counter to intuition but rooted in the self‑attentional bias toward recent tokens.

Thus, good interview answers demonstrate mastery of this trade‑off: how prompts compress task knowledge into a concise context that aligns the model’s probability mass with the desired output.
