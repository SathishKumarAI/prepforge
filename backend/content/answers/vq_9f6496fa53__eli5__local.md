Think of data engineering as a city’s water‑management system.  
**Big data** is the massive, fast‑flowing river that feeds the city—so large and swift that ordinary pipes (small databases) can’t keep up.

The **5 V’s** describe this river:

| V | What it means | Analogy in the river |
|---|---------------|----------------------|
| Volume | How much water comes in. | The sheer width of the river, carrying billions of gallons each day. |
| Velocity | How fast it moves. | The rapid current that can rush downstream in minutes. |
| Variety | Different shapes and colors of water (e.g., clear, muddy, salty). | Multiple tributaries merging—each bringing its own taste and texture. |
| Veracity | Reliability of the water’s quality. | Some streams are clean, others polluted; we must filter out bad parts. |
| Value | The usefulness after processing. | Turning raw river water into clean drinking supply that powers homes and businesses. |

Just as engineers design pumps, filters, and reservoirs to harness a powerful river, data engineers build pipelines, storage, and analytics tools to turn raw big data into insights that drive decisions.
