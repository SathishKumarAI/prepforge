Think of a newspaper as a recipe book written on paper.  
**OCR (Optical Character Recognition)** is like a very fast, precise chef who reads each word in the recipe: he scans the ink, recognizes letters, and writes them down in a digital notebook.  

But before the chef can read the words, he needs to know where they are on the page—whether a line starts a new paragraph, if there’s a headline or a photo caption, and how columns run side‑by‑side. That first step is **layout analysis**: it’s like a kitchen assistant who maps out the recipe book, drawing invisible boxes around text blocks, images, tables, and headings so the chef knows where to look next.

So OCR turns ink into typed letters; layout analysis tells the system “here are the words, here’s the title, here’s the picture.” Together they let computers read printed material just like a human would.
