Imagine training a student to solve math problems by giving them practice tests. At first, each new test gives the student fresh insight and improves their skill. But after many rounds, the student reaches a point where every new test is almost identical to what they’ve already seen—no new concepts appear, and scores stop rising. That plateau is “theoretical saturation.”  

In AI evaluation, we design benchmarks (tests) that probe different abilities of a model: language understanding, reasoning, creativity, etc. As researchers keep adding more tasks, the model’s performance stops improving because it has already learned everything those tasks can reveal. Even if you give it an extra, harder test, it won’t get better unless the new task taps something completely unseen—like learning a new math topic.  

Thus, theoretical saturation is the limit where added evaluation data no longer pushes the model further; any progress would need fundamentally different kinds of challenges or more powerful models.
