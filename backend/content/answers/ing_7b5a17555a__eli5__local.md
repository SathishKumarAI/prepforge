Imagine building a giant library where every book is a sentence and every reader is a curious mind looking for meaning. The **Foundational Architecture** of large‑language models (LLMs) is the blueprint that tells us how this library is organized.

1. **Tokenizer** – Think of it as the librarian who splits each book into readable “words” or sub‑words, so the computer can handle them.
2. **Embedding Layer** – It turns those words into points in a multi‑dimensional space, like giving each word a unique GPS coordinate that captures its flavor and relationships.
3. **Transformer Blocks (Self‑Attention)** – These are the reading rooms where every word looks at all other words to decide how important they are, adjusting its understanding step by step—much like discussing a story with friends who point out hidden clues.
4. **Feed‑Forward Networks** – After discussion, each word gets refined, similar to polishing a draft after feedback.
5. **Output Layer** – Finally, the system predicts the next word, like finishing a sentence based on all the context gathered.
