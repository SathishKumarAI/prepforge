Imagine a chef who wants to make the perfect soup.  
**Evals** are like taste‑tests: after each batch, someone samples it and rates how salty, spicy, or smooth it is. These ratings tell the chef whether the recipe needs more onions or less salt. In AI, evals are systematic tests that score a model’s answers on real questions—just as the chef checks flavor.

**Observability** is like having a camera in the kitchen that shows every step: how much broth is added, when the stove turns up, and whether the pot boils too fast. It lets the chef see why a taste test failed—was the flame too high? Did the vegetables burn? In AI, observability collects logs, timings, and intermediate data so developers can trace why a model behaved oddly.

Together, evals give *what* went wrong and observability gives *why*, letting the model (or soup) improve step by step.
