Think of a graph like a **city map** where every intersection is a *node* (or vertex) and every road connecting two intersections is an *edge*.  
In a “Topleetcode” problem you’re usually asked to find the shortest route, detect if a loop exists, or group connected neighborhoods. That’s exactly what graph algorithms do: they walk along roads, keeping track of distances or visited places.

**Key terms:**  
- **Node (vertex):** a point on the map (e.g., a city intersection).  
- **Edge:** a road linking two nodes; it can have a *weight* that represents distance or cost.  
- **Adjacency list/matrix:** a way to store which roads exist, like a phone book of neighboring intersections.

When you solve a leetcode graph problem, imagine you’re a delivery driver who must choose the fastest path or determine whether two locations belong to the same neighborhood. That concrete image keeps the math behind it clear and intuitive.
