Think of an AI product like a high‑end kitchen appliance that keeps learning new recipes on its own, while a SaaS app is just a reliable vending machine you pay for every time you use it.

**Why the margins are lower for AI**

1. **Training costs (raw materials)** – Every “recipe” the AI learns needs huge amounts of data and powerful computers. That’s like buying premium ingredients that keep getting more expensive.
2. **Hardware and storage (the kitchen space)** – You must own or lease servers, GPUs, and data centers, just as a chef needs ovens and refrigerators. Those costs stay high even if you sell many copies.
3. **Model updates (recipe revisions)** – AI models need continual tweaking to stay competitive, like a chef constantly testing new dishes. That labor adds overhead.

**What engineers do**

- They **share models** (like a cookbook that everyone can use) so the cost of training is spread across many customers.
- They design **lightweight versions** that run on cheaper hardware, similar to making a dish with fewer expensive ingredients.
- They focus on **automation**: building pipelines that train and deploy models without manual intervention, cutting labor costs.

So, AI’s high upfront “kitchen” expenses keep margins lower than SaaS, but clever engineering turns those expenses into scalable, reusable assets.
