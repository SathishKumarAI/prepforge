Imagine you’re cooking a big family dinner.  
**Tools** are the kitchen gadgets: a blender, a slow cooker, a spice rack. They do specific jobs—blend smoothies, simmer stews, add flavor—but they don’t decide what’s on the menu or when to start each dish.

A **subagent** is like a helper in the kitchen who knows how to use those gadgets and follows a small recipe: “First stir the onions, then add the tomatoes.” The subagent decides *when* and *how* to use the tools based on the overall meal plan.

When building an AI agent, you can either give it many separate tools (like all the gadgets) or create subagents that orchestrate those tools. Relying too heavily on a huge set of specialized tools can make the system clunky—just like a kitchen full of gadgets but no clear recipe. Instead, design simple subagents that call only the tools they need and keep the whole process smooth and adaptable.
