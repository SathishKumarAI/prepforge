**Throughput (in machine learning)**  
Think of a factory line that paints cars. Each car is a data point, the paint job is an inference or training step, and the whole line’s speed is *throughput*. In ML, throughput is how many data points your system can process per second while running a model—whether it’s classifying images, translating text, or updating weights during training.

*Why it matters:*  
- A higher throughput means you get results faster, which helps when you need real‑time predictions (like spam filtering) or when you’re training on massive datasets.  
- It also tells you how well your hardware (CPU, GPU, memory) is being used; a low throughput often signals bottlenecks such as slow disk reads or inefficient code.

*Remember:* Throughput counts completed tasks per unit time, not the quality of those tasks. It’s the speed of the conveyor belt, not whether each car looks perfect.
