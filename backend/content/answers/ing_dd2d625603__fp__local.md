### Why Behavioral Interviews Matter

At their core, hiring decisions are *optimization problems*: we want the candidate that maximizes future performance under uncertainty.  
A candidate’s résumé tells us about **potential** (skills, achievements), but it is a static snapshot. The *behavioural interview* is an experiment that probes how the candidate translates potential into action in realistic situations—essentially a *probabilistic inference* of future behaviour.

1. **Signal extraction:** People often exaggerate; behavioural questions force concrete examples, reducing noise and revealing true patterns of thinking, decision‑making, and resilience.  
2. **Contextual generalisation:** By asking about past scenarios that mirror your company’s challenges, the interview estimates how well a candidate will adapt to *your* specific environment (the “distribution shift” problem).  
3. **Cognitive bias mitigation:** Structured questions limit interviewer bias, turning the process into a quasi‑randomised trial where each candidate is evaluated on comparable evidence.

**Non‑obvious insight:** The true value of behavioural interviews lies in their *temporal anchoring*. They ask for events that occurred recently enough to be memorable but far enough back to show sustained behaviour patterns—this balances recall bias against the risk of “momentary luck.” In practice, a question framed as **“Describe a time you handled conflict under tight deadlines”** forces the candidate to demonstrate *self‑regulation*, *prioritisation*, and *communication* simultaneously, providing a multidimensional performance metric that outperforms single‑skill tests.
