Think of a machine‑learning system as a **factory that turns raw ingredients into a finished product**.  
In the interview you’re asked how you’d lay out that factory: what machines (components) it has, where each one sits on the floor (layers), and how they talk to one another.

*Data ingestion* is the intake line—raw data arrives from sensors or logs.  
*A preprocessing stage* cleans and shapes the data, like a kitchen prep station.  
The *model training engine* is the main production unit that learns patterns; it sits on powerful GPUs or TPUs (the “workshops”).  
After training, the *serving layer* is the packaging line—quickly turning new inputs into predictions for users or other systems.

You’ll explain why you choose each machine (e.g., a streaming platform vs. batch jobs), how they’re connected (REST APIs, message queues), and what safeguards you put in place (monitoring, version control).  
The goal is to show that you can design a clear, scalable, and maintainable flow from raw data to useful predictions—just like a well‑planned factory floor.
