Think of a microservice as a kitchen appliance—say, a blender. Each feature is like a specific setting on that blender: “puree,” “churn,” or “pulse.” The appliance (the service) can do many things, but each setting (feature) lets you shape the outcome in a particular way. In software, a microservice is a small, self‑contained program that does one job—like handling payments or sending emails. A feature inside it is a distinct capability it offers: “refund processing,” “fraud detection,” or “currency conversion.” Just as the blender’s settings are optional and can be turned on or off depending on what you’re making, features in a microservice can be enabled, disabled, or updated without touching the whole service. This keeps the appliance simple, focused, and easy to upgrade.
