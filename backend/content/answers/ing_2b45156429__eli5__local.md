Imagine you’re sending a text to one friend who knows exactly what you want—no group chat, no extra chatter. In the **point‑to‑point model** of machine‑learning communication, each piece of data (the “message”) travels directly from a single source (a sensor, an app, or a user) straight to a single destination (a specific algorithm or model).  

Think of it as a private courier: the sender packs a package with a clear label, hands it over to the driver, and the driver delivers it to one pre‑agreed address. There’s no passing through hubs or waiting for other packages. This direct line keeps things simple, fast, and secure—ideal when you need only that one model to react to each new input without involving others.
