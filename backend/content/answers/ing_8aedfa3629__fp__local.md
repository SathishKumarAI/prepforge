**On‑policy distillation** blends two ideas:  
1) *on‑policy learning*—the learner’s actions are the ones that generate its experience;  
2) *distillation*—a student policy mimics a teacher’s action distribution.

---

### Why it must look this way

The goal is to train a compact **student** \(π_s\) that reproduces a high‑capacity teacher \(π_t\) while still improving on the task. The only data we can collect efficiently are trajectories generated by the current student, because they reflect its own action choices and thus respect the Markov decision process (MDP). If we collected off‑policy samples, importance sampling would be needed, adding variance.

The loss is a weighted sum of two terms:

* **Policy gradient** \(L_{PG}\): drives the student to maximize expected return under its own distribution.  
* **KL divergence** \(L_{KD}\): pulls the student’s action logits toward the teacher’s softmax output, effectively *imprinting* the teacher’s knowledge.

The weighting \(\alpha\) balances exploitation (policy gradient) and imitation (distillation). A small learning rate on the KL term prevents catastrophic forgetting of the task while still benefiting from the teacher’s richer representation.

---

### Pseudocode

```text
Initialize student policy π_s(θ), teacher policy π_t
α ← 0.1          # distillation weight
ε ← 1e‑4         # learning rate

for episode = 1 … N do
    trajectory τ ← generate trajectory with π_s   # on‑policy rollouts
    for each step (s, a, r) in τ do
        // Policy gradient component
        L_pg += –r * log π_s(a|s)

        // Distillation component (KL divergence)
        logits_t = π_t(s)
        logits_s = π_s(s)
        p_t   = softmax(logits_t / τ_temp)      # teacher distribution
        p_s   = softmax(logits_s / τ_temp)      # student distribution
        L_kd += KL(p_t || p_s)

    // Total loss and parameter update
    L_total = L_pg + α * L_kd
    θ ← θ – ε * ∇_θ L_total
```

---

### Non‑obvious insight

The **temperature** \(\tau_{\text{temp}}\) used in the softmax is crucial: a higher temperature smooths the teacher’s logits, exposing richer relative preferences between actions. This subtle smoothing lets the student learn not just the most likely action but also the *shape* of the teacher’s policy landscape, which improves exploration and generalization—something that plain cross‑entropy would miss.
