Imagine you’re a chef who wants to prove how good your recipe is. If every taste‑test uses the exact same dish, everyone will say “Yum!”—but that says nothing about whether your cooking truly works in different kitchens. **Harnessing variance** is like giving each tester a slightly different oven temperature or a new set of ingredients so you can see if the recipe still turns out great.  

A **benchmark** is the standard menu you hand to all chefs; it tells them exactly what to cook and how to judge it. A **leaderboard** is the scorecard that ranks those chefs based on their results across many varied test kitchens. By deliberately adding variation (different ovens, ingredient brands, weather conditions) and then measuring performance, we “scaffold” a more realistic challenge. This lets us trust that a high‑ranking model isn’t just lucky on one dataset—it truly handles the wide range of real‑world situations.
