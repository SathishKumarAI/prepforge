Imagine you’re trying to guess how many apples are in a giant orchard, but you only look at the fruit hanging from one very tall tree. The apples on that tree might be bigger or sweeter than those on lower branches. If you base your estimate on just those visible apples, you’ll over‑estimate the overall quality of the orchard’s produce. That’s selection bias: when the data we collect come from a non‑representative sample, our conclusions are skewed.
