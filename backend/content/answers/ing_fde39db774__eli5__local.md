**Machine Learning in Plain English**

Think of machine learning like teaching a child to recognize cats without telling them the exact definition. You show many pictures: some are cats, some aren’t. The child looks for patterns—pointy ears, whiskers, shape of the face—and gradually learns what “cat” feels like. In an app, we give the computer millions of labeled images (the “cats” and “non‑cats”) so it can pick up those subtle clues on its own. Once trained, it can look at a brand‑new photo and say, with high confidence, whether there is a cat or not—just as the child would after practice.

**Key terms**

- **Training data**: The set of labeled examples you feed into the system.  
- **Model**: The computer’s internal “brain” that stores what it has learned from the training data.  
- **Prediction (or inference)**: When the model looks at new, unseen input and gives an answer.
