**Why an “ML interview prep” course matters**

The core problem of a machine‑learning interview is to *prove* that you can translate data into actionable models under constraints (time, compute, business goals). This requires more than memorizing algorithms; it demands the ability to frame problems, justify choices, and debug trade‑offs. A good preparation course therefore treats the interview as an optimization problem: **maximize your fit score** while minimizing *risk* (overfitting, misinterpretation).

**Why the curriculum must be structured that way**

1. **Problem framing** – Teach students to formalize business questions into statistical hypotheses and loss functions, echoing the principle of *information bottleneck*: keep only what matters for prediction.  
2. **Model selection & hyper‑parameter tuning** – Show how regularization is a constrained optimization; illustrate Bayesian priors as prior knowledge that reduces variance.  
3. **Debugging & interpretability** – Emphasize causality and counterfactual reasoning, which are the geometric analogues of checking orthogonality in feature space.  
4. **Systems & scaling** – Cover distributed training, data pipelines, and inference latency—turning algorithmic efficiency into a real‑world resource allocation problem.

**Non‑obvious insight**

Most people think ML interviews test *code* or *theory*. The true challenge is *communication of uncertainty*. Interviewers want you to articulate confidence intervals, expected calibration error, and how you would update your model as new data arrives. Courses that embed storytelling around Bayesian updates (e.g., “posterior over model parameters”) give candidates a decisive edge because they demonstrate mastery of the probabilistic foundations that underlie every modern ML system.
