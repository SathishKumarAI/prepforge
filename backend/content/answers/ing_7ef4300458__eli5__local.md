Imagine your brain as a giant city of streets and traffic lights.  
A **neural network** is like a miniature version of that city built on a computer. Each “neuron” is a tiny traffic light that can turn the flow of information on or off, and the roads (called *connections*) carry signals between them.  

When you feed data—say an image of a cat—into this city, it travels through layers of traffic lights. Some lights open to let the signal pass; others close to block it. The pattern of which lights stay open after many rounds of training becomes your network’s memory, enabling it to recognize cats or other objects.

So, a neural network is just a programmable network of simple switches that learns how to route signals so it can solve tasks, much like traffic lights learn to keep the city moving smoothly.
