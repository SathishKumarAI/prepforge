**In‑context learning** is like a chef tasting a soup while cooking.  
The model (the chef) has already learned the basic recipe for soups (its training). When you give it a new prompt, you’re adding fresh ingredients—ingredients that are part of the “context.” The chef uses these ingredients to adjust flavor on the fly, without rewriting the whole recipe.

**Prompt engineering** is how you choose those ingredients. By carefully selecting words or examples in your prompt (the ingredient list), you steer the model toward a particular style or answer, just as a cook might add more salt or spice to hit the desired taste.

So, think of the model as a flexible chef: it learns from its training, but it uses the context you provide—like a recipe tweak—to produce exactly what you need.
