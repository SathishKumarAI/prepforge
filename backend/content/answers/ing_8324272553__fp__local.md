### Pattern 3 – Server‑Side Composition: Building Tool Agents

**Problem statement.**  
When a single large language model (LLM) is asked to solve a multi‑step task—e.g., “plan a trip, book flights, and recommend restaurants”—the model must internally *compose* sub‑tasks, keep intermediate data, and manage the order of operations. If we let the LLM handle everything in one prompt, we incur high latency, lose fine‑grained control, and cannot reuse specialized APIs efficiently.

**Why a server‑side orchestrator is required.**  
We can view each tool (API call, database query, or domain‑specific model) as an *operator* that transforms input into output. The goal is to find a sequence of operators whose composition yields the desired final state. This is precisely a search problem in the space of execution traces. By moving the composition logic off the LLM—into a server that can maintain context, cache results, and parallelize calls—we reduce the burden on the model to *plan* rather than *execute*. The LLM’s role shrinks to generating high‑level plans or natural language explanations, while deterministic components handle the heavy lifting.

**Connection to deeper principles.**  
- **Optimization:** The orchestrator can formulate the sequencing as a constrained optimization (minimize cost/latency subject to success probability).  
- **Information theory:** By separating planning from execution, we reduce the amount of information the LLM must encode in its prompt; only high‑level intent is needed.  
- **Probabilistic inference:** The orchestrator can maintain a belief over which sub‑tasks have succeeded and propagate that uncertainty to later steps.

**Non‑obvious insight.**  
Most developers assume the LLM will naturally “know” when to call an API. In practice, the *granularity* of prompts matters: too coarse and the model may skip needed tools; too fine and we overwhelm it with low‑level details. A small, well‑structured interface—e.g., a JSON schema for each tool—lets the orchestrator translate natural language into deterministic calls while preserving the LLM’s ability to reason abstractly.

**Bottom line.**  
Server‑side composition turns the LLM from an all‑in‑one executor into a *planner*, enabling scalable, low‑latency, and reliable multi‑tool workflows that respect both computational constraints and the strengths of large models.
