Think of a library where every book is turned into a “fingerprint” that tells the computer what it’s about.  
**Embeddings** are those fingerprints: they turn words or sentences into numbers that live in a big, invisible room called a **vector space**. Each point in this room represents an idea; points close together mean similar ideas.

Now imagine you’re reading a long novel but only want the gist of each chapter. **Chunking** is like cutting the book into chapters first, then creating one fingerprint for each chunk. The computer can quickly compare whole chunks—much faster than comparing every single word—while still keeping enough detail to understand context. This technique lets AI search, summarize, or answer questions about big texts efficiently and accurately.
