In machine learning, an **API** (Application Programming Interface) is a set of rules that lets one software program talk to another without knowing the inside details.  
Think of it like ordering food at a restaurant: you look at the menu (the API), choose what you want, and hand the order to the waiter. The kitchen (the ML model or service) doesn’t need to know how you decided on that dish; it just follows the recipe and brings back your meal.  

The “menu” lists every request you can make—“give me a prediction for this image,” “update the model with new data”—and tells you what format the answer will come in. It keeps the two parts separate: you only need to know how to speak the language of the menu, not the chef’s secret recipe. This separation makes building and scaling ML systems easier and safer.
