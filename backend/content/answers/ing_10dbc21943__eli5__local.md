Imagine your brain as a giant recipe book that learns how to make dishes by tasting many meals. **Deep learning** is like giving this book a stack of layered “ingredient sheets.” Each layer looks at the food in a different way—first it spots colors and shapes, next it recognizes textures, then it understands whole objects, and finally it grasps meaning.

In practice, a computer runs through thousands of images or sounds. The first hidden layer extracts basic patterns (edges, tones). Those outputs feed into the next layer, which learns combinations of those basics (corners, simple shapes). Each successive layer builds on the previous one, forming more complex concepts—like recognizing a cat’s face or a spoken word.

Just as you refine a recipe by tasting and adjusting, the network adjusts tiny numbers called *weights* until its predictions match reality. After many passes, it can classify new pictures, translate languages, or even compose music, all because those layered “ingredient sheets” have learned to represent information in increasingly sophisticated ways.
