In data engineering, an **expression** is a small recipe that tells the system how to transform or calculate something from the data it already has.  
Think of it like a kitchen instruction: “Take two cups of flour, add one cup of sugar, stir until smooth.” The ingredients are columns in a table (the data), and the recipe is the expression. When you run the recipe, the system follows each step—reading values from the columns, adding them together, maybe multiplying or converting types—and writes the result into a new column or output.  

So an expression is simply a reusable formula that operates on input fields to produce a new value, just as a cooking instruction turns raw ingredients into a finished dish.
