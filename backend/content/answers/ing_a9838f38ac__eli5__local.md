Think of AI as a chef trying to bake a perfect cake. A **metric** is the recipe’s “taste test” score—how sweet or dense it should be. The problem is that we only have a handful of taste tests: maybe one panelist says it’s too salty, another says it’s just right. Relying on those few opinions means the chef might keep tweaking the wrong ingredient, missing hidden flaws like uneven texture.

In AI, metrics (accuracy, loss, precision) are similar “taste tests.” They’re easy to compute and give quick feedback, but they capture only a slice of what makes an AI useful—fairness, robustness, safety. If we chase a single metric, the system can look great on paper yet fail in real‑world situations, just as a cake that passes one taste test might still crumble when shared widely. The challenge is to design many balanced tests so the AI truly “tastes” right everywhere.
