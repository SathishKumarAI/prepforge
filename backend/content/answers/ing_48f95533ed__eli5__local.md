Think of your data set like a photo album that you want to show at a school fair.  
Some photos are missing because the camera didn’t work—those are **null values** (or “missing” entries).  

In machine learning, we can’t let those blanks spoil the whole picture. We have three common tricks:

1. **Leave them blank** – tell the algorithm to ignore that spot, just like you skip a photo in your album.  
2. **Fill them in** – replace each missing value with something sensible, such as the average of that column (like guessing the missing photo’s color from the rest).  
3. **Drop the row or column** – remove any photo that has too many blanks, just as you might discard an incomplete page.

Choosing the right trick depends on how much data you have and how important the missing pieces are.
