**The Trust Problem and Security Constraints**

Imagine you’re sending a secret recipe to a friend who lives in another city. You trust that they’ll keep it safe, but you can’t be sure they won’t accidentally share it with someone else or lose the paper. In computing, this is the *trust problem*: we want programs (or “agents”) to act honestly, but we can’t guarantee their behavior because they might be buggy, hacked, or even malicious.

To protect the recipe, you put it in a lockbox that only your friend’s key opens. That lockbox is like a *security constraint*—a rule that says “only these operations are allowed.” It limits what the program can do (e.g., read only certain files, send data only to approved addresses). The trick is designing the lockbox so it stops bad behavior without blocking useful work. Just as you choose a strong lock and a reliable key, developers build constraints that keep systems safe while still letting them function.
