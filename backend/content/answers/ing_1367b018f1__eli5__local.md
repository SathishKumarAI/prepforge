Imagine you’re a chef who wants to train a robot to recognize every type of apple—red, green, ripe, unripe—no matter how it’s held or lit. Image augmentation is like giving the robot a pantry full of “fake” apples: you flip them left‑right, tilt them slightly, change their brightness, and even stretch them a bit. The robot sees many versions of the same apple, so it learns that they’re all still apples.

**Key terms**

* **Image augmentation** – automatically creating altered copies of pictures (e.g., rotated, flipped, color‑shifted) to enlarge training data.
* **Transformations** – the specific changes applied (rotation, scaling, brightness).
* **Dataset** – the collection of images used to teach the model.

By practicing on these varied “fake” apples, the robot becomes robust and can recognize real apples in any setting.
