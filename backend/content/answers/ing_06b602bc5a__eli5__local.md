Imagine your LLM is like a new house that can talk back.  
**Red‑teaming** is the process of inviting a group of “house inspectors” who try every door, window and hidden passage to see how easily someone could trick the house into saying or doing something dangerous—like convincing it to reveal private data or spread misinformation. They think like creative attackers, using real‑world tricks (social engineering, ambiguous prompts, or unusual inputs).  

A **traditional penetration test** is more like a standard security audit of the house’s plumbing and wiring: it checks for obvious leaks, weak locks, or software bugs in the building’s code.

So red‑team testing focuses on *human‑like cleverness* to stress the model’s conversational limits, while a pen‑test looks at technical vulnerabilities in the underlying system. Both are essential, but they probe different “walls” of safety.
