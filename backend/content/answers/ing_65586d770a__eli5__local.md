**Prompt engineering is like giving a recipe to a chef that knows how to cook any dish you want.**  
Think of the machine‑learning model as a very skilled chef who can make almost anything, but only if you tell it exactly what you need. The *prompt* is the recipe: it tells the chef (the model) which ingredients (input data), cooking style (instructions), and finishing touches (desired format) to use.  

A good prompt gives clear, specific instructions—just as a recipe lists precise measurements and steps. If you’re vague (“make something tasty”), the chef may produce an unexpected dish. By adding details (“use only seasonal vegetables, keep it spicy, serve in a bowl”), you guide the model toward the exact output you want.

**Extensions** are like special kitchen gadgets that let the chef do more:  
- *Few‑shot prompting* shows the chef examples of what you like.  
- *Chain‑of‑thought prompting* asks the chef to explain each step, making it easier to spot errors.  
- *Prompt templates* give reusable recipe structures for common tasks.

So, treat prompt engineering as crafting a clear, detailed recipe that turns the model’s vast cooking skills into exactly what you need.
