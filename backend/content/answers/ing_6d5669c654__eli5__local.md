Think of a big machine‑learning project as a kitchen that makes many dishes at once.  
A **microservice** is like an individual cooking station—say the grill, the blender, or the sauce pot—that does one specific job (e.g., preprocessing data, training a model, scoring predictions). Each station runs in its own little container, has its own recipe book (code), and can be updated or replaced without touching the other stations.  

When you need to change the grill’s temperature setting, you tweak only that station; the blender keeps doing what it does. This isolation lets teams work faster, keep code clean, and let the whole kitchen scale—adding more grills for bigger training jobs—without breaking everything else. In short, microservices break a large ML system into small, independent, reusable parts that can grow or shrink on their own.
