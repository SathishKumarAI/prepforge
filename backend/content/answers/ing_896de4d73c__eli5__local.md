Imagine you’re baking a cake for a big party. **Tools** are like the oven, mixer, and measuring cups—each one does a specific job (mixing, heating, measuring). You can use any of them on their own or together, but they don’t need to know about the whole recipe.

A **subagent** is a mini‑chef that’s been trained to do a particular part of the baking process, like whisking eggs or folding in chocolate chips. It has its own small brain and can decide when and how to act, even if you only give it a simple instruction (“whisk until smooth”).

When building an AI “agent” (the master chef that runs the whole kitchen), you don’t have to create a full‑blown subchef for every tiny task. Instead, you can hand off simple jobs to ready‑made tools (like the oven) and let a few lightweight subagents handle the more creative steps. This keeps the system from becoming over‑engineered—just enough brains where they matter, with straightforward tools filling in the rest.
