Think of a machine‑learning model as a smart recipe book that learns how to cook from many sample dishes it has tasted before.  
When you give the recipe book a new ingredient (an input), it guesses what dish will result (the output). It improves its guesses by reading thousands of cooked meals and noting which ingredients produced which flavors—just like a chef tasting and tweaking recipes until they’re perfect.

In this “learning” process, two key parts work together:

1. **Training data** – the set of past dishes the book has studied.  
2. **Model (the recipe)** – a mathematical formula that maps ingredients to taste.  

The book adjusts its internal notes (its parameters) so that, over time, it predicts dishes more accurately. This continual adjustment is called *training*; once finished, the model can be used on new, unseen data.
