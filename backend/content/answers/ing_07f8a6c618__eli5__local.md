**Data lifecycle management is like caring for a garden of secrets.**  
First, you *plant* (collect) the data—just as seeds are sown, only the most essential information should be gathered. Next comes *watering and pruning* (storage). The data sits in secure “beds” (databases or cloud buckets), protected by fences (encryption) so that only authorized gardeners can see it.  

When you need a flower (use the data), you pull it out carefully, noting who touched it—this is *access control*. After its bloom fades, you decide whether to compost it or store it for future use; this is *retention* and *archival*. Finally, when a storm hits, you may *destroy* old plants that are no longer needed (data deletion).  

Throughout the process, every gardener follows a set of rules—our *policy framework*—to keep the garden healthy, compliant, and free from pests.
