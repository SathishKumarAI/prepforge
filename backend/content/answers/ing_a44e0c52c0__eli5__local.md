Imagine a big classroom where every student wants to know the newest school rumor. Instead of everyone shouting at the teacher, each student whispers it to just a few friends; those friends do the same, and so on. That’s the idea behind gossip protocols—efficient ways for computers to share information across a network.

There are three main “whisper styles”:

1. **Push** – Like a student who always starts the rumor: they actively tell others what they know.
2. **Pull** – Like a curious student who asks classmates, “Did you hear anything?” and receives updates that way.
3. **Hybrid (Push‑Pull)** – A mix of both: students both share what they have and ask for new info, speeding up the spread while keeping bandwidth low.

Just as whispers keep chatter from drowning out the whole class, gossip protocols let data travel quickly without overloading any single computer.
