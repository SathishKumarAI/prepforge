**Perplexity** is a way for a language model (like those used by AI companies) to measure how surprised it is by the text it reads or writes.  
Think of it as a guessing game: you’re standing in front of a sentence, and every time the model sees a new word it has to guess what comes next. If the model guesses right most of the time, the room feels calm—its “perplexity” is low. If it keeps getting stumped, the room feels chaotic—its perplexity is high.

In coding‑problem interviews, AI companies ask you to build or tune models with low perplexity on code snippets. That means teaching the model to predict the next line of code accurately, just like training a good chess player who rarely blunders. The lower the perplexity score, the more reliable the model’s predictions are, and the better it will perform in real coding tasks.
