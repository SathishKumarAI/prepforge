Think of training a machine‑learning model like teaching a child to recognize apples.  
During *training* you show the child many pictures of apples and non‑apples, telling it which is which. The child (the model) learns by adjusting its own little “rules” until it can guess correctly most of the time.  

Once training finishes, the child has learned a set of rules that work well on new images it hasn’t seen before. In machine‑learning terms, you have a **trained model**—a collection of numbers (weights) that encode those rules.  
Now you can *use* this model to predict outcomes: give it a brand‑new picture, and it tells you “apple” or “not apple.” That’s the difference between training (learning the rules) and inference (applying them).
