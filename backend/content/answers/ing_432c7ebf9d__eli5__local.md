**Finetuned language models are zero‑shot learners**

Imagine a chef who has been trained to cook many dishes (the “language model”). After learning the basics of flavor and technique, the chef is given a new recipe that he’s never seen before—say, a spicy Thai noodle dish. Even without extra practice, the chef can mix the right ingredients because his training gives him general cooking skills: knowing how heat changes taste, how textures should feel, and which spices pair well.

In machine‑learning terms, “finetuned” means the model has been slightly tweaked on a specific set of examples (like learning to write emails). Once that tuning is done, it can handle completely new tasks—answering questions, translating, summarizing—without being retrained for each one. That’s zero‑shot: **zero additional training** after the initial finetuning.
