Imagine you’re in a kitchen that can make any dish, but the chef (the AI) can work in three different ways.

1. **Local mode** – The chef stays right inside your kitchen. It runs on your own computer or phone, using only the ingredients (data and tools) you’ve already put there. No one else sees what it does, so privacy is tight but it can’t reach out to other kitchens for fresh supplies.

2. **Server‑hosted mode** – The chef moves to a big restaurant’s kitchen (a cloud server). Here it can fetch ingredients from anywhere on the internet and share its recipes with many diners at once. You still control what the chef is allowed to cook, but you rely on that external kitchen for power and storage.

3. **Hybrid mode** – Think of a mobile food truck. The chef keeps some tools in your home kitchen (for quick, private tasks) and pulls from the big restaurant when a special ingredient is needed. This blends privacy with scalability.

In all cases, “tool agents” are the chef’s helpers—small programs that fetch data, run calculations, or manipulate files so the main AI can focus on creative decisions.
