Imagine an AI helper as a chef in a kitchen that can cook many dishes (tasks). *Unit cost per task* is like asking: “How much does it cost to make one dish?” The cost includes ingredients, time the chef spends, and any special tools they need.  

When we evaluate an agentic system—an AI that decides what to do on its own—we want to know how efficiently it works. We give it a set of recipes (tasks), let it choose how to cook them, then count the total resources used: CPU time, memory, or money spent on data. Dividing that by the number of dishes finished gives us the unit cost per task.  

A lower number means the AI can finish tasks faster and cheaper, just as a skilled chef who uses ingredients wisely can serve more plates without overspending. This simple measure lets developers compare different AIs side‑by‑side, ensuring they choose the one that delivers the most value for each job.
