Imagine you’re building a giant Lego castle, but instead of one huge block that holds everything together, you use many small, independent blocks—each one a different part of the castle: the drawbridge, the turret, the moat. In software, those small blocks are **microservices**.

A microservice is a tiny program that does one specific job (like checking payments or sending emails). It runs on its own, talks to other services through simple messages, and can be updated or replaced without touching the rest of the system. Just as you can swap out a broken turret block for a new one without dismantling the whole castle, developers can fix or upgrade one microservice while the others keep working.

This “block‑by‑block” approach makes large applications easier to build, maintain, and scale—just like assembling a Lego masterpiece from many small, well‑designed pieces.
