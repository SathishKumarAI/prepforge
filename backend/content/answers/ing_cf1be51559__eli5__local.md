Imagine you’re looking for a book in a huge library that mixes two types of shelves: one is organized by topic (like a normal catalog), and the other is sorted alphabetically but only for titles that are already known to be important. A *hybrid search* first uses the quick alphabetical shelf to narrow down candidates, then checks those against the topic‑organized shelves to confirm they’re truly relevant.

The **Gap Problem** happens when there’s a big “gap” between these two ways of ordering. The quick alphabet list may point you to books that look promising but are actually irrelevant because their topics don’t match what you need. Conversely, the topic shelf might hide some good matches that the alphabet list never considered. So, the search can miss important items or waste time on unhelpful ones—just like a librarian who jumps between two unrelated filing systems and gets stuck in the overlap.
