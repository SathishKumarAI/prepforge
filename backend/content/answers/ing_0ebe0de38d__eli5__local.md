Imagine a video‑game character that must navigate a maze to collect coins while avoiding traps.  
The **environment** is everything outside the character’s mind: the walls, the coins, the moving enemies, and even the wind that can push it off course. In AI terms, an *agent* (the game character) interacts with its environment by taking actions—moving forward, turning left, jumping—and receiving feedback in the form of **percepts** (seeing a coin, feeling a bump).  

Think of the agent as a robot or software program that lives inside a world it can’t directly change except through its own actions. The environment supplies information (sensor data) and reacts to those actions (changing the maze layout, rewarding points). The agent’s job is to learn how to act so that, over time, it gets more coins and fewer traps.
