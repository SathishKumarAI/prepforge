**Ensemble learning** is a way of getting better predictions by mixing several simple “experts” instead of relying on just one.  
Think of it like a jury deciding a case: each juror (a machine‑learning model) reads the evidence and gives their verdict. A single juror might be biased or miss something, but when all the jurors vote together—through majority rule or averaging—the final decision is usually more reliable and fairer.  

In this analogy:
- **Juror** = individual model  
- **Evidence** = training data  
- **Jury’s verdict** = ensemble prediction  

Just as a diverse jury can catch mistakes each person alone might miss, an ensemble combines different models (sometimes trained differently or on different data) to reduce errors and improve accuracy.
