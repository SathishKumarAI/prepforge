Think of Elastic Cloud like a coffee shop that sells two ways to enjoy your brew.

**Hosted (self‑managed) offering**  
You rent a table and bring your own kettle, beans, and filter. The shop gives you the space and the water, but you’re responsible for heating the kettle, cleaning up after yourself, and making sure the coffee stays hot. You can choose exactly how much space to use and when to add more cups, but every extra cup means you pay a little more rent and spend time handling it.

**Serverless offering**  
Here the shop handles everything: they pour the coffee, keep it at the right temperature, and clean the table automatically. You just tap your phone, say “two cups,” and the shop takes care of the rest. If suddenly everyone orders three cups, the shop expands its kitchen invisibly; you don’t pay extra for that extra space because the service scales on demand.

**Benefits compared**

| Aspect | Hosted (self‑managed) | Serverless |
|--------|----------------------|------------|
| **Control** | Full control over configuration and scaling. | Limited control, but easier to get started. |
| **Cost predictability** | You pay for what you reserve; cost is steady if usage is constant. | Pay only for actual coffee brewed (usage‑based). |
| **Maintenance** | You handle updates, security patches, and backups. | The shop manages all maintenance automatically. |
| **Scalability** | Must manually add more tables or upgrade your kettle. | Scales instantly when demand spikes. |

So if you want total freedom and are comfortable handling the plumbing, go hosted. If you prefer a hands‑off experience where the coffee shop does everything for you and you pay only for what you drink, serverless is the way to go.
