Think of a search engine like a giant library that can read every book in the world and instantly hand you the right page when you ask a question.

1. **Crawling** – A robot (the crawler) walks through the internet, following links just as a librarian would follow signposts to new shelves, collecting pages into its “index.”  
2. **Indexing** – The engine stores a quick summary of each page—its title, words, and where it was found—much like a library card catalog that tells you what books exist and where they sit.  
3. **Ranking** – When you type a query, the search engine compares your words to the index and orders results by relevance: pages that contain many of your keywords, are from reputable sites, and link back to each other get higher scores, like a librarian giving priority to the most useful books.

Thus, crawling gathers data, indexing organizes it, and ranking delivers the best matches—just as a smart librarian would.
