Imagine a post office that forwards letters for you.  
A **proxy server** is like a regular mailbox: when your computer asks the internet for a web page, it first sends the request to the proxy. The proxy fetches the page and returns it to you, keeping your real address hidden from the outside world.  

A **reverse proxy** flips that role. Think of it as a concierge at an apartment building: visitors (web browsers) send their requests to the concierge, who then forwards them to the correct apartment (the actual web server). The visitor never sees which apartment is handling the request, and the server stays protected from direct exposure.

Both act as middle‑man routers, but the proxy serves your side, while the reverse proxy serves the server’s side.
