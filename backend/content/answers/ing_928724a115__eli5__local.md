**Cold‑Cheat Sheet – the “Thermometer’s Secret Language”**

Imagine your body as a busy kitchen. The **temperature** is like the oven’s heat: it tells you if things are hot or cold. In science we use two main units, **Celsius (°C)** and **Kelvin (K)**.

- 0 °C = freezing point of water – the “cold start” of the kitchen.  
- 100 °C = boiling point – the oven’s maximum heat.  
- Kelvin is just Celsius shifted up by 273.15: \( K = °C + 273.15 \). This shift makes zero a true absolute cold (no thermal motion).

When you want to **compare** two temperatures, use the simple difference:

\[
ΔT = T_2 - T_1
\]

If \( ΔT > 0 \), it’s hotter; if \( ΔT < 0 \), it’s colder.  

So, just like reading a kitchen timer, check the number on your thermometer, remember the unit (°C or K), and compare with the reference point (0 °C for freezing). That’s all you need to spot “cold” in everyday life!
