Imagine you’re baking a cake for a party.  
**Context engineering** is like choosing the right recipe and ingredients before you start: you decide what flavor, how much sugar, whether to add chocolate chips or nuts, and which oven temperature works best. In an AI agent, context engineering means giving the system clear instructions about *what* it should do—its goal, constraints, and environment—so it knows the “recipe” for its task.

**Memory engineering** is the next step: once the cake starts baking, you need to remember how long it has been in the oven, whether you turned the pan, or if a batter was added midway. For an AI, memory engineering builds a reliable way for the agent to store and retrieve past actions, observations, and learned patterns—its “history” that informs future decisions.

So, context is the *recipe* (the plan), while memory is the *kitchen logbook* (what happened so far). Both are essential: a good recipe without a log leads to repeated mistakes; a perfect log without a clear recipe yields aimless actions.
