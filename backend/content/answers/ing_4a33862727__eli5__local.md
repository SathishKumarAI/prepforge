Think of the internet as a huge highway system where every city is a computer.  
A **VPN** (Virtual Private Network) is like a secret tunnel that lets you travel from one city to another without anyone on the highway seeing your route or destination.

**How it works:** Your device sends data into the tunnel, where it’s wrapped in an extra layer of code—like putting your car inside a secure box. That box travels through public roads but can’t be read by other cars. When it reaches the other end, the code is removed and your data appears at its destination.

**Disadvantages**

1. **Speed loss** – The extra “boxing” adds time; traffic can slow down because the tunnel has to encrypt and decrypt data.
2. **Reliability issues** – If the tunnel’s server goes offline or gets overloaded, you’re stuck in a dead‑end and lose connection.
3. **Cost and trust** – Free tunnels often log your activity; paid ones require money and trusting a third party with all your traffic.

So while a VPN hides what you do, it can also make your internet slower, less reliable, and sometimes risky if the tunnel operator isn’t trustworthy.
