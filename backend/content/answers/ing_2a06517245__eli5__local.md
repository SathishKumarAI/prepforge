Imagine you’re baking a cake for a big party. The *data pipeline* is like the kitchen workflow that turns raw ingredients into a finished dessert, step by step.

1. **Gathering (Ingestion)** – You collect flour, eggs, sugar, and chocolate from different stores. In ML, this means pulling raw data from databases, sensors, or web APIs.  
2. **Cleaning (Preparation)** – You sift the flour, whisk the eggs, and remove any lumps. The pipeline removes missing values, corrects errors, and standardizes formats so the data is consistent.  
3. **Transforming (Feature Engineering)** – You mix the batter, fold in chocolate chips, and maybe add a dash of vanilla. Here you create new variables or scale numbers that make the model “taste” better.  
4. **Storing (Load)** – The cake sits on a rack waiting to be served. The processed data is saved into a database or data lake for quick access by models.

Just as a well‑planned kitchen makes baking smooth, a good data pipeline ensures machine learning models receive clean, ready‑to‑use information without manual tinkering.
