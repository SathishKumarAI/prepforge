Think of a database as a library.  
A **schema** is the floor plan that tells you where each room (table) goes and what kind of books (data types) can be stored there. A **schema object** is any part of that floor plan—tables, views, indexes, procedures, or triggers—that the librarian uses to organize, retrieve, or protect the books.

- **Table**: a shelf holding rows of records.  
- **View**: a virtual window showing only certain shelves, like a guided tour.  
- **Index**: a quick‑lookup card that speeds finding a book on a shelf.  
- **Stored procedure**: a recipe for preparing a set of books in one go.  
- **Trigger**: an automatic guard that runs when you add or change a book.

Each object lives in the schema’s namespace, just as every room has a unique name on the floor plan. Together they let the database know how to store, find, and protect data efficiently.
