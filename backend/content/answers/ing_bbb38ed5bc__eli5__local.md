Think of a smart robot chef who has never cooked before. You give it a huge cookbook (data) filled with recipes and pictures of finished dishes. Each time the chef tries a recipe, you note whether the dish turns out tasty or not (the label). The chef’s goal is to learn a rule: “If the dough feels soft and the batter is thin, bake at 350 °F for 20 minutes.” That rule is the **model**—a set of instructions that maps ingredients (inputs) to cooking time and temperature (outputs).

To build this model, you let the chef experiment many times. After each trial, you adjust the rule a little: maybe lower the temperature if the bread turns out too brown. This step‑by‑step refinement is **training**. Once the rule works well on new recipes it hasn’t seen before, the chef can cook confidently—that’s **prediction**.
