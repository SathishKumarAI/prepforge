Think of a kitchen gadget like a blender.  
*What it does* (mix, chop, puree) is the **abstraction** – you only care about the result, not how the motor or blades achieve it. In object‑oriented programming, abstraction works the same way: a class hides its internal details and exposes a simple interface (methods).  

Just as you can use “blend” without knowing the gear ratios inside, programmers call methods on objects without worrying about the data structures or algorithms that power them. This keeps code clean, reusable, and easier to change—if the blender’s motor is upgraded, your recipe stays the same.  
So abstraction in OOP is the “black‑box” idea: expose what matters, hide what doesn’t.
