Imagine you’re a librarian who wants to read every book on a long shelf, but you only have one flashlight (the *cursor*). A **cursor** is like that flashlight—it points to a single spot so the computer can “look” at data row by row.

To prepare it for a big search, you first decide where the flashlight starts (set its initial position), choose how fast it moves (step size), and tell it which books matter (apply a filter). Then you turn on the light and let it sweep across the shelf one book at a time. Each time it lands on a new book, the computer can read that row’s information, store it if needed, or decide to skip ahead.
