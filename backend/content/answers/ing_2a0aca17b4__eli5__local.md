Think of a machine‑learning system as a kitchen where data are the ingredients, models are the recipes, and predictions are the dishes you serve to customers. An **API (Application Programming Interface)** is like the kitchen’s service window: it lets chefs (your code) hand off orders (requests) and receive finished meals (responses) without stepping into the cooking area.

When you debug or observe the system, you watch what passes through that window—what ingredients are requested, how long each dish takes to cook, whether any errors pop up. By logging every “order” and “delivery,” you can see if the kitchen is busy, if a recipe needs tweaking, or if a utensil broke down.

So, an API is simply a well‑defined set of rules that lets different parts of your system talk to each other, and it’s the spot where you monitor performance, catch mistakes, and keep everything running smoothly.
