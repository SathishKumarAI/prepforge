### Why the job market in 2026 will shift around AI literacy

The *fundamental problem* is that firms want to **maximise marginal productivity** while keeping costs low.  
AI systems reduce the cost of a worker’s output by automating routine decisions, but they also **raise the skill ceiling**: an employee must now be able to design, supervise, and interpret AI outputs.  
If we model a firm’s total cost \(C = wL + \alpha A\) where \(w\) is wage per human labor hour \(L\), \(A\) is the amount of AI‑enabled work, and \(\alpha\) captures the training & oversight cost, optimisation tells us firms will increase \(A\) until \(\partial C/\partial L = 0\). This happens when the marginal benefit of an extra human equals the marginal cost of their wage plus the extra supervision required.  

Consequently, the *non‑technical* demand—communication, critical thinking, ethical judgment—becomes a differentiator. These skills are **non‑redundant** because they cannot be replicated by current AI models; they also scale with the complexity of problems that AI can’t solve yet.

Thus, employers will value people who can act as *human data curators*, not just workers. This creates a virtuous cycle where demand for soft‑skills drives higher wages and better AI tools, reshaping the 2026 job market.
