Imagine you’re reading a mystery novel where the next word is a surprise. **Perplexity** measures how “surprised” the model gets when it sees that next word. If the book’s sentences are very predictable, the model will be *not surprised* (low perplexity). If the story jumps around wildly, the model feels *confused* (high perplexity).

Think of a weather forecaster: if you can predict tomorrow’s temperature with great confidence, your “surprise” score is low. In language modeling, we ask, “Given all the words so far, how well does the model guess the next one?” The lower the average surprise, the better the model understands the text. Thus, perplexity is a simple way to say: *How much uncertainty does the model have about what comes next?*
