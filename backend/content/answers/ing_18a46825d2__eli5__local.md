Think of an AI as a chef that can cook almost anything, but only if you give it the right ingredients and instructions.

**Prompt engineering** is like telling the chef exactly what dish you want: “Make me a spicy tomato soup.” You’re crafting the sentence (the recipe) so the AI knows what flavor profile to aim for. It’s all about wording, length, and key details that steer the model’s output.

**Context engineering** is like setting up the kitchen before the chef starts cooking. You preload the kitchen with pantry staples—like a list of common spices, previous recipes you liked, or even a short background story about your taste preferences. This “context” lets the AI understand the broader scene and make better decisions while it’s cooking.

So, prompt engineering is the specific recipe line; context engineering is the well‑stocked kitchen that shapes how the chef interprets any recipe. Both work together to get delicious results.
