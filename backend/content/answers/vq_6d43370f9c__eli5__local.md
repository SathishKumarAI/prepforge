Think of your dataset as a garden that you want to harvest fruit from.  
If the soil is full of weeds, stones and leftover pests, any plant that grows will be weak or contaminated, even if it looks healthy at first glance.  
Data cleansing is like weeding, removing dead leaves, and treating the plants so every seedling can grow strong.  

When you clean data you remove duplicates (extra weeds), correct mistakes (potted plants with wrong labels), fill missing pieces (watering dry spots) and standardise formats (making sure all plant tags use the same language).  
A garden that’s been tidied up yields a bountiful, reliable harvest; similarly, a cleaned dataset lets AI models learn true patterns instead of garbage noise.  
Without this step, your model will keep learning wrong things—just as a farmer would waste time picking spoiled fruit.
