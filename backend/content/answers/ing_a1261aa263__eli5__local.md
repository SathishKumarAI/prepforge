Imagine you’re building a smart robot that can learn to play a video game just by watching how people move the controller. In machine learning, that “smart robot” is called an **agent**. The agent’s job is to decide what action to take (like moving left or right) based on what it sees (the current screen).  

Creating an agent means:  
1. **Defining its world** – What information can it see? (The game screen, score, etc.)  
2. **Specifying its actions** – What moves can it make? (Press a button, tap the screen)  
3. **Choosing how to learn** – Pick an algorithm that tells the agent how to improve: maybe “trial‑and‑error” (reinforcement learning), or “copying examples” (supervised learning).  

Just as you’d give a child rules about safe play and then let them practice, we give the agent a set of rules (the algorithm) and let it try many games until it gets better. That’s the heart of building an ML agent: a little mind that observes, acts, learns from feedback, and keeps getting smarter.
