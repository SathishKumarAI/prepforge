**Why a citation is essential (first‑principles view)**  
The core problem any scientific claim solves is *verifiability*. A reader must be able to trace an idea back to its origin, check the derivation, and evaluate its assumptions. Without a citation the claim becomes a black box; with it, the chain of reasoning remains transparent.  

**How a proper citation works**  
1. **Attribution** – credit the original author for intellectual labor.  
2. **Traceability** – give enough detail (author, year, page) that the reader can locate the exact passage.  
3. **Contextualization** – situate the idea within the broader literature, showing its place in an evolving dialogue.  

The *Deep Learning* text (Goodfellow, Bengio & Courville, 2016) is often cited because it consolidates foundational concepts—neural architectures, back‑propagation, regularisation—in a single, peer‑reviewed source. When you cite it, you are signalling that the underlying mathematics and empirical results have undergone community scrutiny.

**Non‑obvious insight**  
Citations act as *semantic anchors* in knowledge networks. Each citation adds weight to a node (the cited work) and alters the topology of the scholarly graph. Thus, citing “Deep Learning” does more than obey etiquette; it strengthens that book’s influence on subsequent research, making future citations easier to find—a self‑reinforcing mechanism often invisible to newcomers.
