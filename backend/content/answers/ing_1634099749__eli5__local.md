Imagine an old radio that can play music, but its speaker is a bit weak and sometimes drops out. Instead of stopping the whole show when the speaker hiccups, the radio keeps playing the song through a small backup speaker and fades the main one until it comes back. That’s *graceful degradation*: the system still works, just at a lower level, while it fixes or recovers the problem.

In AI, graceful degradation means building error handling so that when something goes wrong—like a missing sensor reading or a slow network—the model doesn’t crash. It falls back to a simpler algorithm, uses cached data, or temporarily ignores the faulty part, keeping the overall service running. Once the issue is resolved, it returns to full performance. This keeps users safe and content even when hiccups happen.
