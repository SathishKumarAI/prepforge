Imagine you’re at a kitchen and your friend is cooking on the other side of a wall. You want to ask them to bring you a cup of coffee, but you can’t walk over. Instead, you call out “Hey, please bring me coffee!” Your friend hears the request, goes to the counter, grabs the coffee, and brings it back. In computer terms, that’s Remote Procedure Call (RPC).

**RPC** is a method that lets one program (the caller) ask another program on a different machine or process to run a function for you and return the result. The “procedure” is just a named block of code; “remote” means it lives elsewhere; “call” is the request. RPC hides all the networking details—just like your friend’s trip across the wall—so you can focus on what you want, not how to get it.
