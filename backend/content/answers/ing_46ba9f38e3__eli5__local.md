Think of multimodal AI as a chef that can cook dishes using not just ingredients (text) but also spices, colors, sounds, and textures—anything you can feed it. To see how good the chef really is, we give it a set of recipe challenges that test each sense: “Describe this picture in words,” “Generate a short story from a melody,” or “Summarize a video clip.” These recipe sets are **benchmarks**; they’re standardized tests so every AI can be compared fairly.

When many chefs try the same recipes, we tally their scores on a public **leaderboard**, just like a cooking competition’s score sheet. The leaderboard shows who produces the most realistic descriptions, the most coherent videos, or the best cross‑modal translations. It lets researchers see progress over time and spot which techniques work best across different senses. In short, benchmarks are the recipe cards; leaderboards are the scoreboard that ranks every multimodal model’s performance.
