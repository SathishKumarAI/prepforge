Think of a robot learning to recognize pictures like a child learning to sort toys.  
At first the child just watches many examples: “This is a ball, this is a truck.” The child notices patterns—roundness, color, size—and starts guessing.  
Machine learning does exactly that but with data and math. A **model** is the robot’s brain; it has adjustable knobs called *parameters* (like muscle memory). We give it a big pile of labeled pictures (*training data*) and let it adjust those knobs so its guesses match the labels as closely as possible—this is called **training**.  
After training, when a new picture appears, the model uses what it learned to decide whether it’s a ball or a truck, just like the child who now knows how to sort toys quickly.
