Think of a database as a giant digital filing cabinet that keeps all your data in neat drawers (tables).  
**DML** – “Data Manipulation Language” – are the tools you use to move, change, or remove files inside those drawers.

* **INSERT** is like adding a new file to a drawer.  
* **UPDATE** is like editing an existing file—changing its contents without moving it.  
* **DELETE** is like tearing out a file entirely from the drawer.  

These commands don’t touch the structure of the cabinet itself (that’s handled by DDL). They simply work on the data inside, letting you add new records, fix mistakes, or remove old ones whenever you need. In short, DML statements are the everyday actions that keep your database’s contents current and accurate.
