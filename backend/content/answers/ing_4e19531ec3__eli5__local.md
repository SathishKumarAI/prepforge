Think of building an AI agent like baking a loaf of bread that tastes just right for your family.  

**1. Gather ingredients (Define goals & data).**  
You decide the flavor you want—sourdough, whole‑grain, or sweet—and collect flour, water, yeast, and spices. In AI terms, that’s setting objectives and gathering training data.

**2. Mix & knead (Model design).**  
You combine the ingredients and knead until the dough is elastic. Here you pick a model architecture—like choosing a neural network type—and set its initial “kneading” parameters.

**3. Let it rise (Train).**  
You let the dough sit in a warm spot; it expands as yeast ferments. Training lets the agent learn patterns from data, gradually improving.

**4. Taste test (Evaluate).**  
Baking time is short—just enough to see if the loaf is rising and has good color. Evaluation checks accuracy or performance metrics.

**5. Adjust & bake again (Iterate).**  
If it’s too dense, you tweak flour or yeast; if too sweet, adjust sugar. In AI, you refine data, tweak hyper‑parameters, or change architecture based on evaluation, then retrain.

Repeat this cycle—mix, rise, taste, adjust—until the loaf (agent) consistently meets your taste buds (requirements). That’s iterating across the agent development lifecycle: a continuous loop of design, training, testing, and refinement.
