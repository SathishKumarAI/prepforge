Imagine you’re baking a cake but you never taste it while it’s cooking. The batter might be too dry, the sugar could be missing, or the oven temperature could be off—yet you finish and serve it anyway, hoping everyone likes it. In AI, **evaluations (evals)** are that taste‑test: they check if a model is learning right, spotting mistakes before deployment.

Without evals, problems slip through unnoticed:

* **Bias** – The model may give unfair answers because it never saw enough diverse examples.
* **Hallucination** – It can invent facts, like a cake recipe with nonexistent ingredients.
* **Safety gaps** – Dangerous or inappropriate outputs go unchecked, just as an untested cake could be unsafe to eat.

Evals are the safety net that catches these flaws early. Without them, your AI is like a blind baker: you might finish something, but it may not be good, fair, or safe for users.
