Think of a **tuple** as a *sealed lunchbox*.  
You put a few items inside—maybe an apple, a sandwich, and a cookie—and then close the lid so nothing can spill out or change while you’re on the go. In Python, a tuple is a collection of values that is *immutable* (unchangeable) once created.  

```python
my_tuple = ("apple", "sandwich", "cookie")
```

Just like the lunchbox keeps its contents in order and you can’t rearrange them later, a tuple remembers the order of its items and you can’t add, remove, or modify elements after it’s made. If you need a changeable version, use a **list**, which is an open bag that lets you shuffle things around freely.
