Imagine teaching a puppy to fetch. At first, you show it a ball and give it a treat when it brings the ball back. The puppy repeats that until it learns the pattern: *ball → fetch → treat*.  
In machine learning, we do exactly that—only instead of a puppy, we have a computer; instead of treats, we give “scores” that tell the program how close its guesses are to the real answer. Each time the program makes a guess, we adjust it a little bit so that the next guess is better. Over many trials (the training data), the program learns the hidden rule that turns input into correct output.

Key terms:  
* **Training data** – examples the computer uses to learn.  
* **Model** – the set of rules or weights the computer builds.  
* **Loss function** – a way to measure how wrong a guess is, guiding the adjustments.  

Just as you refine your puppy’s training with more practice and clearer rewards, we train a model by feeding it more data and tweaking its parameters until it performs well on new, unseen situations.
