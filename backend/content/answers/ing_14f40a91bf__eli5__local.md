Imagine your favorite playlist of songs. Each song is turned into a tiny “music card” that lists how many times it has the note C, how often the rhythm slows down, whether the singer uses a high or low voice, etc. Those numbers are like **embeddings** – they give every song a short list of characteristics.

Now picture all those cards spread out on a big table. The distance between any two cards tells you how similar the songs are: cards that look close together mean the songs sound alike; cards far apart sound different. That whole table is a **vector space** – an invisible map where every song lives as a point, and you can measure straight‑line distances between them.

Normalization is like making sure all your music cards have the same size and scale before you spread them out. By adjusting each card so its numbers add up to the same total (or have the same length), you prevent one loud instrument from dominating the map. This lets the real relationships—like shared rhythm or harmony—show up clearly, just as a balanced playlist makes it easier to spot which songs truly belong together.
