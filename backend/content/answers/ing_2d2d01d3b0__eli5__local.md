**Pitfall 19: Ignoring Hallucination Risk**

Imagine a chef who has all the ingredients but keeps adding spices “just because they look nice.” The dish ends up tasting wild and unpredictable—sometimes great, sometimes awful. In AI, a *hallucination* is when a model invents facts or details that aren’t in its training data. It’s like the chef sprinkling random flavors: the answer looks polished but can be wrong or misleading.

Because language models learn patterns, not truth, they often “fill in” gaps with plausible‑looking text. If we ignore this risk and trust every output, we may spread misinformation or make poor decisions. The fix is to treat model responses as drafts that need human review—just as a chef tastes the dish before serving it.
