**How AirTags Work (and Their Limits)**  
Think of an AirTag like a tiny, battery‑powered “digital post‑it” that sits on your keys or backpack. It sends out a weak Bluetooth signal to nearby Apple devices. If any iPhone comes close, the device whispers the AirTag’s ID back to you through the Find My app. The AirTag itself never stores location data; it just hands off the information to other phones.

**Why They’re Not Perfect**  
1. **Bluetooth range** – About 30 feet in open air; walls or metal can block it.  
2. **No GPS** – It relies on strangers’ devices, so if no one passes by, you get “unknown” instead of a map spot.  
3. **Battery life** – One coin‑cell lasts ~1 year; once dead, the tag is silent and invisible to Find My.  

So, AirTags are great for spotting lost items in crowds or near home, but they can’t track you anywhere like a GPS tracker would.
