Imagine you’re a librarian who must answer visitors’ questions quickly, but the books are huge and only one shelf fits in your eye at a time. **Retrieval** is picking out the right book (the information you need). **Context** is remembering why that book matters to the visitor’s question—like noting the specific chapter or page. The *tension* comes when you try to pull a large section of text: it may contain everything you need, but your mind can’t hold all those details at once.

**Chunking strategies** solve this by breaking the big shelf into smaller, meaningful piles. For example, instead of grabbing an entire novel, you first grab the table of contents (context), then only the relevant chapter (retrieval). This keeps each pile small enough for your mind to process while still giving you the precise answer. The trick is balancing how much context you keep with how much detail you retrieve—just enough to answer without overwhelming yourself.
