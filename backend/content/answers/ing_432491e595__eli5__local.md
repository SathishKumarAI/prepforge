Imagine you’re looking for a forgotten recipe in a huge cookbook. A plain search would just give you the page number that says “recipe,” but it won’t tell you which one you want. Contextual retrieval is like having an assistant who knows what you’re craving—say, “I need a chocolate cake with nuts” and remembers where similar recipes are tucked away. The assistant reads the surrounding pages (the context) to decide which recipe matches best.

In AI, the “assistant” is a language model that looks at all the words around your query, not just the exact keywords. It learns from patterns in the text—like how people talk about cakes and nuts—to pull up the most relevant results. This way, retrieval isn’t just matching terms; it’s understanding the context to give you exactly what you’re looking for.
