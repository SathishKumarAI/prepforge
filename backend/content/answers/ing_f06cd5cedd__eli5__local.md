Imagine you’re building a giant, friendly library that can answer any question anyone asks about the world.  
A **system design** is the blueprint of that library: where books go, how visitors find them quickly, and how the librarians keep everything tidy even when thousands of people rush in at once.

For machine‑learning systems, the “books” are data sets, models, and algorithms. The design must decide:

* **Storage** – where to keep all the raw facts (like a warehouse) so they’re fast to fetch.  
* **Processing** – how to run learning jobs (the librarians training new books).  
* **Serving** – how to hand out predictions to users instantly (like a help desk that never waits).  

Just as a library uses shelves, indexes, and catalogues, an ML system uses databases, batch pipelines, and real‑time inference servers. The goal is reliability, speed, and the ability to grow without breaking. Think of it as designing a bustling, ever‑learning city where data flows smoothly and answers arrive on time.
