### On‑Policy Distillation in Reinforcement Learning

**Problem statement:**  
A *student* policy \( \pi_{\theta} \) must learn to imitate a *teacher* policy \( \pi^{*} \), but the teacher is only available as an oracle that can be queried while the agent interacts with the environment. We cannot pre‑collect a dataset of states and actions; instead, we must generate data on‑the‑fly.  

**Why on‑policy?**  
Because the student’s own state distribution \(d_{\pi_{\theta}}\) changes during training, any fixed off‑policy dataset quickly becomes stale: the teacher is queried in regions where the student never visits, yielding useless gradients. On‑policy distillation forces the student to *experience* the same trajectories that the teacher would traverse, ensuring that every gradient step is computed on states actually relevant to the current policy.

**Derivation:**  
The loss for a single trajectory \( \tau=(s_0,a_0,\dots,s_T) \) generated by \( \pi_{\theta} \) is  
\[
L(\theta)= -\sum_{t=0}^{T}\log \pi^{*}(a_t|s_t).
\]
Taking the gradient w.r.t. \( \theta \) and using the policy‑gradient theorem gives
\[
\nabla_\theta L = -\mathbb{E}_{\tau\sim \pi_{\theta}}\!\left[\sum_{t}\nabla_\theta \log \pi_{\theta}(a_t|s_t)\,\frac{\pi^{*}(a_t|s_t)}{\pi_{\theta}(a_t|s_t)}\right].
\]
The ratio \( \frac{\pi^{*}}{\pi_{\theta}} \) is precisely the *importance weight* that corrects for the mismatch between teacher and student actions. This weighting turns the objective into a **KL‑divergence minimization** under the current state distribution, guaranteeing monotonic improvement when the ratio stays close to one.

**Deep insight:**  
The on‑policy importance weights are not merely a correction factor; they encode *how much* the student should trust the teacher at each step. If the student’s action probability is already high where the teacher also assigns high probability, the weight stays near unity and gradients vanish—no learning occurs. Thus, on‑policy distillation naturally focuses updates on *disagreement regions*, accelerating convergence without extra exploration cost.

**Bottom line:**  
On‑policy distillation turns imitation into an adaptive, self‑correcting policy‑gradient problem that respects the dynamic state distribution of reinforcement learning, yielding efficient transfer from expert to novice agents.
