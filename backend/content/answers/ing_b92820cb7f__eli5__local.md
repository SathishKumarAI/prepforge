Imagine you’re a chef preparing a dish for guests who live in different houses, each with its own kitchen setup—different ovens, spices, and utensils. To make sure every guest gets the same delicious meal, you pack your recipe, ingredients, and cooking tools into a single, sealed “kitchen kit.” No matter where it lands, the kit opens exactly as intended, no extra cleaning or adjustments needed.

That’s what Docker does for software. **Docker** is a tool that bundles an application together with everything it needs to run—its code, libraries, settings, and even its operating‑system bits—into a lightweight, portable unit called a *container*. When you ship the container, anyone can run it on any computer (Windows, Linux, macOS) without worrying about missing dependencies or version mismatches. Containers are isolated from each other, so they don’t interfere, but they share the host’s kernel, keeping them slim and fast. In short, Docker lets developers package and move software reliably, just like a chef’s ready‑to‑cook kit that works everywhere.
