Imagine an agent as a chef who runs a busy kitchen.  
**Short‑term memory** is like the countertop where ingredients are kept just before they’re cooked. It holds the current recipe, the spice amounts, and the next step – everything needed for the next few minutes of cooking. When the dish is finished or the counter clears, that information fades.

**Long‑term memory** is the pantry and cookbooks. Here the chef stores recipes learned over years, how to balance flavors, and patterns of customer preferences. These memories are kept for months or even decades, ready to be recalled whenever a new order comes in.

So, short‑term memory lets an agent act quickly on fresh data; long‑term memory gives it lasting knowledge that shapes future decisions.
