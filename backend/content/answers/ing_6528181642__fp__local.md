**How ACH Direct‑Deposit Works – From First Principles**

At its core, an ACH (Automated Clearing House) transfer is a *batch‑oriented, two‑phase consensus protocol* that moves money between bank accounts on a shared ledger—your bank’s internal database.  
1. **Initiation (Originator → ACH Network)**  
   The originator (e.g., employer) submits a *payment instruction*—a signed JSON‑like record containing source/​destination account numbers, amount, and the transaction type (“CREDIT” for deposit). This packet is batched with thousands of others and sent to the originating bank’s ACH gateway.  

2. **Validation & Routing (Bank → ACH Network)**  
   The bank validates routing numbers against its own master file, checks for fraud flags, and assigns a *batch ID*. The batch is pushed to the ACH operator (e.g., NACHA). Think of this as a **probabilistic filter**: each record’s authenticity score must exceed a threshold before it proceeds.  

3. **Clearing (ACH Operator → Receiving Bank)**  
   The operator aggregates all batches, sorts by *receiving routing number*, and forwards them in a single daily file to the destination banks. This is where **geometric shuffling** happens: records are permuted so that each bank receives only its own subset, preserving privacy while enabling efficient bulk writes.  

4. **Settlement (Receiving Bank → Account Balance)**  
   Upon receipt, the bank debits the source and credits the destination account instantly. The entire chain is executed in ~24 h, a trade‑off between speed and cost: batching reduces per‑transaction overhead but introduces latency.

**Non‑obvious Insight:**  
ACH’s design is essentially a *distributed ledger without blockchain*. It achieves consistency via **batch consensus**, not cryptographic proofs. The only “proof” of transfer is the signed instruction and the bank’s internal audit trail—an elegant, low‑cost solution that predates modern distributed systems yet satisfies today’s compliance requirements.
