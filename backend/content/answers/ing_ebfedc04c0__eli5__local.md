Imagine you’re at a cooking contest and each judge tastes a different dish—some love spicy, others prefer sweet, some like salty. You only keep the dishes that everyone likes most. That’s an **ensemble** in machine learning: many “models” (the judges’ taste buds) look at the same data but each has its own way of making predictions.

Each model is a little “recipe.” Some might be very good at spotting sunny‑day patterns, others excel on rainy days. When they all give their answers, you combine them—often by taking a majority vote or averaging scores—so that errors made by one are corrected by the others. The result is a more reliable, balanced prediction, just as the judges’ collective opinion gives a fairer verdict than any single taste alone.

In short: an ensemble lets many simple models work together like a panel of experts to give you a stronger, more accurate final decision.
