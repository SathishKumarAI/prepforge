Imagine your brain as a huge library.  
A **Vector DB** is like a photo album: each picture (a piece of information) is stored with a short description that tells the computer how “close” it feels to other pictures. When you ask a question, the system looks for photos whose descriptions are nearest—quick and great for finding similar ideas.  

A **Knowledge Graph** is more like a map with streets and landmarks: each fact is a node (a person, place, or thing) connected by labeled roads that show how they relate (“works at”, “is a cousin of”). This structure lets you trace chains of meaning—ideal for deep explanations or discovering new connections.  

Choose the album when speed and similarity matter; choose the map when relationships and context are king.
