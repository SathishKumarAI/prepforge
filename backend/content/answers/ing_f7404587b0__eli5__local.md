Imagine you’re a librarian who must decide which shelf a new book belongs on: fiction, history, science, or cooking.  
In machine learning, the computer is that librarian, but it learns from examples instead of being told each rule by hand.

**What is machine learning?**  
It’s a way to let a computer look at many labeled examples (e.g., pictures tagged “dog” or “cat”) and then discover patterns on its own. Once it has learned those patterns, it can **classify** new, unseen items into the correct category—just like placing a brand‑new book on the right shelf.

Key terms:  
- *Training data*: examples the computer learns from.  
- *Model*: the set of rules the computer builds.  
- *Classification*: assigning each new item to one of several categories based on the model.

So, classification is simply the computer’s answer—“This belongs in the fiction shelf” – after it has learned how to recognize what makes that shelf special.
