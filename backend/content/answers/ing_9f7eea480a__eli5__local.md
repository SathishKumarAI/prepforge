Imagine a smart robot that can learn from watching how people play chess, then decide the best move on its own. That’s what an **agent** does in machine learning: it observes a situation (the “state”), picks an action, and receives feedback (a reward) telling it if it did well. Over time it learns which actions lead to higher rewards—just like the robot improves at chess by practicing.

Agents appear everywhere:  
* A home‑assistant that learns your schedule to suggest coffee times.  
* A recommendation engine that recommends shows based on what you’ve liked before.  
* An automated trading bot that buys or sells stocks when patterns it has learned appear.  

In each case, the agent’s “state” is the current data (your calendar, viewing history, market prices). Its “action” is a decision (suggest coffee, recommend a show, trade a stock). The reward tells it whether the choice helped—more coffee in the morning, higher user satisfaction, or profit. By repeating this cycle, the agent becomes better at its job, just like our chess‑playing robot.
