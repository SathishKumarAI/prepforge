Imagine you’re hosting a dinner party. In the past, you’d rent an entire kitchen (a “server”) and keep it running all day, even when no one was cooking. Serverless is like renting just the stove for the exact minutes you need—no extra cost or idle time. Function‑as‑a‑Service (FaaS) takes this a step further: instead of you standing over the stove, you hand a recipe card to a chef who instantly appears, cooks the dish, and disappears when done. Each recipe is a tiny “function” that runs only on demand, scales automatically as more guests arrive, and you pay only for the minutes it actually works. Over time, this shift from renting whole kitchens to hiring chefs for single dishes has made hosting faster, cheaper, and far more flexible.
