**Throughput** is how many data points your machine‑learning system can process in a given time—think of it as the speed of a factory line.

Imagine you’re making lemonade. Each cup takes a few seconds: squeezing lemons, adding sugar, pouring water, and filling glasses. If you can finish 30 cups every minute, that’s your throughput. In ML, each “cup” is one data sample (an image, sentence, etc.) being fed through the model to get an answer or prediction. A higher throughput means more samples are handled per second, which matters when you have a huge dataset or need real‑time responses.

So, just as a faster lemonade line serves more customers, a higher ML throughput lets your algorithm deliver results quicker and keep up with big workloads.
