Think of a data architecture like the blueprint and plumbing that keep a busy kitchen running smoothly.  
Just as a chef needs ovens, prep tables, storage shelves, and clear paths to move ingredients from fridge to stove, a machine‑learning system needs a clear layout for where data lives, how it’s moved, and who can use it.  

- **Data sources** are the raw ingredients (databases, sensors, files).  
- **Storage layers** are the pantry and freezer—where data is kept in its original form or cleaned and organized.  
- **Processing pipelines** are the prep stations that chop, blend, and cook the data into usable “recipes” for models.  
- **Access controls** act like kitchen rules, ensuring only authorized cooks can touch certain ingredients.

A good data architecture keeps everything tidy, fast, and safe, so when a model is called to “cook up” predictions, it gets exactly what it needs without getting stuck in traffic or mixing the wrong flavors.
