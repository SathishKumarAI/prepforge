Think of building a house. **Planning** is deciding what you’ll build, where it goes, and how the parts fit together. On one end of the *planning spectrum* you do all that in one big picture—draw the whole blueprint at once (high‑level planning). On the other end you start with the tiny bricks: lay each stone, nail each board, then slowly assemble them into rooms, floors, and finally the house (low‑level, detailed planning).

**Decomposition** is the process of breaking that big picture into smaller, manageable pieces—like splitting the blueprint into “foundation,” “walls,” “roof.” Each piece can be planned separately, then put back together. In AI, an agent might first decide it wants to *cook dinner* (high‑level plan). It decomposes that goal into *buy ingredients*, *prepare sauce*, *cook pasta*, and each of those becomes a sub‑plan until the steps are simple enough for the robot to execute. The spectrum shows how much you plan at once versus how many layers of detail you break it down into.
