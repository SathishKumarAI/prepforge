**Load balancing in machine learning** is like a busy food‑truck park that serves many hungry customers at once.

Imagine each customer orders a different dish (a data request). If one truck (a server) has to cook all the dishes alone, it gets overwhelmed and waits for everyone else. A load balancer is the friendly manager who watches the queue: when a new order arrives, they send it to the truck that’s least busy or has the fastest cooking time. That way no single truck stalls, all customers get their meals quickly, and the park runs smoothly.

*Key terms:*  
- **Server** – a computer that runs the ML model.  
- **Load balancer** – a piece of software (or hardware) that distributes incoming requests among servers to keep them working efficiently.
