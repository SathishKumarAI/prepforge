Imagine you’re buying a new kitchen robot that can cook any recipe you give it. Before you hand over the money, the shopkeeper checks for obvious problems: does it heat evenly? Does it keep food safe? Likewise, interviewers look for “red flags” when you talk about large language models (LLMs) to see if your understanding is solid.

**Red‑flag signs**

1. **“It just reads data.”**  
   An LLM doesn’t *know* facts; it predicts the next word based on patterns it saw while training. Saying it “understands” is like saying a calculator “knows math.”

2. **“No limits, it can do anything.”**  
   LLMs have biases, can hallucinate (make up facts), and only work well for text they’ve seen before. Expecting perfect accuracy is like trusting a weather app that never updates.

3. **“It’s just software; no training needed.”**  
   Building an LLM requires huge datasets, compute power, and careful tuning—much more than flipping a switch on a piece of hardware.

4. **Ignoring safety and bias.**  
   If you overlook how biased data can make the model repeat stereotypes, it’s like ignoring that your robot might cook food in unsafe ways.

Seeing any of these tells interviewers: “I’m missing the core mechanics.” Just as a chef needs to know how ingredients work together, an AI practitioner must grasp how LLMs learn and what they can’t do.
