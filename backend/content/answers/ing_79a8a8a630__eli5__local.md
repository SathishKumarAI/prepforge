Imagine you’re at a big party where everyone is holding a name tag and a small card that says who they know. The party itself is the **graph data model**: each person is a **node** (a point), and every handshake or friendly wave is an **edge** (a line) connecting two nodes.  

In social‑network sites like Facebook or Instagram, the “party” lives in computers. Each user’s profile becomes a node; friendships, follows, likes, comments are edges that show relationships. The system stores this as a network of linked points so it can quickly ask questions: “Who does Alice know?” or “Which friends of mine also follow Bob?” Because all connections are explicit and direct, the platform can find paths (like mutual friends) and suggest new ones instantly.

**Key terms**  
- **Node:** an individual entity (a user).  
- **Edge:** a relationship between two nodes (friendship, follow, comment).  

This graph structure lets social networks understand and act on people’s connections in real time.
