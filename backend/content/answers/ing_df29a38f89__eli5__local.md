Think of a machine‑learning model as a **recipe book** that turns raw ingredients (data) into delicious dishes (predictions). The *architecture* is the layout of the kitchen: how many stations, what appliances, and where they’re arranged.

In deep learning, the “stations” are layers—groups of tiny calculators called neurons. Some layers act like a whisking station (convolutional layers that spot patterns), others like a mixing bowl (fully‑connected layers that combine everything). The *connections* between stations are the recipe’s instructions: weights that decide how much each ingredient influences the next step.

Just as a chef chooses a specific kitchen layout for the dish he wants, an engineer picks an architecture—number of layers, types, and how they’re wired—to solve a particular problem. The better the layout matches the task, the tastier (more accurate) the final prediction.
