Imagine you’re looking for a recipe in a giant cookbook that’s split into two parts: a big, colorful picture section and a small, text‑heavy index. If you only look at the pictures, you might miss the exact ingredient list; if you only read the index, you’ll ignore the visual clues that help you decide which dish looks best.

Hybrid search in AI does exactly this— it combines two ways of finding information: **semantic matching** (understanding meaning) and **exact keyword matching** (looking for precise words). Think of semantic matching as a friendly librarian who can guess what kind of recipe you want even if you describe it vaguely, while exact matching is the librarian’s ability to pull up every book that contains your exact phrase. By mixing both, the search engine gives you results that are both relevant in meaning and accurate in terms, just like getting the best recipe from a cookbook that uses both pictures and text together.
