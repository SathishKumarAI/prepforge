Think of machine learning like training a puppy to fetch different kinds of toys.  
The *data* are the toys you throw: each toy has a name (the label) and some features—its size, shape, weight—that help you tell them apart.  
A *model* is the puppy’s memory; it learns from many examples how to map those features to the correct toy name.  
When you give it a new toy, the model predicts its name by looking at what it has seen before—a bit like the puppy recognizing patterns in the toys’ feel and sound.

Key practical tricks:  
- **Feature engineering** is choosing which aspects of the toy (size, color) to focus on.  
- **Cross‑validation** checks that the puppy doesn’t just memorize one set of toys but truly learns general patterns.  
- **Regularization** keeps the puppy from over‑exciting and picking up every odd detail.

In an interview, explain this process: show how you collect data (toys), design features (features), train a model (puppy), validate its performance (cross‑validation), and tune it to avoid overfitting (regularization). That’s the core of practical ML.
