**TL;DR – Perplexity**

Think of a language model like a smart guess‑maker at a game show. Every time the host says a word, the model tries to predict what comes next. **Perplexity** is the average “surprise” the model feels for each correct answer. If it’s very surprised (high perplexity), its guesses are often wrong; if it rarely surprises itself (low perplexity), it’s on point.

Imagine you’re reading a mystery novel and trying to guess who’ll shout next. A low perplexity reader quickly narrows down the suspects, while a high perplexity one keeps getting startled by unexpected twists. The lower the perplexity score, the better the model is at predicting words—just like a seasoned detective who rarely gets caught off‑guard.
