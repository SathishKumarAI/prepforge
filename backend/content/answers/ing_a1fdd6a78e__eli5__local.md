Imagine you’re trying to teach a robot how to draw a cat. Every time the robot draws, you look at its picture and compare it with a perfect reference cat. The “loss function” is like a scorecard that tells you how far off the robot’s drawing is from the reference—how many lines are wrong, where the ears should be but aren’t, etc. A lower score means the robot is getting closer to the ideal cat.

In machine learning, this scorecard (the loss) measures the difference between what the model predicts and the true answer. The training process repeatedly tweaks the model’s internal settings so that the loss keeps dropping, just as a painter would keep adjusting strokes until the drawing looks like the reference. Think of the loss function as the feedback loop that turns guesses into sharper predictions.
