Imagine you’re teaching a new recipe to someone who only knows a handful of dishes. Instead of giving them the full cookbook, you show them **just a couple** of example recipes (the “few shots”) and then ask them to create a new one that follows the same style. In machine learning, a large language model is like an expert cook that has read countless recipes (its training data). A *prompt* is what we give it—an instruction or question—to stir its imagination.  

**Few‑shot prompt engineering** means we hand the model a small set of examples in the prompt: “Recipe 1: … ; Recipe 2: … ; Now write a recipe for a vegan chocolate cake.” The model learns from those snippets and then produces something new that fits the pattern. It’s not learning from scratch; it’s simply using the clues we provide to guess what comes next, just as you’d guide the cook with a few sample dishes.
