Think of a big kitchen where you want to bake a cake. A **tool** is like the whisk or the oven—each one does a single job and you use it directly. A **subagent** is a helper who knows how to handle that tool and can also decide when, how often, and in what order to use it, maybe even asking for more ingredients if needed.

In AI, an agent (the kitchen manager) may have many subagents: one to read the recipe, another to fetch data, another to check temperature. Each subagent is a small, self‑contained program that can act on its own but also talks to the main agent and to other subagents. This layered approach lets you build powerful AI without making one monolithic machine that tries to do everything at once—just like a chef who delegates tasks to trusted helpers instead of juggling every step alone.
