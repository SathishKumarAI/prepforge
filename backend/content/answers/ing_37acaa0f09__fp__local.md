## Why a “title” matters

In an era where **AI is the new infrastructure**, every job that orchestrates or consumes it has to answer two questions:

1. *What problem does this role solve?*  
2. *How will others recognise and trust that solution?*

Titles are the public shorthand for both answers. They encode a **latent function**—the set of decisions, risks, and resources a person owns. A well‑named role lets teams align budgets, set expectations, and measure impact without endless negotiation.

## The chaos in 2026

- **Rapidly evolving tech stacks** (LLMs, multimodal agents, edge inference) blur traditional boundaries.  
- **Cross‑disciplinary overlap** (ML Ops meets Data Governance, AI Ethics meets Product).  
- **Talent scarcity** forces companies to hire “AI‑ready” people who may not fit neatly into legacy titles.

Because of this, a CTO must translate a *problem space* into a *role taxonomy* that is both internally coherent and externally marketable. Otherwise the organization pays for the wrong skill set or loses talent to clearer competitors.
