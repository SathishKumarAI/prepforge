Imagine you’re planning a big dinner.

A **Sequence of Chains** is like following one long recipe: each step (chain) feeds into the next, and the food moves linearly from prep to plate. It’s simple, predictable, and great when everything can be done in order.

A **Multi‑Agent Graph** is like having several chefs (agents) working together on different parts of the menu at once. Each chef handles a dish or ingredient, and they share updates through a network (the graph). This works best when tasks overlap, need quick coordination, or you want to use many brains at the same time.

So choose a Sequence of Chains when your problem can be broken into a straight line of steps; pick a Multi‑Agent Graph when you have many interacting parts that benefit from parallel teamwork.
