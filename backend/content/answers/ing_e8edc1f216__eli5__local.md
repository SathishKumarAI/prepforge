Imagine you’re baking a cake and want to know if it’s really good. First, you gather a **sample of taste‑tests**—different people trying the same slice. That sample is your **evaluation set**: a small, representative portion of all possible customers (data points) that lets you judge the cake without cooking an entire batch.

Next, you keep track of every step: how long it takes to bake, how much sugar was added, whether the oven temperature changed. These records are **observability**—the data that tells you *why* the cake turned out sweet or dry. Together, the evaluation set gives you a clear verdict (“good” or “needs improvement”), while observability shows you exactly what parts of your recipe caused it. In AI, building an eval set means selecting a fair slice of test data, and observability is logging model inputs, outputs, and internal states so you can diagnose performance issues.
