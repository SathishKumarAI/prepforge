Think of a grocery store that receives fresh produce from farmers.  
The raw fruits and veggies are the **raw data**—they’re unwashed, unsorted, and not yet ready to eat.  

A **data engineer** is like the store’s prep crew. They wash, cut, label, and package the produce so shoppers can buy exactly what they need. That cleaning, filtering, and organizing is what we call **processed data**.

Processed data has a few key traits:  
- **Cleaned:** bad or missing bits are fixed or removed.  
- **Structured:** it’s arranged in tables or files that programs can read easily.  
- **Enriched:** extra information (like price tags or expiry dates) is added.

So, processed data is the tidy, ready‑to‑use version of raw data—just as prepped fruit makes a recipe easier to follow.
