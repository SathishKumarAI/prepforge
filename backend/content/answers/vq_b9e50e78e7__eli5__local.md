Think of raw data like a first‑draft manuscript you keep before editing. You want to hold onto it long enough that you can go back if something in the finished book turns out wrong or if new readers ask for original wording. In practice, most companies keep raw data for 1–3 years—just long enough to answer future questions and meet legal or regulatory deadlines. After that, you usually archive it (like putting the manuscript in a safe box) so it stays readable but doesn’t clutter your active workspace.
