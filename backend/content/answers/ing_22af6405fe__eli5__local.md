Imagine building a smart robot that learns to play chess.  
**Constraints** are the rules of the real world that limit what the robot can do: it has only a few kilograms of battery, its brain (CPU) can process so many calculations per second, and it must obey safety laws that keep humans safe.  
**Assumptions** are the guesses the designer makes to make the project possible: “The chess board will be standard size,” “Players will use typical opening moves,” or “The robot’s sensors will read a clear board.”  

In AI projects we always list these constraints and assumptions so everyone knows what the system can truly achieve and where it might fail. Without them, we’d build something that looks great on paper but crashes in the real world.
