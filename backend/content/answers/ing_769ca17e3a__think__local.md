**Thinking Process for Explaining Why an Innovation Is “Really Important”**

1. **Clarify the Innovation & Context**  
   - Identify exactly what the innovation is (e.g., transformer architecture).  
   - State any assumptions: audience’s ML background, scope of impact (research vs industry), timeframe.

2. **Choose a Mental Model**  
   - Use *Impact‑Value* framework: **(1) Problem Solved / Gap Closed**, **(2) Scale/Reach**, **(3) Enabling New Possibilities**.  
   - Alternatively, the *Innovation Diffusion* model (relative advantage, compatibility, complexity, trialability, observability).

3. **Step‑by‑Step Reasoning**  
   - **Problem Solved:** Show how previous models failed (e.g., RNNs’ vanishing gradients).  
   - **Technical Leap:** Explain the core mechanism that overcomes it (attention heads, self‑attention).  
   - **Performance Gains:** Cite benchmark improvements, speed, data efficiency.  
   - **Broader Impact:** Discuss downstream products, cross‑domain adoption (NLP → vision, reinforcement learning).  
   - **Future Enablement:** Highlight how it unlocks new research directions or democratizes ML.

4. **Avoid Common Traps**  
   - Don’t over‑generalize (“all problems solved”).  
   - Avoid hype without evidence—use concrete metrics.  
   - Don’t ignore limitations (compute cost, data bias).  
   - Stay clear of jargon; explain terms for mixed audiences.

5. **Sanity‑Check & Communicate**  
   - Re‑read the explanation: does it flow logically?  
   - Ask a peer to summarize in one sentence—if they capture the core impact, you’re good.  
   - When speaking, start with a hook (“Imagine…”), then walk through the three pillars of importance, and finish with a forward‑looking statement.
