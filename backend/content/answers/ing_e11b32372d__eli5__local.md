In machine learning, “relationships” are like the hidden rules that connect what you see (the input) to what you want to know (the output).  
Think of a recipe book: each ingredient (input feature) has a specific taste contribution (weight), and when you combine them in just the right proportions you get a dish (prediction). The model learns those proportions from many example dishes.  

**Key terms:**  
- **Feature** – an input piece of data, like “age” or “price.”  
- **Weight** – how strongly that feature influences the result.  
- **Bias** – a constant offset added to the weighted sum, like a base seasoning.  

The model’s job is to find the best set of weights and bias so that its predictions match real outcomes as closely as possible. This mapping from inputs to outputs is what we call the relationship learned by the algorithm.
