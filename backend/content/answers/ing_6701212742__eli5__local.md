Imagine you’re a detective trying to solve a mystery by exploring many possible clues at once. **Beam search** is like having a team of detectives who keep only the most promising clues (the “best” partial solutions) and drop the rest as they go deeper into the investigation.

In language models, each step in generating a sentence can produce thousands of next‑word options. Beam search keeps a fixed number—say *k* (the **beam width**)—of the top candidate sequences after every step. It then expands only those candidates, scoring them again, and repeats until the whole sentence is built or a stopping rule is met.

So beam search trades exhaustive exploration for focused breadth: it prunes low‑probability paths early while still allowing multiple promising branches to coexist, much like a detective team chasing several strong leads simultaneously.
