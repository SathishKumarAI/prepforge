Think of a bakery that sells cupcakes.  
**Batch economics** is like the bakery baking all its cupcakes at once, then shipping them out later. The cost (flour, oven time) is fixed for each batch, so you know exactly how much it will take to make 100 cupcakes. In AI, batch processing works the same: you pay a set fee to run many tasks together, and the price doesn’t change while they’re queued.

**Async economics** is like the bakery accepting orders one at a time, baking each cupcake as soon as an order comes in. The cost depends on how busy the kitchen is right then—if there’s a rush, you pay more for priority service. In AI, async workloads charge based on real‑time usage: the more CPU or GPU time you consume at that moment, the higher your bill.

**FinOps (Financial Operations)** is the manager who watches both models, deciding when to bake in bulk to save money and when to use quick, on‑demand baking for urgent orders.  
**Token economics** is a way to make those decisions transparent: each cupcake is represented by a token that can be bought, sold, or traded. The price of the token rises when demand spikes (more async jobs) and falls when many batches are idle. This market‑based system lets users see exactly how much they’ll pay for instant AI power versus scheduled batch runs, just as a customer sees whether to buy a whole box of cupcakes or order one fresh at a time.
