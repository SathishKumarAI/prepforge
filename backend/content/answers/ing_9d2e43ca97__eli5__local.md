Think of searching a huge library. **Pure dense search** is like asking a very smart librarian who can read the gist of every book and instantly tells you which ones might be relevant, even if they use different wording. It’s fast but sometimes misses books that talk about the topic in unusual ways.

**Hybrid search** adds a second step: after the librarian gives you a shortlist, you hand each title to a human reader who looks at the exact sentences (or “keywords”) that match your query. This is like having a friend highlight the parts of a book that actually mention what you’re looking for before you read it.

Use hybrid search when you need higher precision—when the exact words matter (e.g., legal or medical documents) and you can afford a bit more time to double‑check the results. If speed and broad coverage are enough, pure dense search is fine.
