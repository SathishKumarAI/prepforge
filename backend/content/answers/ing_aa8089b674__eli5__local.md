Think of building a smart assistant that can answer questions about recipes.  
A **hybrid solution** mixes two kinds of “brain cells”:  

* **Rule‑based cells** – they follow clear, written instructions (like a cookbook).  
* **Learning cells** – they discover patterns from lots of recipe data on their own.

In a *horizontal* hybrid, both types sit side by side in the same layer. When a user asks a question, the rule‑based part checks whether a straightforward answer exists (e.g., “how long to boil pasta?”). If it can’t find a match, the learning part steps in and guesses based on what it has seen before (maybe suggesting alternatives from similar recipes).

So horizontal means the two brains work together at the same level, sharing the task instead of one waiting for the other. It’s like having a chef who follows the recipe but also listens to customer feedback and adjusts seasoning on the spot.
