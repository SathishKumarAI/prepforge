Imagine you’re planning a big city. The **logical data model** is like the city’s blueprint: it shows where roads, parks, and buildings should go, but it doesn’t yet decide what each building will actually be (a library or a grocery store). It tells us the *types of places* needed and how they connect—just as a logical model lists entities (customers, orders) and their relationships without worrying about the exact database tables.

The **data architecture** is the full city plan that turns those blueprints into reality. It chooses the specific materials, building codes, and zoning laws—deciding whether to use a SQL server or a NoSQL store, how to split data across servers, and what security rules apply. Together, the logical model says *what* we need; the architecture decides *how* we build it so everyone can navigate the city smoothly.
