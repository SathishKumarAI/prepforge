**Evaluating generative quality in multimodal systems**

1. **Fundamental problem**  
   A multimodal generator produces samples \(x\) (images), \(y\) (text), etc., from a latent code \(z\). The goal is to approximate an unknown joint distribution \(p_{\star}(x,y)\). Evaluation must decide whether the *marginals* and *joint structure* of generated pairs resemble reality.

2. **Why it must work this way**  
   - **Marginal fidelity**: Each modality should be individually plausible; otherwise a model could cheat by mixing unrelated samples.  
   - **Joint consistency**: The cross‑modal relationship (e.g., an image’s caption) encodes higher‑order structure that cannot be captured by marginal metrics alone.

3. **Deep principle – Information bottleneck**  
   Generative quality is essentially about *mutual information* \(I(z; x,y)\). A good model maximizes this while keeping the latent entropy low, ensuring a concise yet expressive representation. Evaluation metrics can be viewed as estimating how much shared information remains between modalities.

4. **Practical metrics derived from this**  
   - **Fréchet Inception Distance (FID)** for each modality and cross‑modal embeddings.  
   - **Conditional likelihoods** \(p(y|x)\) or \(p(x|y)\) via pretrained discriminators, approximating joint log‑likelihood.  
   - **Mutual information neural estimation (MINE)** between generated pairs.

5. **Non‑obvious insight**  
   *Sampling diversity and joint consistency are inversely coupled*: a generator that overfits to the training joint distribution may produce highly consistent but low‑diversity samples, while one that maximizes marginal entropy can lose cross‑modal coherence. Balancing these requires jointly optimizing a *joint‑entropy regularizer*—a term often omitted in standard benchmarks.

By framing evaluation as an information–theoretic trade‑off between marginal fidelity and joint consistency, we obtain rigorous, interpretable criteria that capture the essence of multimodal generative quality.
