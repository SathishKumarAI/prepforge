### Why a **signed‑authorization + receipt** pair is essential for the Work‑Contract Layer

The core problem any ML system faces is *trust*: who may trigger a model, what actions it can perform, and how we prove that an action actually occurred.  
If the contract layer merely records “request → model”, it treats the request as an oracle: anyone could spoof it, and downstream auditors cannot verify that the model behaved correctly.

1. **Authorization guarantees intent** – a cryptographic signature binds the caller’s identity to the specific tool call. Without this binding, a malicious actor could replay or forge calls, violating non‑repudiation.  
2. **Execution receipts provide auditability** – a signed receipt (hash of inputs, outputs, timestamps) proves that the tool executed *exactly* as requested. It turns the black‑box ML inference into an information‑theoretic evidence chain, enabling rollback or dispute resolution.

These two elements instantiate the deeper principle of *cryptographic accountability*: every state transition in the system is a verifiable event.  
A non‑obvious insight: **receipts should be generated by the tool itself** (not by the contract). This prevents the contract from being a single point of failure; if the tool signs its own output, an adversary cannot forge a receipt even if they control the contract layer. Thus, the Work‑Contract Layer becomes both *authoritative* and *tamper‑evident*, satisfying security, compliance, and reproducibility in ML pipelines.
