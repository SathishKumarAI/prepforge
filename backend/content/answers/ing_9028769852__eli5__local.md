**Paged Attention – a “memory‑bank” trick for big language models**

Imagine you’re reading a long novel, but your brain can only hold one chapter at a time. Every time you finish a chapter, you write a quick summary on a sticky note and put the chapter back in its place. Later, when you need a detail from that chapter again, you flip to the sticky note instead of rereading the whole text.

In large‑language models (LLMs), *attention* lets every word “look” at all other words. For very long texts this is like trying to remember an entire library in one go—impossible for most GPUs. **Paged attention** solves it by keeping only a small, recent “window” of words in fast memory and storing older chunks (“pages”) on slower storage (like disk). When the model needs a word from a distant page, it pulls that page into memory temporarily, just as you’d read a chapter again.

vLLM implements this technique so that huge models can process long conversations or documents without exhausting GPU memory, while still giving quick answers. It’s essentially a smart paging system for neural‑network attention, keeping the workload manageable and the responses fast.
