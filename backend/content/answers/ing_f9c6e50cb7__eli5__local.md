Imagine a teacher who gives every student the same set of tests—math quizzes, reading passages, and logic puzzles—to see how well they can solve different kinds of problems. In AI, those “tests” are called **benchmarks**. A benchmark is just a collection of tasks that all models try to complete; for example, answering trivia questions or translating sentences. By comparing the scores each model gets on the same set of tasks, we get a clear picture of its strengths and weaknesses—just like seeing which subjects a student excels in or needs help with.

When researchers say a new AI “outperforms” another on a benchmark, they mean it scored higher on that standardized set of problems. Benchmarks therefore let us measure **capability**—how good an AI is at the tasks we care about—in a fair and repeatable way, so everyone can see who’s really getting better.
