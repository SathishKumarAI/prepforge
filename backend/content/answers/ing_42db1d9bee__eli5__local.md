Think of a neural network as a **team of tiny decision‑makers** that work together like a city’s traffic lights, but for data.  
Each *neuron* is a small “light” that receives signals (numbers), does one simple math trick—adds them up and passes the result through a squishy switch called an *activation function*—and then sends its output to other neurons.  
The network’s layers are like roads: early layers read raw input (e.g., pixels in a picture), middle layers mix those signals, and the final layer gives the answer (like “cat” or “dog”).  
Just as traffic lights learn which patterns of cars need red or green to keep flow smooth, the network learns by adjusting its tiny weights during training, slowly getting better at recognizing whatever task it’s given.
