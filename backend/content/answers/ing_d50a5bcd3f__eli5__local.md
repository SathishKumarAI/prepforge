Think of a job interview for an AI role like a two‑part cooking contest.

**Part 1 – The Take‑Home Assignment (Recipe Test)**  
You’re given a “recipe” (a coding or data‑analysis problem) and asked to finish it at home. Just as a chef uses their own kitchen, you work on your own machine, following the instructions but also showing how you’d clean up, document, and explain each step. The panel reviews your finished dish: is it tasty (correct), well‑prepared (clean code), and clearly presented? It lets them see how you tackle real problems without constant supervision.

**Part 2 – Behavioral Questions (The Kitchen Talk)**  
After the test, the interviewers ask about past experiences—how you handled a slow oven (deadline pressure), worked with a flaky ingredient (team conflict), or improvised when a key tool was missing. They want to know your cooking style: communication, teamwork, curiosity, and resilience.

Together, the assignment shows *what* you can do, while the behavioral chat reveals *how* you work in a kitchen full of others.
