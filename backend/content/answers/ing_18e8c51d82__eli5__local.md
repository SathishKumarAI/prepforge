Think of an AI **agent** like a smart kitchen robot that can cook on its own.  
It has three parts:  

1. **Sensors (inputs)** – the robot’s “eyes” see the ingredients, and its “ears” hear the timer.  
2. **Brain (policy)** – a tiny program decides what to do next: chop carrots, stir sauce, add salt.  
3. **Actuators (outputs)** – its arms move the spatula, turn on the stove, open the fridge.

Just as the robot follows instructions and reacts to changes in the kitchen, an AI agent receives data from its environment, processes it with a learned rule‑set, and then takes actions that influence that environment. The goal is usually to reach some desired state—like finishing a tasty dish or achieving high scores in a game. This simple “cook‑and‑adjust” picture captures what an AI agent really does.
