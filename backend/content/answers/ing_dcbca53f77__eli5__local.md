**Compound OT – a quick, friendly guide**

Imagine you’re writing a story with two friends on the same laptop. Each of you can type, delete or move words while the others keep working. *Operational Transformation (OT)* is like an invisible referee that watches every edit (“operation”) and makes sure all three screens stay in sync, even when changes happen at the same time.

A **compound operation** is a bundle of simple edits—say, “insert ‘happy’”, then “delete ‘sad’” and “move ‘dog’ to the end.” OT treats that bundle as one single move. The referee first records the whole bundle, then checks how it clashes with any other bundle that arrived at the same moment. If a clash occurs, OT rewrites the conflicting parts so that every user ends up with exactly the same final text.

In short: OT is the smart sync engine for collaborative editing; compound OT lets you group several edits together and still keep everyone’s view consistent.
