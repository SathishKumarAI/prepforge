Imagine you’re baking cookies and want them just right. **Bias** is like using a recipe that’s too simple—maybe it only calls for sugar and flour, so every cookie turns out bland no matter how many ovens you use. The model is “biased” because it can’t capture the real flavor of your dough.

**Variance** is like letting each oven run at slightly different temperatures. One batch might be crisp, another gooey; the results swing wildly. A model with high variance fits every tiny quirk in its training data but fails on new batches.

The **bias‑variance trade‑off** says: if you keep your recipe (model) too simple, you’ll get biased, dull cookies; if you tweak it too much to match every past batch, you’ll overreact to noise and get inconsistent results. The sweet spot is a balanced recipe that captures the essential flavor without being swayed by every oven’s quirks—just like a well‑tuned machine‑learning model that generalizes nicely.
