Think of an application as a house you’re building on a piece of land.  
**Software architecture** is the set of blueprints and construction rules that decide where walls go, how rooms connect, and what kind of roof will keep water out. It tells developers *where* to put code, which parts should talk directly with each other, and how to change one room without shaking up the whole house.

Just as a solid foundation (footings, concrete slab, or pile system) gives a building its stability and lets you add floors safely, software architecture provides the **stability** that lets an application grow, stay reliable, and be maintained. Without it, adding a new feature is like throwing furniture into a flimsy frame—you’ll end up with wobbling walls and a shaky user experience.

*Unavoidable terms:*  
- **Application:** A piece of software that performs a specific task for users.  
- **Architecture:** The high‑level design plan that organizes how the application’s parts fit together.
