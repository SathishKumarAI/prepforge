### Pitfall 13: Technical Jargon Without Explanation

**Problem at its core:**  
When a communicator throws in domain‑specific terms—*neural network*, *gradient descent*, *overfitting*—without unpacking them, the audience’s **mutual information** about the message drops sharply. Information theory tells us that transmission efficiency depends on shared *entropy*. If the sender’s symbol set (the jargon) and the receiver’s prior knowledge diverge, the probability of correct decoding plummets.

**Why it must be avoided:**  
1. **Cognitive load increases**: The listener must allocate extra working‑memory resources to infer meanings, which reduces attention for the substantive content.  
2. **Signal distortion**: Misunderstandings lead to wrong inferences and suboptimal decisions—critical when AI recommendations influence policy or safety.  
3. **Barrier to interdisciplinary collaboration**: Different fields have distinct ontologies; jargon acts as a linguistic “wall” that blocks cross‑pollination of ideas.

**Deeper principle:**  
This is an instance of *semantic entropy* in communication theory: the more symbols are opaque, the higher the uncertainty in interpretation. Effective AI communication should therefore minimize semantic entropy by mapping technical terms to universally accessible concepts (e.g., “model learns patterns like a child’s memory” instead of “backpropagation”).

**Non‑obvious insight:**  
Jargon often signals *social identity* rather than information content. By using field‑specific language, speakers reinforce group cohesion and establish expertise. When omitted or explained, the message becomes more inclusive but may lose perceived authority—an unintended trade‑off that can erode trust in AI systems if stakeholders feel “talked down to.” Thus, transparency is not only a technical necessity; it’s also a social calibration tool.
