Imagine a vending machine that offers three snacks: chips, chocolate, and soda. When you press a button, the machine checks which one you chose and then gives you that snack.

In Python, an `if…elif…else` block works just like that machine’s decision logic.  
- **`if`** is the first check: “Did you pick chips?” If yes, give chips.  
- **`elif`** (short for *else if*) is the next test: “If it wasn’t chips, did you pick chocolate?” If yes, give chocolate.  
- **`else`** covers everything else: “Otherwise, you must have chosen soda,” and the machine gives soda.

Each part runs only once; as soon as one condition is true, the rest are skipped. This lets your program decide among several options in a clear, step‑by‑step way—just like the vending machine decides which snack to dispense based on your button press.
