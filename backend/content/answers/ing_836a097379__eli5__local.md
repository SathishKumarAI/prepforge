Imagine a detective solving a mystery.  
**Reasoning** is the detective’s overall strategy: looking at clues, asking “why” and “how,” and piecing together a story that explains everything. In language models, reasoning means the model moves from one idea to another in a logical way, much like the detective connects evidence.

**Chain‑of‑Thought (CoT)** is what you see when the detective writes down each clue and deduction on sticky notes before making the final conclusion. Each note shows the step that led to the next—“Saw footprints → suspect is barefoot” → “Footprints match size 8” … until the culprit is identified.  
In LLMs, CoT prompts the model to generate intermediate sentences (the sticky notes) before giving the answer, improving accuracy on complex questions.

So, reasoning is the big picture plan; chain‑of‑thought is the step‑by‑step notebook that makes that plan clear and reliable.
