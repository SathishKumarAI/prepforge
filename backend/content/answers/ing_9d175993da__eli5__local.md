Imagine you’re a librarian who has just finished cataloguing every book in the library. Each book is now labeled as “fiction,” “non‑fiction,” “mystery,” or “biography.” When a new book arrives, the librarian can quickly look at its cover and decide which category it belongs to—this is **classification**.

In supervised machine learning, you give the computer a *training set* of examples that already have labels (like the librarian’s catalog). The computer learns a rule that maps features (the book’s cover details) to the correct label. When it sees a new example, it applies that rule to predict its category—just like the librarian classifies a new book.

**Regression** is similar but instead of choosing from discrete categories, the model predicts a continuous value—think of estimating how many pages a new book will have based on its cover and title. Both tasks rely on learning patterns from labeled data, but classification outputs labels while regression outputs numbers.
