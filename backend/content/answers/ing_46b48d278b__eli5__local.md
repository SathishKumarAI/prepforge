Imagine your data as a huge library that’s constantly growing.  
**Data architecture** is the blueprint of that library: it tells you where each book (data) lives, how shelves (databases) are arranged, and which doors (interfaces) let people read or add new books.

When someone asks for a book, the **query engine** is like the librarian’s desk. You hand over a request (“Show me all books by this author”), and the query engine looks through the catalog, finds the right shelves, and pulls out the exact pages you need—fast and precise.

The **compute engine** is the workshop next to the library. Once the librarian gives you the raw books, the compute engine does heavy lifting: it might sort them, combine different sections, or run calculations (like finding averages). Think of it as a powerful computer that processes the data you’ve fetched to give you new insights.

Together, the blueprint (data architecture), the librarian’s desk (query engine), and the workshop (compute engine) keep your information organized, accessible, and useful.
