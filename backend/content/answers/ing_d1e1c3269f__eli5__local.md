**Learning ML on Your Own – The “DIY Lab” Analogy**

Think of machine learning (ML) like building a tiny robot that can learn to recognize pictures of cats and dogs. Instead of buying a pre‑built model, you start with raw parts: data (the images), a recipe (an algorithm), and a test bench (evaluation metrics). You assemble the robot by feeding it many examples—each time it tweaks its internal gears (weights) to improve accuracy. The “DIY lab” is your learning space: you experiment, adjust parameters, and observe how changes affect performance, just as a hobbyist tinkers with a circuit board until it works.

**Key Terms**

- **Algorithm:** Step‑by‑step recipe that tells the robot what to do.
- **Model:** The finished robot, ready to make predictions.
- **Training data:** The set of labeled examples used to teach the model.
- **Evaluation metric:** A score (like accuracy) that tells you how well the model performs.

By treating ML as a hands‑on lab project, you learn by doing—just like building and refining your own robot.
