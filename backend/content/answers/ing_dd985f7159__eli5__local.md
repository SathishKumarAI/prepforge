Imagine you’re learning how to bake a cake, but you’ve already mastered making a simple sponge in school. Instead of starting from scratch, you take that sponge recipe and just add new flavors—chocolate chips, lemon zest, or frosting—so the cake becomes something fresh yet familiar.  

Transfer learning works the same way for deep‑learning models. A big, powerful neural network is first trained on a huge, general dataset (like your basic sponge). That training lets it learn useful “ingredients” such as edge detectors and color patterns. When you face a new task—say recognizing cats in photos—you don’t need to rebuild the whole cake. You keep most of the pre‑trained layers (“the sponge”) and only tweak a few top layers (add the chocolate chips) to adapt to your specific goal.  

So, tutorials on transfer learning guide beginners through this “recipe remix”: how to load a pre‑trained model, choose which parts to freeze, fine‑tune the rest, and evaluate performance—all while saving time and computing power.
