Think of procedural memory as a recipe book that a chef keeps in the back pocket of their apron.  
When the chef needs to sauté onions, they don’t read the instructions again; they simply reach into the pocket, pull out the familiar pattern, and follow it—heat pan, add oil, toss onions, stir for two minutes. The recipe is learned, stored as a sequence of steps that can be executed automatically without conscious thought.

In AI, procedural memory works the same way. It’s the part of the system that has “learned” how to perform tasks—like translating sentences or driving a car—by storing sequences of operations (workflows). Once trained, the AI can run these workflows on its own, just as the chef can sauté onions without looking at the recipe book each time. This memory is *implicit*: you don’t have to remember every detail; you simply execute the skill when needed.
