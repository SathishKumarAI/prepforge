Imagine you’re cooking a big pot of soup (batch) versus making a steady stream of hot chocolate for guests as they arrive (stream).  
**Batch processing** takes a whole set of data—like all the ingredients at once—and works on it in one go, then spits out the finished dish. It’s great when you can afford to wait: you can use powerful tools, optimize heavily, and get perfect results, but nothing is ready until the entire batch finishes.  
**Stream processing** handles each piece of data as it comes—like adding a single chocolate bar into the pot right when a guest orders. The output (a warm drink) appears almost instantly, so you never have to wait for all the ingredients. You can’t do as many heavy optimizations because you must keep up with the flow, but you get real‑time results.

In machine learning, batch training uses the whole dataset at once, giving high accuracy but slower updates; stream (online) learning updates a model on each new example, staying current but often with simpler algorithms.
