**Docker Volumes – the Data‑centric Glue of Containerized ML**

At its core, a container is a *stateless* execution sandbox. When training or serving a model, we need persistent artefacts—datasets, checkpoints, logs—that survive restarts and migrations. Docker volumes are the only abstraction that guarantees data durability **outside** the fleeting life of a container, while still offering fast I/O, isolation, and cross‑host portability.

From an information‑theoretic angle, a volume is a *memory‑cell* shared among processes: it keeps entropy (your training data) intact across snapshots, enabling reproducible experiments. It also decouples the **write‑amplification** of storage from container churn; every write hits the same underlying block device, preventing fragmentation that would otherwise degrade GPU‑bound I/O.

### 8 Must‑Know Concepts

| # | Concept | Why it matters |
|---|---------|----------------|
|1|`docker volume create` | Explicitly names volumes, avoiding implicit random IDs. |
|2|Mount points (`-v /host:/container`) | Path mapping determines read/write permissions and SELinux context. |
|3|Named vs anonymous volumes | Named volumes persist beyond container removal; anonymous are cleaned automatically. |
|4|Driver selection (`local`, `nfs`, `gluster`) | Drives I/O performance, sharing scope, and encryption. |
|5|Data migration (`docker cp`, `volume backup`) | Enables versioned checkpoints across clusters. |
|6|Container data isolation | Prevents accidental overwrite of shared datasets. |
|7|Volume lifecycle hooks | `docker run --mount` supports read‑only or bind mounts for hot‑reloading models. |
|8|Security contexts | AppArmor/SELinux labels on volumes guard against privilege escalation. |

**Non‑obvious insight:** *A volume’s underlying storage can be tuned like a database index.* By selecting the right driver and configuring block size, you effectively shape query latency for large tensors—often the bottleneck in distributed training pipelines. Thus, mastering volumes is not just about persistence; it’s an optimization lever that directly impacts ML throughput.
