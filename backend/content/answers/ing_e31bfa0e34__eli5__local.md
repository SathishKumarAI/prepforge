Imagine you’re a painter looking at a photograph of a city street. **Image segmentation** is like drawing a different colored outline around every object—cars, trees, sidewalks—so the computer can “see” each part separately. In **semantic segmentation**, every pixel gets a label such as *car*, *person*, or *road*. The machine learns this by being shown thousands of pictures where a human has already labeled the pixels; it then tries to copy that labeling on new images.

Think of the algorithm as a very fast, automatic color‑in tool: it receives the raw photo (input) and produces a map of colored zones (output). Each zone tells the system what type of object is there, letting it make smart decisions—like a self‑driving car avoiding obstacles or a medical scanner highlighting tumors.
