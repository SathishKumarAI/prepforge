**Context pollution** is like a messy kitchen where every time you cook, someone keeps dropping old crumbs on the counter. In an AI agent, “context” is all the recent messages or data it uses to decide what to say next. If the agent keeps adding new information without cleaning up the old, its memory gets cluttered—full of irrelevant details that confuse it. That’s why a chatbot might start giving wrong answers or repeating itself after many turns: it’s “polluting” its own workspace with stray pieces of conversation.

Think of a smart assistant as a whiteboard. Each new task is written on the board, but you never erase the previous notes. After enough tasks, the board becomes unreadable and the assistant can’t focus on the current job. Periodic “erasing” (clearing or summarizing old context) keeps the AI sharp and reliable.
