An **SSD** (Solid‑State Drive) is a storage device that keeps data on tiny memory chips instead of spinning disks, so it’s super fast and quiet.

Think of an SSD like a high‑speed bicycle that rides on a smooth, flat road: you can get to your destination quickly and with no engine noise. But just as that bike has a limited battery life and the tires wear out faster than a car’s wheels, SSDs have some downsides:

* **Cost per gigabyte** – they’re pricier than hard drives, so buying a lot of space is expensive.
* **Write limits** – each memory cell can be overwritten only about 3 – 10 million times before it starts to fail, like a rubber ball that eventually cracks after many bounces.
* **Data recovery** – if an SSD crashes, retrieving lost files is harder because the data isn’t stored on magnetic tracks but spread across chips.

So while SSDs give lightning‑fast access and quiet operation, they cost more, have finite write endurance, and can be tougher to recover from a failure.
