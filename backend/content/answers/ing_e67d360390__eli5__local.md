**Imagine you’re building a house that must stay safe, look good, and be easy to maintain.**  
In a system‑design interview you do the same for software—except your “house” is a large, scalable system.

1. **Ask the right questions first** – like checking if the plot can support a big roof or if there’s enough plumbing. Clarify constraints: traffic volume, latency limits, data size, budget, and legal rules (privacy, compliance).  
2. **Define the core components** – think of walls, foundation, HVAC as services (databases, caches, load balancers).  
3. **Map trade‑offs** – a thicker wall (more replicas) costs more but gives stronger security; a thinner one is cheaper but may fail under pressure.  
4. **Show how parts talk** – like plumbing pipes: use clear APIs, message queues, or gRPC.  

By turning vague “build something fast” into concrete, measurable questions, you give the interviewer a solid blueprint to evaluate your design and communication skills.
