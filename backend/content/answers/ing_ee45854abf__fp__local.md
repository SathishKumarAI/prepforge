   - *Real‑time notifications*.  

2. **Non‑functional drivers**  
   - **Scalability**: throughput ≈ 10⁴–10⁵ writes/second, read latency < 200 ms.  
   - **Availability**: 99.999% SLA; partition tolerance is mandatory (CAP).  
   - **Consistency trade‑offs**: eventual consistency for feeds (acceptable lag) vs strong consistency for likes/comments (atomicity).  

3. **Derived constraints**

   - *Indexing*: inverted index on hashtags, geotags; graph structure for follower‑following relations.  
   - *Caching*: LRU caches for hot feeds; pre‑computed timelines to avoid per‑request joins.  

4. **Deep principle** – **Information bottleneck**: the feed is a compressed representation of all posts that maximises relevance while minimising bandwidth. This justifies pre‑computing “snapshots” of a user’s timeline rather than ad‑hoc aggregation.

They must be generated by an event stream (Kafka/Redis Streams) that guarantees at‑least‑once delivery and idempotent processing, otherwise users may see duplicate likes or missing comments. This subtlety directly impacts the design of the notification microservice and its interaction with the user’s feed state.
