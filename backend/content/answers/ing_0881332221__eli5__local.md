Imagine you’re running a busy kitchen and someone orders a pizza. While the oven is heating, the waiter takes another order for “pizza” from a different table. If you hand out two pizzas before the first one finishes baking, customers will get duplicates—unnecessary work and wasted dough.

In software, an **in‑progress request** is like that partially baked pizza. **Idempotency** is the rule that says “no matter how many times I ask for a pizza, you’ll only ever give me one.” The kitchen keeps a note of the first order; when the second comes in, it sees the note and simply re‑serves the same finished pizza instead of making another.

So, idempotency is a safety net that prevents duplicate work and guarantees consistency even if clients retry or network glitches happen. It’s like having a recipe card that says “serve one pizza per order” no matter how many times you ask.
