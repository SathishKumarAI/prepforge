**Conclusion – “When AI Remembers Too Much”**  
Imagine a child who keeps every story from the library in their head. When they’re asked to explain a new book, they blur past plots into the answer, even though those old stories aren’t relevant now. In the same way, an AI agent that stores *all* of its past interactions in memory can start repeating patterns it learned long ago—behaviors that were once useful but are no longer appropriate.  
**Key terms:**  
- **Memory** – the part of the AI that keeps a record of what it has seen or done.  
- **Persistent behavior** – a pattern the agent follows because it kept it in memory, even when newer information suggests a different action.
