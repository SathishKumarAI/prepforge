Think of a huge library that wants to teach a robot how to read and write about anything. The **“curriculum”** is the organized list of books it will study first. Instead of picking random pages, the library arranges the books from simple, common topics (like “cats” or “weather”) to more complex ones (“quantum physics” or “legal contracts”).  

During pre‑training, a large language model (LLM) reads these books word by word, learning how words usually follow each other. The curriculum decides **which parts of the text come next** in that reading order—much like a teacher giving a student practice worksheets that gradually increase in difficulty. By starting with easy patterns and slowly adding harder ones, the LLM builds a solid foundation before tackling the full range of language it will eventually use.
