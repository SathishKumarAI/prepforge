**Maintenance Cadence – the “Day 30 Problem”**

Imagine you’re training a pet robot dog to fetch. At first it learns quickly: you give treats, and it runs to the ball. After about a month, though, the dog starts to misbehave—maybe it stops running or pulls back on the leash. That’s because the dog has begun *forgetting* what worked before and is instead picking up new habits from the noisy kitchen floor, stray cats, or even other dogs it sees.

In AI terms, an “agent” (the robot) is built to act in a world by learning from data. As time passes, its environment changes—new user inputs, updated websites, shifting slang. If you let the agent keep learning without *resetting* its knowledge or carefully checking what’s new, it can start to think the old rules are still true and ignore the fresh ones. The result is a model that performs worse than when it first started.

**Key idea:**  
Just like a dog needs regular training sessions to stay sharp, an AI agent must have *maintenance cadence*—periodic review, cleaning of its learning data, and recalibration—to avoid slipping into old habits and losing accuracy after about 30 days.
