Think of a library that wants to help people find books quickly.  
A **data architecture** is the blueprint for that library: it tells you where shelves go, how books are grouped, what labels they carry, and how visitors move through the space. In machine‑learning terms, data architecture decides *where* raw measurements (the “books”) live—databases, cloud buckets, or edge devices—how they’re labeled (tags), how they’re linked to one another (relationships), and which tools can read them quickly for training models.

A **physical data model** is the actual floor plan: it lists each shelf (table) with its exact dimensions (columns, types, indexes) and shows the concrete wiring (data pipelines) that bring new books in.  
So, a data architecture is the high‑level design of the library, while a physical data model is the detailed construction plan you follow to build it.
