**Prompt engineering** is the art of talking to an AI so it gives you exactly what you want. Think of the AI like a smart kitchen robot that can bake anything, but only if you give it the right recipe.

| Type | What it does | Kitchen‑robot analogy |
|------|--------------|-----------------------|
| **Simple prompts** | A single question or instruction (e.g., “Explain photosynthesis.”) | Telling the robot: “Bake a cake.” |
| **Chain‑of‑thought prompts** | You ask the AI to explain its reasoning step by step. | The robot says, “First I mix flour… then I add sugar…” |
| **Few‑shot prompts** | You give a few examples of the style you want before asking for more. | Show the robot two cakes: one chocolate, one vanilla, then ask it to make a strawberry cake. |
| **Zero‑shot prompts** | No examples—just a clear instruction (the AI must guess). | The robot gets only “Make a rainbow smoothie.” |
| **Instruction tuning** | You give a set of instructions that shape the whole model’s behavior. | Reprogramming the robot so it always uses eco‑friendly ingredients. |

A **prompt** is simply the text you feed to the AI; a **fine‑tuned model** is one that has been trained on many prompts in advance, like a chef who already knows how to make every recipe. By mixing these techniques—choosing the right style of prompt—you can guide the robot (AI) to cook up answers that taste just right for your needs.
