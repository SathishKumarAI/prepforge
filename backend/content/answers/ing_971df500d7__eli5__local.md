Imagine you’re baking a huge batch of cookies for a big party. Instead of using one oven that can only bake a few at a time, you line up several ovens side by side, all working together. Each oven is a *server*—a computer that does the heavy lifting. A *server cluster* is just that: a group of servers joined together so they share the workload.

In machine learning, training models can be like baking many batches at once; one server would take forever. By clustering servers, each one handles part of the data or computation, and they communicate to keep everything in sync—just like the ovens share heat and timing instructions. The result is a faster, more reliable “bake” that can handle larger recipes (datasets) without breaking down.
