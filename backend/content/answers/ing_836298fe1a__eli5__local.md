Imagine you’re ordering coffee at a busy café that has only one barista. You ask for a latte, the barista looks up the recipe, grabs milk and espresso, blends it, then hands you the cup. The time between your “hello” and the AI’s reply is like that whole process.

In conversational AI, *latency* is the delay from when you type or speak until the model sends back an answer. It happens because the computer must first fetch data (the user’s words), run it through a huge network of mathematical operations (the “recipe”), and then send the result over the internet to your device.

Latency matters because if it’s too long, conversations feel stilted—like waiting forever for that latte. Shorter delays make interactions smoother, almost as natural as talking to a human.
