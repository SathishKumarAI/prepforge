At its core, shopping is a *decision‑making under uncertainty* task: you must weigh many attributes (price, quality, brand) across numerous alternatives while constrained by time and money. In behavioral economics this is framed as **bounded rationality**—the brain seeks satisficing solutions rather than optimal ones because of limited cognitive resources.

People who enjoy shopping typically have:

1. **High *cognitive flexibility*** – they can rapidly update preferences when new information arrives, turning the process into a dynamic exploration game.
2. **Low *loss aversion* in the domain** – they are comfortable with small price fluctuations and see novelty as an intrinsic reward.

Those who find it stressful usually display:

1. **High *need‑for‑cognitive‑closure*** – they dislike ambiguity, so the sheer number of choices creates overload.
2. **Strong *time pressure sensitivity*** – when deadlines loom (e.g., holiday shopping), the decision cost spikes, triggering cortisol and a “fight‑or‑flight” reaction.

**Non‑obvious insight:**
