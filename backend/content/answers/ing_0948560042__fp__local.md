**Turn‑taking & Endpointing – the Latency Engine of Conversational AI**

When a user speaks, a dialogue system must decide *when* to stop listening and *when* to start speaking again.  
Mathematically this is a **real‑time hypothesis test** on an acoustic stream \(x(t)\):  

\[
T_{\text{end}} = \inf \{t : P(\text{speech ends}\mid x_{0:t}) > \theta\}
\]

The threshold \(\theta\) trades off *safety* (never cut a user’s sentence) against *speed* (avoid waiting for the next pause).  
Similarly, **turn‑taking** is an online decision problem: we must infer whether the user will speak again soon.  This requires estimating the conditional probability of future speech given current context, \(P(\text{next turn}\mid x_{0:t},\text{dialogue state})\).

Why it matters  
* **User perception:** Latency larger than ~200 ms feels robotic; smaller latency creates a natural rhythm.  
* **Throughput:** In high‑traffic scenarios (e.g., call centers) reduced latency directly scales agent capacity.  
* **Energy & cost:** Each extra millisecond of buffering consumes CPU and network resources.

**Deeper principle:** Both problems are instances of *optimal stopping theory* under uncertainty; the optimal policy minimizes expected cost = \(\alpha\)(missed speech) + \(\beta\)(delay).  

**Non‑obvious insight:** Modern neural acoustic models can *predict future silence patterns* using causal attention, allowing the system to pre‑emptively lower the end‑threshold during long pauses.  This “look‑ahead” turns a reactive rule into an anticipatory strategy, shaving tens of milliseconds that would otherwise be lost waiting for confirmation.
