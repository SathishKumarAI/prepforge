### Phenomenology of **On‑Policy Distillation**

At its core, on‑policy distillation seeks to *reproduce* a policy \( \pi_{\theta} \) by training a student \( q_{\phi} \) on trajectories generated by the teacher itself.  
1. **Sampling principle** – The teacher’s rollouts are the only data source; thus the empirical distribution of states visited, \( d^{\pi_{\theta}}(s) \), governs what the student can learn.  
2. **Kullback–Leibler (KL) objective** – Minimizing \( \mathbb{E}_{d^{\pi_{\theta}}}\!\big[ D_{\text{KL}}\!\big(\pi_{\theta}(\cdot|s)\,\|\,q_{\phi}(\cdot|s)\big)\big] \) guarantees that the student’s action distribution matches the teacher at every state it actually encounters.  
3. **Implicit regularization** – Because only on‑policy states appear, the student is *forced* to mimic the teacher where it matters for future rewards; off‑policy mismatches are never penalized. This yields a smoother value landscape: the KL term acts as a local “trust region” that prevents large policy deviations in high‑reward regions.

**Non‑obvious insight:** The quality of distillation is *not* limited by the student’s capacity alone; it is bounded by the **coverage** of \( d^{\pi_{\theta}} \). If the teacher visits only a narrow manifold (e.g., due to exploration deficiency), the student cannot generalize beyond that manifold, regardless of its expressive power. Thus, on‑policy distillation implicitly enforces *distributional fidelity*, making exploration strategies of the teacher critical for successful knowledge transfer.
