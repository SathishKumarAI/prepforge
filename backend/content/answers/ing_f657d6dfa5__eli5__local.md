Think of a dataset as a giant cookbook that an AI “chef” uses to learn how to cook (make predictions).  
Each **row** is one recipe: it lists the ingredients (the *features*), like age, height, or color, and tells the chef what the dish turned out to be (the *label*, such as whether a fruit is ripe).  
The chef reads many recipes, learns which combinations of ingredients usually give good dishes, and then can guess the label for new, unseen recipes.

- **Feature** – an ingredient in the recipe.  
- **Label** – the dish’s final quality or category.  
- **Training set** – the cookbook pages the chef studies first.  
- **Test set** – a fresh batch of recipes the chef hasn’t seen yet; we use them to check how well the chef has learned.

Just as a good cook needs enough diverse recipes, a machine‑learning model needs a large, varied dataset to learn reliably.
