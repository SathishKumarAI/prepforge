**Why pacing matters in an AI‑agents curriculum**

At the core of any training program is *knowledge retention*, a process that follows the **spacing effect**: concepts are best consolidated when revisited after intervals that grow with mastery. An AI agents course, where students juggle theory (probability, optimization) and practice (Python, reinforcement learning), must therefore balance two competing forces:

1. **Cognitive load** – too many new ideas in a single week overwhelm working memory, leading to shallow learning.
2. **Motivation decay** – pacing that is too slow risks boredom; students lose the momentum that keeps them engaged with complex material.

The optimal compromise is a **“fast‑track, spaced‑review” schedule**:  
- **Week 1–3 (Intensive phase)**: 5 h of lecture + 10 h of hands‑on per week. The first weeks deliver foundational blocks—probability basics, policy gradients, environment design—at a brisk pace because students have fresh prior knowledge from the course’s pre‑readings.  
- **Week 4–6 (Retention phase)**: reduce new content to 3 h/week while allocating 12 h for project work and quizzes. Here we apply *interval‑learning*: revisiting core concepts every 2–3 days through coding challenges, micro‑projects, or peer explanations.  

**Non‑obvious insight:** The “retention phase” should be *interactive*, not passive. Peer‑to‑peer teaching doubles the active recall effect, turning the classroom into a living knowledge network that self‑corrects and adapts to each student’s learning curve.
