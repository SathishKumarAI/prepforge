**Ramp – the “warm‑up” of a machine‑learning model**

Imagine you’re teaching a child to ride a bike. At first, you let them push off with their feet—slow, steady, and safe. That’s the *ramp*: a short period where the learning rate (how quickly the model updates its weights) starts low and gradually climbs until it reaches the target value.  

Why? If the learning rate jumps straight to a high number, the child (the model) might wobble or even fall (the loss explodes). By “ramping” up, we give the algorithm time to settle into good patterns before taking bigger steps.  

In FAANG interviews you’ll see questions like: *“Why use a learning‑rate ramp? What happens if you skip it?”* The answer is simple—stability and faster convergence, just like a cautious bike rider who gains confidence before pedaling hard.
