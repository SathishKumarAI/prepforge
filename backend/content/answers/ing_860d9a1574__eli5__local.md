Imagine you’re reading a mystery novel and trying to guess what word comes next. If the story is very predictable, your guesses are usually right; if it’s full of surprises, you often miss. **Perplexity** measures how “surprised” a language model feels when it sees new text, like that guessing game.

Think of perplexity as a score on a quiz: the lower the number, the fewer mistakes the model makes and the better it understands the pattern. A high perplexity means the model keeps guessing wildly—like a detective who can’t decide which clue is important.

So when you “prepare” a language model, you train it on lots of books so its perplexity drops, making it sharper at predicting what comes next in any story.
