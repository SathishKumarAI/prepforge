Think of building an AI platform like cooking a big, fancy meal.  
First, decide what dish you want: do you need a quick snack (a simple chatbot) or a multi‑course feast (an end‑to‑end recommendation engine)? That tells you the flavors—data pipelines, model training, and deployment—that must be in place.

Next, gather your kitchen staff. A **platform engineer** is like a sous‑chef who sets up the stove, oven, and prep stations so everyone can cook efficiently. Look for people who know how to build and maintain reliable “kitchen infrastructure” (cloud services, container orchestration, monitoring) and who can talk both to chefs (data scientists) and diners (product managers).

Start with a clear job description that lists the kitchen tools you’ll use—AWS/GCP, Kubernetes, CI/CD pipelines—and ask candidates to explain how they’d set up a simple “recipe” from raw data to a live model.  
Finally, give them a small test project: build a tiny pipeline that trains a model and serves it on a web page. The way they organize the steps shows whether they can keep your AI kitchen running smoothly.
