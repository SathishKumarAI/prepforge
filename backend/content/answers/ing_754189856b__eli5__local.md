Imagine you’re looking for a missing sock in a huge laundry room.  
**Contextual Retrieval** is like remembering the exact drawer, the color, and the pattern of the sock that was last worn – all those little clues help you pull it out quickly.  
Other approaches are more like opening every drawer one by one (a blind search) or just pulling out any sock that looks similar in shape (a generic match).  

**Contextual Retrieval** uses *context*—the recent words, the topic of conversation, and the user’s intent—to decide which part of a huge knowledge base to pull from.  
Other methods either scan everything without looking at context (full‑text search) or only consider surface features (keyword matching), missing deeper meaning.  

So, contextual retrieval is the smart “memory” that narrows down the search with clues already known, while other approaches are broader, slower, or less precise.
