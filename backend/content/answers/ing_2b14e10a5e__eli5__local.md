**Latency is like waiting for a cup of coffee at a busy café**

Imagine you’re in line for coffee. The *latency* is the time between you ordering and when the barista hands you your drink. In computer systems, latency is the delay from sending a request (your order) to getting the response back (the coffee).  

Just as a crowded café slows everyone down, many factors can stretch system latency:  
- **Distance** – data travels farther, like a long line.  
- **Processing speed** – slower baristas or processors take more time.  
- **Bottlenecks** – a single machine handling too many orders at once.

Lowering latency means shortening that wait: fewer steps, faster servers, and smarter routing—so you get your coffee (or data) quicker.
