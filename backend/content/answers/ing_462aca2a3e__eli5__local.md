**Encapsulation and decapsulation in the OSI model are like sending a letter through a post office.**

1. **Encapsulation (Packing)** – When you write your message (the data), you put it into an envelope (layer 7). The post office adds stamps, an address label, and a return address (layer 4–6). Each layer adds its own “header” so the mail can travel safely across roads, railways, and air routes.  
2. **Decapsulation (Unpacking)** – When the letter reaches the destination post office, it removes each stamp in reverse order, finally revealing your original message to the recipient.

**Key terms:**  
- *Layer* – a step in the communication process that handles a specific task (e.g., transport, network).  
- *Header* – information added by a layer to guide routing and delivery.  

Just as an envelope protects and directs a letter through many hands, encapsulation lets data travel across networks while keeping each part of the journey organized.
