Imagine you’re writing a grocery list in a notebook that’s only big enough to hold a few lines at a time. Every new item you add pushes the oldest ones out of view so you can keep adding without spilling over. That’s what “short‑term contextual compression” does for an AI: it keeps a sliding window of the most recent words or sentences (the *context*) and discards older parts that no longer fit. Like a chef tasting a dish, the model focuses on the latest flavors—what just happened—to decide what to say next. This keeps the conversation fresh and manageable while still remembering enough detail to stay coherent. The key term: **context** = the bits of text the AI currently “sees” to guide its response.
