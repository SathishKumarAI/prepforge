Think of an **AI agent** like a smart robot in a kitchen that’s been given a recipe book (its knowledge) and a set of tools (its skills). The chef’s job is to cook a dish; the AI agent’s job is to perform a task—answering questions, playing chess, or controlling a drone.  

The **agent** has three parts:  
1. **Sensors** – its eyes and ears (e.g., reading text or seeing images).  
2. **Actuators** – what it can do (typing a reply, moving a robot arm).  
3. **Brain** – the learning algorithm that decides which action to take next based on what it sees.

Like the chef who reads the recipe and adjusts seasoning while cooking, an AI agent observes its environment, processes information with its “brain,” and takes actions that bring it closer to its goal. It learns from experience, so over time it gets better at the job—just as a seasoned cook improves with practice.
