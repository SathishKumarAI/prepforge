Imagine you’re at a bakery and want to know if a cookie is chocolate‑chip or oatmeal.  
Bayes’ theorem is the recipe that lets you update your guess when new crumbs (data) appear.

**Concrete analogy:**  
*The baker’s kitchen = the world of possibilities.*  
You start with a *prior belief*: “In this shop, 60 % of cookies are chocolate‑chip.”  
Then you taste a crumb and notice it’s crunchy. That crumb is *evidence*.  
Bayes’ theorem says: combine your prior (60 %) with how likely each cookie type produces a crunchy crumb to get the *posterior* probability—your updated guess after tasting.

In machine learning, the “crumb” is any new data point, the “prior” is what you believed before seeing it, and Bayes’ theorem gives you the best possible update. This simple rule lets algorithms learn continuously from evidence without complicated equations or jargon.
