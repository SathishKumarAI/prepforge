Think of programming like building a LEGO city.  
In **Object‑Oriented Programming (OOP)** each LEGO block is an *object*—a self‑contained piece that knows its own shape, color and how to behave when you press it. An object is made from a *class*, which is the blueprint telling every block of that type what parts it has and what actions it can do.

When you want a new building, you don’t have to rebuild everything; you just snap together blocks (objects) that already know how to connect and cooperate. The city’s traffic lights, cars, and people are all objects, each with its own data (“speed”, “color”) and methods (“turn on”, “accelerate”).

OOP is one of the top eight programming paradigms—others include procedural, functional, logic, event‑driven, concurrent, declarative, and reactive. Each paradigm offers a different way to organize code, but OOP keeps everything tidy by grouping data and behavior into reusable objects, just like LEGO bricks in a bustling city.
