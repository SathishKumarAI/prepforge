Imagine you’re a chef in a busy kitchen. Every time a customer orders a dish, you could walk to the pantry (the database) to fetch fresh ingredients. That trip takes time and energy, especially if the pantry is far away or crowded with other chefs—this is the “cost of hitting the database.”  

Caching is like keeping a small, well‑organized station right next to your prep area where you store the most frequently used ingredients (data). When a new order comes in, you grab what you need from that station instead of running back to the pantry. The station is called a *cache*, and the act of storing data there for quick access is *caching*.  

You still keep the pantry for all the special or rarely used items, but most orders are satisfied instantly from the cache, saving time, reducing traffic, and keeping the kitchen running smoothly.
