**Pitfall 4: Ignoring Variance – The “One‑Size‑Fits‑All” Test**

Think of an AI model like a student who takes a single practice exam to decide if they’re ready for the real test. If we only look at that one score (the *mean*), we might think the student is perfect, even though their performance could swing wildly on other days. In AI, “variance” measures how much a model’s results change when it sees different data or runs with slightly different settings.

If you ignore variance, you’ll over‑confidently claim your system is reliable, but in reality it might fail on a small shift—like a new type of image or a slight noise spike. So, just as teachers give multiple tests to gauge consistency, we must evaluate AI across varied scenarios to see how stable its performance truly is.
