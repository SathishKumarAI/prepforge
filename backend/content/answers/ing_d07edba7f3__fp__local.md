### Why salaries differ by geography  
At the core, a salary is an *equilibrium* between **opportunity cost** (what a worker could earn elsewhere) and **firm‑specific value** (how much the firm gains from that worker). In cities with high living costs or tight talent pools, opportunity cost rises; firms must pay more to attract the same skill set. Conversely, in regions where similar roles are abundant or cheaper to hire, wages compress. Thus salary is a *price* balancing supply‑side constraints (local talent density) and demand‑side benefits (project complexity, IP creation).
