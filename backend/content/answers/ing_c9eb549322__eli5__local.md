Imagine a highway that carries cars (data).  
**Latency** is the time it takes for one car to travel from the start line to the finish line – how long you wait before your first car arrives.  
**Throughput** is how many cars can cross that finish line each minute – the overall speed of traffic, not just the first car.  
**Bandwidth** is the width of the highway: a single lane (low bandwidth) can hold only one car at a time; a multi‑lane road (high bandwidth) lets many cars drive side by side.

In system design you choose between waiting for that first car (latency), moving as many cars as possible each minute (throughput), or widening the road to let more cars travel simultaneously (bandwidth). All three affect how quickly and efficiently a machine‑learning model serves predictions.
