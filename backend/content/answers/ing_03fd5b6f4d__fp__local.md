**Why the “AI boom” still favors big firms**

At its core, an AI system is a *data‑driven optimizer*.  
To build a useful model you need three things:

| Element | What it supplies | Why it matters |
|---------|------------------|----------------|
| **Massive, high‑quality data** | The statistical signal that the algorithm learns from. | Small firms usually own far less data; the noise overwhelms the signal. |
| **Compute & storage** | The hardware that turns data into predictions. | Cloud costs are fixed per gigabyte of training time; a small firm cannot spread those costs over many projects, so the unit cost is prohibitive. |
| **Domain expertise + iterative experimentation** | The knowledge to label, debug and deploy models safely. | Building a model that works in production is a *learning‑curve* problem; early failures can be costly (reputation, compliance). |

These three pillars are interdependent: without data you cannot justify compute, and without compute you cannot process the data fast enough for rapid experimentation.

Large firms already have:

1. **Data pipelines** – millions of transactions per day.
2. **Budget‑scale cloud or on‑prem clusters** – economies of scale reduce per‑GB cost by 10–30 %.
3. **Dedicated AI teams** that can iterate quickly and absorb early failures.

Consequently, the *marginal benefit* of adding AI is higher for them; their revenue already justifies the upfront investment. Small firms face a classic “high fixed‑cost, low demand” trap: the initial outlay exceeds the immediate payoff, so they postpone or avoid adoption.

**Non‑obvious insight:**  
AI adoption isn’t merely about technology; it’s a *network effect* in talent and data. Once a firm deploys AI successfully, it attracts better analysts (who want to work with cutting‑edge tools) and more data partners (because its predictive insights unlock new revenue streams). Small firms that lag lose out on this virtuous cycle even before the first model goes live.

In short, the economics of data, compute, and learning curve create a *critical mass threshold*—only firms above that threshold can afford—and thus AI adoption remains concentrated among the largest players, even as the overall pace accelerates.
