**Image augmentation in AI training**

Think of teaching a child to recognize apples. If you only show one picture of an apple, the child learns that exact shape and color—any other apple might look unfamiliar. Image augmentation is like giving the child many versions of the same apple: rotating it, zooming in, changing lighting, or adding slight blur. Each version keeps the apple’s identity but presents a new “view.” For AI, this means the computer sees dozens or hundreds of slightly altered copies of each training image. It learns to focus on what truly matters—like the round shape and red color—rather than memorizing one exact pixel pattern. In short, augmentation tricks the model into being robust, so it can spot apples (or cats, cars, etc.) no matter how they appear in real life.
