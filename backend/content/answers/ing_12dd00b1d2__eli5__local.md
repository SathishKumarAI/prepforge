Imagine you’re baking a cake that everyone will eat at a party.  
**Problem one – “over‑baking” (overfitting).** You put the cake in the oven for so long that it turns black on the outside but is still raw inside. In machine learning, over‑training a model makes it perfect on the data you used to teach it, but it fails when new guests (new data) arrive because it can’t generalize beyond its “blackened” experience.

**Problem two – “under‑baking” (underfitting).** You rush it out of the oven after just a minute. The cake is raw everywhere and nobody can enjoy it. Likewise, an under‑trained model is too simple; it can’t even capture patterns in the training data, so it performs poorly on both old and new examples.

Both problems mean the model isn’t “just right.” A good learner is like a baker who watches the timer, checks the cake’s center, and knows when to pull it out: accurate enough for the training set but still tasty for anyone who tries it later.
