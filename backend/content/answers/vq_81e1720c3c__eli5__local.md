Imagine you’re trying to learn how fast all cars on a road can go, but you only ever look at the fastest sports cars that park in the parking lot. The “selection bias” is like that shortcut: it happens when the data we choose to study isn’t a fair sample of everything we care about. In AI, we use selection bias to make models easier or faster by focusing on a limited set of examples—like training a voice assistant only with clear recordings from a quiet room so it learns quickly what “hello” sounds like in that setting. The purpose is convenience and speed, but the downside is that the model may fail when faced with real‑world noise, accents, or different languages because it never saw those variations during training.
