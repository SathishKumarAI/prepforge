Think of a coding interview as a treasure hunt where the map is made of two parts: **tools** (data structures) and **tricks** (algorithms).  

*Data structures* are like containers you choose to store clues—think of a stack as a “stack of plates” that lets you grab only the top plate, a queue as a “line at a ticket counter” where the first person in line is served first, or a hash map as a “phone book” that instantly pulls up someone’s number when you know their name.  

*Algorithms* are the strategies you use to move through the hunt—such as sorting all the clues from easiest to hardest (quick sort), searching for a specific clue among many (binary search), or finding the shortest path between two points on a map (Dijkstra).  

Mastering both means you can quickly decide which container fits your data and then apply the right trick to solve problems fast. That’s what interviewers look for: knowing the right tool, when to use it, and how to combine them efficiently.
