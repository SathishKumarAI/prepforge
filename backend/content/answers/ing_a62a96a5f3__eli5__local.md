Think of a school cafeteria where every student follows a routine: they line up at the same time, grab their usual lunch, and sit in their usual spots. Anomaly detection is like having a friendly teacher who watches this routine to spot anything unusual—maybe someone standing outside the line, or a new student eating without a ticket. The teacher (the algorithm) learns what “normal” looks like from past observations: the times, the positions, the food choices. When something deviates—an outlier—the teacher flags it for attention.

Key terms:  
- **Algorithm** – a set of rules that tells the teacher how to look at data and decide if it’s normal or not.  
- **Normal pattern** – what most students do every day; the algorithm learns this from historical data.  
- **Anomaly (outlier)** – anything that doesn’t fit the learned pattern, like a sudden spike in traffic on a website or a sensor reading far outside its usual range.

Applications are everywhere: fraud detection in credit card use, spotting equipment failures before they break, finding rare disease symptoms among health records, and even catching security breaches in computer networks. By flagging these oddballs early, we can investigate and act before problems grow.
