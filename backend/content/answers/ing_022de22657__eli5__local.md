Think of a **system design interview** like being asked to build a new playground for a town that’s already crowded with kids, parents, and traffic.  
You first ask the town (the interviewer) what they need: how many children will play at once, whether you have to keep the swings safe from weather, if you can reuse parts of an existing park, and how quickly you must get the playground ready.  

Next, you sketch a rough layout on paper—decide where the slides go, where the sandbox sits next to the snack bar, and how kids will move between zones without bumping into each other.  
You then pick materials (databases, servers, caches) that fit the size of the crowd and the budget, explaining why you chose them over alternatives.  

Finally, you walk through a “playtest”: show how a child (a request) moves from one feature to another, what happens if the slide breaks (fail‑over), and how you’ll monitor safety (logging, metrics).  

In short, a system design interview tests your ability to ask clarifying questions, sketch a high‑level plan, choose appropriate building blocks, anticipate problems, and explain trade‑offs—all while keeping the whole playground safe, fast, and fun.
