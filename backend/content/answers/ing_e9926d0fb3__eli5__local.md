**Transport Layer – the “Post Office” of data**

Imagine sending a letter (your message) across town. The **transport layer** is like the post office that decides how to package and route it so it reaches the right mailbox safely. It breaks your big message into smaller parcels, keeps track of their order, and makes sure each parcel arrives intact. If something goes wrong—like a parcel gets lost or corrupted—it asks for a resend.

In network security, this “post office” also uses special envelopes called **TLS/SSL** (think of them as tamper‑proof mailbags). They encrypt the contents so nobody can read or alter the parcels in transit, and they verify that the recipient is who they claim to be. Thus, the transport layer protects data integrity, confidentiality, and correct delivery—just like a reliable, secure post office.
