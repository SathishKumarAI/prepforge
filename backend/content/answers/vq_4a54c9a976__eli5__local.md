Imagine you’re sorting mail in an office. **Classification** is like putting each envelope into one of several labeled folders—“Invoices,” “Letters,” “Package.” You just need to know the category; the exact amount inside doesn’t matter. Use classification when the answer is a *label* or *type*—for example, deciding whether a picture shows a cat or a dog, predicting if an email is spam or not, or diagnosing a disease as “flu” or “cold.”

**Regression** is like measuring how many pages each envelope contains and writing that number down. It’s used when the answer is a *continuous value*—predicting house prices, estimating next month’s sales, or forecasting tomorrow’s temperature.

So choose classification when you need a discrete label; choose regression when you need a precise numeric estimate.
