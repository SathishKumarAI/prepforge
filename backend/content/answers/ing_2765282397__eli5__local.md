Imagine a bakery that sells custom cakes. Every cake order must be handled exactly as the customer wants: the recipe (data) is correct, the ingredients are added one by one without mistake, the oven stays at the right temperature until the cake finishes baking, and if anything goes wrong the whole batch is thrown out so no half‑baked cake gets sold.

In databases this “cake‑making” rule set is called **ACID**:

* **A**tomicity – an operation is all or nothing.  
* **C**onsistency – data stays in a valid state before and after the change.  
* **I**solation – concurrent changes don’t interfere with each other, like separate ovens.  
* **D**urability – once committed, the change survives crashes, like a cake that stays firm even if the kitchen power goes out.

ACID is critical when you need reliable records: banking transactions (every debit must match a credit), airline seat bookings (no double‑booking seats), and medical records (patient data must never be corrupted). In these cases a broken “cake” would mean money lost, missed flights, or wrong treatment—something that can’t be tolerated.
