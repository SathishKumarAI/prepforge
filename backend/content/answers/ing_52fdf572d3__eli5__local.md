Think of a machine‑learning interview as a friendly cooking class where you’re asked to whip up a dish from a pantry of ingredients (your knowledge).  
**1. Brush up on the basics:** Know what *data* is (the raw ingredients), how a *model* learns patterns like a recipe, and what *training* means (tasting and tweaking until it tastes right).  
**2. Practice the “recipes”:** Work through common problems—linear regression, decision trees, neural nets—so you can explain the steps clearly, just as you’d describe making spaghetti sauce from scratch.  
**3. Master the kitchen tools:** Be comfortable with Python, NumPy, scikit‑learn or TensorFlow; think of them as your knives and pots.  
**4. Review the menu (common questions):** “What’s overfitting?” (your dish tastes great on the training data but not on new customers). “How do you handle missing values?” (decide whether to discard or fill in gaps).  
**5. Do mock tastings:** Practice explaining your code and reasoning aloud, as if teaching a classmate.  

By treating each topic like a familiar recipe step, you’ll walk into the interview confident that you can turn raw data into delicious insights.
