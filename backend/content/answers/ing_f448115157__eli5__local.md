Imagine a safety guard standing beside a busy highway. When the guard sees a clear, wide lane, they let cars drive on their own. But when traffic gets tight or an unexpected obstacle appears, the guard steps in and takes control, directing the flow to keep everyone safe.

In AI this is “Confidence‑Based Escalation.” The system first checks how sure it is about its answer (its confidence). If confidence is high—like a wide lane—the model gives the result directly. If confidence drops below a set threshold—just as traffic gets congested—the human “guard” (the Human‑in‑The‑Loop) steps in, reviews the case, and makes or verifies the final decision. This way, AI handles routine jobs alone while humans intervene only when uncertainty rises, ensuring safety without constant oversight.
