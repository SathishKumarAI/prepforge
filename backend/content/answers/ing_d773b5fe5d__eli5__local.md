Imagine you’re at a huge library where every book is written in a secret language that computers can read. An *embedding model* is like a super‑smart librarian who turns each word or sentence into a tiny point on a map—called an “embedding.” Words that mean similar things (like “happy” and “joyful”) end up close together on this map, while unrelated words are far apart.

The librarian uses patterns from millions of books to decide where each point goes. When you ask the computer about a topic, it looks at where your question lands on the map and finds the nearest points—books that talk about similar ideas. That’s how embeddings let AI quickly find related information without reading every word in full.
