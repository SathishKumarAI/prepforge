Imagine you’re listening to a song on a radio. The sound you hear is a mix of many simple “notes” (sine waves) played at different pitches and loudness. A **Fourier transform** is the music‑studio tool that takes that mixed signal and tells you exactly which notes are in it, how loud each one is, and what their exact frequencies (pitches) are.

In math terms, a Fourier transform rewrites any time‑varying waveform as a sum of pure sine waves. It “translates” the signal from the *time domain* (what happens over time) to the *frequency domain* (how much of each frequency is present). This lets engineers and scientists see hidden patterns—like the hum of an engine or the rhythm in brain waves—that are hard to spot just by listening.
