Think of a neural network like a tiny city where many traffic lights (neurons) sit on streets (connections). Each light decides whether to let cars (information) pass based on how bright the street is and what signals it receives from neighboring lights. When you “feed in” data, it’s like sending cars through the city: they travel along roads, stop at each light, and the lights adjust their settings gradually so that traffic eventually flows smoothly toward a destination—your answer.

A neural network is just a collection of these adjustable lights wired together. It learns by slowly tweaking the lights’ settings (weights) whenever the flow doesn’t match what you expect, similar to how drivers learn the best routes over time. In deep learning we stack many layers of such cities so that each layer extracts higher‑level patterns, allowing the system to solve complex tasks like recognizing faces or translating language.
