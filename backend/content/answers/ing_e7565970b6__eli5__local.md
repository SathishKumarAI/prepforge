Imagine you’re at a farmer’s market deciding which apples to buy.  
Each apple shows you a few things: its color, weight, shape, and maybe a little scent. Those visible clues are the **features** – measurable attributes that describe each apple.

Now picture someone who can’t see the apples but still wants to choose the best ones. They might rely on a *rest* of information that isn’t part of those features: the farmer’s reputation, the season, or how long the apples have been stored. In machine‑learning terms, **“rest”** refers to everything else you don’t explicitly measure as a feature—background context, hidden variables, or unobserved factors that still influence the outcome.

So, while features are the clear, measurable parts of an item (like apple color), *rest* is the unseen background that can still affect what you’re trying to predict. Understanding both helps build more accurate models, just as knowing the farmer’s reputation might make you choose better apples even if they look identical.
