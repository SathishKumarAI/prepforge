Imagine a neighborhood where every house has its own small bakery instead of one big supermarket. Each baker (computer) can bake bread (share files or data) directly for any neighbor who wants it, without going through a central store. That’s the essence of a **peer‑to‑peer (P2P) network**: every participant is both a client and a server, sharing resources directly with each other.

**Key features**

* No single master hub – all nodes can initiate or receive connections.
* Resources are spread across many machines, so no single point of failure.
* Each node contributes its own bandwidth and storage.

**Pros**

* Scales naturally; more users mean more shared capacity.
* Resilient—if one baker stops, others keep supplying bread.
* Often cheaper because it uses existing home or office computers.

**Cons**

* Harder to control – anyone can join or leave, making security a challenge.
* Performance can fluctuate as each node’s speed varies.
* Central coordination (e.g., searching for a file) must be handled by the network itself.

In short, P2P is like a community of bakers who trade directly, sharing what they have and relying on everyone else to keep the supply flowing.
