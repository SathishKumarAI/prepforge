According to *information‑theoretic learning*, skill acquisition is maximized when the learner encounters examples that are just beyond current performance (the “zone of proximal development”). Humans calibrate effort against observable standards; the mere presence of others elevating their performance nudges us to close the gap. This is rooted in *self‑determination theory*: competence, autonomy, and relatedness combine to enhance intrinsic motivation.
