### Why the “loop” of an interview matters

In any hiring pipeline the *loop*—the cycle of feedback, iteration, and decision—must converge on a candidate’s fit for a specific role.  
The **fundamental problem** is that different firms have distinct **utility functions**: a startup maximizes rapid growth (high risk tolerance), while an incumbent bank optimizes stability and compliance. The loop length and shape are simply the *gradient descent* toward the optimum of each utility function.

| Company type | Loop characteristics | Why it works |
|--------------|----------------------|--------------|
| **Tech startup** | 1–2 rapid rounds, heavy coding + informal “culture‑fit” chat. | Gradient step is large: they can afford many low‑confidence hires and adjust quickly based on early results. |
| **Mid‑size SaaS** | 3–4 structured interviews (tech, product, team), with a final stakeholder panel. | The loop balances exploration (technical depth) and exploitation (team cohesion). Each round reduces uncertainty in both dimensions. |
| **Financial institution** | Multi‑day onsite, technical exam + regulatory scenario + behavioral “stress” test. | The loop is long to ensure *risk minimization*: every step eliminates a candidate’s probability of non‑compliance or poor audit performance. |

A **non‑obvious insight**: the *behavioral* part of the loop is not merely cultural fit; it encodes the company’s **latent constraint set** (e.g., regulatory constraints for banks). By structuring behavioral questions to probe these constraints, firms effectively perform a dimensionality reduction on the candidate’s latent feature space, ensuring that only those who satisfy hard bounds survive the loop.

In short, loops differ because each firm solves a different constrained optimization problem; the interview stages are the algorithmic steps that drive the solution toward its optimum.
