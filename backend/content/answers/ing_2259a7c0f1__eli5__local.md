Imagine you’re watching a favorite TV show online. If the stream keeps buffering or stops altogether, you’re disappointed. In the cloud, **availability** is the promise that your service (like that streaming platform) will stay on air whenever you need it—ideally 99.9 % of the time. To achieve this, providers spread the same software across many servers in different data centers, so if one server or whole region goes down, others pick up the slack without you noticing. Think of it as having several backup power generators: when one fails, another kicks in instantly. Availability is a key part of reliability, but it’s not absolute; outages can still happen, just less often and with quicker recovery.
