Imagine a bakery that makes dozens of identical loaves every morning. If one oven breaks, another still has fresh bread to sell. In the same way, a “distributed system” is like many small bakeries spread across a city, all working together to serve customers.  
“Reliable replication” means each bakery keeps an exact copy of the recipe book and a stock of dough in its own kitchen. If one bakery’s oven stops, another can hand over a loaf without waiting for help from far away. This backup copying makes sure that even when some parts fail—power outages, network hiccups, or hardware breaks—the whole service keeps running smoothly, just as the city still has bread.  
In short, replication is a safety net of duplicate data and processes that keeps the system alive, no matter what individual component goes down.
