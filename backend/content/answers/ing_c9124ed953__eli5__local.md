Imagine a smart robot that can play chess, write poems, or help with homework. But you don’t want it to wander off the chessboard and accidentally break your computer or steal your secrets. So we put the robot in a **sandboxed environment**—a special, isolated “playground” inside the computer where it can do its job but cannot touch anything outside that playground.

A **computer use agent** is just a program that helps you with tasks (like booking flights or answering questions). In a sandbox, the agent runs in its own little box: it has its own memory space and only specific permissions. If something goes wrong—say the agent misbehaves—it stays trapped inside the box and can’t harm your main system.

Think of the sandbox like a playground with safety gates: the child (the AI) is free to play, but the gates keep them from running into traffic or taking toys from other children’s rooms. This keeps both the computer safe and the agent useful.
