Imagine you have a huge library of books, but instead of flipping through pages, you want to know which books are similar just by looking at a tiny card that summarizes each one. An **embedding** is that summary card for data—words, images, or anything else. It turns the complex item into a list of numbers (a vector). Think of a map: every place on Earth has coordinates (latitude, longitude). Those coordinates let you find how far apart places are and see which are close together. In the same way, embeddings give each item a “location” in a **vector space**—a math world where distance means similarity. If two words have nearby coordinates, they share meaning; if images cluster together, they look alike. This numeric representation lets computers compare, search, and learn from data quickly, just like using a map to navigate cities.
