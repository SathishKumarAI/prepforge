Imagine a kitchen where you have a big cookbook full of recipes. Each recipe tells you exactly how to make a dish: mix flour, eggs, sugar, bake for 30 minutes, etc. In the same way, most programming languages give you built‑in “recipes” (functions) like `print()` or `len()`. A *user‑defined function* (UDF) is just your own recipe that you write and name yourself—maybe `make_sandwich()` or `calculate_average()`. You decide what ingredients (variables) it takes, what steps (code) to perform, and what comes out (return value). Once written, you can call this UDF anywhere in your program, just like calling a recipe. It lets you reuse the same logic without rewriting it each time, keeping code clear and organized.
