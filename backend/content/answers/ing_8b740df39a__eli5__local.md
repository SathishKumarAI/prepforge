Think of a huge, old house (the monolith) where every room is connected by a single hallway that everyone must walk through to get anywhere. It’s cozy at first, but when more people move in, the hallway gets crowded and any change—adding a new kitchen or fixing a window—requires shutting down the whole house.

Breaking it into microservices is like converting that house into separate apartment buildings on the same plot. Each building (a microservice) has its own entrance, kitchen, bathroom, and can be renovated or upgraded independently without disturbing the others. The buildings still share the common land (the overall system), but they communicate only through well‑defined doors—APIs—that let residents exchange mail, not walk through each other’s living rooms.

**Unavoidable term defined:** *API* – a set of rules that lets different software parts talk to each other like a standardized mailbox.
