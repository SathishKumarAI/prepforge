Choosing an AI model is like picking a tool for a DIY project.  
First, **look at the job size** – if you’re building a tiny birdhouse (a simple classification task), a small “lightweight” model will finish fast and use little memory. For a huge shed (complex language generation), you’ll need a bigger, more powerful model that can hold lots of knowledge.  
Second, **consider the budget of time and money** – training a large model is like renting a workshop; it costs more in GPU hours and electricity. If you’re on a tight budget, choose a cheaper, pre‑trained model that still does the job well.  
Third, **think about how much data you have** – a model needs enough examples to learn; if your data set is small, use a simpler model or one that can “learn from fewer samples.”  
Finally, **check how fast it must run** – like a cordless drill versus a corded one, some models are designed for real‑time responses while others are slower but more accurate.  

By matching job size, budget, data amount, and speed to the right model, you’ll pick the tool that gets the work done efficiently and correctly.
