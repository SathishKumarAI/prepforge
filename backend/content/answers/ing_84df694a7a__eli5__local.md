Think of a large office where every employee needs to know the latest company news, but there’s no central bulletin board. Instead, each person whispers the update to just a handful of coworkers, who then whisper it further. By the time the word has travelled around the building, almost everyone knows the news—without any single person having to tell everyone directly.

That’s what a **gossip protocol** does in computer systems. Each node shares information with only a few random peers; those peers spread it again. Because the workload is split among many nodes, the system can grow to thousands or millions of participants without getting clogged. The “high scalability” comes from this decentralised, peer‑to‑peer spreading—no single bottleneck, and updates reach everyone quickly even as the network expands.
