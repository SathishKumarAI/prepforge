**LLM-as‑Judge** is like having a very knowledgeable referee in a game of “human vs. computer.”  
Imagine you’re playing chess with an opponent who can think many moves ahead but sometimes forgets the rules or makes a mistake. You ask a large language model (LLM) to watch the whole match and, when a rule‑breaking move happens, to point it out, explain why it’s wrong, and suggest the correct play.  

The LLM reads the game transcript, remembers the rules you set, and judges each move. It’s useful when the task is:  
* **Rule enforcement** (legal contracts, grading essays, code reviews).  
* **Transparent explanations** of why something passes or fails.  
* **Speedy, consistent feedback** on many items at once.

It works best when the rules are clear and can be described in words; it’s less reliable for tasks that need deep intuition, creativity, or domain‑specific expertise beyond the text it was trained on.
