Imagine you’re writing a story but can only use sentences that already exist in your notebook. **Retrieval** is the act of flipping through the notebook to find the best‑matching sentences, then pasting them into your draft so they fit together.

In AI language models, *context construction* is how the model decides what text it should consider before answering a question. The model first **retrieves** relevant pieces from a huge database—like pulling out specific paragraphs from books that mention “photosynthesis.” Those retrieved snippets become the new “context” that the model reads and combines to generate an answer.

So, retrieval is the search step; context construction is the assembly of those search results into a coherent background for the final response. This keeps answers grounded in real information rather than pure guesswork.
