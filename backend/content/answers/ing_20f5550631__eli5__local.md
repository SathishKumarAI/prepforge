Think of a **closure** like a secret garden that you plant in your backyard (a function). When you finish planting, you close the gate so nobody can wander off, but you leave a special note inside that remembers where all the seeds are and how much water they need.  

In JavaScript, a *function* can “remember” the variables that were around when it was created—those variables stay tucked away even after the outer function has finished running. The combination of the function **and** those remembered variables is the closure. It lets you keep data private and reuse logic later, just like your garden stays intact while you go about your day.  

So a closure = *function + its hidden memory* that lives on after its parent finishes.
