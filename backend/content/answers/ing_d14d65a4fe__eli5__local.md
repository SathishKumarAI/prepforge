Think of a recipe book (offline) versus a live cooking show (online).  
**Offline computation** is like reading the whole recipe before you start: all the ingredients, steps, and timing are known in advance. Your computer can plan every move ahead of time, optimizing for speed or memory, because it already has the full “menu.”  

**Online computation** is like watching a cooking show where the chef receives secret spice packets one by one. You must decide what to do with each packet as soon as you get it—maybe add it now or wait for later steps—without knowing what will arrive next. Your program works in real time, making decisions on the fly and often only using current information.  

Both methods aim to finish a dish (solve a problem), but offline can pre‑calculate everything, while online must adapt immediately as new data arrives.
