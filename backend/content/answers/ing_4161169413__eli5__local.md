Think of a web crawler as a curious librarian who visits every library in the city and copies each book’s cover into a giant index.  
**Storage** is that index: it keeps a record of every page found, where it lives (URL), when it was last visited, how long it might change (age), and a short excerpt to decide if it needs re‑checking.  

The librarian uses two shelves:  
* **Queue shelf** – holds the URLs waiting to be read, sorted by priority (new or likely to change).  
* **Archive shelf** – stores the actual page copies (the “book covers”).  

When the librarian finishes a book, she writes its details on the queue shelf and puts the cover on the archive shelf. A small database keeps both shelves fast and searchable. This simple two‑shelf system lets the crawler keep growing without losing track of what’s been seen or needs revisiting.
