Think of training an AI as teaching a child how to throw a ball into a bucket. Every time the child throws, we check if it lands inside (good) or misses (bad). The **cost function** is like the teacher’s scorecard: it takes the child’s throw and assigns a number that tells us how far off the target was—zero if perfect, higher numbers for larger misses.  

In AI, the “throw” is the model’s prediction, the “bucket” is the correct answer, and the cost function turns the difference into a single number. The learning algorithm then tries to lower this number step by step, just as the child keeps practicing until the score gets close to zero. This simple scoring system guides every adjustment, making sure the AI improves steadily toward accuracy.
