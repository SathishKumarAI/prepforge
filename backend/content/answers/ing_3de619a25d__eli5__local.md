A **solid‑state drive (SSD)** is like the first, instant‑access library: it stores data on tiny flash chips that can be read or written almost instantly, with no moving parts. In contrast, an older **hard disk drive (HDD)** uses spinning magnetic disks and a mechanical arm to locate information, which slows everything down.
