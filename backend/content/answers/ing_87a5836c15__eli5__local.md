**MEM = “Multi‑Layer Embedded Memory” – a special kind of on‑chip RAM that stacks many tiny memory chips right next to the processor so data can flow in and out at lightning speed.**

Think of your computer’s brain (the CPU or GPU) as a chef who needs ingredients (data) from the pantry (memory). Ordinary pantry shelves are narrow aisles; the chef has to walk back and forth, carrying one basket at a time. That limits how fast the meal can be prepared.

Now imagine the pantry is built like a multi‑storey supermarket with *hundreds of wide conveyor belts* that run parallel to each other, all connected directly to the kitchen floor. The chef can drop a basket on any belt and instantly grab it from anywhere in the store without walking. That’s what MEM does: by stacking memory layers and giving them many parallel “baskets” (memory ports), it lets data stream straight into the processor at extreme bandwidth—much faster than conventional, single‑lane memory.
