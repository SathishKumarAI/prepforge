**Why technology reshapes human conversation**

At its core, communication is an *information‑transfer* problem: two agents must exchange a signal so that each can update their internal state (beliefs, intentions) with minimal error. Classical models treat the channel as noisy; Shannon’s capacity theorem tells us we need bandwidth and coding to overcome noise. Mobile phones, instant messaging, and social media have simply *expanded the channel’s bandwidth* and *reduced its effective noise* by allowing multimodal cues (video, emoji, geolocation) to accompany text.

The deeper principle is **contextual redundancy**: humans rely on shared background knowledge to infer meaning from sparse signals. Technology supplies *digital breadcrumbs*—timestamps, metadata, usage patterns—that act as explicit priors. Algorithms learn these priors and can predict user intent (autocomplete, smart replies), effectively shrinking the cognitive load.

**Non‑obvious insight:** The real shift is not in speed or reach but in **“communication fidelity over time.”** Digital traces persist, enabling asynchronous, cumulative conversations that evolve like a living document. This transforms the *temporal dimension* of dialogue: people can revisit, edit, and build upon earlier messages, creating a self‑organizing archive that feeds back into future interactions—an emergent form of collective memory that no analogue medium could sustain.
