Think of an AI project like a kitchen where you’re cooking a complex dish (the final model). “Agents” are the sous‑chefs that automatically pick ingredients, stir, or taste while you sit at the counter. “Tool use” is when you hand over parts of the recipe to specialized appliances—like a blender for raw data or a spell‑checker for code.

Interviewers look out for red flags that the dish might be improvised without your full control:

1. **Blind delegation** – letting an agent finish everything without reviewing its choices, like trusting the sous‑chef to add salt and then never tasting the soup yourself.  
2. **Opaque decisions** – agents making moves you can’t trace or explain; you’d be unable to justify a flavor change to a customer.  
3. **Over‑reliance on external tools** – treating a blender as a “magic wand” that replaces your understanding of ingredients, leading to errors when the appliance malfunctions.  
4. **Hidden dependencies** – using third‑party agents or APIs without knowing their update schedule or data privacy policies; it’s like borrowing someone else’s secret sauce and not being sure what’s in it.

Good practice is to keep a clear line of sight: you design the recipe, you monitor the sous‑chefs, and you can step in whenever something tastes off. This shows interviewers that you understand both the power and the responsibility of agents and tools.
