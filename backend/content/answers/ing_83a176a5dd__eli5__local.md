Think of an **embedding** as a secret translator that turns every word, sentence or image into a tidy list of numbers—just like turning a picture into a set of Lego bricks where each brick has a specific color and shape.  
A **vector space** is the big room where all those Lego lists sit together; it lets us add, subtract, or stretch them while staying inside the same room.

When we pick which “translator” (embedding) to use for a task—say finding similar books—we look at **selection criteria**:

* **Relevance** – Does the translator give numbers that capture meaning?  
* **Compactness** – Are the lists short enough to store and fast to compare?  
* **Generalization** – Will it work well on new, unseen data?  

So choosing an embedding is like picking the right Lego set: you want bricks that fit your model’s needs, are easy to handle, and will still build useful shapes when you encounter fresh ideas.
