Imagine building a smart city where every street, traffic light, and parking meter must talk to one another so the whole place runs smoothly. In machine‑learning projects, the “smart city” is your data pipeline, models, and user apps; the “street plan” is the software architecture that tells each part how to connect.

A good architecture lays out clear neighborhoods:  
* **Data ingestion** (collecting raw sensor feeds)  
* **Feature storage** (a shared library of reusable inputs)  
* **Model training & serving** (the engine room that churns predictions on demand)  
* **Monitoring & governance** (traffic lights that alert when something breaks)

Design resources—books, online tutorials, and code templates—are like city‑planning manuals. They show you how to choose the right “road type” (microservices vs monolith), how to keep traffic flowing without bottlenecks, and how to scale when the city grows.

By treating your ML system as a well‑planned city, you can add new features or replace old ones without shutting everything down. That’s what software architecture for machine learning is all about: organized, modular, and ready for future expansion.
