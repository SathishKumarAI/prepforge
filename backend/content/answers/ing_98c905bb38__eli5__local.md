Imagine you have a recipe book that learns how to cook better each time you try it. At first, the book just follows instructions blindly—no matter what ingredients you use, the dish comes out the same. Machine learning is like giving that book a way to taste every batch it makes and then adjust its own recipe based on what tastes good or bad.

In this analogy:

* **Data** are the ingredients and cooking results you feed into the book.
* A **model** is the recipe itself, a set of rules the book uses to decide how much salt, heat, or time to apply.
* The book **learns** by repeatedly testing its recipes (making dishes), comparing the outcome with your feedback (taste test), and tweaking its rules so the next dish comes closer to perfection.

So, machine learning is a method that lets computers create and refine these “recipes” automatically, using past examples to make better predictions or decisions in the future.
