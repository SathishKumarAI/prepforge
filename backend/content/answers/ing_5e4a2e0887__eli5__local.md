Imagine your computer is a kitchen where you can cook any recipe (run any program).  
**Sandboxing** is like putting that kitchen inside a separate room with its own windows: the food you make stays in that room and can’t spill onto the rest of the house. It keeps other parts of the system safe from accidental spills or deliberate sabotage.

**Agentic Security** is the idea that each “recipe” (software agent) should act only as it’s told—no surprise moves. Think of a robot chef that follows a set of instructions but can’t decide to steal ingredients or open the fridge on its own. It must stay inside the kitchen room and obey the recipe book.

So, sandboxing isolates where the code runs, while agentic security guarantees the code behaves predictably within that isolated space.
