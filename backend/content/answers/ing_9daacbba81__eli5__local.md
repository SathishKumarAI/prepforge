Think of a smart robot as a new house‑mate who can do chores but also has a “remote control” that lets anyone change its settings. Interviewers are like the roommates’ checklist: they want to make sure the robot won’t accidentally lock you out, share your private photos, or start doing things it shouldn’t.

**Red flags** are warning signs in the robot’s design:

1. **Safety gaps** – The robot can act on unsafe commands (e.g., “open the door”) without checking if that would hurt someone.  
2. **Security holes** – Easy ways for outsiders to log into the robot and alter its behavior or steal data.  
3. **Responsibility slips** – The robot’s creators ignore how it could bias people, spread misinformation, or violate privacy.

If any of these flags pop up during an interview, the hiring team will pause and ask for tighter safeguards before bringing the robot home.
