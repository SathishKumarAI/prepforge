Imagine you’re at a crowded kitchen and you want to bake a cake. “Loading upfront” is like gathering all the ingredients—flour, sugar, eggs, butter—before you even start mixing. Context Engineering is the recipe book that tells you which ingredients fit together, how much of each to use, and in what order to combine them so the cake turns out just right.

In machine learning, we first collect a large set of data (the “ingredients”). Then we design the *context*—which features matter, how they should be scaled or transformed, and which parts of the data we’ll feed into the model. This preparation lets the algorithm focus on the most useful patterns, just as a good recipe guides you to mix flour with sugar before adding eggs. The result is a smoother training process and more reliable predictions.
