Think of machine learning as a smart kitchen that learns how to cook better over time.  
**Hardware** (the stove, pots, and utensils) is the computer’s physical parts—CPU, GPU, memory—that can run recipes quickly.  
**Software** (the cookbook and instructions) are the algorithms: the step‑by‑step rules telling the machine what to do with data.  

In this kitchen, the “recipes” aren’t fixed. After each meal, the chef tastes it, notes which ingredients made it taste better, and rewrites the recipe so the next dish comes out even tastier. That tasting and rewriting is **learning**: the computer adjusts its internal settings (weights) based on experience.  

So machine learning isn’t just a hardware‑or‑software mix; it’s an interactive loop where hardware runs software that continually improves itself through feedback—just like a chef refining recipes after every meal.
