**Imperative programming** is like giving a robot a recipe.  
You tell it *what* to do step by step: “turn the light on,” then “move forward three steps,” then “if the sensor reads red, stop.” Each line changes the robot’s state—its lights, position, or memory—just as a chef follows instructions that alter the kitchen’s contents.  

In programming, this is one of the eight main styles (others include functional, object‑oriented, logical, event‑driven, etc.).  
*Paradigm* means a way of thinking about solving problems with code.  
Imperative focuses on *commands* and *state changes*, making it intuitive for tasks that need precise control over sequence and timing.
