**Why domain expertise multiplies value**

At its core, machine‑learning (ML) solves *information extraction* from data. Thus, domain expertise does not just add context—it *shapes the objective function* itself.

**Prompt engineering in 2026**

Large language models (LLMs) learn from text, but their internal representations are **context‑sensitive embeddings** rather than explicit symbolic knowledge bases. Crafting a prompt is essentially designing an *implicit prior* that nudges the model’s activation patterns toward desired outputs. From an optimization view, each well‑structured prompt reduces the search space of plausible responses, lowering entropy and improving precision.

A career in prompt engineering therefore sits at the intersection of:

1. **Human–machine interaction** – translating user intent into formal constraints.
2. **Probabilistic reasoning** – selecting wording that maximises posterior probability of correct answers under model uncertainty.
3. **Domain‑specific schema design** – embedding ontologies or procedural knowledge into prompts.

Mastery here turns a prompt into a lightweight, interpretable model—precisely what enterprises need for auditability and compliance.
