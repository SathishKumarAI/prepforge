**First Contentful Paint (FCP)** is the moment your page shows *something* useful—text, image, or button—to a visitor. Imagine you’re in a dark room and someone flips on a single light bulb that illuminates a picture frame on the wall. That instant the frame becomes visible is like FCP: it tells users that the site has started to load.

Think of your website as a theater production. The curtain (the browser) opens, and the first spotlight (FCP) shines on the actors’ costumes or scenery. If the spotlight appears quickly, people feel the show is starting promptly; if it lags, they might think the theater is stuck.  

In performance terms, FCP measures how fast the browser can parse HTML, build a render tree, and paint the first piece of content. It’s a key signal that the page is progressing toward full interactivity, so ignoring it means missing an early warning about delays that frustrate users.
