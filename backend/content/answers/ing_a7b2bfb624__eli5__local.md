Think of an online library that gives you books on demand.  
Every time you ask for a book, the library can pull it from its shelves (costly) or hand you a copy you already have in your backpack (cheap).  

In AI “context caching economics,” the library is the model and the books are pieces of text you want it to remember.  
**Pricing** is how much you pay for each new book pulled from the shelf—more pages, more money.  
**Costs** come from two places: (1) the energy to fetch and store the book, and (2) the memory space you give up in your backpack.  

If you keep a popular book on hand (cache it), future requests are free or cheap, but if too many books crowd your backpack, you might need a bigger backpack—more cost.  
Balancing what to cache and when to fetch keeps the library running smoothly and affordably.
