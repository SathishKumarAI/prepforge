Think of machine learning as baking a cake that learns to taste better each time you bake it.

1. **Data (Ingredients)** – The raw facts you give the model, like images or numbers.  
2. **Model (Recipe)** – A set of mathematical instructions (layers of neurons) that combine ingredients into an output.  
3. **Training (Mixing & Baking)** – You let the model make guesses, compare them to the true answers, and adjust its recipe using *gradient descent*—a way to “tilt” the recipe toward better results.  
4. **Loss function (Taste Test)** – A score that tells how far off the cake is from the desired flavor; lower loss means tastier cake.  
5. **Optimizer (Chef’s Knife)** – The tool that cuts the recipe in the right direction, deciding how much each ingredient changes after every bake.  
6. **Evaluation (Tasting Panel)** – After training, you test the cake on fresh data it hasn’t seen to see if it still tastes good.

Just as a chef refines a recipe by tasting and adjusting, an ML model improves its predictions by iteratively learning from data.
