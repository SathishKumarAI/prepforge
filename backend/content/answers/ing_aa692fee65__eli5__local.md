Think of a cursor like the little red arrow that moves through a long text document on your computer. It marks one spot, lets you read or edit that word, and can jump to another place with a click. In databases (the big data warehouses many AI teams use), a cursor does the same thing: it points to a single row inside a huge table of information. When an interview question asks about cursors, they’re checking whether you know how to walk through rows one by one, pull out or change specific data, and do it efficiently without grabbing the whole table at once.
