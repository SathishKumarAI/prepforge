Imagine you’re at a library where every book has been read by a super‑smart librarian who knows how people talk and write. That librarian is **the GPT model**. 

First, the librarian spends years (pre‑training) reading millions of books, news articles, recipes, poems—anything written in English. While reading, she learns patterns: which words often appear together, how sentences flow, and what topics usually follow each other. She doesn’t memorize the exact text; instead she builds a mental “grammar map” that lets her guess what comes next in any sentence.

When you ask her a question or give her a prompt (inference), she consults that map, predicts the most likely next word, then the next, and so on—like filling in blank spaces in a story. The result is fresh, original text that sounds natural because it’s grounded in the patterns she learned during pre‑training.
