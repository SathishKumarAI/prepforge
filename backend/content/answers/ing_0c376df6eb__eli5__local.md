Think of learning like cooking a stew.  
An **episode** is one single recipe you cook: the exact ingredients, the order you add them, and how long it takes. While you’re stirring, that episode is a *process*—you can pause or change it.

A **fact** (or semantic knowledge) is the finished dish on the plate, labeled “beef stew.” It’s a reusable recipe: anyone can grab the label and make it again without remembering every stir.

Your consolidation policy decides when to move from the messy pot to the neatly labeled dish.  
1. **Stability check:** If you’ve cooked the same episode many times in a row (the taste doesn’t change), you’re ready.  
2. **Value check:** The episode solves a problem people often ask about (high demand).  
3. **Storage check:** Your kitchen has space for a new label.

When all three are satisfied, you write down the recipe, store it in your cookbook, and free up the pot for a new experiment. That moment—stable, useful, and stored—is when an episode becomes a fact.
