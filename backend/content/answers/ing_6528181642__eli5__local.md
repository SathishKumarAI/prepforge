Think of your bank account as a mailbox, and the money you receive or send as letters.  
**ACH (Automated Clearing House)** is like the postal service that moves those letters between mailboxes on the same network.

When someone sends you an ACH direct‑deposit check, they write a “letter” with the amount and your account number. The sender’s bank puts this letter into the ACH system, which sorts it, verifies it, and then delivers it to your bank’s mailbox. Your bank opens the mailbox, reads the letter, and deposits the money into your account—usually within one or two business days.

If you want to pay someone with ACH, you write a letter that says “transfer $X from my mailbox to theirs.” The sender’s bank sends it through the same postal system; the recipient’s bank receives it and credits their mailbox.  
So, ACH is simply an electronic mail service that moves money safely between accounts without paper checks or cash.
