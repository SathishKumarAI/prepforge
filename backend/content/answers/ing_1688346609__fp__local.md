*Fundamental problem.*  
Hiring a software engineer is an *optimization* of two competing objectives: (1) **predict future performance** and (2) **minimize uncertainty** about that prediction. The interview loop is Palantir’s stochastic algorithm for balancing these.

*Why the loop works.*

After every iteration we update our posterior belief \(p(\theta|X_{1:t})\) via Bayesian inference. The expected loss (e.g., hiring a bad engineer vs. rejecting a good one) is minimized when we continue interviewing until the marginal benefit of another interview equals its cost—exactly the *optimal stopping* condition. This yields an adaptive “loop” that stops early for clear outliers and continues longer for borderline cases.

*Deeper principle.*
