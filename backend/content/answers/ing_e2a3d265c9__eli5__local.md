Imagine a city where every shop has its own unique phone number, but the numbers change often because new shops open and old ones close. A **service registry** is like a giant phone book that keeps an up‑to‑date list of every shop’s current number. When someone (a computer program) wants to talk to a shop (another program), they first look in this phone book. This lookup process is called **discovery**: the caller asks, “Who’s on 555‑1234?” and receives the correct address or IP.  

So, a service registry stores all available services and their locations; discovery lets programs find and connect to those services automatically, even as the city (the network) changes. This keeps everything flexible and prevents hard‑coding addresses in every program.
