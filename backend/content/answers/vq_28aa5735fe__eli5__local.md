Imagine you’re looking at a crowd of people standing in a line from shortest to tallest, and you want a quick snapshot that tells you how the heights are spread out without naming every single person. That snapshot is called a **five‑number summary**.

It consists of five simple values:

1. **Minimum** – the very shortest person.
2. **First quartile (Q1)** – the height below which 25 % of people fall; think of it as the “low‑quarter” marker.
3. **Median (Q2)** – the middle height, where half the crowd is shorter and half is taller.
4. **Third quartile (Q3)** – the height above which only 25 % remain; the “high‑quarter” marker.
5. **Maximum** – the tallest person.

Together these numbers show the spread (range), the central tendency (median), and where most people cluster (the two quartiles). It’s like a concise “height map” that lets you compare different crowds or spot outliers without getting lost in every detail.
