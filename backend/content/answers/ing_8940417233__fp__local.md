**Why a webhook system needs to be *scalable***

A webhook is an asynchronous “push” from a service A to consumer B.  
The fundamental problem is **to guarantee that every event emitted by A reaches B exactly once, without blocking A or B, even when the event rate spikes to millions per second**.

---

### 1. The underlying principle: *decoupled publish–subscribe with bounded‑latency guarantees*

- **Publish–Subscribe** lets producers (A) and consumers (B) evolve independently.  
- To keep latency low we must avoid a *tight coupling* where A waits for B’s acknowledgment.  
- Therefore the system must *buffer* events, apply back‑pressure to producers, and replay lost messages.

### 2. Architectural building blocks

| Block | Role | Why it works |
|-------|------|--------------|
| **Event queue (e.g., Kafka, SQS)** | Decouple emission from delivery | Guarantees durability & ordering; horizontal scaling via partitions |
| **Consumer pool** | Parallel processing of webhook calls | Each worker can retry independently, enabling *at‑least‑once* semantics |
| **Circuit breaker / rate limiter** | Protect downstream services | Prevents cascading failures when B is slow or down |
| **Idempotency key** | Avoid duplicate deliveries | Enables safe retries without side‑effects |

### 3. The “deep” insight

> **Latency ≈ (Queue depth) × (Consumer throughput)**  

When the event rate exceeds consumer capacity, queue depth grows linearly with backlog. Thus *elastic scaling of consumers* is the only way to keep latency bounded; horizontal auto‑scaling based on queue metrics is essential.

### 4. One non‑obvious trick

**Use *push* for high‑frequency events and *pull* for low‑frequency ones**.  
Pulling (e.g., long‑polling or scheduled fetch) reduces the need for a massive consumer pool when traffic is sparse, saving compute while still satisfying “exactly‑once” guarantees via checkpointed offsets.

---

By treating webhooks as an event‑driven pipeline and grounding the design in queue theory + idempotent retries, you get a system that scales linearly with load, maintains reliability, and keeps costs predictable.
