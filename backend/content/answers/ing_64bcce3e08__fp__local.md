**Why interview questions for an AI engineer should be “role‑guided”**

The *core problem* of AI engineering is to turn a specification (accuracy, latency, robustness) into a production‑ready system that reliably serves users. The interview must therefore probe three intertwined dimensions:

1. **Problem‑solving mindset** – can the candidate formulate a modeling objective from noisy data?  
2. **Systems awareness** – does he understand how training pipelines, inference graphs, and deployment constraints interact?  
3. **Ethical & operational judgment** – can he anticipate bias, explainability, and compliance issues?

These dimensions are not independent; they map onto a deeper principle: *end‑to‑end optimization*. A model is only useful if the entire pipeline—from data ingestion to user feedback—optimizes a joint objective. Questions that mirror each stage (e.g., “How would you handle concept drift in a live recommendation system?”) reveal whether the candidate thinks of the AI solution as part of a larger control loop.

**Non‑obvious insight:** Many candidates excel at algorithmic tricks but fail to articulate how they translate into *loss functions* that capture real‑world constraints (e.g., “What loss would you use if inference latency is capped at 50 ms?”). Probing this forces the interviewee to bridge theory and practice, exposing a hidden skill set: **designing composite objectives**. This is what distinguishes an engineer who can build models from one who can deploy them sustainably.
