**Gleaning Documentation in Machine Learning**

Imagine you’re a detective trying to solve a mystery, but the clues are hidden inside a huge library. *Documentation* is like the library’s catalog: it tells you where each book (code file, model description, data set) lives and what it contains. *Gleaning* means carefully reading that catalog—looking at titles, summaries, and tags—to pick out only the books that are relevant to your case.

In practice, a machine‑learning team writes short, clear notes next to each script or notebook: “This function trains the CNN on ImageNet” or “Here we preprocess the CSV into tensors.” When someone else (or you later) needs to understand how a model works, they *glean* that documentation—scan the comments, check the file names, read the short explanations—so they don’t have to sift through lines of code. It’s like having a map in a maze: you still walk the path, but you know exactly where each turn leads.

**Key terms**

- **Documentation** – written notes that explain what code or data does.  
- **Gleaning** – selectively reading and collecting useful information from those notes.  

By keeping documentation tidy and always gleaning it before diving into the code, teams avoid confusion and save time, just like a detective who knows where to look for clues in a crowded library.
