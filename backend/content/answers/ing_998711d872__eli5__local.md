Think of an agent like a chef who has two special drawers in the kitchen.  
The **context drawer** holds the recipe book for the dish you’re cooking right now—ingredients, steps, timing. It’s accessed quickly every time you taste or adjust the dish; it contains only what matters for the current task.  

The **memory drawer** is a pantry that keeps all the spices and leftovers from past meals. When you need to add flavor later or recall how a particular spice was used before, you open this drawer. It’s slower to reach but stores richer, longer‑term information.  

In agentic AI, choosing between these drawers means deciding whether the system should focus on immediate “context” (fast, task‑specific data) or deeper “memory” (historical knowledge that can guide future actions). The right mix lets the agent be both quick and wise.
