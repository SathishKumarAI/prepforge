Think of a model as a student who has read a huge textbook and now takes a surprise quiz.  
**Perplexity** is the average “surprise” the student feels for each word it sees on the test: the lower the perplexity, the less surprised the student is, meaning the model’s guesses are more accurate.  

When you want to *prep for* this quiz, you first run a quick practice round using the same book—this gives you an estimate of how perplexed the model will be on new material. If that practice perplexity is high, you know the model still has gaps and should study harder (train more or tweak its settings).  

So, “use perplexity to prep for perplexity” means: measure the model’s current surprise level first; then use that measurement to decide how much more training or data it needs so that when the real test arrives, its perplexity will be low.
