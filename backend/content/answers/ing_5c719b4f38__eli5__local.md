Imagine an agent as a chef preparing a meal.  
**Working memory** is the countertop where ingredients are temporarily laid out while the recipe is being followed—flour, eggs, butter all visible and easy to grab. The chef keeps only what’s needed right now; once the dish is finished that space clears.

**Persistent memory** is the pantry or fridge where the chef stores staples for later use—spices, canned beans, frozen vegetables. These items stay in place across many meals; the chef can retrieve them whenever a new recipe calls for them.

So, working memory holds the “active” items the agent uses at this moment, while persistent memory keeps long‑term knowledge and resources that survive beyond any single task. The countertop is volatile and cleared quickly; the pantry is durable and retains content until it’s needed again.
