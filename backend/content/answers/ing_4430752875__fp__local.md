**Blob store – the “object” storage backbone of modern ML pipelines**

The *fundamental problem* is persisting unstructured data (images, logs, checkpoints) at scale while keeping access fast and costs low. A blob store solves this by treating each file as an opaque key‑value pair: **key → byte stream**.  

Why this works:  
1. **Horizontal scalability** – keys are distributed hash‑partitioned across many nodes; read/write traffic is spread evenly, avoiding bottlenecks.  
2. **Durability & replication** – data is replicated automatically (e.g., 3× in Amazon S3), guaranteeing availability even with node failures.  
3. **Cost‑efficiency** – storage tiering lets hot blobs live on SSDs and cold ones migrate to cheaper archival media, matching the *storage hierarchy* principle from information theory: keep only the entropy you need frequently.

The deeper principle is *data locality vs. consistency trade‑off*. By accepting eventual consistency (writes propagate asynchronously), we gain massive throughput; ML workloads tolerate slight staleness because training can proceed with “good enough” checkpoints.

**Non‑obvious insight:**  
Many designers treat blob stores as mere file systems, overlooking that the **access pattern matters**. In ML, *parallel read bursts* (e.g., distributed training) benefit from *range‑indexing* or *prefix partitioning*, not just hashing. If you ignore key shape, you’ll hit hot‑spotting and hurt throughput—so design your keys to encode both data type and time window.

In short, a blob store is the scalable, cost‑effective substrate that lets ML systems move from “I can’t fit this many checkpoints” to “I can spin up dozens of workers instantly”.
