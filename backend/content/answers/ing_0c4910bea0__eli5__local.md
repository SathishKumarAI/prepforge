**Core Idea – The “Timeout Pattern” in Machine Learning**

Think of building a machine‑learning model like cooking a big pot of soup. You add ingredients (data), stir (train the algorithm), and taste (evaluate). If you keep simmering forever, you’ll waste time and energy, and the soup might even burn. The timeout pattern is the rule that says “stop cooking after a set amount of time.” In ML, we set a maximum training duration or number of iterations. When that clock rings, the model stops learning, even if it hasn’t finished perfecting every detail. This keeps experiments fast, prevents over‑fitting to noise, and lets you compare different models side by side. It’s like setting an oven timer: you get a tasty dish without waiting endlessly.
