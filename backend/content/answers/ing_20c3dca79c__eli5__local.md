Imagine you’re a chef who’s mastered making chocolate cake once. You remember the steps: mix flour, sugar, eggs; bake at 350 °F for 30 minutes. When you try again, something goes wrong—maybe the oven temperature is off or you forgot to sift the flour. The recipe itself didn’t change, but your execution did.

AI agents work the same way. They learn a task by seeing examples and then “memorize” patterns that led to success. When they later face a slightly different situation—different data, a new order of inputs, or even just noise—they can’t apply the exact same pattern because their internal model expects a very specific arrangement. Small changes break the match, so the agent fails even though it once succeeded.
