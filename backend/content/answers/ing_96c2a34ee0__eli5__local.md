Imagine the internet as a huge library that people all over the world want to visit at the same time. If every reader had to walk straight from the main library in New York, the roads would be crowded and books would arrive slowly. A **Content Delivery Network (CDN)** solves this by building small “branch libraries” all across the globe—near where readers live. When someone asks for a webpage or a video, the CDN sends it from the nearest branch instead of the distant main library.

In India, many websites still route traffic directly to servers in other countries because they haven’t set up local branches yet. Until those “branch libraries” (regional CDN nodes) are in place, users wait longer and use more bandwidth, just like a traveler who must travel all the way across the country for a book that could have been delivered locally.
