**1. Clarify the goal & context**  
   - Identify whether the interview is technical (algorithmic coding), applied (project/portfolio review), or behavioral.  
   - Assume the interviewer expects you to explain concepts clearly, solve coding problems, and discuss past ML projects.

**2. Adopt a mental framework**  
   - *Core pillars*: math fundamentals → ML theory → practical implementation → system design → soft skills.  
   - Think of each pillar as a layer you can drill into during questions.

**3. Step‑by‑step reasoning**  
   1. **Math & statistics**: review linear algebra, probability, optimization; be ready to derive gradients or explain bias‑variance trade‑off.  
   2. **Algorithms & data structures**: practice coding linear regression, decision trees, k‑means from scratch; know time/space complexity.  
   3. **Frameworks & tools**: rehearse building a pipeline in scikit‑learn/TensorFlow/PyTorch; understand hyperparameter tuning and cross‑validation.  
   4. **System design**: sketch end‑to‑end ML systems—data ingestion, feature store, model serving, monitoring.  
   5. **Portfolio & storytelling**: pick 2–3 projects that showcase problem definition, data cleaning, modeling choices, evaluation, and impact; practice the “five Ws” (what, why, how, results, lessons).  
   6. **Mock interviews**: use platforms like LeetCode, Pramp, or friends to simulate real Q&A flow.

**4. Avoid common traps**  
   - Don’t over‑explain trivial code; focus on intuition and trade‑offs.  
   - Beware of “black‑box” answers—be ready to derive or justify.  
   - Skip lengthy personal anecdotes unless they illustrate a technical point.

**5. Sanity‑check & communicate**  
   - Pause after each answer: “Does this address the question? Any hidden assumptions?”
