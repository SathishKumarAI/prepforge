Think of a machine‑learning model like a very eager student who has read a huge textbook but hasn’t been told what question to answer yet.  
**Instruction prompting** is the way we give that student a clear, step‑by‑step instruction—like “Explain how photosynthesis works in two sentences.” The prompt tells the model exactly *what* to do and *how* to format the answer.  

In this approach the prompt itself becomes part of the “input” that the model sees, so it learns from many examples of good instructions and starts producing consistent, useful responses. It’s like giving a recipe card: the student follows the directions instead of guessing what you want. The key terms are:

- **Prompt** – the text we feed to the model (the instruction).  
- **Instruction prompting** – using that prompt to tell the model exactly what task to perform.

By crafting clear prompts, we guide the model’s output without hard‑coding new code, making it flexible and powerful.
