Think of building a huge, complex house—rooms, plumbing, wiring, and décor all need to fit together perfectly.  
TOGAF (The Open Group Architecture Framework) is the master blueprint for that house, but instead of rooms it plans entire businesses or IT systems.  

**Analogy:** Imagine you’re a builder who wants every part of the house to match your owner’s wishes and local building codes. TOGAF gives you a step‑by‑step recipe:  
1. **Gather requirements** – ask what the owner needs (business goals).  
2. **Create an architecture vision** – sketch a rough floor plan.  
3. **Design detailed plans** – decide exact dimensions, materials, and wiring.  
4. **Build and test** – construct rooms and check for leaks or weak spots.  
5. **Operate and evolve** – keep the house updated as new furniture arrives.

The “architecture” in TOGAF means a set of ideas that describe how all parts—people, data, applications, technology—work together. By following this structured process, organizations avoid mismatched systems, reduce costs, and adapt smoothly to change—just like a well‑planned house grows with its occupants.
