**Latency in conversational AI**

Imagine you’re texting a friend on a phone that has to wait for the signal to travel, process your message, then send it back. In automatic speech recognition (ASR), *latency* is the time between when you finish speaking and when the system shows or uses what you said. It’s like the delay before your friend sees your reply.

Why it matters: If the lag is long—say you hear a pause of several seconds before the AI responds—it feels unnatural, as if you’re talking to someone who’s listening but not paying attention. Short latency keeps the conversation fluid, just like real‑time chat where each person can react immediately. For voice assistants and call centers, keeping latency low (ideally under 200 milliseconds) makes interactions feel human and reduces frustration.
