**What is Machine Learning?**  
Imagine a chef who never tastes the food he prepares but learns from every plate that comes out of the kitchen. He watches how many customers order each dish, notes which ingredients make them smile or frown, and gradually adjusts the recipe without being told exactly what to do. That chef’s learning process is like machine learning: a computer program observes data (the “plates”), identifies patterns (which ingredients matter), and tweaks its internal rules (the “recipe”) so that future predictions—like recommending a dish—become better over time.

**Types of ML Systems**  
1. **Supervised Learning** – the chef is given labeled plates (“delicious” or “not delicious”) to learn from.  
2. **Unsupervised Learning** – no labels; the chef discovers hidden groupings in the menu, like similar flavor profiles.  
3. **Reinforcement Learning** – the chef gets rewards for successful dishes and penalties for failures, learning through trial‑and‑error.  

These three families cover most ML systems: they all learn from data, but differ in how feedback is provided and what patterns they seek.
