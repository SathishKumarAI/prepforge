Imagine AI as a big science fair where students build robots that can do tasks—think of a robot that sorts shapes or answers questions. **Coding** is the student’s blueprint and programming language; it tells the robot exactly how to move its arms or read data.

When many students finish, they test their robots on the same set of challenges: “How fast does your robot sort 100 objects?” These tests are the **benchmarks**—standardized tasks that everyone can try. They let us compare speeds, accuracy, and efficiency in a fair way.

The results from all teams go into a shared scoreboard called a **leaderboard**. It shows which code (or algorithm) performed best on each benchmark.
