Think of a restaurant kitchen as a **Service‑Oriented Architecture (SOA)**.  
Each dish—burger, salad, dessert—is a *service*. The chef (or the kitchen staff) doesn’t cook everything from scratch every time; instead, they pull pre‑made ingredients (pre‑prepared buns, sauces, etc.) that are ready to be combined into any menu item.  

When an order comes in, the front‑desk system (the “client”) tells the kitchen which dishes to make. The kitchen pulls the needed services—“bread service,” “sauce service,” “protein service”—and assembles them according to the recipe. If you want a vegan burger, the system simply swaps out the protein service for tofu; no other part of the kitchen changes.

In software, SOA breaks an application into independent, reusable components (services) that communicate over a network, just like a kitchen re‑uses pre‑made ingredients to create many different meals. Each service has a clear interface—what it offers and how to use it—making the whole system flexible and easier to maintain.
