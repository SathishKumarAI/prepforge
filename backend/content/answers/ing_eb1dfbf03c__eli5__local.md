Imagine you’re running a big kitchen where each chef is an AI agent cooking a dish. As the recipes (tasks) become more complicated—multiple steps, special ingredients, timing tricks—you need to make sure every chef still follows the instructions correctly and produces tasty food.

**Testing** is like having a taste‑tester walk through the kitchen: they watch the chefs, check each step, and note if anything goes wrong (e.g., missing an ingredient or burning a sauce). They also ask the chefs to explain what they’re doing so you can see whether their reasoning matches the recipe.  

**Evaluation** is the final plate review. The taste‑tester scores the dish on flavor, presentation, and timing. If the score is high, the chef’s algorithm works well; if it’s low, you tweak the recipe or retrain that agent.

In short: testing checks *how* the AI behaves during cooking, while evaluation judges *the finished meal*. This keeps your agents reliable even as their “recipes” grow more complex.
