**Why synthetic data can collapse a learner**

A learning algorithm optimizes an objective \(L(\theta)=\mathbb{E}_{p_{\text{train}}}[\ell(f_\theta(x),y)]\).  
If the training distribution \(p_{\text{train}}\) is almost entirely generated by a model \(G\), then

\[
p_{\text{train}}\approx p_G
\]

and the objective becomes \(\mathbb{E}_{p_G}[\ell]\). The learner can *over‑fit* to idiosyncratic artifacts of \(G\) (e.g., texture bias, frequency content, or mode collapse patterns). When later evaluated on real data \(p_{\text{real}}\), the discrepancy \(\|p_{\text{real}}-p_G\|\) causes a sharp performance drop—*model collapse*.

**Deriving a safe construction**

1. **Define a mixture distribution**  
   \[
   p_{\text{train}} = (1-\alpha)p_{\text{real}} + \alpha\,p_G,\qquad 0<\alpha<1
   \]
   The KL‑divergence from the true data is bounded by \(D_{KL}(p_{\text{train}}\|p_{\text{real}})\le -\log(1-\alpha)\).  
   Choosing \(\alpha\) small enough keeps this divergence negligible, guaranteeing that gradients are still dominated by real examples.

2. **Adversarial reweighting**  
   Train a discriminator \(D(x,y)\) to estimate the likelihood ratio
   \[
   r(x)=\frac{p_{\text{real}}(x)}{p_{\text{train}}(x)}.
   \]
   Weight each synthetic sample by \(w(x)=\min(r(x),1)\). This re‑weights away over‑represented synthetic modes, restoring the effective empirical distribution to match \(p_{\text{real}}\).

3. **Regularization toward real priors**  
   Add a penalty \(\lambda\,\mathbb{E}_{p_G}[d(f_\theta(G(z)),y)]\) where \(d\) measures deviation from known real‑data statistics (e.g., spectral norms). This discourages the learner from exploiting synthetic artifacts.

**Non‑obvious insight**

Even with a tiny \(\alpha\), if *all* synthetic samples share a systematic bias, the model can still latch onto it. By explicitly estimating and correcting the density ratio \(r(x)\) (step 2), you remove that bias *without* needing any real data beyond a handful of calibration examples—turning a seemingly “purely synthetic” curriculum into a robust, near‑real training regime.
