Imagine a toolbox where every tool has a special job: a screwdriver twists screws, a hammer drives nails, and a wrench tightens bolts. In Python, **data types** are the tools of the language; they tell the computer how to treat each piece of information.

* **int** (integer) is like a ruler—countable, whole numbers.
* **float** (floating‑point number) behaves like a measuring cup—it can hold fractions.
* **str** (string) is a label or note written on tape; it’s just text.
* **bool** (boolean) works like a light switch: either `True` (on) or `False` (off).
* **list**, **tuple**, and **dict** are organized storage containers—lists keep items in order, tuples are fixed lists, and dictionaries map keys to values.

Just as you choose the right tool for a job, picking the correct data type lets Python know how to store, compare, or calculate with your data. That’s the core of learning machine learning: knowing which “tool” fits each piece of information you feed into your models.
