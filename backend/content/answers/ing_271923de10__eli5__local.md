Imagine your program is a long, winding river that carries water (the “state”) from one point to the next. When something goes wrong—a rock, a whirlpool—you want to go back upstream and change what happened there so the rest of the flow fixes itself.

**Time‑Travel Debugging** lets you do exactly that: pause the river at any moment, pull back the water level, tweak a variable or a function’s output, then let the river continue. The “Human In The Loop” part means you’re the captain who decides where to stop, what to change, and when to release it again.

Think of it like editing a video: you rewind to a frame, adjust a clip, and then play forward to see how the whole scene changes. This pattern keeps debugging interactive, precise, and surprisingly intuitive.
