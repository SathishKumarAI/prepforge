Imagine a chef who can cook any dish you ask for by mixing ingredients from a huge pantry, but only using recipes it’s already seen in its cookbook. That’s what generative AI does: it stitches together text, images, or music from patterns it learned during training. Because the chef never actually tastes food, it can’t guarantee the flavor will be perfect; sometimes it mixes too many spices and ends up with a weird taste (errors or “hallucinations”). It also only knows what’s in its pantry – if you ask for something brand‑new that wasn’t in its cookbook, it’ll improvise but might miss key details. Finally, the chef can’t understand context like a human; it won’t know why a certain ingredient is bad for someone with allergies. These gaps—mistakes, lack of true understanding, and reliance on past data—are the main limits of generative AI.
