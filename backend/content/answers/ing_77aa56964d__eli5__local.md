Think of a computer network as a **train system**.  
The *Physical* layer (Layer 1) is the **tracks and rails** that carry the train cars. It deals only with raw electrical or optical signals—just like the metal rails are just metal, no passengers yet. The tracks set the speed limits, spacing, and shape of the carriages; they provide the physical path but do not decide where the train goes.

In networking terms, Layer 1 sends bits as voltage pulses (or light flashes) over cables or airwaves. It cares about cable type, connector quality, signal strength, and noise—nothing about data format or meaning. Just as a well‑built rail keeps cars moving safely, a solid Physical layer ensures signals arrive intact for higher layers to interpret.
