Think of a **biomedical knowledge graph** as a giant, interactive library map. Each book (node) is something like a gene, protein, disease, or drug, and the shelves that connect them (edges) show how they relate—“causes,” “interacts with,” or “treats.” You can walk from one shelf to another and see all the stories linking biology together.

A **language model** is like a very smart librarian who has read every book in that library. When you ask a question, it doesn’t just pull out one pre‑written answer; instead, it predicts the most likely next words based on patterns it learned from all those books. In biomedical research, the librarian uses the knowledge graph to ground its predictions—so when you ask about a drug’s side effects, it can “remember” which diseases that drug is linked to and give an informed reply.

Together, the map tells *what* is connected, while the librarian predicts *how* they might interact in new, unseen situations.
