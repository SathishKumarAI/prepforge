Imagine a car’s dashboard as the **observability** tool and a driving test as the **evals** tool.

Observability is like the dashboard lights, gauges, and warning signals that tell you what’s happening inside the engine right now—temperature, oil pressure, battery status. It gives you real‑time visibility into the AI system’s internal state, letting you spot trouble early.

Evals are more like a driving test: you set up a specific course (a set of questions or tasks) and see how well the car performs—does it accelerate fast enough? Does it stay on the lane? For an AI model, evals run predetermined inputs and measure outputs against expected results to judge performance quality.

Some vendors bundle both because dashboards and tests are often sold together as a complete “health check” package. But observability watches what’s happening now; evals assess how well it behaves under defined conditions.
