**Perplexity in AI is like a detective’s “confusion score.”**  
When a language model reads text, it tries to guess the next word. If the model can predict many possible next words equally well, its “confidence” drops—its perplexity rises. Imagine a student taking a multiple‑choice quiz: if only one answer fits, the test is easy (low perplexity); if several answers look plausible, the student feels confused (high perplexity). In an interview, candidates often face questions that probe how a model handles such uncertainty—asking about *token‑level* predictions, *temperature* settings, or how to reduce perplexity with better data. Interviewers expect you to explain this detective analogy, show why lower perplexity means sharper language understanding, and discuss techniques (like smoothing or larger vocabularies) that help the model “solve” its own confusion.
