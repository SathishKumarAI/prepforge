**Context Engineering (in machine‑learning “compaction”)**

Think of a recipe book that has thousands of recipes, but you only want the ones for a quick weeknight dinner. *Context engineering* is like giving the kitchen assistant a clear list: “Show me recipes that use less than 30 minutes, need only two ingredients, and are vegetarian.” The assistant then filters the huge cookbook down to a short, relevant set.

In machine learning, the “cookbook” is all the data a model can see. Context engineering means carefully selecting or shaping the input text (or other data) so that the model focuses on what matters for the task—like telling it which parts of a conversation are relevant, which dates matter, or which domain terms to pay attention to. It’s a smart way to “compact” the information space, letting the model work faster and more accurately without getting lost in irrelevant details.
