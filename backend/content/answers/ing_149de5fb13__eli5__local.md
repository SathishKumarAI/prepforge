Think of a recipe book that has many ways to make the same dish.  
**Best‑of‑N** is like flipping through N different recipes, picking one at random, and hoping it turns out good. You get variety but you might miss the best version because you only tasted one.

**Self‑Consistency** is like taking a single recipe, cooking it multiple times on the same stove, and then looking for the most consistent result across those attempts. If every batch tastes similar, you’re confident it’s right; if they differ wildly, you know something in the process is off.

Use **Best‑of‑N** when you want to explore many distinct ideas quickly—good for creative brainstorming. Use **Self‑Consistency** when you need a reliable answer from a single underlying model—better for precise facts or when consistency matters more than novelty.
