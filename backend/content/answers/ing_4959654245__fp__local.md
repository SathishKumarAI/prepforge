### “Read Now” – an information‑theoretic web‑optimisation trick  
(≈6 min read)

When a user lands on a page, the browser must decide which resources to fetch first. The classic *lazy loading* strategy is optimal only if every request carries equal value; in practice, **read intent** varies dramatically across users and sessions.  

**Fundamental problem:** minimize *expected waiting time* for the next token a reader will consume.  
Let \(R_i\) be the random variable “reader needs resource \(i\)” with probability \(p_i\). Fetching order \(\pi\) incurs cost
\[
C(\pi)=\sum_{k=1}^{n} p_{\pi_k}\Bigl(\sum_{j<k} t_j + t_k/2\Bigr),
\]
where \(t_j\) is the transfer time of resource \(j\).  
The optimal \(\pi^\*\) sorts resources by decreasing *value density* \(p_i/t_i\), a direct consequence of minimizing expected latency under a linear cost model.

**Why “Read Now” works:**  
1. **Predictive prefetching** – server-side logs reveal that the first 30 % of a page’s content accounts for 70 % of reading time (law of diminishing returns).  
2. **Entropy reduction** – by sending only the high‑\(p_i\) resources immediately, we reduce the user’s *information uncertainty* about what will appear next, lowering perceived lag.  
3. **Bandwidth efficiency** – low‑probability assets are deferred to background fetches, keeping the first paint fast without sacrificing completeness.

**Non‑obvious insight:**  
The trick is *not* merely to prefetch everything early; it’s to prefetch **in proportion to the conditional entropy of user intent**. A resource that rarely appears in a reader’s next view still merits early transfer if its absence would drastically increase the entropy (e.g., a navigation bar that determines subsequent clicks). Thus, “Read Now” is an application of *rate‑distortion theory* to web rendering: we encode the page with a distortion budget equal to acceptable waiting time and allocate bandwidth where it most reduces expected surprise.
