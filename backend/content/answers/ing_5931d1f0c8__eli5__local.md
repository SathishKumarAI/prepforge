Think of a sentence as a little book.  
An **empty string** is the blank page at the very start of that book—no letters, no spaces, nothing written on it.  

In Python we write it as `""`. It’s still a *string* (a piece of text), but its length is zero: `len("")` gives 0.  
Just like a blank page can be printed onto later, an empty string can be filled with characters later, or used to check if someone typed anything at all.  

So, when you see `""`, picture that pristine, untouched page ready for whatever words you’ll put on it next.
