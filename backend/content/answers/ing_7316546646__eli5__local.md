**Availability in a nutshell**

Imagine you run an online bakery that sells cakes to customers all over the world. *Availability* is the promise that whenever someone orders a cake, the bakery’s website will be up and ready to process the order—no matter what time of day or how many people are trying to buy at once.

Think of your bakery as a set of ovens (servers) spread across different neighborhoods (data centers). If one oven breaks, another still keeps baking. Availability is like having backup ovens in every neighborhood so that customers never see a “site down” sign. It’s measured by the fraction of time the site can actually accept orders—so 99 % availability means it works most of the time but may have brief outages.

In system design interviews, you’ll often ask: *How many backup ovens (replicas) do we need to reach a target availability?* That’s the core trade‑off between cost and reliability.
