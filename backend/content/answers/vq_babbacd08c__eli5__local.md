Imagine you’re teaching a child to sort fruit. You show them an apple and say “apple,” then a banana and say “banana.” Each time the child hears the word while seeing the fruit, they learn which label belongs to which shape, color, or texture. In AI, supervised learning works the same way: we give the computer a big list of examples (the fruits) paired with correct answers (their names). The machine watches many pairs—“image of an apple → ‘apple’”—and learns patterns that link the visual features to the label. Once trained, it can look at a new picture and predict the right word, just as the child recognizes a fruit they’ve never seen before. This “supervised” process relies on labeled data so the computer knows what’s correct during training.
