**Why treat your résumé as a product?**

At its core, a résumé is *a contract*—a promise that you can deliver the value a hiring manager seeks. In software engineering this translates to *deliverables*: code, tests, architecture decisions, and performance gains. Treating it like a product forces us to ask the same questions we ask about any feature:  
- **Value proposition** (what problem does my work solve?)  
- **Target user** (which team or stakeholder benefits most?)  
- **Metrics of success** (how do I quantify impact?)  
- **Iterative refinement** (feedback loops from code reviews, CI failures, and post‑deployment telemetry).  

This perspective aligns with the *Lean Startup* principle that a product must be validated early. A résumé should therefore showcase **validated outcomes**—e.g., “reduced API latency by 35 % on a 10 M‑request/day service,” rather than vague skill lists.  

A non‑obvious insight: *contextual depth beats breadth.* Hiring managers infer competence from the narrative arc of a project, not from isolated bullet points. By framing each entry as a mini‑case study (problem → solution → impact), you implicitly communicate your design thinking, trade‑off analysis, and ability to iterate—skills that are far more valuable than a static list of languages or frameworks.
