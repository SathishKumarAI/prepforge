**Human‑in‑the‑Loop Approval Gates**

Imagine a factory that builds cars with an automatic conveyor belt. The belt works great, but every few minutes a worker pops up at a checkpoint to check the car’s brakes, lights, and seatbelts before it moves on. If something looks wrong, the worker stops the belt, fixes the issue, or sends the car back for another inspection.  

In AI, “human‑in‑the‑loop” is the same idea: the system runs automatically, but at key moments—called *approval gates*—a human reviewer checks the model’s output, decisions, or data. If the check fails, the AI stops, an alert is sent, and a person intervenes. This process keeps the technology safe (no harmful mistakes slip through) and governed (rules are enforced by real people).  

**Key terms:**  
- *AI model*: software that learns patterns from data to make predictions or decisions.  
- *Approval gate*: a checkpoint where a human inspects and approves the AI’s action before it proceeds.
