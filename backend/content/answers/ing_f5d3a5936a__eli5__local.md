Think of training a machine‑learning model like learning to play a new sport—say, soccer.  
**Bias** is the coach’s *preconception* about how the ball should behave: if you always assume the ball will roll straight, you’ll miss curves. A high‑bias model sticks to simple rules (e.g., “always kick forward”) and never adapts.  

**Variance** is your *reaction to every single game:* a model that changes its strategy wildly with each new dataset overfits, like a player who keeps changing footwork after every match—great on the practice field but shaky in real play.

The **bias‑variance trade‑off** says: make the model simple (low variance) and you’ll miss details (high bias); make it complex (low bias) and you’ll be too sensitive to noise (high variance). The goal is a sweet spot where the model generalizes well—just like a player who knows basic techniques but can adjust to different opponents without overreacting.
