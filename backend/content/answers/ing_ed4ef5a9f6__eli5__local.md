Imagine your favorite coffee shop is open all day, but sometimes a power outage or a busy rush forces it to close for a few minutes. **Availability** measures how often the shop actually serves customers when you walk in. In computer systems we use the same idea: the fraction of time the service is up and running versus the total time it should be available.

Think of availability as a “ready‑to‑serve” score: if the coffee shop stays open 99 % of the time, its availability is 0.99 (or 99 %). The higher the percentage, the fewer interruptions customers experience. Designers aim for very high availability by adding backups and quick recovery steps so that downtime becomes almost invisible to users.
