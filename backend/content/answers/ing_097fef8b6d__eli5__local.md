**Inference and production in AI are like a chef reading a recipe and then serving the dish.**  
When an AI model “infers,” it’s the chef looking at the ingredients (the input data) and, using the recipe (the trained network), deciding what to do next—mixing, heating, seasoning—to produce a single answer or prediction. Think of inference as a quick, one‑shot cooking step: you only need to taste once before serving.

“Production” is when that same chef runs the kitchen every day, preparing many dishes at once for customers. The AI system must be fast, reliable, and able to handle dozens of requests simultaneously—just like a busy restaurant must keep orders coming out smoothly while staying consistent in flavor.

*Inference*: a single prediction made by the model.  
*Production*: deploying that model so it can serve predictions continuously in real‑world applications.
