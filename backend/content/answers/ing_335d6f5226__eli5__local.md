Imagine you’re planning a road trip with friends. **Ensemble learning** is like having each friend drive their own car to the destination, then combining all the routes they took to decide the best one—maybe by voting or averaging turns. The more cars (models) you have, the smoother and safer the final route tends to be.

**Model arbitration**, on the other hand, is a bit different: it’s as if only one car is chosen to drive, but you keep all the others in reserve. When the chosen driver gets stuck or sees a roadblock, you switch to another friend’s car that’s better suited for that specific situation. The decision about which car to use (which model) happens on the fly, based on what the current road looks like.

So: ensemble blends many models together all at once; arbitration picks one model at a time, switching when needed. Both aim to get you to the goal, but they do it in distinct ways.
