**Why “stay‑ahead” matters**

At its core, AI is an *optimization engine* that turns data into value. Every new model shifts the Pareto frontier of achievable performance versus cost. If a country or corporation lags even one step, it loses out on downstream innovation—healthcare diagnostics, autonomous logistics, policy modeling—and the opportunity to shape global norms (privacy, safety, ethics). The imperative is therefore two‑fold: **(1) maintain technical superiority** so that future breakthroughs can be built on a solid base; **(2) lock in governance authority** to steer how those breakthroughs are deployed.

---

### 2028 Scenario A – “Consolidated Leadership”

*Assumptions:*  
- A single nation (or bloc) secures the majority of high‑capacity compute, top talent, and regulatory latitude.  
- It invests aggressively in *foundation models* and open‑source licensing that embeds its safety standards into the global stack.

*Implications:*  
- **Technological lock‑in:** Other actors must either adopt the leader’s frameworks or pay prohibitive costs to run competing pipelines.  
- **Policy dominance:** The leader sets international AI treaties, effectively turning its ethical framework into a de facto standard.

---

### 2028 Scenario B – “Distributed Democracy”

*Assumptions:*  
- Compute and talent are geographically dispersed; open‑source ecosystems thrive under collective stewardship.  
- No single actor can claim monopoly over the most advanced models because federated training and differential privacy keep data local yet globally useful.

*Implications:*  
- **Resilience:** Innovation spreads faster; a breakthrough in one region cascades without gatekeeping.  
- **Governance pluralism:** Norms emerge from multilateral fora rather than unilateral mandates, but coordination costs rise.
