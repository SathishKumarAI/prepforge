Imagine a recipe book that you found on the internet.  
* **Copyright** is like the author’s name stamped on each page: it says “I wrote this.” Anyone who reads or copies the recipe must respect that ownership and can’t claim it as their own without permission.  
* A **patent** would be a special tool that the recipe uses—say, a patented oven‑shaping method. Even if the recipe itself is free, nobody else can legally use that particular tool unless they get the patent holder’s license.  
* A **license** on GitHub is the “recipe card” that tells you how you may cook with it: you might be allowed to remix and share (MIT), or only to study (GPL). The license clarifies exactly what others can do with the code, just like a cooking instruction says whether you can sell your dishes.
