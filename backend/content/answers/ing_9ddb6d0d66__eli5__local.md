Imagine you’re looking for a secret recipe in a huge cookbook that’s constantly being updated. Instead of opening the whole book each time, you first skim the table of contents (quick search) and find a page number. You pull that page, read it, then notice a note pointing to another related page. You follow that note, add its details to your own copy, and keep looping—each new page gives you clues to more pages. This is **Iterative Document Enrichment** in advanced retrieval: the system starts with an initial query, fetches documents, extracts useful snippets or “enrichments,” then feeds those back into a new search. The loop continues until the answer is fully fleshed out.  

*Unavoidable terms:*  
- **Query** – the question you ask the system.  
- **Document** – any piece of information the system can read (text, PDF, etc.).  
- **Enrichment** – extra facts or context added to a document that help find better answers.
