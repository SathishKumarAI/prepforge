Imagine a chef (the AI) who has learned many recipes but never cooked the exact dish you want. **In‑context learning** is like giving the chef a short handwritten recipe card while they’re in the kitchen—no extra training, just a quick cue that tells them exactly what to do for this one meal. You decide to use it when you need a fast, flexible answer and don’t have time or data to retrain the chef.

**Fine‑tuning** is like sending the chef on a specialized cooking course: they practice many times with that dish, gradually getting better at it. You choose fine‑tuning when you’ll be making this dish repeatedly, need consistent quality, and have enough examples (data) to train them well. In short, use in‑context learning for quick, one‑off tasks; fine‑tune for long‑term, specialized performance.
