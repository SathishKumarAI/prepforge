Think of a detective who has two main tools: **data analytics** is the magnifying glass that looks at clues already on the scene—counting footprints, noting patterns in fingerprints, and summarizing what happened. It tells you “here’s how many cars passed by last month” or “these sales spike every Friday.”  

Data science is the full investigative kit. After the detective gathers clues (analytics), they build a model—a predictive map that says, “If we add X new product, sales will rise 15% next quarter.” It uses statistical tools and computer code to create these models, test them, and turn raw numbers into future‑looking recommendations.  

So, analytics = describing the past; data science = using that description to predict and decide what to do next.
