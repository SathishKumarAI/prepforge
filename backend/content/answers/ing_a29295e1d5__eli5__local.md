**Back‑of‑the‑Envelope Scaling**

Think of a recipe that works for one person but needs to be doubled or tripled for a big family dinner. In machine learning, *scaling* means adjusting the size of our “recipe” – the data, model size, or compute power – and seeing how performance changes.

A *back‑of‑the‑envelope* estimate is a quick, rough calculation you can do with a pen and paper (or a calculator) to guess whether a larger model will be worth the extra cost. You might say, “If we double the number of training examples, accuracy should improve by about 10 %.” It’s not exact; it ignores many details, but it gives a ball‑park idea.

*Unavoidable terms:*  
- **Model size** – how many parameters (like knobs) a neural network has.  
- **Compute cost** – the amount of computer time and energy needed to train or run the model.  

Using this simple math helps teams decide whether investing in more data, bigger models, or faster hardware is likely to pay off before diving into full experiments.
