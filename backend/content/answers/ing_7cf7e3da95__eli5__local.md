Think of a smart home system that learns to keep you comfortable without you telling it every single rule. In **Agentic Engineering**—the way designers build such “agents”—there are three familiar patterns, like different kitchen tools:

1. **Rule‑Based Agent (The Recipe Book)** – It follows fixed if‑then rules (“If the temperature drops below 68°F, turn on the heater”). Simple and predictable but limited.

2. **Learning‑From‑Data Agent (The Chef’s Notebook)** – It watches what you do, learns from past data, and updates its own “recipes.” It can adapt when new situations appear, but it needs a lot of examples to get good at them.

3. **Hybrid Agent (The Smart Sous‑Chef)** – It blends both: it has basic rules for safety (“Never let the stove stay on”) and uses learning to fine‑tune comfort (“When you’re in bed, lower the lights gradually”). This pattern gives reliability plus flexibility.

In short, agentic engineering chooses the right mix of hard rules and adaptive learning so an AI can act responsibly while still growing smarter over time.
