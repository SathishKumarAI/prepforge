Imagine a bakery that sells cupcakes.  
**Vertical scaling** is like adding more ovens to the same shop floor—each oven can bake more cupcakes at once, but you’re still limited by how many ovens fit in the space. You get faster output until the kitchen gets crowded and cramped.  

**Horizontal scaling** is like opening new bakery shops in different neighborhoods. Each shop has its own ovens, but together they produce a huge number of cupcakes. If one shop runs out of flour or breaks down, the others keep going, so the system stays strong even if one part fails.

In computing, vertical scaling means giving one server more CPU, memory, or storage—like adding ovens to one kitchen. Horizontal scaling means adding more servers (new kitchens) that share the workload. The latter is usually more flexible and fault‑tolerant because the load spreads across many machines instead of piling on a single one.
