Imagine you’re building a smart robot that can play chess, drive a car, or help a doctor diagnose disease. Think of the robot as a *character* in a story: it has a name (its purpose), a set of tools (algorithms), and a way to see the world (sensors). The defining traits of any AI agent are:

1. **Perception** – It receives data from its environment, like a camera seeing a chessboard or a microphone hearing a patient’s symptoms.
2. **Goal‑driven behavior** – It has a clear objective: win the game, reach a destination, or give the right diagnosis.
3. **Decision making** – It chooses actions based on what it sees and how close those actions get it to its goal, just as a chess player picks a move that improves its chances of winning.
4. **Learning/Adaptation** – Over time it gets better by remembering past games or patient outcomes, refining its strategy.
