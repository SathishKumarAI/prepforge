Imagine a spinning top that can wobble around its axis.  
An **eigenvector** is like a special direction in space—think of it as the line along which the top’s wobble stays exactly on the same line no matter how fast or slow the top spins.  
The **eigenvalue** tells you how that wobbling speed changes: if the eigenvalue is 2, every time the top completes one rotation, points on that line move twice as far from the center; if it’s 0.5, they shrink to half the distance.

So, an eigenvector is a “steady‑direction” vector that never turns away, and its eigenvalue is the factor by which the system stretches or shrinks along that direction when the transformation (the spin) happens.
