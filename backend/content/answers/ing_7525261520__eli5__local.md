Think of a production machine‑learning system like a well‑trained chef who runs a busy restaurant.  
The **model** is the recipe book: it tells the chef (the algorithm) how to turn raw ingredients (data) into a dish (prediction).  
In *production* the kitchen is open all day, so the chef must keep cooking reliably while handling many orders at once.  

To do that, the chef uses a **pipeline**—pre‑treating the food, plating it, and sending it out—just as data moves through cleaning, feature extraction, inference, and post‑processing steps in a ML system.  
A **monitor** watches the kitchen’s temperature and taste scores; similarly, production systems track accuracy, latency, and data drift to catch problems early.  

Finally, if an order changes (new menu item), the chef updates the recipe book and re‑trains the model—this is called **model retraining**.  
So, a production ML system is like a restaurant that serves consistent, high‑quality predictions at scale, always ready for new orders and quick to fix any hiccups.
