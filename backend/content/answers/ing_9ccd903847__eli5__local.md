Imagine you’re sending a postcard from New York to someone in Tokyo. If the post office sends it through one long route, the paper can get stuck or delayed. A Content Delivery Network (CDN) is like a global network of tiny post offices that sit near many people all over the world. When your website wants to show an image or video, the CDN hands the file off to the nearest post office (a server), so it travels only a short distance and arrives faster.

A **server** is just a computer that stores files. A **cache** is a temporary storage spot where the CDN keeps copies of popular content, ready for quick delivery. By using many servers close to users, CDNs reduce latency, lower bandwidth costs, and keep websites responsive even during traffic spikes.
