Think of your company’s IT world as a big city that never sleeps. Every building (computer), streetlight (network device), and traffic signal (application) sends out tiny messages—“I’m alive,” “I just received a request,” or “An alarm has triggered.”  
A **Security Information and Event Management (SIEM)** system is the city’s central control tower. It collects all those messages, stores them in one big log book, and watches for patterns that look suspicious, like a sudden rush of cars through a normally quiet intersection.  

- **Log**: a written record of an event.  
- **Event**: any notable action or change (e.g., login attempt).  
- **Correlation**: matching different logs to spot a larger story.

When the SIEM sees clues that match a known threat—say, repeated failed logins followed by a successful one—it raises an alarm so security teams can act before damage happens. It’s not predicting the future; it’s reading the city’s chatter in real time and pointing out red flags.
