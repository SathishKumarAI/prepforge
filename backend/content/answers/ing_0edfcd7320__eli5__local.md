**Tool registration and discovery is like adding new gadgets to a smart kitchen.**

When you bring a new appliance—say a blender—to your kitchen, you first **register** it: you label its name (“Blender”), tell the kitchen system what it can do (blend, puree), and give it a spot in the pantry (a unique address). Next, every other gadget or cookbot in the kitchen learns about this new device by looking at the shared “kitchen registry.” That’s **discovery**: the cookbots ask, “What gadgets are available?” The registry replies with the blender’s name and how to use it. Then any cookbot can send a recipe command—“blend 200 ml of fruit”—and the kitchen system routes that request to the right appliance.

*Unavoidable terms:*  
- **Agent**: a software program that acts on your behalf, like a robot chef.  
- **Registry**: a central list where all available tools and their abilities are stored.  

With this setup, adding or removing gadgets is easy, and every agent can find the right tool to finish its job quickly.
