**Batch processing** is like cooking a big pot of soup in one go: you gather all the ingredients (data), turn on the stove, let it simmer for hours, and then serve the finished dish to everyone at once. **Stream processing** is more like making a cup of tea as people walk by: you add water, pour, sip, and keep going, giving each person a fresh drink right away.

In machine learning, batch processing takes a whole dataset, trains a model on it, then outputs results in one batch. Stream processing feeds data continuously—each new example is handled immediately, updating the model or producing predictions on the spot. Batch gives you a solid, finished result; stream offers real‑time, incremental insight. Both are useful, but choose batch when you can wait for all data, and stream when instant feedback matters.
