**Real‑time voice agents: the “instant dialogue engine”**

The core problem is *interactive communication*: a human speaks, an AI must transcribe, understand intent, generate a reply, and speak it—all within milliseconds so that conversation feels natural.  

1. **Signal → Symbol pipeline**  
   The acoustic waveform \(x(t)\) is first compressed to a spectrogram via short‑time Fourier transform (STFT). This reduces dimensionality while preserving phonetic structure—an optimal trade‑off between information rate and latency (Shannon’s rate–distortion theory).  

2. **Symbol → Intent**  
   A lightweight encoder (e.g., a causal Transformer) maps the spectrogram to a contextual embedding \(h_t\). Causality forces the model to process tokens sequentially, guaranteeing bounded delay. The embedding is fed into a policy network that outputs a probability distribution over intents, optimized by minimizing cross‑entropy plus a *latency penalty*—an instance of constrained reinforcement learning.

3. **Intent → Response**  
   A decoder conditioned on \(h_t\) generates text tokens one step at a time. Beam search is limited to width = 1 (greedy decoding) during live inference, trading off quality for determinism and speed. The generated text is then fed into a neural vocoder (e.g., HiFi‑GAN), which reconstructs \( \hat{x}(t)\) from the mel‑spectrogram. The vocoder’s architecture—convolutional upsampling with skip connections—provides sub‑20 ms inference, satisfying real‑time constraints.
