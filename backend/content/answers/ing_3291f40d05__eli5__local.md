Imagine a nurse’s station that lights up every time a patient needs something—an alarm for low blood pressure, another for a new medication order, and yet another when the charting system asks a question.  
**Alert fatigue** is like having all those lights on at once; after seeing so many, the nurse starts ignoring them because they’re too frequent to be useful.  

**Cognitive load** is the mental space the nurse has to juggle—reading each alert, checking vitals, recalling protocols, and deciding what to do next. When the station fires dozens of alarms, that mental space fills up, leaving little room for deeper thinking.  

**On‑call toil** is the extra work that keeps coming while the nurse is “on call” (like late‑night rounds or emergency triage). It’s repetitive, routine tasks that drain energy but are necessary to keep patients safe. Together, too many alerts and constant on‑call duties overload a clinician’s mind, making it harder to spot real emergencies.
