Imagine a smart gardener who learns how to grow plants by watching many gardens and tweaking the soil, water, light, and timing. In machine learning the “gardener” is an algorithm that learns from data—just like the gardener watches pictures of flowers, numbers on weather, or words in sentences.

The four basics are:

1. **Data** – the garden’s seeds (examples) the model sees.  
2. **Model** – the gardening tool (a mathematical formula) that tries to predict or classify new plants.  
3. **Training** – the gardener’s practice: feeding data into the model and letting it adjust its internal knobs so predictions improve.  
4. **Evaluation** – checking a sample of unseen gardens to see if the model’s predictions match reality, just as a gardener checks whether new seedlings thrive.

Together these steps let the algorithm grow smarter over time, turning raw information into useful decisions.
