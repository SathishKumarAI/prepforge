**What “FFT” means**

FFT stands for **Fast Fourier Transform**. Think of a song: the whole track is one long wave, but inside that wave are many musical notes—each note has its own pitch (frequency) and loudness (amplitude). The FFT is like a super‑fast music tuner that listens to the entire track and instantly tells you which notes are playing and how strong they are.

**Why it matters for AI**

Many AI tasks, especially those dealing with images or sounds, need to know what “frequencies” are hidden inside data. Instead of looking at every pixel or sample one by one (which would take ages), the FFT lets the computer break the data into its frequency components in a fraction of the time. This speed‑up is essential for real‑time applications—like speech recognition, image compression, or neural networks that learn patterns from raw audio.

So, the FFT is simply a clever shortcut to discover the hidden tones inside any signal, and it’s necessary because without it, AI would spend too much time crunching data.
