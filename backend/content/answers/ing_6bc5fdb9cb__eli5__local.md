Imagine you’re a chef who wants to serve the same delicious recipe to people all over the world. If every diner had to wait for the ingredients to ship from your kitchen, some would be hungry while others enjoy fresh food. A **Content Delivery Network (CDN)** solves this by keeping copies of those ingredients in many kitchens (servers) closer to each diner.

In machine learning, models and data are the “ingredients.”  
* **Speed**: The CDN brings the model close to where it’s needed, so predictions arrive quickly—just like a local kitchen can cook instantly.  
* **Reliability**: If one kitchen goes down, others still supply the dish; similarly, if one server fails, another keeps your ML service running.  
* **Scalability**: When thousands of people request predictions at once, the CDN distributes the load across many kitchens, preventing any single point from getting overwhelmed.

So a CDN is like a global network of local kitchens that keeps machine‑learning “dishes” fresh, fast, and always available.
