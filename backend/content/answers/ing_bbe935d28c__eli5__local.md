Imagine you’re baking a cake with many friends. Each friend takes care of one simple task—one chops the carrots, another mixes the batter, someone else pours the glaze. When everyone finishes their part, all those little pieces are assembled into a single delicious cake.  
In software, a **microservice** is like that individual helper: it does one small job (e.g., “predict user churn,” “store images”) and can run on its own server. Many microservices talk to each other through simple messages, just as the friends share their finished pieces.  

Key terms  
- **Microservice**: a tiny, self‑contained program that performs one specific function.  
- **Service communication**: the way these programs send requests and receive answers (often over HTTP or message queues).  

Just like your cake can be made faster and with fewer mistakes when each helper focuses on one task, microservices let machine‑learning systems grow, update, and scale more easily without messing up the whole thing.
