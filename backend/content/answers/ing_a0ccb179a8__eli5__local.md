**Latency Breakdown in Computer‑Use Agents**

Imagine a delivery driver who must pick up a package, drive to the customer, and drop it off. The total time the customer waits is the *latency*. In a computer‑use agent (like a chatbot that sends commands to a server), latency breaks into three parts:

1. **Input delay** – the time it takes for the user’s message to reach the agent (the driver getting the pickup order).  
2. **Processing delay** – how long the agent thinks about what to do and formulates a reply (the driver deciding the fastest route).  
3. **Output delay** – the time the reply travels back to the user (the driver delivering the package).

Each part adds up, just as each step in delivery adds to the customer’s wait. Knowing where most of the time goes helps engineers speed up the whole system—just like a driver would find shortcuts or faster roads to reduce delivery time.
