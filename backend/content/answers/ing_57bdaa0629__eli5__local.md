**Semantic Memory (Layer 3)**  
Think of your brain’s long‑term storage like a city library that only keeps the *ideas* about books, not the actual books themselves. In AI, this is called **semantic memory**: it stores facts and concepts—what things are, how they relate—without remembering every single word you ever heard.

A “memory architecture” is just the blueprint of that library: how shelves (data structures) are arranged, who gets to read or borrow a shelf (access control), and how new ideas find a home (learning rules).  
So, Layer 3 in an AI system is like that city library’s master plan—organizing all the abstract knowledge so the system can answer questions, make connections, and reason about the world.
