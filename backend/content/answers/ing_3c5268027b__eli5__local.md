Think of a library that offers six different “lending desks.”  
1. **Web Server** – the desk that reads books (web pages) and hands them to visitors.  
2. **Application Server** – the desk that runs recipes (programs) and gives back cooked dishes.  
3. **Database Server** – the desk that stores all the recipe cards and pulls out what’s needed.  
4. **File Server** – the desk that keeps the actual books (files) safe for checkout.  
5. **Mail Server** – the desk that delivers letters between people.  
6. **Proxy Server** – the friendly “middleman” desk that sits between visitors and the other desks.  

A proxy receives a visitor’s request, decides which of the other desks can best answer it, forwards the request there, then returns the answer to the visitor. It hides the real location of the resources, speeds up repeat requests (by keeping copies), and can block or filter content for safety. Just like a helpful librarian who knows where every book is and can keep your identity private while you browse.
