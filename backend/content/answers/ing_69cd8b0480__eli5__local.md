Think of a solid‑state drive (SSD) like a library that stores books on tiny, super‑fast shelves made of silicon chips instead of paper. The “form factor” is just the size and shape of that library’s building so it can fit in different rooms—your computer case, laptop, or even a phone.

The most common form factors are:

* **2.5 inch** – a rectangular box that slides into a standard hard‑disk bay (about the size of a paperback book).  
* **M.2** – a slim, keyboard‑key–shaped card that plugs directly onto the motherboard, saving space like a compact desk lamp.  
* **PCIe add‑on cards** – larger than M.2, they sit in a computer’s expansion slot and look like a tiny printer.

Each form factor keeps the same internal electronics (flash memory, controller) but just changes how it physically connects to your machine. That way, whether you have a big desktop or a pocket‑sized phone, there’s an SSD that fits perfectly.
