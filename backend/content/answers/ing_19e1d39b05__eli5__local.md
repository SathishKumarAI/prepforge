Think of machine learning (ML) as a librarian who can learn patterns from many books and then help you find the right one quickly.  
In **data archiving**, that librarian is asked to store huge piles of old records—emails, sensor logs, or customer chats—in a way that keeps them safe but still lets you retrieve useful information later.

The ML “librarian” first scans all the archived data and learns which parts are important (like names, dates, or keywords) and how they’re related. Then it tags each file with those labels automatically.  
When you need something—say, all complaints about a product in 2023—the system instantly pulls the relevant files from deep storage without sifting through everything manually.

So, ML turns a slow, manual filing job into a smart, searchable archive that saves time and reduces human error.
