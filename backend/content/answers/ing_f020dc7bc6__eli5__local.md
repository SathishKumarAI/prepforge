Think of a system‑design interview like planning a road trip. The interviewer gives you a destination (the problem) and a map (the constraints). You then decide the route: how many stops (components), which highways to take (data flow), and what tools to bring (technologies). A typical “road‑trip” interview lasts **45–60 minutes**—enough time to sketch the map, pick the main roads, discuss detours for traffic (scalability) or bad weather (fault tolerance), and answer questions about each stop.

Key terms:  
- **Component** – a building block of the system (e.g., database, cache).  
- **Scalability** – how well the system handles more users or data.  
- **Fault tolerance** – ability to keep working when something fails.  

Just as you’d plan a trip by laying out your route and anticipating hiccups, in 45–60 minutes you outline the architecture, justify choices, and show how it will stay on track under load.
