Imagine a city map where every landmark is a person or idea and each road shows how they’re connected—friends, coworkers, classmates, or rivals. A knowledge graph is that map for an AI: it stores facts as nodes (the landmarks) and relationships as edges (the roads). When the AI wants to answer a question, it can “walk” along these roads instead of guessing from scratch. For example, if asked about *Elon Musk’s ventures*, the AI follows edges from the node “Elon Musk” to connected nodes like “Tesla,” “SpaceX,” and “Neuralink.” This structured navigation gives the agent context, reduces errors, and lets it combine pieces of information quickly—just as a city map helps you find your way without getting lost.
