Imagine your AI is a chef who can cook many dishes (models). To know if the chef is truly good, you don’t just taste one bite; you run a series of “taste tests” that cover every part of the meal: how spicy it is, how balanced the flavors, whether it looks appealing, and if it satisfies diners on different days.  
In AI terms those “taste tests” are **metrics**.  

* **Accuracy / BLEU / ROUGE** – tells you how close the answer is to the ideal recipe (overall correctness).  
* **Latency** – measures how fast the chef can serve a dish (response time).  
* **Resource usage (CPU/GPU, memory)** – shows how many ingredients and kitchen space the chef needs.  
* **Fairness / Bias tests** – checks that no group of diners is served worse than another.  
* **Robustness** – tests if the chef still makes a good dish when the pantry changes or an ingredient is missing.

Just as a great restaurant uses all these checks to guarantee quality, a solid AI evaluation relies on a balanced set of metrics—accuracy, speed, cost, fairness, and robustness—to see whether the model truly performs well in real‑world kitchens.
