Imagine a robot that learns to pick up objects by watching you do it.  
At first it can’t tell which item is the right one; it just tries and sees if you smile (a *feedback signal*). Each time it succeeds, it remembers what the object looked like and how it moved. Over many trials, it starts to predict the correct pick‑up before you even finish showing it.

In machine learning, that robot is a *model*, the objects are data, and your smiles are *labels* (the right answers). The model looks at lots of labeled examples, adjusts its internal knobs (*parameters*) so its guesses get closer to the labels, and finally can guess correctly on new, unseen data. Just as you train the robot by repetition, we train a machine learning algorithm by feeding it many examples and letting it learn from the feedback.
