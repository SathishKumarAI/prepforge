Imagine you’re baking a cake. **Feature engineering** is like chopping your own ingredients—choosing the exact size and shape of each carrot, slicing onions just right, and deciding whether to add cinnamon or not. You hand‑pick what matters and mix it into the batter yourself.

**Representation learning** is like letting a smart kitchen robot (the neural network) decide how to cut, blend, and combine those ingredients automatically. The robot discovers patterns you might miss—maybe it learns that a tiny dash of nutmeg plus a pinch of salt together create a flavor profile you never thought of.

In 2026, hand‑engineering still shines when data are scarce or the stakes are high. If you have only a few samples, a clear rule (“use age and income to predict loan risk”) can outperform a black‑box model. Also, in regulated fields—healthcare, finance—human‑readable rules help auditors understand decisions. So, while representation learning is powerful, careful feature crafting remains valuable when data are limited or interpretability is crucial.
