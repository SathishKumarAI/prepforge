Imagine your computer is a student who has never taken any class yet. **Machine learning** is like giving that student a huge pile of homework problems and then letting them figure out the patterns on their own, instead of telling them every step.

First, you show the student many examples: pictures of cats and dogs, each labeled “cat” or “dog.” The student looks at all these pictures and tries to spot what makes a cat a cat and a dog a dog. They might notice that cats have pointy ears and dogs have floppy ears. That noticing process is called **training**.

After training, you give the student a brand‑new picture it hasn’t seen before. Because it has learned the patterns, it can now guess whether the new picture is a cat or a dog—this is **prediction**.

So, machine learning is: show many labeled examples → let the computer learn the hidden rules → use those rules to make smart guesses on new data.
